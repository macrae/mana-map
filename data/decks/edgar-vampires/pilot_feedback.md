# Pilot feedback — edgar-vampires

## 2026-08-03 — the direction changes: make tokens, stop filling the graveyard

The deck is strong and the pilot says so. This is a direction, not a complaint.

> "I should be creating crazy tokens not putting stuff in the graveyard, it
> doesn't belong."

Edgar Markov's eminence trigger makes a 1/1 Vampire every time a Vampire is
cast, and the card pays off width with first strike and +1/+1 counters. The
pilot wants the deck built around **that** — go wide, make the tokens matter,
and win with a board.

Explicitly steering AWAY from:

- **Aristocrats-style sacrifice-and-drain** — turning a creature into a
  graveyard event and a life-loss trigger.
- **Graveyard recursion as a value engine** — reanimation, self-mill, "return
  from your graveyard" loops.
- **Drain payoffs that reward death rather than width.**

That is a real strategy and a good one. It is simply not the deck the pilot
wants here, and it competes for the same slots as the token plan.

**A concrete signal, not a hypothetical.** Four cards are leaving this deck for
yawgmoth-swarm, and three of them are exactly that package: **Exquisite Blood**,
**Grave Pact** and **Black Market Connections**. The fourth is **Agadeem's
Awakening // Agadeem, the Undercrypt**, a mass-reanimation land. Treat all four
as gone. If a diagnosis wants to argue one should stay, that argument has to
beat both the pilot's stated preference and the fact that yawgmoth-swarm has
checker-passed kills using them.

**This supersedes the token/graveyard half of the 2026-07-28 entry below.** That
entry named the Grave Pact edict shell and the attack-drain package as engines
to build toward; the pilot has since decided otherwise. The *appetite* in it
still stands.

What "better" means now, in rough priority:

1. **More tokens, and more that cares how many you have** — anthems, mass pump,
   Vampire-making outside the eminence trigger, payoffs that scale with width.
2. **Making the board survive a sweeper.** The pilot did not raise this; a
   go-wide deck folding to one wrath is a known trap and it is fair to name it.
3. **Not more graveyard.** If the evidence says a graveyard card is genuinely
   the best available for a slot, say so plainly and let the pilot decide — but
   do not fill the list with them on ROI alone.

## 2026-08-23 — the pilot closes two of the debrief's open questions

The `debrief` on game 001 raised three open questions and routed all three. The
pilot answered two of them directly, and those answers close them: they are the
pilot's own judgment about his own game, which is the one kind of evidence no
loop here can generate.

**Was Edgar arriving late structural, or piloting?** Piloting.

> "Edgar was NOT structurally late, that was piloting (and in my opinion an
> error)."

This settles a question `diagnose` was routed. It also resolves the disagreement
recorded in the debrief's takeaways: the goldfish reports the commander landing
late (mean cast turn 6.705) and *cannot* explain why, because it casts the
commander on the first affordable turn and models no interaction. The note gave
an attention reason. The pilot now confirms the attention reason and calls it an
error. **Do not open a list-level fix for this** — the deck is not too slow to
deploy its commander; the commander was not deployed. Nothing should be cut or
added on account of it.

**Three board wipes eaten and no protection spell named — is rebuild speed the
whole answer?** No, and there is nothing to fix.

> "yup, I ate three wipes without protection... I have it in the deck, it just
> wasn't in hand. some games that's the way it is."

The protection is present and was not drawn. The goldfish already prices exactly
this: the protection group assembles in 61.0% of games and 49.8% by turn six, so
roughly half of games reach turn six without it, and this was one of them. A
single game that lands on the wrong side of a measured rate is evidence about
variance, not about the list. **Do not treat this as a protection shortfall.**

which is derived: the debrief agent wrote what it could see, correctly, and
rewriting its output to add what only the pilot knows would put the pilot's
