## Summary

A mono-green 60-card teaching deck for brand-new players, played against the four other mono-colour teach decks. Suggested first matchup: teach-black. It is on the bench, unplayed and still being tuned.

The lesson is ramp. [Llanowar Elves](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Llanowar+Elves), [Rampant Growth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Rampant+Growth), [Overgrowth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Overgrowth), [Explore](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Explore) and [Sakura-Tribe Springcaller](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Sakura-Tribe+Springcaller) each make extra mana a different way, and big green bodies like [Colossal Dreadmaw](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Colossal+Dreadmaw) are the payoff. The rest of the deck teaches counters and landfall, reach against flyers, instant versus sorcery, exert, and the end step.


## How it plays

Turn one, play a Forest and [Llanowar Elves](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Llanowar+Elves) if you have it. Then spend the extra mana a turn early: [Rampant Growth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Rampant+Growth) or [Explore](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Explore) on two, [Overgrowth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Overgrowth) on a land, and a six-drop [Colossal Dreadmaw](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Colossal+Dreadmaw) much sooner than a normal curve allows. [Drumhunter](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Drumhunter) and [Sakura-Tribe Springcaller](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Sakura-Tribe+Springcaller) are four-mana ramp bodies that pay you back in later turns.

Every ramp spell is a different rule to learn. A creature that taps for mana can be killed. A land-fetch puts a basic onto the battlefield tapped. An aura on a land is gone if the land is. [Explore](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Explore) lets you play a second land. [Sakura-Tribe Springcaller](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Sakura-Tribe+Springcaller) gives mana in your upkeep that does not vanish between steps.

Landfall is the second thread. [Territorial Scythecat](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Territorial+Scythecat) grows a permanent +1/+1 counter each time a land arrives, and [Canopy Baloth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Canopy+Baloth) gets a temporary +2/+2 until end of turn. Fetch lands and [Explore](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Explore) feed both, which shows why ramp and landfall belong together.

In combat, [Hooded Brawler](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Hooded+Brawler) shows exert: it hits bigger, then skips your next untap. [Giant Spider](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Giant+Spider) is the reach blocker that stops flyers, which green has no other answer to. [Fanatical Strength](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Fanatical+Strength) is an instant trick, while [Rabid Bite](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Rabid+Bite) is a sorcery-speed one-way fight where only your creature deals damage. Putting the trick on the stack in response to removal is where stack resolution gets taught. [Drumhunter](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Drumhunter) draws at your end step if you control a power-5 creature, so the end step gets its own lesson.

The deck is unplayed, so none of this is yet checked against real beginners.
