## Summary

A mono-green 60-card teaching deck for brand-new players, played against the four other mono-colour teach decks. Suggested first matchup: teach-black. It is v1.1.0 and unplayed by people. It is calibrated by argument and by Forge AI round robins, nothing more.

The lesson is still ramp, but less of it. [Llanowar Elves](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Llanowar+Elves) and [Rampant Growth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Rampant+Growth) are the main ramp, with single copies of [Overgrowth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Overgrowth) and [Sakura-Tribe Springcaller](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Sakura-Tribe+Springcaller). Big green bodies like [Colossal Dreadmaw](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Colossal+Dreadmaw) are the payoff. The deck also teaches counters and landfall, exert, the end step, and a new block of reach lessons: reach, deathtouch, vigilance and a creature that leaves a token when it dies. The spiders and [Thornweald Archer](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Thornweald+Archer) carry those.

## How it plays

Turn one, play a Forest and [Llanowar Elves](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Llanowar+Elves) if you have it. Then spend the extra mana early: [Rampant Growth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Rampant+Growth) on two, [Overgrowth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Overgrowth) on a land if you drew it, and a six-drop [Colossal Dreadmaw](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Colossal+Dreadmaw) sooner than a normal curve allows. Ramp is a lighter package now, so the deck does not flood on mana with nothing to cast.

Every ramp card is a different rule. A creature that taps for mana can be killed. A land-fetch puts a basic onto the battlefield tapped. An aura on a land is gone if the land is. [Sakura-Tribe Springcaller](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Sakura-Tribe+Springcaller) gives mana in your upkeep. [Drumhunter](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Drumhunter) draws at your end step if you control a power-5 creature. Each of those is one copy now, so the upkeep and end-step lessons come up less often.

Landfall is the second thread. [Territorial Scythecat](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Territorial+Scythecat) grows a permanent counter each time a land arrives, and [Canopy Baloth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Canopy+Baloth) gets a temporary boost until end of turn. [Rampant Growth](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Rampant+Growth) feeds both.

The third thread is defence against flyers. [Penumbra Spider](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Penumbra+Spider) and [Sentinel Spider](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Sentinel+Spider) have reach, so they block flyers. [Penumbra Spider](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Penumbra+Spider) leaves a token behind when it dies. [Sentinel Spider](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Sentinel+Spider) has vigilance, so it attacks and still blocks. [Thornweald Archer](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Thornweald+Archer) has reach and deathtouch, so a small body trades up with anything it touches. [Plummet](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Plummet) is the clean instant answer to a flyer.

In combat, [Hooded Brawler](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Hooded+Brawler) shows exert: it hits bigger, then skips your next untap. [Fanatical Strength](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Fanatical+Strength) and [Plummet](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Plummet) are instants, while [Rabid Bite](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Rabid+Bite) is a sorcery-speed one-way fight where only your creature deals damage. Putting a trick or [Plummet](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Plummet) on the stack, and answering in response, is where stack resolution gets taught.

The deck is unplayed by people. Forge's AI attacks cautiously with it, so how it plays against a real group is still open.

## Change history

<!-- ctx:gen history -->
- **v1.1.0 · V2** (2026-11-02) — in: Penumbra Spider, Sentinel Spider, Thornweald Archer; out: Giant Spider. _G2: Forge round robins showed Green flooding and losing to flyers; 42% in the confirming sample, up from about 28%_
- **v1.0.0 · V1** (2026-11-02) — first list, 60 cards. _first teaching list: calibrated by argument, unplayed_
<!-- /ctx:gen history -->

First list, drafted from the pilot's brief at about $100 with rares allowed. Both copies of Explore came out and two Plummet went in, saved as v1.0.0, unplayed and calibrated by argument only. Green's only answers to flyers were blocking with reach, and Plummet is also a simple instant for the stack lesson.

v1.1.0: Forge AI round robins, run outside the normal harness, showed the deck flooding on mana with little to spend it on, and losing to flyers. Telling the AI to attack more helped only partly, and so did adding big creatures. Less ramp plus more reach helped most. So all three Giant Spider came out, and Overgrowth, Drumhunter and Sakura-Tribe Springcaller went to one copy each, with Rampant Growth at three. In came three Penumbra Spider, three Sentinel Spider, three Thornweald Archer and one Forest, making 23 lands. New lessons: reach, deathtouch, vigilance, and a creature that leaves a token when it dies. The tradeoff is that the upkeep and end-step lessons (Springcaller, Drumhunter) come up less often. Still unplayed by people.
