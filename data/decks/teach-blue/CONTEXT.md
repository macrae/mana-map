## Summary

Teach Blue is one of five mono-colour teaching decks. Brand-new players play it against the other four. It is saved as v1.0.0, has never been played, and was calibrated by argument only. Budget is about $100, rares allowed, no sideboard.

Blue's lesson is the stack. Players learn counterspells, flash, the untap step and upkeep. They also learn that card flow is a resource and that flyers win games. It is best taught last, after combat is understood. It leans on [Essence Scatter](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Essence+Scatter) and [Quench](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Quench) for the stack, [Pestermite](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Pestermite) and [Spectral Sailor](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Spectral+Sailor) for flash, and [Air Elemental](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Air+Elemental) to win in the air. [Plumecreed Escort](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Plumecreed+Escort) and [Mystic Restraints](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Mystic+Restraints) add two more small lessons.

## How it plays

You hold your mana and let the other player act first. Cheap flash creatures and counterspells make "what can I do right now?" a real question. The deck is built to teach that, and nothing here is measured yet.

The stack lesson is the heart of it. [Essence Scatter](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Essence+Scatter) answers a creature and [Quench](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Quench) answers anything, but the opponent can pay for Quench. Counterspells are capped on purpose so that new players do not sit through games where nothing resolves.

The flash lesson comes next. [Pestermite](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Pestermite) and [Spectral Sailor](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Spectral+Sailor) arrive on the opponent's end step. That teaches that you can cast things outside your own turn. Pestermite also taps or untaps a permanent, which shows the untap step from the other side.

Responding to removal is a second stack lesson. [Plumecreed Escort](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Plumecreed+Escort) gives hexproof in response to removal. Its text has no reminder for hexproof, so the teacher has to explain what the word means.

Stopping attackers is taught with [Mystic Restraints](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Mystic+Restraints) and [Frost Lynx](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Frost+Lynx). Frost Lynx and [Claustrophobia](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Claustrophobia) keep a creature tapped, so the lesson is why it stays tapped, not responding. Mystic Restraints must be cast before attackers are declared, because tapping an attacker does not stop it. New players will want to cast it too late.

Card flow comes from [Opt](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Opt), [Merfolk Looter](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Merfolk+Looter), [Curator of Mysteries](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Curator+of+Mysteries) and [Cloudkin Seer](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Cloudkin+Seer). They show that looking at cards and drawing are different things. Merfolk Looter's tap ability shows summoning sickness, so it teaches why you wait a turn. Curator adds cycling. [Geist of the Archives](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Geist+of+the+Archives) brings upkeep, scrying at the start of each upkeep.

The win is in the air. [Air Elemental](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Air+Elemental), [Cloudkin Seer](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Cloudkin+Seer), [Spectral Sailor](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Spectral+Sailor) and [Pestermite](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Pestermite) fly over ground blockers, which also reinforces what combat taught earlier.

The last change was about the curve. Three mana was overloaded and two mana was empty, so the new cards fill the two-drop slot.

Nothing has been played. Everything above is design intent, not a result.

The brief for the five teach-* decks is mana ramp, the differences between colours, stack resolution, counters, combat and the phases of the turn. Blue carries the stack, counterspells, flash, untap and upkeep lessons. Teach it last, once combat is understood.

Things to explain at the table: hexproof on Plumecreed Escort, casting Mystic Restraints before attackers, Merfolk Looter's summoning sickness, and cycling on Curator of Mysteries.
