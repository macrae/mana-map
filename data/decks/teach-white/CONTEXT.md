## Summary

This is the white deck of five mono-colour teaching decks. It is a 60-card Modern list with no sideboard, built to a budget of about $100 with rares allowed. It is written for brand-new players, and it plays against the other four teach-* decks.

White's job is to teach combat. Small fliers, lifelinkers and tokens attack, the opponent decides how to block, and a few instants and one aura change the result. The deck also teaches casting after combat ([Mardu Hordechief](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Mardu+Hordechief)), +1/+1 counters ([Ajani's Pridemate](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Ajani%27s+Pridemate), [Blade Instructor](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Blade+Instructor)), the upkeep ([Nyx-Fleece Ram](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Nyx-Fleece+Ram)), the end step ([Otherworldly Journey](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Otherworldly+Journey)) and responding at instant speed ([Mighty Leap](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Mighty+Leap), [Village Bell-Ringer](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Village+Bell-Ringer)).

It has not been played yet and is still being tuned. Suggested first game: against teach-red, because red's damage makes the combat lessons bite.

## How it plays

The deck is a cheap creature curve that attacks. [Healer's Hawk](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Healer%27s+Hawk) is the one-drop: it flies and gains life. At two mana, [Ajani's Pridemate](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Ajani%27s+Pridemate) grows whenever you gain life, and [Nyx-Fleece Ram](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Nyx-Fleece+Ram) gains a life each upkeep, so the two feed each other and show that counters stay on a creature.

At three, [Blade Instructor](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Blade+Instructor) puts a counter on an attacker each time it attacks, so the team grows as the game goes on. Play [Mardu Hordechief](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Mardu+Hordechief) after combat so the raid bonus makes a Warrior token. That teaches why the order of phases matters.

At four and five, the deck turns up the pressure. [Falconer Adept](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Falconer+Adept) and [Leonin Warleader](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Leonin+Warleader) make tokens every time they attack, and the Warleader's cats have lifelink. [Serra Angel](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Serra+Angel) attacks without tapping, so she is also ready to block.

White now has an answer to a big creature. [Pacifism](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Pacifism) sits on whatever the opponent cannot be raced past, and it can no longer attack or block. It teaches that some answers are permanent and some are tricks.

The tricks teach the stack. Hold [Mighty Leap](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Mighty+Leap) for a block or an attack: +2/+2 and flying until end of turn flips a trade, or sends a ground creature over the blockers. It is cleaner than a lifegain trick because the result is easy to see on the board. [Village Bell-Ringer](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Village+Bell-Ringer) has flash, so you can cast it mid-combat as a surprise blocker. [Otherworldly Journey](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Otherworldly+Journey) saves a creature from removal and brings it back at the end step with a counter.

White has no ramp, so the deck cannot show mana ramp itself. The other four decks cover that and the differences between colours. Flying, vigilance and lifelink are what white contributes.

## Change history

<!-- ctx:gen history -->
- **v1.0.0 · V1** (2026-11-03) — first list, 60 cards. _first teaching list: calibrated by argument, unplayed_
<!-- /ctx:gen history -->

The first list had Karametra's Blessing as the trick and a third Nyx-Fleece Ram. Both are gone before any game. We took out all three Blessings and one Ram and put in two Mighty Leap and two Pacifism. Why: white gained its first answer to big creatures, and a cleaner trick. Mighty Leap replaces the Blessing as the trick in the stack lesson. Saved and tagged v1.0.0, unplayed, calibrated by argument only.

## Open questions

<!-- ctx:gen queue -->
- Nothing queued for this deck yet. After a game, `/incubate` turns what you noticed into testable claims.
<!-- /ctx:gen queue -->

- Is it fair against the other four teach-* decks? Test: play each of them. The decks should win about evenly, and white should not run away or fold. The goldfish cannot measure this, so it takes real games.
- Does [Nyx-Fleece Ram](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Nyx-Fleece+Ram) stall boards? Test: play games with it and watch whether it blocks forever and slows the game down. Expected effect: it makes games longer but teaches the upkeep clearly. With one fewer copy than the first list, the stall should show less. If it drags, a more aggressive card could replace it.
- Does [Pacifism](https://manamap.seanmacrae.com/viz/index.html?cards=Pacifism) make white too hard to beat? Test: watch whether a new player on the other side has an answer to an enchanted creature, since only a few colours can remove it. Expected effect: it teaches that an answer can be permanent, but it may feel unfair to a deck with no enchantment removal.
- Should the budget buy stronger cards? Test: compare the games as they stand with a version that spends more of the roughly $100 on stronger white cards. This needs the prices from `prices.json` and a pilot decision.
