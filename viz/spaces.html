The Embedding Spaces

What the atlas is drawn from, how it is measured, and which one to ask

Every card in the corpus is a point in a 128-dimensional space. Which space you ask decides what “similar” means — and the five below disagree, on purpose. None of them is the best one; each wins a different question, and the numbers on this page say which.

1 · Where each space comes from

function (ability) the default

A small neural net trained with in-batch InfoNCE: show it a card, a card that should be near it, and a batch of cards that should not. Its positives are mined from this repo's own regexes — the rarest matching role from ROLE_PATTERNS first, then any card sharing ≥2 MECHANICAL_TAGS.

That is the whole story of its strengths and its blind spot. It is excellent at function because function is literally what it was trained on. It discards tribe because “Vampire” is neither a role nor a mechanical tag, so nothing in training ever asked it to care.

cardbert (masked fields) new

BERT, where the tokens are fields. A card is 73 typed fields (cmc, each colour's pip count, 26 keyword flags, what it taps for, power, toughness…) plus 6 text spans, and [CLS]. Masking hides correlated groups of fields and bidirectional attention imputes them; [CLS] is the embedding.

Groups rather than single fields because a recoverability audit found 19 of 73 fields are solved by a linear probe from the others — cmc is the pips added up (R² 0.96). Masking one is arithmetic, not a task.

It reads types, subtypes, keywords and ability spans, so tribe is legible to it in a way it never was to a role regex — and it is weaker at function, which the incumbent was built for.

text baseline (frozen MiniLM)

Sentence vectors from an off-the-shelf model, 384-d and never trained here. It is the bar every trained space has to clear, and it is still the default for commander search because it measures better there.

Never offered in the atlas: the frontend hardcodes 128 dimensions against a headerless binary, so a 384-d file would parse as plausible garbage rather than fail.

layout (color + type)

Colour and type only. It draws the Color + Type map and is never a similarity source — it uses 3.89 of its 128 dimensions, so asking it for neighbours returns arbitrary same-colour cards. Included here as the floor: if another space looks like this one, that is a finding about the other space.

vae (masked imputation) superseded

An earlier attempt: serialise a card into one string, push it through a sentence encoder, reconstruct it behind a variational bottleneck. Abandoned by its own control — a random 128-d projection of frozen MiniLM beat it on theme, and PCA beat it on everything. Kept only as a scored baseline.

It is on this page because it is the clearest evidence for the argument in §3: it retrieves better than cardbert and maps far worse.

3 · How to read the numbers

Every figure below carries its definition, because a number a reader has to look up gets guessed at — and the guesses go one way.

recall@10
Of a hand-authored set of cards that should be neighbours, how many turn up in the nearest 10. The retrieval question. High means “ask it for similar cards and you get them”.
effective dimensions (participation ratio)
How many of the 128 dimensions carry real variance. Low means the space has collapsed — cards piled into a narrow cone, where ranking among them is float noise. Beware: pure noise scores high, so this detects collapse and never proves quality.
neighbour spread
How far apart the top-50 neighbours actually are. Near zero means the ordering is arbitrary — the cards are all equidistant and the “ranking” is rounding error.
centroid headroom
Average a random 20 cards into one vector; how distinguishable are two such averages? This is the deck question. Commander search, build-deck's commander scoring and any deck-gap query are centroid operations, and a space where every deck averages to nearly the same vector has nothing left to rank on.
hard-negative separation
Hand-picked pairs that should not look alike — the fastland/slowland cycle, where one word inverts which stage of the game the card is good in. Higher is better. This is the failure mode where “similar phrasing, different function” gets scored as similar.
candidate pool (100 / 500 / 2000 / …)
Recall measured against a realistic number of competitors rather than all 34,890. Nothing in the product ranks against the whole corpus — commander search ranks 79, Find Similar shows 12, build-deck ranks within a pool. A corpus-scale number is not the decision the product makes.

And every comparison carries the interval on the difference. Two overlapping error bars imply nothing at all; the interval that matters is the one on the gap. Where a gap below is called real, its 95% interval excludes zero.

5 · Which space to ask

Use function when

  • “What else does this job?” — another sweeper, another ramp piece, another way to draw three cards.
  • You are filling a role slot in a 99 and want mechanical substitutes.
  • You are reading a deck's own constellation — deck-map clusters by function, deliberately.

Use cardbert when

  • “What else belongs to this tribe / theme?” — it doubles the incumbent on tribe at pool 500.
  • You are exploring rather than substituting: it separates hard negatives 2.8× better, so things that merely sound alike stay apart.
  • You are doing anything with deck averages — 50× the centroid headroom means decks stay distinguishable when averaged.

Neither — use text

  • Commander search, today. Frozen MiniLM still wins it outright (top-1 0.584 against cardbert's 0.458 and function's 0.410), which is why that is its default.

Nothing has been cut over. function remains the default everywhere. CardBERT is offered because it is better at things the incumbent is worst at — not because it is better.

The honest caveats

  • Neighbours are precomputed. Discover reads a prebuilt table, so retraining a space leaves it answering from the old one until its artifacts are rebuilt.
  • Obsolescence is not selectable. Its 0.75 similarity threshold sits barely above the mean cosine in the function space (+0.719, 31.8% of random pairs) and at the 99.6th percentile in cardbert (−0.002, 0.42%). The thresholds do not transfer, so that feature stays pinned to one space.
  • The projection is itself a model. A space that looks clumpier in 2D is not necessarily clumpier in 128D. Identical settings make the comparison fair; they do not make any single panel true.
  • 12,000 of 34,890 cards are plotted — enough to show structure, not enough to see a small tribe.
