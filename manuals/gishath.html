THE PLAN

THE WAVE AND THE WEB

Everyone at the table knows engine one: the eight-mana swing that free-rolls an army. Version 2 of this deck grew engine two — a checker-verified web that converts any Dinosaur's arrival into removal, ramp, and card draw without attacking at all. Here is how a battlecruiser learns to idle dangerously.

You win with one trigger — and, new in v2, you accrue with one ping. Engine one is unchanged: Gishath, Sun's Avatar hits a player, you reveal that many cards from the top of your library, and every Dinosaur among them lands on the battlefield for free. Thirty-four bodies (deck-facts: 34 threat:body roles in 65 nonland spells) keep the reveal dense, and stack 001 prices one clean 7-damage connection at five bodies, three draws, and a discover 6 — one attack that replaces a turn cycle of casting. Engine two is what the seven-card swap block bought, and it is checker-verified on the exact board the block creates: the enrage ping web. With Forerunner of the Empire out, any Dinosaur entering lets you deal 1 damage to each creature, and stack 005 resolves what a single ping is worth — an opponent's 1/1 dead to state-based actions, their 3/3 exiled under Trapjaw Tyrant, a card off Ripjaw Raptor, a tapped Forest off Ranging Raptors, and a Polyraptor copy token whose own entry re-arms the trigger. No combat, no 8 mana, stoppable at every link. The goldfish says the web is now the deck's most reliable engine: enrage pieces are drawn by turn 6 in 72.1% of games, against 62.0% for the Dinosaur payoff engines.

The honest cost of the headline plan is still the number 8. In the goldfish model the commander's mean cast turn is 7.982 (median 8), and only 8.8% of games cast him by turn 6 — but the simulator explicitly models neither the cost reducers nor the swap block's two accelerants, so treat 7.98 as the ceiling of the problem, not your expectation. Real games beat it three ways: ramp (87.1% of games see ramp or fixing by turn 6, mean turn 2.0 — Cultivate, Kodama's Reach, Nature's Lore, Skyshroud Claim, five rocks, the dorks), the cheat/reducer layer (56.5% by turn 6 — Urza's Incubator and Otepec Huntmaster shave the cast; Belbe's Portal, Monster Manual, and Descendants' Path skip it entirely), and the new big mana: Selvala taps for X equal to your greatest power, which any maindeck 5/5 makes 5-plus, and Regal Behemoth doubles every land tap while you hold the crown. How many turns those two actually shave is deliberately unstated — no artifact quantifies it yet. The deck-facts traps stand: Unclaimed Territory's mana casts creature spells only and can never activate Belbe's Portal or Monster Manual; Secluded Courtyard pays creature-source abilities — Zacama yes, the artifacts no.

One honest sentence belongs in every Rule 0 conversation: the bracket report computes floor 4 because the combo database tags Polyraptor + Forerunner of the Empire as a two-card infinite, and stack 003 verified that exact pairing as bounded — a maximum of seven tokens, reached only by declining a trigger, self-terminating when Forerunner dies to its own sweep — so present the 4, show the verified stack, and let the table land on 3.

What the swaps did not change is the wrath math. The Gishath board still arrives as one sweeper-sized package — the trigger is the wave — so the swing rules govern: attack only when Gishath can actually connect (his own vigilance, trample, and haste do most of it; Rogue's Passage and Kessig Wolf Run force the rest — the connection kit is drawn by turn 6 in 50.9% of games) and preferably with protection held (49.3% by turn 6; Flawless Maneuver is free with your commander out, which makes it the swing-turn card). Assume the sweeper. What v2 adds is a better morning after: the ping web restarts at two cards — Forerunner plus any Dinosaur — while the Gishath plan restarts at 8-plus-tax, and Ranging Raptors' lands and Ripjaw's draws stay banked through the wrath. Pantlaza, Sun-Favored plus the draw engines remain the other rebuild. The one soft point to guard: every web line routes through Forerunner of the Empire, a 1/3 Human Soldier that none of the deck's tribal shields cover — only the board-wide protection instants do.

Pilot TipNever plan to activate Belbe's Portal or Monster Manual with Unclaimed Territory's mana — it casts creature spells only, so it accelerates the cast plan and never the cheat plan; it cannot even cast the non-Dinosaur Forerunner.
The trigger is still the wave. The web is what the tide does while you wait.

Marauding Raptor + Polyraptor: infinite tokens, or a mandatory draw?

This scenario exists to stop a mistake, and it is the reason the swap block stopped at seven: Marauding Raptor + Polyraptor is a DRAW-MACHINE, not a win condition. Mulligan for an engine — either engine. The goldfish's own policy — keep seven-card hands with two to five lands — kept 77.4% of hands with a mean of 0.278 mulligans, and that is your floor, not your standard. On top of the land count, demand acceleration: 87.1% of games see ramp or fixing by turn 6 (mean turn 2.0), so a keepable hand almost always has it — a hand without Cultivate, Kodama's Reach, Nature's Lore, a rock, a dork, or Selvala is below your deck's average and should usually go back. The reason is the naked number the ramp exists to fix: Gishath's mean cast is turn 7.982, and only 8.8% of goldfish games cast him by turn 6.

What v2 changes is that a second kind of hand became keepable: the web curve. Still prize the cheat/reducer layer above raw ramp when you can get both — Urza's Incubator, Otepec Huntmaster, Belbe's Portal, Monster Manual, or Descendants' Path shows up by turn 6 in only 56.5% of games, the deck's scarcest resource. Protection (49.3% by turn 6) and the connection kit (50.9%) are keep bonuses, not requirements. Ship: hands that are all payoff (two or more 5+ drops and no acceleration — mean available mana at turn 6 is just 5.497, and the land-drop hit rate has already fallen to 52.4% by then, so the deck does not organically climb to eight), and hands whose only lands are Unclaimed Territory plus tapped duals, which can't deploy a turn-two rock or Signet on time. Most upgrade columns are shopping lists. This one is a receipt: seven swaps, applied only after five checker-passed stacks priced what they buy — plus the eighth swap we refused, the one live trial still gated behind a scenario, and a bench whose job just changed from step-up package to answer box.

V2's upgrade section is a history, not a shopping list, because the upgrade already happened — and the way it happened is the lesson. Nothing moved off the bench until a checker-passed stack said what it actually did: five verifications first, seven swaps after, one branch refused. Stack 001 priced the core engine on a real board. Stack 002 asked whether Marauding Raptor + Polyraptor was the free infinite the combo database promises and got the answer that shaped everything: a mandatory whole-table DRAW-machine — every trigger forced, the 2-damage ping never lethal to a fresh 5/5, no exit anywhere in the deck, CR 732.4 / 104.4b ending the game for everyone if no opponent intervenes. Stack 003 verified the same Polyraptor engine behind Forerunner of the Empire's OPTIONAL trigger as bounded: max 7 tokens, self-terminating, safe to stop at every link. Stack 004 verified Wrathful Raptors converting Blasphemous Act under Temple Altisaur into a one-sided sweep with 16 redirected at a player. Stack 005 then re-verified the whole ping web on the exact board the proposed block would create — one ping yielding exile, ramp, draw, and a re-arming copy token.

On that evidence the block was applied (HISTORY.md, 2026-07-28, decklist fb99de15993a). In: Selvala, Heart of the Wilds, Regal Behemoth, Forerunner of the Empire, Polyraptor, Wrathful Raptors, Ranging Raptors, Trapjaw Tyrant. Out to the bench: Ravenous Sailback, Verdant Sun's Avatar, Goring Ceratops, Zetalpa, Primal Dawn, Earthshaker Dreadmaw, Drover of the Mighty, and Kinjalli's Sunwing — seven fair-value or redundant bodies traded for a verified second engine plus the acceleration the 7.982 mean cast demanded. And the branch not taken: swap eight would have been Marauding Raptor, and it was declined BECAUSE of stack 002's verdict. It stays benched under a warning label — never promote it without first adding a loop-breaker you control, because until then its Polyraptor pairing is a liability wearing a wincon's clothes.

The engine does not yet consult passing stacks, so the report presents 4; the verified-reality argument is 3, and the pregame conversation is where you make it, stack in hand.

What remains on the bench is tech and spares, not a queue: Marauding Raptor under its warning, a spare Wakening Sun's Avatar beside the maindeck copy, graveyard and meta options like Deathgorge Scavenger and Runic Armasaur, and further enrage bodies (Bellowing Aegisaur, Frilled Deathspitter, Apex Altisaur) for tables where the web wants depth. Gishath connects: what seven damage actually unleashes

    Type-line check from oracle text: Regisaur Alpha (Creature — Dinosaur, 4/4), Ripjaw Raptor (Creature — Dinosaur, 4/5), Carnage Tyrant (Creature — Dinosaur, 7/6), and Ravenous Sailback (Creature — Dinosaur, 3/4) are all Dinosaur creature cards and may be put onto the battlefield. You choose to put all four Dinosaurs onto the battlefield — a single event, so they enter simultaneously.

Regisaur Alpha itself is not granted haste ('Other Dinosaurs'). All your creatures also have trample from Garruk's Uprising.

Verified
  1. Polyraptor resolves.
    Polyraptor is a creature spell with no targets; it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under your control as a 5/5 Dinosaur.
    CR 608.3a — “If the object that’s resolving has no targets, it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under the control of the spell’s controller.”

    CR 603.6a — “Enters-the-battlefield abilities trigger when a permanent enters the battlefield.”
    CR 603.2 — “Whenever a game event or game state matches a triggered ability’s trigger event, that ability automatically triggers.”
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority.”
  3. All players pass priority; the ping trigger resolves.
    The active player receives priority, and when all players pass in succession the trigger resolves: Marauding Raptor deals 2 damage to Polyraptor. That damage is marked on Polyraptor.     CR 117.4 — “If all players pass in succession (that is, if all players pass without taking any actions in between passing), the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves”
    CR 302.7 — “Damage dealt to a creature by a source with neither wither nor infect is marked on that creature”

    Before any player receives priority, state-based actions are performed. Polyraptor has 2 damage marked against toughness 5 — 2 is not greater than or equal to 5, so it is not dealt lethal damage and is not destroyed. The 2 damage stays marked until cleanup, but each individual Polyraptor is pinged exactly once (only when it enters), so no Polyraptor ever accumulates more than 2 damage. No token ever dies in this loop.
    CR 117.5 — “Each time a player would get priority, the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event”
    CR 704.5g — “If a creature has toughness greater than 0, it has damage marked on it, and the total damage marked on it is greater than or equal to its toughness, that creature has been dealt lethal damage and is destroyed.”
    CR 120.6 — “Damage marked on a creature remains until the cleanup step”
  5. Polyraptor's Enrage triggers and resolves, creating a token copy.
    Being dealt damage matches Enrage's trigger condition ('Whenever this creature is dealt damage, create a token that's a copy of this creature'). It contains no 'may' — it is mandatory. After priority passes again, it resolves and a token copy of Polyraptor is put onto the battlefield.
    CR 603.2 — “that ability automatically triggers”
    CR 701.7a — “To create one or more tokens with certain characteristics, put the specified number of tokens with the specified characteristics onto the battlefield.”

    The copy acquires only Polyraptor's copiable values — including its rules text, so every token has Enrage, and its printed 5/5 power/toughness. Damage marked on the original is NOT among the copiable values (those are only the values derived from the printed text), so each new token enters with no damage marked. Every iteration starts from a full-toughness 5/5.
    CR 707.2 — “The copiable values are the values derived from the text printed on the object (that text being name, mana cost, color indicator, card type, subtype, supertype, rules text, power, toughness, and/or loyalty)”
  7. The loop closes: the token entering re-triggers Marauding Raptor.
    The token is another creature you control entering, so Marauding Raptor's ping triggers again (mandatory), deals 2 to the new token (2 < 5, it survives per step 4), the token's Enrage triggers (mandatory), a new token is created, and that token entering triggers the ping again. Each iteration adds one 5/5 Polyraptor token with 2 damage marked and nothing ever leaves the battlefield. This is a set of actions that could be repeated indefinitely — a loop in the rules' sense — and every action in the repeated sequence is mandatory.
    CR 603.6a — “Enters-the-battlefield abilities trigger when a permanent enters the battlefield.”
    CR 732.1b — “Occasionally the game gets into a state in which a set of actions could be repeated indefinitely (thus creating a “loop”).”
  8. What could break it: players DO receive priority inside the loop.
    Triggers resolve one at a time; each one is put on the stack when a player would receive priority, the active player then receives priority, and every trigger only resolves after all players pass. So every iteration contains real priority windows in which any player could cast an instant or activate an ability — e.g. killing Marauding Raptor (no more pings; the loop ends and you keep the tokens created so far) or removing the Polyraptors. From YOUR position that is impossible: you hold no instants and control no sacrifice outlet, so you cannot break the loop. Your opponents' boards are empty; whether they can break it depends on their unknown hands, and nothing can force them to.
    CR 603.3 — “its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority”
    CR 117.3b — “The active player receives priority after a spell or ability (other than a mana ability) resolves.”
    CR 117.4 — “If all players pass in succession (that is, if all players pass without taking any actions in between passing), the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves”
  9. Game outcome: if no player intervenes, the game is a draw.
    The repeated sequence itself (enters trigger → ping → Enrage trigger → token → enters trigger ...) contains only mandatory actions and has no way to stop on its own — no token dies, no choice exists within any trigger. Under the loop rules, a loop containing only mandatory actions ends the game in a draw. If some player injects an optional action (removal in a priority window, per step 8), the loop is broken and the game continues; but no player can be forced to do so, and if no one does, the whole game — all three players — ends in a draw.
    CR 732.4 — “If a loop contains only mandatory actions, the game is a draw.”
    CR 104.4b — “If a game that’s not using the limited range of influence option (including a two-player game) somehow enters a “loop” of mandatory actions, repeating a sequence of events with no way to stop, the game is a draw.”
    CR 104.4b — “Loops that contain an optional action don’t result in a draw.”
