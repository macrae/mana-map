Start with the clause the whole deck is built on: your commander waits in the command zone, and you may cast him from there for as long as the game lasts (CR 903.4). That guarantee is why a 65-spell list can afford to aim half its mana at one eight-drop — Gishath, Sun's Avatar is not a card you hope to draw, he is an appointment you keep. Every mulligan verdict in this issue assumes it: no opening hand needs the commander, because the zone already holds him.

Now the compounding clause. Each time you cast him from the zone after the first, he costs two more generic mana (CR 903.8). Read the tax ladder against Ledger's mana table and the problem states itself: mean available mana on turn six is 5.486, the first cast is eight, the second is ten, the third is twelve. One recast is a real plan; two is a fantasy. The record therefore holds that protection is the better spend — Flawless Maneuver is free with your commander on the battlefield, Heroic Intervention is two mana, and either is cheaper than the recast rider you would otherwise pay. That is the commander-insurance trade, and this deck takes it every time.

Color identity next: every card in the 99 must fit inside Gishath's white, red, and green (CR 903.4, 903.5c). The identity buys the best board-wide protection instants in the format and the entire enrage package — and it permanently locks out counterspells. When Know Your Enemy tells you the combo matchup is a race, this is the clause that made it one.

The short clock is real here, not a coincidence. A player dealt 21 or more combat damage by the same commander loses the game (CR 903.10a), and Gishath swings for seven with vigilance, trample, and haste: three connections end a player, two if Overwhelming Stampede or Kessig Wolf Run stretches the arithmetic. Against a 40-life table thick with lifegain, the 21-point line is frequently the nearer finish — count both clocks every combat.

And the coach's closing paragraph, because the zone is also a stage: an eight-mana commander is the best political cover in the format. Nobody wraths a board of ramp pieces, and nobody counts you as the threat while the big man sits in the zone. The table's patience is a resource. Spend six turns of it building two engines, and when you finally make the cast, do it with a shield in hand and no apology.

THE PLAN

ACT ONE IS A BLUFF

They see an eight-mana commander and they relax. Good — while they wait for act two, you're loading two engines that pay off whether or not the big turn ever comes.

Two engines, and only one of them needs your commander. That is the whole plan, and it is why the first six turns are never a waiting room. The median game casts Gishath, Sun's Avatar on turn eight, so a deck that only starts when he lands starts too late. Build both, and let the table relax at an eight-drop for as long as it likes.

Engine one is the swing. Connect, and every Dinosaur in the reveal walks onto the battlefield for free -- stack 001 turns one seven-damage hit into five new bodies, three cards and a discover at X=6. Take that discover off your biggest toughness before any token enters. Get the order right and the swing pays twice.

Engine two never attacks. With Forerunner of the Empire out, any Dinosaur entering lets you ping the whole board for 1, and stack 005 cashes that single point five ways: a dead token, an opposing 3/3 exiled under Trapjaw Tyrant, a card off Ripjaw Raptor, a tapped Forest off Ranging Raptors, and a fresh 5/5 off Polyraptor. Take the ping every turn you can. The damage wipes off at cleanup; the yield does not.

Now the one thing that beats you, named out loud: you deploy in a single wave, so a sweeper aimed at your swing turn takes all of it. Swing holding Heroic Intervention, Flawless Maneuver, Boros Charm or Akroma's Will -- you will have one by turn six in roughly half your games (48.5%), so steer toward the turn you do. If somebody wipes you anyway, rebuild the Human first: the web comes back on two cards, the commander comes back at eight plus tax.

And you own a wipe that only hurts them. Blasphemous Act under Temple Altisaur deals your other Dinosaurs exactly 1 apiece and 13 to everything else, and stack 004 sends 16 upstairs at a player on the way through. Cast it from behind. You come out further ahead than you went in.

THE ROSTER

EVERY SLEEVE EARNS IT

Ninety-nine slots, ranked the way the deck actually leans on them: the two engines first, the martyr package, the money, the shields — and a mana base with more jobs than a summer resume.

The two engines

Forerunner of the Empire

Bounce + ETBCopy + ETB

The hub of engine two: it stacks a Dinosaur on top when it enters, and every Dinosaur entering afterwards offers a 1-damage sweep that switches enrage on (stacks 003 and 005). Protect the 1/3 Human with your board-wide instants, because Temple Altisaur will not, the typal lands will not, and Gishath's reveal never finds it.

Polyraptor

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Damage in, another Polyraptor out -- one ping makes a 5/5 (stack 005), and the full Forerunner chain caps at seven tokens before shutting itself off (stack 003). Never share a battlefield with Marauding Raptor without an exit: stack 002 makes that loop mandatory, and mandatory loops draw the game.

Ranging Raptors

Ramp + Cost Reduction

Ramp that runs on damage: each ping fetches a tapped basic, verified live in stack 005. Anyone's damage counts, so let it block, let it get swept, let it soak your own sweeps.

Ripjaw Raptor

Counterspell + DrawDraw + Discard

Damage in, card out, verified drawing live in stacks 004 and 005. Let it get hit -- by blockers, by your own pings, by anybody's spite; that is the business model, not an accident.

Trapjaw Tyrant

Removal + Death Trigger

Repeatable exile removal, one ping one hostage (stack 005). Track what it is holding -- the moment Trapjaw leaves the battlefield, everything comes home at once.

Pantlaza, Sun-Favored

Free spells off every Dinosaur entering, once a turn, with no combat and no eight mana required -- the engine that still works the turn after a wrath. Sequencing is everything: stack 001 verifies that you take the discover off your largest toughness before any token enters.

Garruk's Uprising

Blink + ETBBounce + ETB

Trample for the whole team plus a card for every 4-power body that enters -- stack 001 draws exactly three off one reveal. Know the cutoff: 3-power tokens miss, so do not count them.

Curious Altisaur

Every Dinosaur that connects turns into a card, and reach keeps fliers honest while it happens. Attack wide and read the payoff.

The one-sided apocalypse

Blasphemous Act

Your catch-up button, and with Temple Altisaur out it is a sweeper you are on the right side of. Stack 004 verifies the full package throwing 16 at a player -- and budget for the Altisaur itself, which eats its own 13.

Temple Altisaur

Your enrage governor: every damage source hits your other Dinosaurs for exactly 1, which turns sweepers into triggers. Two traps verified in stack 004 -- it never shields itself, and a Human is not a Dinosaur, so Forerunner of the Empire stays exposed.

Wrathful Raptors

Trample + Damage Trigger

Every point your Dinosaurs absorb gets thrown at something that is not one, which under Blasphemous Act is 16 upstairs (stack 004). One honest unknown: whether a Forerunner ping with this out still marks damage on your own Dinosaurs is unresolved rules territory -- do not bank the enrage yield with both on the battlefield.
