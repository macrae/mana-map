THE PLAN

BATTLECRUISER IN NAME ONLY

Everyone at the table thinks they know this deck: big lizards, big turns, fair fun. So why does the pilot's manual tell you to plan your attack like a combo turn? Because under the scales this is a linear critical-mass deck — seven quiet turns of deflection compressed into one protected pivot.

You win with one trigger. Gishath, Sun's Avatar hits a player, you reveal that many cards from the top of your library, and every Dinosaur among them lands on the battlefield for free. Thirty-four bodies (deck-facts: 34 threat:body roles in 65 nonland spells) make the reveal dense, and stack 001 shows exactly what a single clean connection is worth: seven damage became five new bodies, three cards drawn, and a discover 6 — one attack that replaces an entire turn cycle of casting. There is no combo behind the curtain: the maindeck contains zero combo lines (deck-facts: 0 contained, 0 two-card infinites), the bracket floor is 3, and the deck is exactly what it looks like — a fair battlecruiser that wins through combat, twice if it has to.

The honest cost of that plan is the number 8. In the goldfish model the commander's mean cast turn is 7.932 (median 8), and only 9% of games cast him by turn 6 — but the simulator explicitly does not model cost reducers or cheat-into-play effects, so treat 7.93 as the ceiling of the problem, not your expectation. Your real games beat it two ways: ramp (82% of games see ramp or fixing by turn 6, mean turn 2.2 — Cultivate, Kodama's Reach, Nature's Lore, Skyshroud Claim, five rocks, four dorks) and the cheat/reducer layer (56% drawn by turn 6 — Urza's Incubator and Otepec Huntmaster shave the cast; Belbe's Portal, Monster Manual, and Descendants' Path skip it entirely). One trap from deck-facts worth memorizing: Unclaimed Territory's mana casts creature spells only — it can never activate Belbe's Portal or Monster Manual. Secluded Courtyard pays creature-source abilities, so it activates Zacama, but not the artifacts either.

The swing itself must be planned like a combo turn, because the deck's structural weakness is that the payoff arrives as one wrath-sized package. A board built by one Gishath trigger is one sweeper from zero, and you cannot deploy in waves — the trigger is the wave. So the strategic frame's rule governs: swing only when Gishath can actually connect (his own vigilance, trample, and haste do most of the work; Rogue's Passage and Kessig Wolf Run force the rest — the connection kit is drawn by turn 6 in 51% of games) and preferably with protection held (49% by turn 6; Flawless Maneuver is free with your commander out, which makes it the swing-turn card). Assume the sweeper. When it comes anyway, Pantlaza, Sun-Favored plus the draw engines (Garruk's Uprising, Curious Altisaur, Bonehoard Dracosaur) are the rebuild — Pantlaza needs neither combat nor eight mana, and keeps converting every Dinosaur you cast into more spells the turn after the board dies.

Pilot TipNever plan to activate Belbe's Portal or Monster Manual with Unclaimed Territory's mana — it casts creature spells only, so it accelerates the cast plan and never the cheat plan.

Verified line 002

Marauding Raptor + Polyraptor: infinite tokens, or a mandatory draw?

This scenario exists to stop a mistake, and the checker verdict is unambiguous: Marauding Raptor + Polyraptor — both on this deck's bench — is a DRAW-MACHINE, not a win condition. Every trigger in the loop is mandatory: Polyraptor resolves, Marauding pings it for 2, the 5/5 shrugs it off and copies itself, the copy enters, the ping is forced again, forever. As built, this deck has no exit — no sacrifice outlet, no instant-speed way to remove Marauding Raptor — so the loop either gets broken by an opponent, on their terms and at a token count they choose, or CR 732.4 / 104.4b end the game in a draw for the whole table. Never present this pair as a wincon and never promote it from the bench without first adding a loop-breaker you control.

The question
Resolve Polyraptor and walk the resulting loop: each copy that enters is pinged for 2 (mandatory), each ping triggers enrage creating another copy (mandatory), each copy entering triggers another ping. Does any token ever die (2 damage per ping, 5 toughness, does damage accumulate on distinct tokens)? Is there any legal way for the controller to stop the loop from this position? Under the comprehensive rules on mandatory loops that no player can interrupt, what is the GAME OUTCOME — infinite tokens and a win, or a drawn game? This verdict decides whether the sideboard pair is a wincon or a liability.

Result. VERDICT: as built, this sideboard pair is a LIABILITY, not a wincon. Resolving Polyraptor with Marauding Raptor on the battlefield locks the table into a mandatory loop: every ping is forced, 2 damage never kills a fresh 5/5 token (each token is pinged exactly once and copies enter with no damage marked), and neither trigger contains a choice. You cannot exit — no instants, no sacrifice outlet. Opponents receive priority between every trigger resolution and MAY break the loop (killing Marauding Raptor is the clean out, leaving you the tokens made so far — but the break happens on an opponent's terms, at a token count they choose). If nobody acts, CR 732.4 / 104.4b end the game in a draw for all players. To promote this pair, the deck must add its own loop-breaker: a free sacrifice outlet, an instant-speed way to remove Marauding Raptor, or an effect that makes the ping lethal / the tokens bigger-than-lethal converts the draw engine into an arbitrarily-large token wincon. Without one, boarding both cards in threatens your own game.

You are the most telegraphed deck at the table. So the swing waits for cover, and Flawless Maneuver is THE swing-turn card because it is free while you control your commander. On connection: the trigger reveals cards equal to damage DEALT, so a Kessig Wolf Run pump grows the reveal — but only if the damage lands. Rogue's Passage beats the pump whenever a real blocker exists: a 4-toughness chump eats lethal-assignment and shaves your reveal from 7 to 3 even through trample, a deathtouch or first-strike blocker kills the commander and hands you the tax. The goldfish's own policy — keep seven-card hands with two to five lands — kept 77% of hands with a mean of 0.28 mulligans, and that is your floor, not your standard. On top of the land count, demand acceleration: 82% of games see ramp or fixing by turn 6 (mean turn 2.2), so a keepable hand almost always has it — a hand without Cultivate, Kodama's Reach, Nature's Lore, a rock, or a dork is below your deck's average and should usually go back.
