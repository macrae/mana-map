THE PLAN

ACT ONE IS A BLUFF

They see an eight-mana commander and they relax. Good — while they wait for act two, you're loading two engines that pay off whether or not the big turn ever comes.

Let me tell you what the table thinks you're playing, and then let me tell you what you're actually playing. They see an eight-mana commander and they relax. Good. Let them. Because this deck is a two-act play, and Gishath, Sun's Avatar is only the second act. Act one is six quiet turns of ramp with a smile on your face -- 35 lands, five rocks, three dorks, five land-ramp spells, three cost reducers -- while the deck assembles two engines that both convert one small event into a pile of material. You are not behind during those turns. You are loading.

Engine one is the headline: when Gishath connects, you reveal that many cards and every Dinosaur among them hits the battlefield free. That's not hope, that's a verified line -- stack 001 resolves one 7-damage connection into five new bodies, three Garruk's Uprising draws, and a Pantlaza discover at X=6, with the sequencing lesson attached: take the discover off your biggest toughness BEFORE any token enters, because Pantlaza's once-a-turn clause means the trigger you waste is a trigger you never get back. Engine two doesn't need combat at all: the enrage ping web. With Forerunner of the Empire on the table, any Dinosaur entering lets you ping every creature for 1 -- and stack 005 shows one voluntary ping buying an exiled blocker (Trapjaw Tyrant), a card (Ripjaw Raptor), a land (Ranging Raptors), a fresh 5/5 (Polyraptor), and a dead opposing token, all for damage that wipes off at cleanup. One ping, five payoffs, and the Polyraptor copy re-arms the trigger.

Now the coach's honesty, because I only promise wins I can deliver: the naked numbers say Gishath's mean cast turn is 7.969, and he's down by turn six in just 8.3% of simulated games -- and that simulation can't even see Selvala, Regal Behemoth, or your reducers, so treat it as the floor of the problem, not your ceiling. What it CAN see is your act-one machinery firing: ramp in hand by turn six in 86.9% of games, the enrage engine in 72.6%, a Dinosaur payoff in 62.3%, a reducer or cheat piece in 56.2%. Your job in act one is to spend that abundance while respecting the one thing that beats you: the board wipe. This deck deploys in one wave -- the trigger IS the wave -- so you swing big only with a shield held (you'll have one by turn six about 48.5% of the time; play like it's coming), and when somebody does wipe you, rebuild the web first. It restarts at two cheap cards; the commander plan restarts at eight plus tax. And you have your own wipe that isn't really a wipe: stack 004 verifies Blasphemous Act under Temple Altisaur and Wrathful Raptors as a one-sided apocalypse that throws 16 damage at a player on the way through.

Last thing, and this is your pregame speech: the bracket report computes a floor of 4, driven entirely by a database tag calling Polyraptor plus Forerunner a two-card infinite. Stack 003 put that tag under oath and it came back bounded -- a maximum of SEVEN tokens, reached only by declining one trigger, and the chain kills its own engine on the third sweep. So you present the deck at 4 and you argue it at 3, out loud, before the game, with the verified line in hand. That conversation isn't a weakness. That conversation is you running the table before a single land hits it.

Gishath connects: what seven damage actually unleashes

The court calls its favorite witness: Gishath's own text -- 'reveal that many cards from the top of your library. Put any number of Dinosaur creature cards from among them onto the battlefield.' Seven damage, seven cards, four Dinosaurs, and nine triggered abilities all demanding a place on the stack. The clause that decides the whole case is Pantlaza's rider, 'Do this only once each turn' -- and per CR 603.2h, that rider polices the TRIGGERING itself, not merely the payoff. Once you've discovered this turn, later entries don't produce a trigger at all; the ability simply declines to appear. So the holding, plain enough to carry to any table: you control the order, so put the Carnage Tyrant discover on top and the Regisaur Alpha token on the bottom -- discover at X=6 first, token last, and the token enters into a world where Pantlaza has nothing left to say. Misorder it and you discover at X=3 instead. Perfectly legal, and strictly worse -- the rules punish nothing here except carelessness.

The question
Resolve Gishath's trigger putting ALL FOUR Dinosaur creature cards from the stipulated reveal onto the battlefield (Regisaur Alpha, Ripjaw Raptor, Carnage Tyrant, Ravenous Sailback — confirm each is a Dinosaur creature card and that Cultivate/Mountain/Path go to the bottom in random order). The four enter simultaneously: how many Garruk's Uprising draws (which of the four have power 4+), does Regisaur Alpha's token trigger fire and does THAT token also trigger Uprising and Pantlaza, which single Pantlaza discover may you take (choose the best toughness among simultaneous enterers — the once-per-turn constraint), and do the entering Dinosaurs have haste for a second combat? Final count of new bodies, cards drawn, and the one discover.

Result. One 7-damage Gishath connection becomes five new bodies, three cards drawn, and a discover 6. All four revealed Dinosaur creature cards (Regisaur Alpha, Ripjaw Raptor, Carnage Tyrant, Ravenous Sailback) enter simultaneously; Cultivate, Mountain, and Path to Exile go to the bottom in random order. Garruk's Uprising draws exactly 3 (Sailback's power 3 misses; the 3/3 token's power 3 misses). Pantlaza triggers four times but discovers exactly once — the pilot stacks the Carnage Tyrant trigger on top so the X=6 discover resolves FIRST and Regisaur Alpha's token trigger resolves LAST; the token then enters after the once-per-turn action is used and cannot re-trigger Pantlaza. Everything new has haste and trample but cannot attack this turn without an extra-combat effect.

Verified line 002

Marauding Raptor + Polyraptor: infinite tokens, or a mandatory draw?

A cautionary case, and a standing verdict this court is proud of. The exhibit is CR 732.4 with 104.4b: a loop of mandatory actions that no player can stop does not win the game -- it DRAWS the game, for everyone. Read the two texts side by side and savor the absence: Marauding Raptor's ping is 'deals 2 damage to it' -- no 'may.' Polyraptor's enrage is 'create a token' -- no 'may.' Two damage never kills a fresh 5/5 token, each new token enters clean and gets pinged exactly once, and this deck fields no sacrifice outlet and no free exit. Resolve Polyraptor with Marauding Raptor on the battlefield and you have not assembled a win; you have filed a motion to dismiss the entire game, granted automatically unless an opponent chooses to intervene -- on their terms, at a token count they select, most cleanly by killing the Raptor. The holding: the combo database calls this pair an infinite; the record calls it a liability. That is the whole reason Marauding Raptor resides in the sideboard while Polyraptor plays -- and any future reunion requires a loop-breaker to be sleeved first.

The question
Resolve Polyraptor and walk the resulting loop: each copy that enters is pinged for 2 (mandatory), each ping triggers enrage creating another copy (mandatory), each copy entering triggers another ping. Does any token ever die (2 damage per ping, 5 toughness, does damage accumulate on distinct tokens)? Is there any legal way for the controller to stop the loop from this position? Under the comprehensive rules on mandatory loops that no player can interrupt, what is the GAME OUTCOME — infinite tokens and a win, or a drawn game? This verdict decides whether the sideboard pair is a wincon or a liability.

Result. VERDICT: as built, this sideboard pair is a LIABILITY, not a wincon. Resolving Polyraptor with Marauding Raptor on the battlefield locks the table into a mandatory loop: every ping is forced, 2 damage never kills a fresh 5/5 token (each token is pinged exactly once and copies enter with no damage marked), and neither trigger contains a choice. You cannot exit — no instants, no sacrifice outlet. Opponents receive priority between every trigger resolution and MAY break the loop (killing Marauding Raptor is the clean out, leaving you the tokens made so far — but the break happens on an opponent's terms, at a token count they choose). If nobody acts, CR 732.4 / 104.4b end the game in a draw for all players. To promote this pair, the deck must add its own loop-breaker: a free sacrifice outlet, an instant-speed way to remove Marauding Raptor, or an effect that makes the ping lethal / the tokens bigger-than-lethal converts the draw engine into an arbitrarily-large token wincon. Without one, boarding both cards in threatens your own game.

Forerunner of the Empire + Polyraptor: the controllable token bomb

The case that decides your bracket conversation. Exhibit one, CR 603.5: an optional triggered ability's choice is made on resolution -- every one of Forerunner of the Empire's sweeps asks 'you may,' and you answer each time, in the moment. Which means this so-called infinite has a steering wheel and a brake. The finding, verified step by step: the ceiling is SEVEN Polyraptor tokens -- eight Polyraptors counting the original -- and reaching seven requires the connoisseur's move of DECLINING one intermediate trigger so a token enters before the final sweep; say yes to everything like an amateur and you cap at six. The chain then closes its own file: Forerunner's third sweep is its own third point of damage, and the 1/3 is destroyed by the very engine it was running. The holding: bounded above by seven, terminated by its own operation, stoppable at every single link. When the combo database testifies 'infinite,' this record is your cross-examination -- present the deck at bracket 4, argue it at 3, and enter stack 003 into evidence.

The question
Walk the chain while you keep saying yes: each sweep deals 1 to each creature; each surviving Polyraptor (original + tokens) triggers enrage, creating that many new copies; each new copy entering gives Forerunner another optional trigger. Track accumulated damage: Forerunner dies after how many sweeps (3 toughness), each Polyraptor dies at 5 accumulated damage, and new tokens enter with 0 damage. What is the maximum number of Polyraptor tokens this generates before the chain self-terminates (Forerunner dead), is the chain safely stoppable at any point by declining, and what dies on the opponents' boards (the 2/2)? Verified line 004

Wrathful Raptors + Blasphemous Act + Temple Altisaur: the one-sided apocalypse

My favorite kind of case: one where the deceased gives testimony. Blasphemous Act deals 13 to each of nine creatures as a single event, and Temple Altisaur's prevention shield -- 'all but 1' -- covers every Dinosaur you control EXCEPT the one drafting the policy: Altisaur is not 'another Dinosaur' to itself, takes the full 13, and dies. But observe CR 113.7a, the finest clause in the book: a trigger whose source has left the battlefield resolves using last known information. The dead Altisaur was a Dinosaur dealt 13, so Wrathful Raptors throws 13 on its behalf. Better still, CR 704.5g clears every lethally-wounded creature off the battlefield BEFORE the redirect triggers choose targets -- the opponents' creatures are already gone, so the only legal non-Dinosaur targets left are players. Sixteen damage upstairs, a Ripjaw card drawn, your board intact minus one noble martyr. The holding: the killing blow still triggers, and the dead still owe testimony -- your board wipe is the only one at the table that comes with a forwarding address.

The question
Resolve Blasphemous Act through Temple Altisaur's prevention: exactly how much damage is DEALT to each of your four Dinosaurs (Altisaur takes 13 and dies; the other three take 1 each — do they survive), how many Wrathful Raptors redirect triggers fire and for how much each (the dealt amount after prevention — does the dead Altisaur's 13 still trigger a redirect via last-known information, and is Altisaur 'another Dinosaur' for its own prevention), does Ripjaw Raptor's enrage draw a card, what legal non-Dinosaur targets remain after the sweep kills the opponents' creatures, and what are the final life totals if all redirects go at opponent 1?

Result. One-sided apocalypse confirmed with one correction of scale: Temple Altisaur eats its own unprevented 13 and dies (it is not 'another Dinosaur' to itself), but its death converts into a 13-point Wrathful Raptors redirect via last known information. Your other three Dinosaurs are each dealt exactly 1, survive, and each throw 1 more. Because state-based actions destroy all six lethally-damaged creatures BEFORE the triggers are put on the stack, the opponents' creatures are already gone when redirect targets are chosen — the only legal non-Dinosaur targets are players. All redirects at opponent 1 total 16: they drop to 10, alive. Ripjaw's enrage draws you a card.

Hand clinic. Fall in, and smile -- the numbers are on your side. The simulation keeps 77.1% of opening sevens and averages 0.28 mulligans a game, so this deck WANTS to keep. Your whole job is spotting the minority of hands that are lying to you.

Case A-001 Gishath connects: what seven damage actually unleashes
Rules version 2026-06-19 · status: cleared in 2 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
  1. Gishath's trigger (the only object on the stack) resolves. You reveal the stipulated seven cards and follow the trigger's instructions in order.
    Type-line check from oracle text: Regisaur Alpha (Creature — Dinosaur, 4/4), Ripjaw Raptor (Creature — Dinosaur, 4/5), Carnage Tyrant (Creature — Dinosaur, 7/6), and Ravenous Sailback (Creature — Dinosaur, 3/4) are all Dinosaur creature cards and may be put onto the battlefield. Cultivate (Sorcery), Mountain (land), and Path to Exile (Instant) are not Dinosaur creature cards; per the trigger's text they go on the bottom of your library in a random order. You choose to put all four Dinosaurs onto the battlefield — a single event, so they enter simultaneously.
    CR 608.2c — “The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written.”
    CR 603.6a — “Each time an event puts one or more permanents onto the battlefield, all permanents on the battlefield (including the newcomers) are checked for any enters-the-battlefield triggers that match the event.”
  2. Static abilities apply the moment the four are on the battlefield: Regisaur Alpha's 'Other Dinosaurs you control have haste' and Garruk's Uprising's 'Creatures you control have trample'.
    Because Regisaur Alpha enters simultaneously with the others, its haste-granting static ability applies to Ripjaw Raptor, Carnage Tyrant, and Ravenous Sailback (and to Pantlaza and Gishath) immediately — the newcomers are never on the battlefield without haste. Regisaur Alpha itself is not granted haste ('Other Dinosaurs'). All your creatures also have trample from Garruk's Uprising.
    CR 603.6b — “Continuous effects that modify characteristics of a permanent do so the moment the permanent is on the battlefield (and not before then). The permanent is never on the battlefield with its unmodified characteristics.”
  3. Count the triggers from the simultaneous entry. Garruk's Uprising's 'Whenever a creature you control with power 4 or greater enters, draw a card' checks each entering creature.
    One event, four occurrences — Uprising triggers separately for each qualifying enterer. Regisaur Alpha (power 4), Ripjaw Raptor (power 4), and Carnage Tyrant (power 7) qualify: THREE Uprising triggers. Ravenous Sailback is a 3/4 — power 3 — so it does NOT trigger Uprising.
    CR 603.2c — “An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs. However, it can trigger repeatedly if one event contains multiple occurrences.”
  4. Pantlaza's ability checks the same event. Its 'Do this only once each turn' rider restricts triggering, not just resolution — but you have not discovered yet this turn.
    Because you have not yet taken the discover action this turn, Pantlaza triggers once for each of the four entering Dinosaurs (X would be 4 for Regisaur Alpha, 5 for Ripjaw Raptor, 6 for Carnage Tyrant, 4 for Ravenous Sailback — X is the entering creature's toughness). Regisaur Alpha's own ETB (create a token) and Ravenous Sailback's modal ETB also trigger: nine triggered abilities total are waiting.
    CR 603.2h — “A triggered ability may have an instruction followed by “Do this only once each turn.” This ability triggers only if its source’s controller has not yet taken the indicated action that turn.”
  5. Gishath's trigger finishes resolving and leaves the stack. Before you would receive priority, state-based actions are checked (none apply — nothing has lethal damage, no zero-toughness creatures), then all nine waiting triggers go on the stack in any order you choose (you control them all). THE PILOT'S ORDERING CHOICE, stated explicitly: put Regisaur Alpha's token trigger on the stack FIRST (bottom), then Ravenous Sailback's modal trigger, then the three spare Pantlaza triggers (X=4, X=5, X=4), then the three Garruk's Uprising triggers, and put the Pantlaza trigger from Carnage Tyrant on LAST (top).
    The stack resolves last-in, first-out, so this order makes the Carnage Tyrant Pantlaza trigger (the X=6 discover) resolve FIRST and Regisaur Alpha's token trigger resolve LAST — the token will not enter until after the once-per-turn discover has already been taken. This sequencing is why the token cannot re-trigger Pantlaza (step 9). Ravenous Sailback's trigger is modal; you announce the mode as you put it on the stack. Its only artifact/enchantment target on any board is your own Garruk's Uprising, so you choose 'This creature gains haste until end of turn' (redundant with Regisaur Alpha's static haste, but harmless).
    CR 704.3 — “Whenever a player would get priority (see rule 117, “Timing and Priority”), the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event.”
    CR 603.3b — “First, each player, in APNAP order, puts each triggered ability they control with a trigger condition that isn’t another ability triggering on the stack in any order they choose.”
    CR 603.3c — “If a triggered ability is modal, its controller announces the mode choice when putting the ability on the stack.”
    CR 405.2 — “The stack keeps track of the order that spells and/or abilities were added to it. Each time an object is put on the stack, it’s put on top of all objects already there.”
  6. The top trigger — Pantlaza from Carnage Tyrant — resolves first. It is optional ('you may discover X'); the choice is made on resolution, and you take it: discover 6, the best X among the simultaneous enterers (Carnage Tyrant's toughness).
    Discover 6: exile cards from the top of your library until you exile a nonland card with mana value 6 or less; cast it free or put it into your hand; the rest go to the bottom in random order. The once-per-turn discover action has now been TAKEN this turn — this fact governs everything below it on the stack.
    CR 405.5 — “When all players pass in succession, the top (last-added) spell or ability on the stack resolves. If the stack is empty when all players pass, the current step or phase ends and the next begins.”
    CR 603.5 — “These abilities go on the stack when they trigger, regardless of whether their controller intends to exercise the ability’s option or not. The choice is made when the ability resolves.”
    CR 701.57a — “Exile cards from the top of your library until you exile a nonland card with mana value N or less. You may cast that card without paying its mana cost if the resulting spell’s mana value is less than or equal to N. If you don’t cast it, put that card into your hand. Put the remaining exiled cards on the bottom of your library in a random order.”
  7. The three Garruk's Uprising triggers resolve one at a time.
    You draw a card for each: three cards drawn (Regisaur Alpha, Ripjaw Raptor, Carnage Tyrant). Ripjaw Raptor's enrage does not trigger — nothing has dealt damage to it.
    CR 608.2c — “The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written.”
  8. The three spare Pantlaza triggers (X=4, X=5, X=4) and Ravenous Sailback's trigger resolve.
    The discover action has already been taken this turn, so the 'Do this only once each turn' instruction means each remaining Pantlaza trigger resolves with no effect — you cannot perform the action again this turn. Exactly ONE discover total, at X=6. Sailback's resolved mode grants it haste until end of turn, redundant with Regisaur Alpha's static grant.
    CR 603.2h — “A triggered ability may have an instruction followed by “Do this only once each turn.””
    CR 603.5 — “These abilities go on the stack when they trigger, regardless of whether their controller intends to exercise the ability’s option or not. The choice is made when the ability resolves.”
  9. Regisaur Alpha's ETB trigger — deliberately placed at the bottom in step 5 — resolves last: create a 3/3 green Dinosaur creature token with trample.
    The token is put onto the battlefield, and a token enters the battlefield as it is created — so entry triggers are checked again. Garruk's Uprising does NOT trigger: the token's power is 3, not 4 or greater. Pantlaza does not trigger at all: you have already taken the once-per-turn discover action (step 6), and per 603.2h the ability triggers only if that action has not yet been taken this turn. This is the payoff of the step-5 ordering. (Honest note on the alternative: had you resolved this token trigger BEFORE the discover, Pantlaza would have triggered a fifth time at X=3 — a strictly worse discover you would decline, so the chosen order both plays better and keeps the count at four.)
    CR 701.7a — “To create one or more tokens with certain characteristics, put the specified number of tokens with the specified characteristics onto the battlefield.”
    CR glossary:enters-the-battlefield — “A token enters the battlefield as it’s created.”
    CR 603.2h — “This ability triggers only if its source’s controller has not yet taken the indicated action that turn.”
  10. Haste and the 'second combat' question. All new Dinosaurs have haste from Regisaur Alpha's static ability (the token too — it is an 'other Dinosaur'), and Sailback's chosen mode is redundant extra haste.
    Haste only exempts them from the continuous-control requirement for attacking; attackers are chosen only in the declare attackers step, which has already passed this combat, and a turn has only one combat phase by default. With no additional-combat effect in the scenario, none of the new bodies attack this turn. The haste matters only if some other effect grants an extra combat (or for tap abilities) — but they are all ready to swing next turn regardless.
    CR 702.10b — “If a creature has haste, it can attack even if it hasn’t been controlled by its controller continuously since their most recent turn began.”
    CR 508.1a — “The chosen creatures must be untapped, they can’t also be battles, and each one must either have haste or have been controlled by the active player continuously since the turn began.”
    CR 500.1 — “A turn consists of five phases, in this order: beginning, precombat main, combat, postcombat main, and ending.”

Case A-002 Marauding Raptor + Polyraptor: infinite tokens, or a mandatory draw?
Rules version 2026-06-19 · status: cleared in 2 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
  1. Polyraptor resolves.
    Polyraptor is a creature spell with no targets; it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under your control as a 5/5 Dinosaur.
    CR 608.3a — “If the object that’s resolving has no targets, it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under the control of the spell’s controller.”
  2. Marauding Raptor's enters trigger triggers.
    Polyraptor entering matches Marauding Raptor's trigger condition ('Whenever another creature you control enters, this creature deals 2 damage to it'). The ability triggers automatically — it contains no 'may', so nothing about it is optional — and is put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority.
    CR 603.6a — “Enters-the-battlefield abilities trigger when a permanent enters the battlefield.”
    CR 603.2 — “Whenever a game event or game state matches a triggered ability’s trigger event, that ability automatically triggers.”
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority.”
  3. All players pass priority; the ping trigger resolves.
    The active player receives priority, and when all players pass in succession the trigger resolves: Marauding Raptor deals 2 damage to Polyraptor. That damage is marked on Polyraptor. Because Polyraptor is a Dinosaur dealt damage this way, Marauding Raptor gets +2/+0 until end of turn (part of the same ability's resolution — irrelevant to the loop's outcome).
    CR 117.3b — “The active player receives priority after a spell or ability (other than a mana ability) resolves.”
    CR 117.4 — “If all players pass in succession (that is, if all players pass without taking any actions in between passing), the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves”
    CR 302.7 — “Damage dealt to a creature by a source with neither wither nor infect is marked on that creature”
  4. State-based actions are checked: nothing dies.
    Before any player receives priority, state-based actions are performed. Polyraptor has 2 damage marked against toughness 5 — 2 is not greater than or equal to 5, so it is not dealt lethal damage and is not destroyed. The 2 damage stays marked until cleanup, but each individual Polyraptor is pinged exactly once (only when it enters), so no Polyraptor ever accumulates more than 2 damage. No token ever dies in this loop.
    CR 117.5 — “Each time a player would get priority, the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event”
    CR 704.5g — “If a creature has toughness greater than 0, it has damage marked on it, and the total damage marked on it is greater than or equal to its toughness, that creature has been dealt lethal damage and is destroyed.”
    CR 120.6 — “Damage marked on a creature remains until the cleanup step”
  5. Polyraptor's Enrage triggers and resolves, creating a token copy.
    Being dealt damage matches Enrage's trigger condition ('Whenever this creature is dealt damage, create a token that's a copy of this creature'). It contains no 'may' — it is mandatory. After priority passes again, it resolves and a token copy of Polyraptor is put onto the battlefield.
    CR 603.2 — “that ability automatically triggers”
    CR 701.7a — “To create one or more tokens with certain characteristics, put the specified number of tokens with the specified characteristics onto the battlefield.”
  6. The token copy enters fresh: 5/5, has Enrage, zero damage marked.
    The copy acquires only Polyraptor's copiable values — including its rules text, so every token has Enrage, and its printed 5/5 power/toughness. Damage marked on the original is NOT among the copiable values (those are only the values derived from the printed text), so each new token enters with no damage marked. Every iteration starts from a full-toughness 5/5.
    CR 707.2 — “The copiable values are the values derived from the text printed on the object (that text being name, mana cost, color indicator, card type, subtype, supertype, rules text, power, toughness, and/or loyalty)”
  7. The loop closes: the token entering re-triggers Marauding Raptor.
    The token is another creature you control entering, so Marauding Raptor's ping triggers again (mandatory), deals 2 to the new token (2 < 5, it survives per step 4), the token's Enrage triggers (mandatory), a new token is created, and that token entering triggers the ping again. Each iteration adds one 5/5 Polyraptor token with 2 damage marked and nothing ever leaves the battlefield. This is a set of actions that could be repeated indefinitely — a loop in the rules' sense — and every action in the repeated sequence is mandatory.
    CR 603.6a — “Enters-the-battlefield abilities trigger when a permanent enters the battlefield.”
    CR 732.1b — “Occasionally the game gets into a state in which a set of actions could be repeated indefinitely (thus creating a “loop”).”
  8. What could break it: players DO receive priority inside the loop.
    Triggers resolve one at a time; each one is put on the stack when a player would receive priority, the active player then receives priority, and every trigger only resolves after all players pass. So every iteration contains real priority windows in which any player could cast an instant or activate an ability — e.g. killing Marauding Raptor (no more pings; the loop ends and you keep the tokens created so far) or removing the Polyraptors. From YOUR position that is impossible: you hold no instants and control no sacrifice outlet, so you cannot break the loop. Your opponents' boards are empty; whether they can break it depends on their unknown hands, and nothing can force them to.
    CR 603.3 — “its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority”
    CR 117.3b — “The active player receives priority after a spell or ability (other than a mana ability) resolves.”
    CR 117.4 — “If all players pass in succession (that is, if all players pass without taking any actions in between passing), the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves”
  9. Game outcome: if no player intervenes, the game is a draw.
    The repeated sequence itself (enters trigger → ping → Enrage trigger → token → enters trigger ...) contains only mandatory actions and has no way to stop on its own — no token dies, no choice exists within any trigger. Under the loop rules, a loop containing only mandatory actions ends the game in a draw. If some player injects an optional action (removal in a priority window, per step 8), the loop is broken and the game continues; but no player can be forced to do so, and if no one does, the whole game — all three players — ends in a draw.
    CR 732.4 — “If a loop contains only mandatory actions, the game is a draw.”
    CR 104.4b — “If a game that’s not using the limited range of influence option (including a two-player game) somehow enters a “loop” of mandatory actions, repeating a sequence of events with no way to stop, the game is a draw.”
    CR 104.4b — “Loops that contain an optional action don’t result in a draw.”

Case A-004 Wrathful Raptors + Blasphemous Act + Temple Altisaur: the one-sided apocalypse

  1. Blasphemous Act resolves, dealing 13 damage to each of the nine creatures on the battlefield as a single simultaneous event; Temple Altisaur's prevention effect is applied as the damage is dealt.
    One damage event hits all nine creatures at once. Temple Altisaur's static ability ('If a source would deal damage to another Dinosaur you control, prevent all but 1 of that damage') is a prevention effect and modifies the event as it happens — it was not locked in ahead of time.
    CR 608.2f — “Some spells and abilities include actions taken on multiple players and/or objects. In most cases, each such action is processed simultaneously.”
    CR 615.1 — “prevention effects apply continuously as events happen—they aren’t locked in ahead of time. Such effects watch for a damage event that would happen and completely or partially prevent the damage that would be dealt.”
  2. Apply the prevention: the Act's 13 is prevented down to 1 for each Dinosaur you control OTHER than Temple Altisaur. Altisaur is not 'another Dinosaur you control' relative to itself, so its own 13 is not prevented.
    Damage actually DEALT: Temple Altisaur 13; Wrathful Raptors 1; Ripjaw Raptor 1; Gishath, Sun's Avatar 1 (all four are Dinosaurs per their type lines; the shield partially prevents each 13 down to 1). Opponents' creatures are not Dinosaurs you control, so the four 2/2s and the 6/6 are each dealt the full 13.
    CR 615.1 — “completely or partially prevent the damage that would be dealt. They act like “shields” around whatever they’re affecting.”
  3. Damage is marked and trigger conditions are checked against the event: Wrathful Raptors' ability triggers once per damaged Dinosaur you control (four times), and Ripjaw Raptor's enrage triggers once.
    Marked damage: Altisaur 13 (toughness 4), Wrathful Raptors 1 (5/5), Ripjaw 1 (4/5), Gishath 1 (7/6), four 2/2s 13 each, the 6/6 13. Wrathful Raptors ('Whenever a Dinosaur you control is dealt damage, it deals that much damage to any target that isn't a Dinosaur') triggers four times — one occurrence per damaged Dinosaur in the single event — with 'that much' fixed by the event: 13 (Altisaur), 1 (Wrathful Raptors), 1 (Ripjaw), 1 (Gishath). Ripjaw's enrage ('Whenever this creature is dealt damage, draw a card') triggers on being dealt 1. Prevented damage was never dealt, so the surviving Dinosaurs' triggers are for 1, not 13.
    CR 120.6 — “Damage marked on a creature remains until the cleanup step”
    CR 603.2c — “An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs. However, it can trigger repeatedly if one event contains multiple occurrences.”
  4. Before any player receives priority — and therefore BEFORE the waiting triggers are put on the stack — state-based actions are performed: every creature with lethal damage marked is destroyed simultaneously.
    Destroyed as one SBA event: Temple Altisaur (13 ≥ 4), the four 2/2s (13 ≥ 2), and the 6/6 (13 ≥ 6). Six creatures die. Your other three Dinosaurs survive: Wrathful Raptors 1 < 5, Ripjaw Raptor 1 < 5, Gishath 1 < 6. The SBA check repeats, finds nothing further, and only then are triggered abilities put on the stack.
    CR 704.5g — “If a creature has toughness greater than 0, it has damage marked on it, and the total damage marked on it is greater than or equal to its toughness, that creature has been dealt lethal damage and is destroyed.”
    CR 704.3 — “performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event. If any state-based actions are performed as a result of a check, the check is repeated; otherwise all triggered abilities that are waiting to be put on the stack are put on the stack”
  5. You put all five triggers on the stack in an order you choose, choosing targets for the four Wrathful Raptors triggers AS THEY ARE PUT ON THE STACK — at which point every opposing creature is already dead.
    Because SBAs (step 4) resolved before triggers go on the stack, the four 2/2s and the 6/6 are no longer on the battlefield when targets are chosen. 'Any target' means a creature, player, planeswalker, or battle; excluding Dinosaurs (all four of your creatures, including Gishath, a Dinosaur Avatar) and with no opposing creatures, planeswalkers, or battles left, the only legal targets for each redirect are players. Per the scenario, all four triggers target opponent 1. Ripjaw's enrage trigger has no targets.
    CR 603.3b — “First, each player, in APNAP order, puts each triggered ability they control with a trigger condition that isn’t another ability triggering on the stack in any order they choose.”
    CR 115.1d — “The target(s) are chosen as the ability is put on the stack; see rule 603.3d.”
    CR glossary:any-target — ““Any target” is the same as “target creature, player, planeswalker, or battle.””
    CR 603.3d — “If a choice is required when the triggered ability goes on the stack but no legal choices can be made for it, or if a rule or a continuous effect otherwise makes the ability illegal, the ability is simply removed from the stack.”
  6. The Temple Altisaur redirect trigger resolves: the dead Altisaur deals 13 damage to opponent 1 using last known information.
    The trigger exists on the stack independently of Altisaur; even though Altisaur is in the graveyard, it still performs the action using its last known information. Opponent 1 is dealt 13 damage and loses 13 life: 26 -> 13.
    CR 113.7a — “if the source is no longer in the zone it’s expected to be in at that time, its last known information is used. The source can still perform the action even though it no longer exists.”
    CR 119.2 — “Damage dealt to a player normally causes that player to lose that much life.”

    CR 120.3a — “Damage dealt to a player by a source without infect causes that player to lose that much life.”
  8. Ripjaw Raptor's enrage trigger resolves: you draw a card.
    Ripjaw was dealt 1 damage (prevention leaves 1, which is still 'dealt damage'), so its enrage instruction 'draw a card' is followed. You draw one card.
    CR 608.2c — “The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written.”
  9. Final state-based action check: no player has 0 or less life and no creature has lethal damage; the game continues.
