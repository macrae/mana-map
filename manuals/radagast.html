Exhibit one: the text. The first creature spell you cast each turn costs {2} less and can be cast as though it had flash. The load-bearing words are 'each turn' — the printed template 'each of your turns' exists, and Radagast does not use it. A turn is a structural unit every player takes (CR 500.1), so the count zeroes at every untap, yours and theirs alike: four windows per round in a four-player pod. One candour the record keeps: no single rule states that an each-turn count resets. The holding is derived from the ability's text plus the definition of a turn, and the checker judged the derivation sound exactly as hedged (stack 001).

Now the zone. Your commander begins the game in the command zone and is the only card you always have (CR 903.4). That matters doubly for a deck that is a posture rather than a pile — you cannot hold mana open around an engine you might never draw. The goldfish puts him on the field on a mean turn 4.0, and by turn six in 94.1% of runs. Every other card here assumes he is there; the format is what makes that assumption legal.

The tax, on the record: {2}{G}{G} the first time, and each recast from the zone costs {2} more (CR 903.8). Four, then six, then eight. Six is affordable in a deck whose mana is 36 Forests and nine creature producers. Eight is a lost round of windows.

Coach's ledger on that: insure, don't re-buy. Nine protection copies are the premium on a commander-gated posture — Guardian Augmenter flashes in mid-removal-spell and leaves a hexproof 4/7 where the target used to be, and Tyvar's Stand protects him for one mana at X=0. Yeva, Nature's Herald is insurance of a different kind: a second flash enabler, so answering Radagast no longer switches the whole posture off. The recast is the fallback plan; the insurance is the plan.

Colour identity: mono-green, one line of pips, 63 spells that all agree. What it locks out is just as legible — zero counterspells, and the deck's one board wipe reads only the sky (Whiptongue Hydra eats fliers, nothing else). Veil of Summer is the closest thing to 'no' the deck sells; everything else answers permanents, not spells.

Is the 21-damage clock a plan (CR 903.10a)? A coincidence. He is a 2/5 — eleven clean connections. Your clock is X=6 trample.

The political read is the department's last word. The table sees a four-mana 2/5 Wizard and 36 Forests: fair stompy, zero combos, zero Game Changers, computed floor 1. Let them keep believing it. After he resolves, the true text of this deck is two untapped Forests and a pass.

THE PLAN

NEVER TAP OUT

Turn four, six Forests open, and the table has already filed you under ramp. Every untapped Forest is a loaded question — and the answer, four times a round, is a discounted body nobody voted for.

The table sees mono-green and waits for the turn you tap out. That turn never comes. Radagast makes your first creature spell each turn cost {2} less and lets you cast it at instant speed — and "each turn" means all four of them, verified to the letter in stack 001, where Wood Elves cost a single {G} on an opponent's end step. Pass with mana open and deploy in whichever window hurts the table most — and when Yeva, Nature's Herald lands, the cap comes off: every green creature spell gains flash, leaving Radagast's once-per-turn clause binding only the discount.

The open mana is never just a bluff — a trap is in hand by turn six in 91% of games, so the table has to respect it every time. Ohran Frostfang flashed in after blockers are declared hands your attackers deathtouch in time for damage: on the record, a 1/1 killed a 6/6 and the turn drew two cards (stack 002). Fecundity turns their wrath into your hand — four cards off a Day of Judgment while indestructible Toski just stands there (stack 004). Attacking you is bad, removing you is bad, wrathing you is bad.

The kill is arithmetic, so do the arithmetic — and cast it at home. Craterhoof over four bodies is 36, four short of a 40-life seat (stack 003); over five bodies it is exactly 40 into one seat, and the verified line is your own precombat main, haste carrying the attack (stack 005). Flash the Hoof on an end step and the pump dies at that turn's cleanup — legal, and it kills nobody. The end step is for bodies, not finishers: everything you flash there wakes up attack-ready on your turn (stack 006).

The goldfish hands you floors, and your job is to beat them. Radagast lands turn four on average and by six in 94% of games; a metronome body is drawn by six in 94.5% of games; the average board is 3.8 bodies at turn six — short of the five the kill needs. The sim models neither the discount, nor flash casts, nor the ramp auras, so the real numbers climb every time you use a window. Use the windows.

Pilot TipExile Elvish Spirit Guide from a hand the table priced at zero — the trap that fires through an empty-looking board is the one that ends someone's attack phase for the night.

OPENERS

TWO LANDS AND A DORK

81.5% of first sevens are keeps, so the skill here is not courage — it's recognising the three shapes you still ship. Cover the verdicts and commit.

Keepable first sevens (simulated)82%

KEEP

Forest, Forest, Llanowar Elves, Wood Elves, Ohran Frostfang, Cultivate, Harmonize

Two lands and an accelerant is the keep shape — kept hands cluster at two to four lands, and turn-one Elves is turn-three Radagast in about a third of games. Frostfang is the trap you hold, Cultivate patches the land drops, and Harmonize is gas that asks for no board. Draw-go from turn three: keep.

SHIP

Forest, Forest, Castle Garenbrig, Craterhoof Behemoth, Overwhelming Stampede, Hornet Queen, Harmonize

Two or more five-plus drops is a stated ship shape — the finishers are the payoff, not the plan, and a finisher is already in hand by turn six in 42.4% of games without you hoarding two of them on turn zero. Three real lands cannot carry a hand whose first meaningful play is turn five.

KEEP

Forest, Forest, Forest, Three Visits, Sakura-Tribe Elder, Beast Whisperer, Heroic Intervention

Three lands, an untapped-Forest accelerant, an engine, and something to do at instant speed. This hand passes with mana open from turn two and never has to tap out to develop — the exact posture the deck is built to hold.

SHIP

Forest, Forest, Forest, Forest, Llanowar Elves, Elvish Mystic, Birds of Paradise

The question put to the record

  1. Timing legality: you cast Wood Elves (a creature spell, normally sorcery-speed) during opponent 1's end step. Once the end step begins the active player gets priority, and after each pass priority moves around the table, so you legitimately hold priority in that step.
    You may act during opponent 1's end step, but a noninstant spell needs a timing permission to be cast there. Radagast of Rhosgobel supplies it: 'The first creature spell you cast each turn ... can be cast as though it had flash.' The permission is conditional, so its condition must be checked as you begin to cast.

    CR 117.1a — “A player may cast an instant spell any time they have priority. A player may cast a noninstant spell during their main phase any time they have priority and the stack is empty.”

You cast a (discounted, flashed) creature spell during YOUR OWN turn earlier this round — but that was a different turn. Note the boundary precisely: a creature that enters at any point before your turn begins — including the end step of the turn immediately before yours — clears this test; a creature entering during your own turn does not (absent haste).

    CR 702.10b — “If a creature has haste, it can attack even if it hasn’t been controlled by its controller continuously since their most recent turn began. (See rule 302.6.)”
  5. Verify the activated-ability half: could a {T} activated ability on such an end-step arrival be activated on your turn?
    Yes. The activated-ability counterpart is not a separate rule number in this CR — it is the FIRST sentence of the same rule 302.6, which restricts only abilities whose activation cost includes the tap symbol or untap symbol, using the identical 'controlled continuously since their most recent turn began' test. An end-step arrival passes that test on your turn exactly as it passes the attack test, so a {T} ability on such a creature could be activated — the same permission haste would grant (702.10c), obtained by timing instead. Two precision notes: (a) the equivalence to haste holds only from your turn onward; during the remainder of opponent 3's turn the Elder still fails the test, whereas a hasty creature would not; (b) Sakura-Tribe Elder's own ability ('Sacrifice this creature: Search your library for a basic land card...') has no tap or untap symbol in its cost, so 302.6 never restricted it at all — it could legally be activated the moment the Elder resolved, even mid-end-step.
    CR 302.6 — “A creature’s activated ability with the tap symbol or the untap symbol in its activation cost can’t be activated unless the creature has been under its controller’s control continuously since their most recent turn began.”
    CR 702.10c — “If a creature has haste, its controller can activate its activated abilities whose cost includes the tap symbol or the untap symbol even if that creature hasn’t been controlled by that player continuously since their most recent turn began. (See rule 302.6.)”
    CR glossary:summoning-sickness-rule — “Informal term for a player’s inability to attack with a creature or to activate its abilities that include the tap symbol or the untap symbol unless the creature has been under that player’s control since the beginning of that player’s most recent turn. See rule 302.6. See also Haste.”

Case A-007 What Chord is not: no discount, no Garenbrig mana, no Whisperer draw — the three ways Chord of Calling refuses Radagast's gifts
Rules version June 19, 2026 · status: cleared in 2 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
The question put to the record
Resolve the Chord. Which of Radagast's gifts (discount, flash window), Garenbrig's mana, and Beast Whisperer's draw apply to this cast — and which do not, each with the citation? May Selvala tap for mana on your following turn?
  1. Legality and announcement: Chord of Calling is cast during opponent 3's end step with X announced as 3.
    Chord of Calling is an Instant ({X}{G}{G}{G}), so casting it on an opponent's end step needs only priority — no permission from Radagast is required or used. The value of X is announced during casting. Chord finds 'a creature card with mana value X or less' and Selvala, Heart of the Wilds costs {1}{G}{G} — mana value 3 — so X must be at least 3; the announced X=3 meets that minimum, for a total cost of {3}{G}{G}{G}.
    CR 304.1 — “A player who has priority may cast an instant card from their hand. Casting an instant as a spell uses the stack.”
    CR 601.2b — “If the spell has a variable cost that will be paid as it’s being cast (such as an {X} in its mana cost; see rule 107.3), the player announces the value of that variable.”
    CR 202.3 — “The mana value of an object is a number equal to the total amount of mana in its mana cost, regardless of color.”
  2. Convoke payment: tap Wood Elves, Llanowar Elves and Beast Whisperer to cover the {3} generic portion; pay {G}{G}{G} from the three Forests.
    Convoke applies after the total cost ({3}{G}{G}{G}) is determined. Each of Wood Elves, Llanowar Elves and Beast Whisperer is tapped in place of one generic mana; the three Forests' {G}{G}{G} covers the coloured portion. All three creatures are now tapped — they have 'convoked' Chord and are not available as blockers or convokers for the rest of the turn cycle. All payment happens while casting, before the spell can resolve. Radagast stays untapped, and Castle Garenbrig is never touched.
    CR 702.51a — “For each generic mana in this spell’s total cost, you may tap an untapped creature you control rather than pay that mana.”
    CR 702.51b — “The convoke ability isn’t an additional or alternative cost and applies only after the total cost of the spell with convoke is determined.”
    CR 702.51c — “A creature tapped to pay for mana in a spell’s total cost this way is said to have “convoked” that spell.”
    CR 601.2h — “The player pays the total cost. First, they pay all costs that don’t involve random elements or moving objects from the library to a public zone, in any order.”
  3. Negative claim 1 — Radagast's gift does not apply and is not consumed.
    Radagast reads 'The first creature spell you cast each turn costs {2} less to cast and can be cast as though it had flash.' A description that includes the word 'spell' means a spell matching that description on the stack; Chord of Calling on the stack is an instant spell, never a creature spell, so it can never be 'the first creature spell you cast' — the {2} reduction does not apply to it, and casting it leaves the turn's discount window untouched for an actual creature spell. The flash grant is equally irrelevant: an instant already needs only priority to be cast.
    CR 109.2b — “If a spell or ability uses a description of an object that includes the word “spell,” it means a spell matching that description on the stack.”
    CR 304.1 — “A player who has priority may cast an instant card from their hand.”
  4. Negative claim 2 — Castle Garenbrig's restricted mana cannot pay for Chord.
    Garenbrig's second ability ('{2}{G}{G}, {T}: Add six {G}. Spend this mana only to cast creature spells or activate abilities of creatures.') produces mana with a spend restriction; Chord is an instant spell, so that mana is not legal payment for it, and the stated payment correctly avoids the Castle. One precision the tutor guide should keep: only the six-mana burst is restricted — Garenbrig's first ability, '{T}: Add {G}', carries no restriction and COULD legally pay for Chord.
    CR 106.6 — “Some spells or abilities that produce mana restrict how that mana can be spent, have an additional effect that affects the spell or ability that mana is spent on, or create a delayed triggered ability (see rule 603.7a) that triggers when that mana is spent.”
    CR 109.2b — “If a spell or ability uses a description of an object that includes the word “spell,” it means a spell matching that description on the stack.”
  5. Negative claim 3a — Beast Whisperer does not trigger on the cast of Chord.
    Beast Whisperer reads 'Whenever you cast a creature spell, draw a card.' Chord is an instant spell on the stack, not a creature spell, so the trigger condition is not met and no card is drawn for casting Chord. Being tapped to convoke changes nothing here — the trigger fails on the spell's type, not on Beast Whisperer's tapped status.
    CR 109.2b — “If a spell or ability uses a description of an object that includes the word “spell,” it means a spell matching that description on the stack.”
  6. All players pass; Chord of Calling resolves: search finds Selvala, shuffle, Chord to the graveyard.
    With no responses, Chord resolves and its controller follows its instructions in order: 'Search your library for a creature card with mana value X or less, put it onto the battlefield, then shuffle.' Selvala, Heart of the Wilds (mana value 3) matches the description with X=3, is found, and is put onto the battlefield; the library is then shuffled and Chord is put into its owner's graveyard. (Note: an earlier draft of this scenario announced X=2, which could not have found a mana value 3 card; the scenario on disk now states X=3.)
    CR 117.4 — “If all players pass in succession (that is, if all players pass without taking any actions in between passing), the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves”
    CR 608.2c — “The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written.”
    CR 701.23a — “To search for a card in a zone, look at all cards in that zone (even if it’s a hidden zone) and find a card that matches the given description.”
    CR 701.24a — “To shuffle a library or a face-down pile of cards, randomize the cards within it so that no player knows their order.”
    CR 304.2 — “When an instant spell resolves, the actions stated in its rules text are followed. Then it’s put into its owner’s graveyard.”
  7. Negative claim 3b — Selvala is PUT onto the battlefield, not cast; no cast triggers fire.
    Being put onto the battlefield by a resolving spell is not casting — casting means taking a card from where it is, putting it on the stack, and paying its costs, none of which happens here. The enter event is checked only for enters-the-battlefield triggers; Beast Whisperer's ability triggers on casting a creature spell, not on a creature entering, so it does not fire for Selvala's arrival either. Selvala's own trigger reads 'Whenever another creature enters' and does not trigger for her own arrival. No cast triggers, no draws.
    CR glossary:cast — “To take a card from where it is (usually the hand), put it on the stack, and pay its costs, so that it will eventually resolve and have its effect.”
    CR 603.6a — “Enters-the-battlefield abilities trigger when a permanent enters the battlefield. These are written, “When [this object] enters, . . . “ or “Whenever a [type] enters, . . .” Each time an event puts one or more permanents onto the battlefield, all permanents on the battlefield (including the newcomers) are checked for any enters-the-battlefield triggers that match the event.”
  8. Positive claim — Selvala may activate her {G}, {T} mana ability on your following turn.
    Selvala entered during opponent 3's end step and you are next in turn order. When your turn begins she is already under your control, so from that moment she has been under your control continuously since your most recent turn began — the tap-symbol restriction is satisfied for the whole of your turn. Her '{G}, {T}: Add X mana...' ability is therefore activatable on your turn (untap-step or later), producing mana equal to the greatest power among your creatures.
    CR 302.6 — “A creature’s activated ability with the tap symbol or the untap symbol in its activation cost can’t be activated unless the creature has been under its controller’s control continuously since their most recent turn began.”
    CR glossary:summoning-sickness-rule — “Informal term for a player’s inability to attack with a creature or to activate its abilities that include the tap symbol or the untap symbol unless the creature has been under that player’s control since the beginning of that player’s most recent turn.”
