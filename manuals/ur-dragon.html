THE PLAN

LET THEM WATCH THE WRONG CLOCK

The table budgets its answers for a nine-mana commander who averages a turn-8.967 arrival. Your actual clock is one enchantment and a connecting Dragon — and the checker already signed off on it.

Unlearn the obvious read first: The Ur-Dragon is not the plan, and that is your edge. The goldfish simulator (seed 42, 10,000 iterations, conservative model — cost reducers not simulated) puts his mean cast on turn 8.967, with a 0.0% cast-by-turn-6 rate and a cast at all by turn 10 in just 13.7% of runs. Nobody budgeting answers for a turn-8 commander is watching your actual clock. Good — keep them watching the wrong one.

The real engine never leaves home. Eminence discounts every other Dragon spell by {1} from the command zone, all game, starting turn zero. Stack that with Dragonspeaker Shaman, Herald's Horn, and Urza's Incubator and a nine-drop curve plays like a six-drop curve. Korlessa, Scale Singer turns the top of your library into a second hand for those discounted Dragons.

The fair half of the kill is critical mass. Dragon Tempest, Scourge of Valkas, and Terror of the Peaks turn each Dragon entering into targeted damage; Garruk's Uprising and Temur Ascendancy turn big bodies into cards; Miirym, Sentinel Wyrm turns every nontoken Dragon into two. Below the threshold you have undersized, expensive bodies — past it, every new Dragon draws, burns, and copies itself. The verified stacks show the payoff math: one Ancient Gold Dragon connection under Dragon Tempest produced fifteen tokens and up to 255 points of divisible damage (stack 001), and a single Lathliss cast under Miirym plus Panharmonicon produced two extra Lathlisses and 64 damage across twelve triggers (stack 002).

The unfair half is three contained two-card infinites, every one routed through Aggravated Assault — that concentration is why the computed bracket floor reads 4. Two are checker-verified: Savage Ventmaw's attack mana pays the activation with {R} left over, a self-funding unbounded-combats loop at +1 mana per cycle (stack 003), and Old Gnawbone alone banks net +2 Treasures per unblocked connection, killing opponents on combats 6 and 12 in the verified game (stack 006). The third pairing, Cavern-Hoard Dragon plus Assault, still needs a stack scenario — treat it as a candidate, not a fact.

Mind the funding trap deck-facts flags. Five of your sources — Haven of the Spirit Dragon, Maelstrom of the Spirit Dragon, Unclaimed Territory, Rivaz of the Claw, and an attacking Klauth — make spells-only mana, and an activated ability is not a spell, so none of it can ever pay Assault's {3}{R}{R}. Treasures are the legal wallet, and Atsushi, the Blazing Sky dies forward into three unrestricted ones. Around the engines sit velocity and insurance: Sneak Attack cheats any Dragon in the 99 into play for {R} with every trigger live (stack 005 — which also settled that it cannot reach the command zone; Hellkite Courser is the real commander cheat, renting The Ur-Dragon out for one hasty attack), and Wrathful Red Dragon turns any damage-based sweeper into a 39-point execution aimed at one opponent (stack 004).

Your seat for the first five turns is control-slash-value, and you should love it. The deck averages 4.9 available mana and 1.5 bodies at turn 6 — it loses every early race, so never enter one. Ramp or fixing is drawn by turn 6 in 71.6% of games, a cost reducer in only 33.2%, and the reducer-plus-ramp “turn-5 Ur-Dragon hand” in just 23.1% — so ramp, fix, deploy in waves, and do not promise yourself an early commander.

Protection is in hand by turn 6 in 66.0% of games; hold it for the one turn that matters. That turn is concrete: Aggravated Assault live with a connecting Gnawbone or Ventmaw, or a Sneak Attack turn with a payoff already down. Take that window once, decisively — every other turn, stay boring and let the table watch a commander you were never in a hurry to cast.

The second thing to want is an early engine piece — Dragon Tempest, Garruk's Uprising, Korlessa, Roiling Dragonstorm — because the payoff layer pays per Dragon and wants to be down before the first one lands. A payoff is drawn by turn 6 in 62.2% of games; help the average.

A protection spell (Teferi's Protection, Heroic Intervention, Flawless Maneuver) is a genuine keep-bonus — held protection is what lets you commit the wave later, and it is in hand by turn 6 in 66.0% of games. The simulator's 75.1% keep-7 rate says most sevens are fine. Mulligan for function, not for glamour.

THE SLOWEST DECK IN THE ROOM

That concentration is also your single point of failure: one Naturalize effect turns off every infinite at once, and the deck has no second extra-combat effect. THE AUDIT

RED WRITES THE CHECKS

Red is 46.5% of the pip bill on 26.2% of the land sources. Black runs eleven lands against a double-black lump. Sixteen of twenty-nine lands arrive tapped. The audit prices all of it — and names the Treasury that never appears on the books.

Thirty-four lands, 16 of them entering tapped — 47%, over the one-in-three budget the deck builder allows itself. That tap rate is the toll for five colours on a battlecruiser curve. The goldfish shows where you pay it: mean available mana 3.042 on turn 3, 4.937 on turn 6. This deck rarely misses colours. It misses turns.

The land sources read W10 / U11 / B11 / R20 / G18, and the pip side reads nothing like it. Red is 46.5% of the pip load on 28.6% of the sources — 47 red pips across 32 cards, up to three at once. Green is nearly square: 21.8% of pips on 25.7% of sources.

Source counts alone will mislead you here; pip weight settles it. Green's 18 lands deliver a green pip on curve 84.9% of the time, because green asks one pip at a time. Red's 20 lands deliver 58.0%, because red's effective ask is two. Blue lands 67.0%, white 63.3%.

Black is the genuine hole, and it is worth naming plainly: 11 land sources, 26.1% on curve. Black is only 7.9% of the pip load, but it arrives in lumps — Patriarch's Bidding wants double black on the turn you need it. Six rocks and three dorks lift black to 16 effective sources and 44.6%, red to 27 and 76.4%, green to 24 and 92.7%. The fixing package, not the lands, is doing the colour work.

Two things the audit refuses to count. Haven of the Spirit Dragon, Maelstrom of the Spirit Dragon, Unclaimed Territory, Rivaz of the Claw, and Klauth make restricted mana — spells only, so it cannot activate Aggravated Assault. Treasures from Old Gnawbone, Goldspan Dragon, Atsushi, and Cavern-Hoard Dragon are the opposite: unrestricted income no table can forecast, and stack 006 shows that ledger funding an entire night of combats. The 90% yardstick asks 36 red and 36 black sources in a 34-land deck — a yardstick, not a possibility. Plan around black at 44.6%, and let the Treasures pay for everything else.

  8. The loop continues into opponent 2 (38 life). Their only creature is a 2/2 with no flying and no reach, which cannot block the flying Gnawbone — every attack remains unblocked for 7.
    Combats 7–12 take opponent 2 from 38 to −4 (38→31→24→17→10→3→−4); opponent 2 loses the game on combat 12, your eleventh Assault activation. Net Treasures grow +2 per cycle through combat 11 (17→12→19→14→21→16→23→18→25→20→27); the combat-12 activation is paid before damage (27→22), but the final cycle never completes — see step 9.
    CR 702.9b — “A creature with flying can’t be blocked except by creatures with flying and/or reach.”
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
  9. Game end, combat 12: after combat damage is dealt, state-based actions are performed before any waiting triggered ability is put on the stack. Opponent 2 is at −4 life and loses; they leave the game, and — as your last remaining opponent has now left — you win the game immediately. The game ends at that moment, so Old Gnawbone's twelfth combat damage trigger is never put on the stack and never resolves: its 7 Treasures are never created.
    You win on combat 12. Final Treasure count is 22 (11 triggers resolved for +77, 11 activations paid −55, from a base of 0), not 29 — the state '29 Treasures after combat 12's trigger' never exists.
    CR 704.3 — “the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event. If any state-based actions are performed as a result of a check, the check is repeated; otherwise all triggered abilities that are waiting to be put on the stack are put on the stack”
    CR 104.2a — “A player still in the game wins the game if that player’s opponents have all left the game. This happens immediately and overrides all effects that would preclude that player from winning the game.”
    CR 104.1 — “A game ends immediately when a player wins, when the game is a draw, or when the game is restarted.”
  10. Bounds: the loop is not infinite in the rules sense — each cycle requires a real activation, a real combat, and a real damage event. It ends if (a) Gnawbone is removed or the attack is blocked/prevented so the trigger refunds under 5 mana and the cost eventually can't be fully paid, (b) an opponent presents flying/reach blockers, or (c) every opponent has lost and left the game, at which point you win and the game itself ends.
    Bounded by Gnawbone's survival, opposing flying/reach blockers, and the supply of opponents. In this scenario none of those bounds bite before both opponents are dead.
