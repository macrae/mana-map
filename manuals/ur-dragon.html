Start with what the zone guarantees: your commander is always available to cast from it (CR 903.4). No other format promises you a specific card every game. This deck files that promise differently from every other seat at the table, because The Ur-Dragon's eminence works while he sits in the zone — every other Dragon spell you cast costs {1} less, from your first upkeep, whether or not he ever moves. The guarantee is not that you will draw him. It is that you are already being paid.

Now the tax. Each cast from the zone after the first costs an additional {2} (CR 903.8), so the ladder reads nine, then eleven, then thirteen. Set that against the ledger: mean available mana is 6.165 on turn nine, and in 8,634 of 10,000 simulated games he never lands inside ten turns at all. The first cast is an event, the second is a stretch, the third is fiction. So protect the one you get. Deflecting Swat and Flawless Maneuver are free only while a commander is on your battlefield, which means keeping him beats recasting him by a distance.

There is a rental clause, and the Counselor commends it to your attention. Hellkite Courser's enters-trigger puts The Ur-Dragon onto the battlefield from the command zone, hasty, for exactly one attack — a card per attacking Dragon plus a free permanent — then returns him at end step. The tax counts casts, and this never was one: the zone gets its tenant back untaxed. The route everyone assumes works does not. Sneak Attack reaches a creature card in your hand, and the command zone is not your hand (CR 903.6; the full holding is case A-005).

Colour identity costs you nothing in exclusions and everything in logistics. Five colours means no card in the format is outside your identity. It also means 34 lands answering 101 coloured pips, sixteen of which have 'enters tapped' printed somewhere on the card. Sources Say prices that bill in full. Learn one clause now: five of your mana sources make spells-only mana. It casts Dragons beautifully and it can never pay an activated ability.

The 21-point commander clock (CR 903.10a) is arithmetic worth savouring and then setting down. A 10/10 deals 20 commander damage in two connections — one point short — so it takes three clean hits, and even Thrakkus the Butcher doubling stops at 20 on a single swing. Commander damage here is a coincidence, not a plan. The kills are filed under The Kill.

The Coach's close, and it is a table read. A nine-mana commander is the best decoy in Commander: every seat prices you at turn nine while your actual game is a discounted Dragon on turn four. Let them keep the read. Cast him when the table's interaction has been spent elsewhere, shield him the turn he lands, and never mulligan a hand hunting for him. The zone already holds him, and the zone is where he earns.

THE PLAN

PAYOFF FIRST, DRAGON SECOND

The loop the whole table fears assembles in 6.4% of your games. The payoff layer that actually kills is in hand by turn six in 62.2% of them. Build for the second number and take the first one when it walks in.

They count to nine in your command zone, see five colours, and file you under durdle. Let them keep that read — it is the only free thing you get all game. Eminence runs from the command zone, so every other Dragon spell you cast is {1} cheaper from your first upkeep, and nothing has to resolve for it to be true.

So stop building turns around the commander. He is cast on turn 8.967 on average, 0.0% of the time by turn six, and in 8,634 of 10,000 simulated games he never lands at all inside ten turns. Cast the discounted Dragon instead. Every time.

Your median kill is critical mass, and the order is the whole trick: payoff first, Dragon second. Dragon Tempest, Scourge of Valkas and Terror of the Peaks pay for every Dragon that enters after them and pay nothing for the ones already down. A payoff is in hand by turn six in 62.2% of games — so the turn one shows up early, play it into an empty board and be patient about it. Land Miirym, Sentinel Wyrm ahead of your top end; with Panharmonicon, Terror of the Peaks and Dragon Tempest already down, one Lathliss cast is 64 damage across twelve separately aimed instances (stack 002).

The unfair kill assembles in 3.6% of games by turn six. Aggravated Assault plus a mana dragon is your ceiling, not your schedule — take it when it arrives and never wait on it. When it does arrive, hold the enchantment until the turn it kills someone: all three of your infinites run through that one card, and Scourge of the Throne, the only other extra-combat effect in the 99, works once each turn and only when you attack the player at the highest life.

Count real mana before you swing. Haven of the Spirit Dragon, Maelstrom of the Spirit Dragon, Unclaimed Territory, Rivaz of the Claw and an attacking Klauth all make spells-only mana, and an activated ability is not a spell — none of it pays Assault's {3}{R}{R}. Treasures do, so bank them: Old Gnawbone, Goldspan Dragon, Cavern-Hoard Dragon and a dead Atsushi all print mana nobody has restricted.

Early, you are the value seat, and you should enjoy it. Ramp, fix, let a louder player eat the removal, and keep one protection spell in hand — you have one by turn six in 66.0% of games, and that is the number that tells you when to commit the wave. Then take a single window. Hellkite Courser rents the commander out of the zone for one hasty attack, Sneak Attack drops Drakuseth for {R} (stack 005), Wrathful Red Dragon turns the next sweeper into a 39-point execution (stack 004). Take the turn that wins, not the turn that impresses.

THE ROSTER

NINETY-NINE SEATS, RANKED

The engine room first — the cards the checker has already been through — then the payoffs, the discounts, the wallet, and 34 lands carrying a five-colour bill. Twenty-two of your twenty-four creatures are Dragons, and your own sweepers know exactly which two are not.

The engine room — on the record

Aggravated Assault

The win condition and the loudest tell in one card: {3}{R}{R} untaps your team and buys another combat, and all three infinites in the 99 run through it. Hold it for the turn it kills somebody, and remember your Dragon-restricted mana cannot pay the activation.

Every point of combat damage your team deals comes back as Treasure, and Treasure is the one mana here that can pay Aggravated Assault. With Assault down, one unblocked connection ends the whole table in sequence (stack 006).

Savage Ventmaw

Attacking adds {R}{R}{R}{G}{G}{G} that survives the phase change, which is a whole extra Dragon in your postcombat main. With Aggravated Assault it pays for its own extra combat and leaves {R} behind (stack 003).

Dragon Tempest

Flying + Damage Trigger

Two mana, and every Dragon entering afterwards throws damage equal to your Dragon count. Get it down before the Dragons — it pays nothing at all for what has already landed.

Ancient Gold Dragon

Flying + Damage TriggerTrample + Damage Trigger

One connection rolls a d20 and hands you that many flying bodies, which is your single biggest swing with Dragon Tempest already down (stack 001). Point it at the emptiest board at the table, not the scariest one.

Miirym, Sentinel Wyrm

She copies every nontoken Dragon you land after her, from behind ward {2}. Sequence her ahead of your top end and expect the table to answer her first — she is the scariest fair card you own.

Panharmonicon

It doubles every trigger caused by a creature entering — Miirym, Lathliss, Terror, Tempest, all of it (stack 002). Play it before the wave, never after it.

Sneak Attack

One red mana puts any Dragon from your hand onto the battlefield with haste, and every attack and enters trigger fires before the end-step sacrifice (stack 005). It reaches your hand only — never plan on it for the commander.

Wrathful Red Dragon

Flying + Damage Trigger

Damage dealt to your Dragons gets thrown at any non-Dragon target, so a sweeper becomes an execution: three Dragons dealt 13 is 39 at one opponent (stack 004). Get it down early and let them cast the wrath.

Blasphemous Act

It costs {1} less per creature on the battlefield, so by the time you want it the table has usually made it a one-mana reset. With Wrathful Red Dragon out it stops being a reset and becomes an execution (stack 004).

Hellkite Courser

This is the card that genuinely pulls The Ur-Dragon out of the command zone — hasty, for one attack, with the eminence draw trigger live and no tax added (stack 005). Attack with everything the turn it enters.

    The mandatory sacrifice trigger is on the stack. (Players may respond to it — e.g., a blink effect would exile Drakuseth first — but nothing in this scenario does.)
    CR 603.7b — “A delayed triggered ability will trigger only once—the next time its trigger event occurs”
    CR 513.1 — “Once it begins, the active player gets priority.”
    CR 608.2c — “The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written.”
  9. The sacrifice trigger resolves: you move Drakuseth from the battlefield to its owner's graveyard. The damage it dealt this turn was a set of one-shot effects that already happened; sacrificing the creature does not undo them.
    Drakuseth is in the graveyard. All 17 damage stands: opponents remain at 30 and 36.
    CR 701.21a — “To sacrifice a permanent, its controller moves it from the battlefield directly to its owner’s graveyard.”
    CR 610.1 — “A one-shot effect does something just once and doesn’t have a duration. Examples include dealing damage”
  10. (b) Zone check for The Ur-Dragon. As a commander it began the game in the command zone, and it has not been cast, so it is there now. The command zone and the hand are different zones — a card in the command zone is not 'a creature card from your hand.'
    Sneak Attack's ability has nothing legal to reach: The Ur-Dragon is not in your hand, and Sneak Attack's text reaches only your hand.
    CR 903.6 — “At the start of the game, each player puts their commander from their deck face up into the command zone.”
    CR 402.1 — “The hand is where a player holds cards that have been drawn.”
  11. (b) conclusion — REFUTATION. From the command zone, the only thing the rules let you do with The Ur-Dragon is cast it (nine mana, plus commander tax on later casts). Sneak Attack cannot put it onto the battlefield from there. The narrow exception: if The Ur-Dragon were genuinely in your hand — you drew it after it was shuffled away, or you declined the replacement that returns it to the command zone — Sneak Attack could then cheat it in, but that is not the commander's normal zone and not what the strategic frame described.
    The strategic frame's 'commander-cheat vector' claim is refuted: Sneak Attack does not dodge the nine-mana cast for a commander sitting in the command zone. (The deck's actual commander-cheat is Hellkite Courser, whose oracle text reads 'put a commander you own from the command zone onto the battlefield.')
    CR 903.8 — “A commander cast from the command zone costs an additional {2} for each previous time the player casting it has cast it from the command zone that game.”
    CR 903.9b — “If a commander would be put into its owner’s hand or library from anywhere, its owner may put it into the command zone instead.”

Case A-006 Old Gnawbone + Aggravated Assault: does one dragon fund the whole night?
Rules version 2026-06-19 · status: cleared in 2 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
The question put to the record
Resolve the trigger: 7 Treasures enter. In the post-combat main phase, activate Aggravated Assault paying {3}{R}{R} entirely from Treasures — legal? Walk one full cycle: activation, extra combat, Gnawbone untaps and attacks again unblocked for 7, another 7 Treasures. What is the net Treasure count per cycle, what sustains the loop (combat damage of 5 or more to a player each combat), what bounds it (blockers, Gnawbone's survival, running out of players to hit), and on which combat does opponent 1 at 40 life die?
  1. Old Gnawbone's combat damage trigger resolves. Gnawbone dealt 7 combat damage to opponent 1, so 'that many' is 7: you create 7 Treasure tokens.
    Seven Treasure tokens — colorless Treasure artifacts with '{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color.' — are put onto the battlefield under your control. Treasures: 7. Opponent 1 is at 33.
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority.”
    CR 701.7a — “To create one or more tokens with certain characteristics, put the specified number of tokens with the specified characteristics onto the battlefield.”
    CR 111.10a — “A Treasure token is a colorless Treasure artifact token with “{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color.””
  2. Combat 1 ends and the game moves to the postcombat main phase. With the stack empty and priority yours during your main phase, you activate Aggravated Assault — its 'Activate only as a sorcery' restriction is satisfied.
    Aggravated Assault's ability goes on the stack; its activation cost {3}{R}{R} must be paid as part of activating it.
    CR 602.5d — “Activated abilities that read “Activate only as a sorcery” mean the player must follow the timing rules for casting a sorcery spell, though the ability isn’t actually a sorcery.”
    CR 505.1a — “Only the first main phase of the turn is a precombat main phase. All other main phases are postcombat main phases.”
  3. You pay {3}{R}{R} entirely from Treasures: while paying the cost you activate five Treasures' mana abilities, tapping and sacrificing each to add one mana of any color (two red for {R}{R}, three of any color for {3}). This is legal — cost payment explicitly allows activating mana abilities, and Treasure's ability is a mana ability that resolves immediately without using the stack.
    Five Treasures are sacrificed producing five mana; the five mana are removed from your mana pool to pay {3}{R}{R}. Treasures: 7 → 2. Your five lands remain untapped and untouched.
    CR 118.2 — “If a cost includes a mana payment, the player paying the cost has a chance to activate mana abilities.”
    CR 605.1a — “An activated ability is a mana ability if it meets all of the following criteria: it doesn’t require a target (see rule 115.6), it could add mana to a player’s mana pool when it resolves, and it’s not a loyalty ability.”
    CR 605.3b — “An activated mana ability doesn’t go on the stack, so it can’t be targeted, countered, or otherwise responded to. Rather, it resolves immediately after it is activated.”
    CR 118.3a — “Paying mana is done by removing the indicated mana from a player’s mana pool.”
  4. Aggravated Assault's ability resolves: untap all creatures you control, and an additional combat phase followed by an additional main phase is added after this main phase.
    Old Gnawbone untaps. Combat 2 (with a fresh postcombat main phase after it) is inserted directly after the current main phase.
    CR 701.26b — “To untap a permanent, rotate it back to the upright position from a sideways position. Only tapped permanents can be untapped.”
    CR 500.8 — “Some effects can add phases to a turn. They do this by adding the phases directly after the specified phase.”
  5. Combat 2: you declare Old Gnawbone attacking opponent 1, tapping it. Opponent 1 controls no creatures, so Gnawbone is unblocked and assigns its 7 combat damage to opponent 1; the damage trigger creates 7 more Treasures.
    Opponent 1: 33 → 26. Treasures: 2 → 9. One full cycle is complete: 5 Treasures spent, 7 created — net +2 Treasures per cycle.
    CR 508.1f — “The active player taps the chosen creatures. Tapping a creature when it’s declared as an attacker isn’t a cost; attacking simply causes creatures to become tapped.”
    CR 510.1b — “An unblocked creature assigns its combat damage to the player, planeswalker, or battle it’s attacking.”
    CR 119.2 — “Damage dealt to a player normally causes that player to lose that much life.”
    CR 701.7a — “put the specified number of tokens with the specified characteristics onto the battlefield”
  6. Sustaining condition: each activation costs 5 mana, so the loop self-funds only if the combat produces at least 5 Treasures — i.e., a creature you control deals 5+ combat damage to a player that combat. An unblocked 7/7 Gnawbone yields +7, a surplus of 2. If the trigger ever refunds fewer than 5, the Treasure count drains, and once you hold fewer than 5 Treasures (with no other mana) you cannot pay the activation cost at all.
    Loop is sustained while Gnawbone (or any creature) connects for 5+ each combat; it halts the moment the cost can no longer be fully paid.
    CR 118.3 — “A player can’t pay a cost without having the necessary resources to pay it fully.”
    CR 111.10a — “Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color.”
  7. Kill math, opponent 1 (40 life): combat 1 dealt 7 (40→33). Each subsequent cycle repeats steps 2–5 in the new postcombat main phase: combat 3 (26→19), combat 4 (19→12), combat 5 (12→5), combat 6 (5→−2). When state-based actions are checked after combat damage in combat 6, opponent 1 is at −2 life and loses the game, leaving the game.
    Opponent 1 dies on combat 6 — the fifth Aggravated Assault activation. Treasure ledger stays solvent throughout: 7→2→9→4→11→6→13→8→15→10→17 (17 after combat 6's trigger, which resolves because opponent 2 is still in the game).
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 104.5 — “If a player loses the game, that player leaves the game.”
  8. The loop continues into opponent 2 (38 life). Their only creature is a 2/2 with no flying and no reach, which cannot block the flying Gnawbone — every attack remains unblocked for 7.
    Combats 7–12 take opponent 2 from 38 to −4 (38→31→24→17→10→3→−4); opponent 2 loses the game on combat 12, your eleventh Assault activation. Net Treasures grow +2 per cycle through combat 11 (17→12→19→14→21→16→23→18→25→20→27); the combat-12 activation is paid before damage (27→22), but the final cycle never completes — see step 9.
    CR 702.9b — “A creature with flying can’t be blocked except by creatures with flying and/or reach.”
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
  9. Game end, combat 12: after combat damage is dealt, state-based actions are performed before any waiting triggered ability is put on the stack. Opponent 2 is at −4 life and loses; they leave the game, and — as your last remaining opponent has now left — you win the game immediately. The game ends at that moment, so Old Gnawbone's twelfth combat damage trigger is never put on the stack and never resolves: its 7 Treasures are never created.
    You win on combat 12. Final Treasure count is 22 (11 triggers resolved for +77, 11 activations paid −55, from a base of 0), not 29 — the state '29 Treasures after combat 12's trigger' never exists.
    CR 704.3 — “the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event. If any state-based actions are performed as a result of a check, the check is repeated; otherwise all triggered abilities that are waiting to be put on the stack are put on the stack”
    CR 104.2a — “A player still in the game wins the game if that player’s opponents have all left the game. This happens immediately and overrides all effects that would preclude that player from winning the game.”
    CR 104.1 — “A game ends immediately when a player wins, when the game is a draw, or when the game is restarted.”
  10. Bounds: the loop is not infinite in the rules sense — each cycle requires a real activation, a real combat, and a real damage event. It ends if (a) Gnawbone is removed or the attack is blocked/prevented so the trigger refunds under 5 mana and the cost eventually can't be fully paid, (b) an opponent presents flying/reach blockers, or (c) every opponent has lost and left the game, at which point you win and the game itself ends.
    Bounded by Gnawbone's survival, opposing flying/reach blockers, and the supply of opponents. In this scenario none of those bounds bite before both opponents are dead.
    CR 118.3 — “A player can’t pay a cost without having the necessary resources to pay it fully.”
    CR 702.9b — “A creature with flying can’t be blocked except by creatures with flying and/or reach.”
    CR 104.5 — “If a player loses the game, that player leaves the game.”
