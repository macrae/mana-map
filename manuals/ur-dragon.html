Start with what the zone guarantees: your commander is always available to cast from the command zone (CR 903.4). This deck files that guarantee differently from every other list at the table. The Ur-Dragon's eminence ability works while he sits in the zone — every Dragon spell you cast costs {1} less, from turn zero, for as long as the game lasts. The guarantee is not that you will draw him. It is that you are always being paid.

Now the tax. Each cast from the zone after the first costs an additional {2} (CR 903.8). The ladder reads nine, eleven, thirteen — against a deck whose mean available mana on turn nine is 6.165. The record is blunt: the first cast is an event, the second is a stretch, the third is fiction. Protection is therefore the better spend, and the deck's own insurance argues the same — Deflecting Swat is free only while a commander is on your battlefield, so landing him once and keeping him is worth more than any recast plan.

There is a rental clause, and the Counselor commends it to your attention. Hellkite Courser's enters-trigger puts The Ur-Dragon onto the battlefield from the command zone, hasty, for exactly one attack — a card per attacking Dragon plus a free permanent from hand — then returns him at end step. The tax counts casts (CR 903.8), and this never was one. The zone gets its tenant back untaxed. The route everyone assumes works, Sneak Attack, does not: it reaches creature cards in your hand, and the command zone is not your hand (CR 903.6; the full holding is case A-005).

Color identity: five colours costs you nothing in exclusions — every card in the format's identity rules is available to this list — and everything in logistics. The 99 pays for WUBRG with sixteen tapped lands and an eleven-source black column; Sources Say prices that bill in full.

The 21-point clock (CR 903.10a) is arithmetic worth savoring: a 10/10 commander deals 20 commander damage in two connections — one point short. Three clean hits close it, and a Thrakkus the Butcher doubling still stops at 20 on one swing. The holding: commander damage here is a coincidence, not a plan. The deck's real kills are filed under The Kill.

The Coach's close: cast him when the table's interaction is spent, shield him once he lands, and never mulligan a hand looking for him. The zone already holds him — and the zone is where he earns.

THE PLAN

LET THEM WATCH THE WRONG CLOCK

The table budgets its answers for a nine-mana commander who averages a turn-8.967 arrival. Your actual clock is one enchantment and a connecting Dragon — and the checker already signed off on it.

The table looks at a nine-mana commander in five colours and files you under slow. Let them. Your engine has been running since your first upkeep — eminence takes {1} off every other Dragon spell from the command zone, and nothing has to resolve for that to be true.

So stop building turns around the commander. The simulator puts his mean cast at turn 8.967 and his cast-by-turn-six rate at 0.0%; cast the discounted Dragon instead, every single time. He is a luxury you take when nine mana is spare, never a plan you wait on.

Stack the reducers and the curve stops being a curve. Dragonspeaker Shaman takes {2} off, Urza's Incubator another {2}, Herald's Horn {1} and a look at the top of your library. Two of those down and Terror of the Peaks is a three-mana play, while Korlessa, Scale Singer casts Dragons off the top so your hand is not your only hand.

The fair kill is critical mass, and the order is the whole trick: payoff first, Dragon second. Dragon Tempest, Scourge of Valkas and Terror of the Peaks pay per Dragon entering and pay nothing for the ones already down. Land Miirym, Sentinel Wyrm before your top end and every nontoken Dragon after her comes with a copy — one Lathliss under her and Panharmonicon is 64 damage across twelve targets (stack 002).

The unfair kill is Aggravated Assault, and three contained two-card infinites run through that one enchantment — which is exactly why the computed floor reads bracket 4. Old Gnawbone funds the activation out of Treasures at net +2 a cycle and kills the whole table (stack 006); Savage Ventmaw's attack mana pays it with {R} left over (stack 003). So protect the enchantment, not the commander, and never cast it early to look busy. It is your loudest tell, and a bracket-4 table kills it on sight.

One trap will cost you a game if you let it. Haven of the Spirit Dragon, Maelstrom of the Spirit Dragon, Unclaimed Territory, Rivaz of the Claw and an attacking Klauth all make spells-only mana, and an activated ability is not a spell — none of it pays Assault's {3}{R}{R}. Count real mana before you swing, and lean on Treasures: Goldspan Dragon, Old Gnawbone, Cavern-Hoard Dragon and a dying Atsushi, the Blazing Sky all pay for anything.

Early, you are the value seat, and you should enjoy it. Ramp, fix, hold Deflecting Swat and a protection spell, and let someone louder eat the removal. Then take one window: Hellkite Courser rents your commander out for a hasty attack, Sneak Attack drops Drakuseth for {R} (stack 005), Wrathful Red Dragon turns the next sweeper into a 39-point execution (stack 004). Take the turn that wins, not the turn that impresses.

The question put to the record

    CR 800.4a — “This is not a state-based action. It happens as soon as the player leaves the game.”

It ends if (a) Gnawbone is removed or the attack is blocked/prevented so the trigger refunds under 5 mana and the cost eventually can't be fully paid, (b) an opponent presents flying/reach blockers, or (c) every opponent has lost and left the game, at which point you win and the game itself ends.
    Bounded by Gnawbone's survival, opposing flying/reach blockers, and the supply of opponents. In this scenario none of those bounds bite before both opponents are dead.
