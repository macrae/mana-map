THE PLAN

THE DECK THAT UPGRADED ITSELF

The table reads five-colour Dragons as a battlecruiser, and for four turns they're right. Then the verification loop's six swaps show up — and the deck they earned is a combo deck wearing a battlecruiser costume, built around a commander it almost never casts.

Start by unlearning the obvious read: The Ur-Dragon is not the plan. The goldfish simulator (seed 42, 10,000 iterations, conservative model — cost reducers not simulated) puts his mean cast on turn 8.967, with a 0.0% cast-by-turn-6 rate and a cast at all by turn 10 in just 13.7% of runs. What IS the plan is his Eminence line, which works from the command zone starting on turn zero: every other Dragon spell you cast costs {1} less, all game, without him ever leaving home. Stack that with Dragonspeaker Shaman, Herald's Horn, and Urza's Incubator and a nine-drop curve plays like a six-drop curve — and Korlessa, Scale Singer turns the top of your library into a second hand for those discounted Dragons.

The fair half of the kill is the enters-and-attacks payoff layer. Dragon Tempest, Scourge of Valkas, and Terror of the Peaks turn each Dragon entering into targeted damage; Garruk's Uprising and Temur Ascendancy turn big bodies into cards; Miirym, Sentinel Wyrm turns every nontoken Dragon into two. This is a critical-mass deck: below the threshold you have undersized, expensive bodies, and past it every new Dragon draws, burns, and copies itself. The verified stacks show the payoff math — one Ancient Gold Dragon connection under Dragon Tempest produced fifteen tokens and up to 255 points of divisible damage (stack 001), and a single Lathliss cast under Miirym plus Panharmonicon produced two extra Lathlisses and 64 damage across twelve triggers (stack 002).

The unfair half is new in this version: the deck now carries three contained two-card infinites, and every one of them routes through Aggravated Assault — that concentration is why the computed bracket floor reads 4. Two of the three are checker-verified: Savage Ventmaw's attack mana pays the activation with {R} left over, a self-funding unbounded-combats loop at +1 mana per cycle (stack 003), and Old Gnawbone alone banks net +2 Treasures per unblocked connection and kills the table sequentially — opponents dead on combats 6 and 12 in the verified game (stack 006). The third pairing, Cavern-Hoard Dragon plus Assault, still needs a stack scenario; do not present it as fact. Mind the funding trap deck-facts flags: five of your sources — Haven of the Spirit Dragon, Maelstrom of the Spirit Dragon, Unclaimed Territory, Rivaz of the Claw, and an attacking Klauth — make spells-only mana, and an activated ability is not a spell, so none of it can ever pay Assault's {3}{R}{R}. Treasures are the legal wallet, which is exactly why Atsushi, the Blazing Sky is here: he dies forward into three unrestricted Treasures. Around the engines sit the velocity and insurance pieces: Sneak Attack cheats any Dragon in the 99 into play for {R} with all its triggers live (stack 005 — which also refuted the commander-cheat reading), Hellkite Courser is the deck's real commander cheat, renting The Ur-Dragon out of the command zone for one hasty attack, and Wrathful Red Dragon makes any damage-based sweeper a 39-point execution aimed at one opponent (stack 004).

Your seat for the first five turns is control-slash-value, whether you like it or not: the deck averages 4.9 available mana and 1.5 bodies at turn 6, so it loses every early race. The goldfish says the development plan is real but not fast — ramp or fixing is drawn by turn 6 in 71.6% of games, a cost reducer in only 33.2%, and the reducer-plus-ramp “turn-5 Ur-Dragon hand” in just 23.1% — so ramp, fix, deploy in waves, and do not promise yourself an early commander. Protection is in hand by turn 6 in 66.0% of games; hold it for the one turn that matters. That turn is concrete: Aggravated Assault live with a connecting Gnawbone or Ventmaw, or a Sneak Attack turn with a payoff already down. Take that window once, decisively — every earlier turn, stay boring and let the table watch for a commander you were never in a hurry to cast.
