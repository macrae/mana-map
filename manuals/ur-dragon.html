FROM THE EDITOR

THE DRAGON YOU NEVER CAST

The card on the cover costs nine mana and mostly never arrives. This is an issue about what the deck does in the meantime, and about the one enchantment that decides whether the table comes for you first.

MANA MAP
From the Editor
Vol. 5 · THE UR-DRAGON

The card on the front of this deck costs nine mana, and the argument in the Pilot's Log is about whether you should ever pay it.

The Ur-Dragon has an ability that runs from the command zone, the one place nobody can reach. Whether that ability is what actually carries the deck is what the panel fights over, and it is a better fight than I expected.

What the deck does meanwhile is put enormous fliers onto the table in waves and charge everybody for each one. One Dragon arriving becomes three. Each arrival is priced in damage, or cards, or Treasure. Underneath it sits one enchantment your whole table can name on sight, which is either how you win or why you get attacked first — a distinction Counselor Dictum spends most of the log drawing.

Coach Brightside opens by telling you to stop trying to cast your commander. He is right, his reasoning has not been proved, and he loses a second argument later about how fast the famous loop kills. Ledger has the rate that settles the first; the Counselor has the arithmetic that settles the second. Watch him take both.

You will have a good time here if you like being enormous and patient at once — a battlecruiser with a certified escape hatch it mostly does not need. You will not, if you want your win legible on turn four. This deck asks you to hold five colours and a top-heavy hand and trust that everyone else's clock is slower than yours.

Start with what the zone guarantees: your commander is always available to cast from it (CR 903.4). No other format promises you a specific card every game. This deck files that promise differently from every other seat at the table, because The Ur-Dragon's eminence works while he sits in the zone — every other Dragon spell you cast costs {1} less, from your first upkeep, whether or not he ever moves. The guarantee is not that you will draw him. It is that you are already being paid.

Now the tax. Each cast from the zone after the first costs an additional {2} (CR 903.8), so the ladder reads nine, then eleven, then thirteen. Set that against the ledger: mean available mana is 6.165 on turn nine, and in 8,634 of 10,000 simulated games he never lands inside ten turns at all. The first cast is an event, the second is a stretch, the third is fiction. So protect the one you get. Deflecting Swat and Flawless Maneuver are free only while a commander is on your battlefield, which means keeping him beats recasting him by a distance.

There is a rental clause, and the Counselor commends it to your attention. Hellkite Courser's enters-trigger puts The Ur-Dragon onto the battlefield from the command zone, hasty, for exactly one attack — a card per attacking Dragon plus a free permanent — then returns him at end step. The tax counts casts, and this never was one: the zone gets its tenant back untaxed. The route everyone assumes works does not. Sneak Attack reaches a creature card in your hand, and the command zone is not your hand (CR 903.6; the full holding is case A-005).

Colour identity costs you nothing in exclusions and everything in logistics. Five colours means no card in the format is outside your identity. It also means 34 lands answering 101 coloured pips, sixteen of which have 'enters tapped' printed somewhere on the card. Sources Say prices that bill in full. Learn one clause now: five of your mana sources make spells-only mana. It casts Dragons beautifully and it can never pay an activated ability.

The 21-point commander clock (CR 903.10a) is arithmetic worth savouring and then setting down. A 10/10 deals 20 commander damage in two connections — one point short — so it takes three clean hits, and even Thrakkus the Butcher doubling stops at 20 on a single swing. Commander damage here is a coincidence, not a plan. The kills are filed under The Kill.

The Coach's close, and it is a table read. A nine-mana commander is the best decoy in Commander: every seat prices you at turn nine while your actual game is a discounted Dragon on turn four. Let them keep the read. Cast him when the table's interaction has been spent elsewhere, shield him the turn he lands, and never mulligan a hand hunting for him. The zone already holds him, and the zone is where he earns.

THE PLAN

PAYOFF FIRST, DRAGON SECOND

The loop the whole table fears assembles in 6.4% of your games. Build for the payoff layer that kills in 62.2% of them, and take the loop the turn it walks in.

They count to nine in your command zone, see five colours, and file you under durdle. Let them. It is the only free thing you get all game. Eminence works from the zone, so every other Dragon costs {1} less from your first upkeep.

So stop building turns around the commander: mean cast turn 8.967, 0.0% by turn six, and none at all inside ten turns in 8,634 of 10,000 games. Cast the discounted Dragon. Every time.

Dragon Tempest, Scourge of Valkas and Terror of the Peaks pay for every Dragon entering after them, nothing for those already down. You hold one by turn six in 62.2% of games; play the early one into an empty board and be patient. Land Miirym, Sentinel Wyrm before your top end: with Panharmonicon, Terror of the Peaks and Dragon Tempest down, one Lathliss cast is 64 damage in twelve separately aimed instances (stack 002).

Aggravated Assault plus a mana dragon assembles in 3.6% of games by turn six — your ceiling, not your schedule. Never wait on it; hold the enchantment until the turn it kills someone. All three infinites run through it, and Scourge of the Throne, your only other extra-combat effect, works once a turn and only on the highest-life player.

Count real mana before you swing. Haven of the Spirit Dragon, Maelstrom of the Spirit Dragon, Unclaimed Territory, Rivaz of the Claw and an attacking Klauth make spells-only mana, and an ability is not a spell — none of it pays Assault's {3}{R}{R}. Treasures do: bank Old Gnawbone, Goldspan Dragon, Cavern-Hoard Dragon, a dead Atsushi.

Early, be the value seat: ramp, fix, let a louder player eat the removal, hold protection. You have one by turn six in 66.0% of games — your signal to commit the wave. Then take a window. Hellkite Courser rents the commander out for a hasty attack, Sneak Attack drops Drakuseth for {R} (stack 005), Wrathful Red Dragon turns the next sweeper into a 39-point execution (stack 004).

What this does not model

  • Stack 003's eleven damage a combat is a three-card board — Old Gnawbone is attacking alongside Savage Ventmaw. The named pair alone puts four into a forty-life seat, which is the tenth combat, not the fourth.
  • Stack 006 is the half that kills on schedule: seven Treasures a swing, net two a cycle, one seat dead on combat six and the second on combat twelve. It assumes nothing blocks a 7/7 flier and nobody answers the enchantment across twelve combats.
  • The discount this plan is built on is eminence, and no checker-passed stack has run a Dragon spell through it. The commander's own mean cast turn is 8.967, with 0.0% of games by turn six.
  • Unsettled Stack 001 kills every opponent on its board, but its board assumes Ancient Gold Dragon has already connected in combat, and its d20 needs 12 or better rather than the 15 the scenario fixes (derived: three seats at 40 needs 3 x ceil(40/(N+2)) <= N, so N=12; nine faces of twenty). Build the reachable version of THE ENTRY KILL instead — Dragon Tempest or Terror of the Peaks or Scourge of Valkas, with a widener, and one nontoken Dragon cast — and resolve whether the cascade reaches three opponents at 40 or state the exact shortfall, as stack 002 does at 64 against 72.
  • Unsettled Hellkite Courser's enters-the-battlefield trigger, one domain and three sub-questions: does it put The Ur-Dragon onto the battlefield from the command zone, does the haste it grants make an attack legal that turn, and does the return to the command zone at the beginning of the next end step add nothing to the commander tax under CR 903.8?
  • Unsettled The Ur-Dragon's attack trigger with the commander on the battlefield: does 'whenever one or more Dragons you control attack' draw exactly one card per attacking Dragon, and may the permanent it then puts onto the battlefield from hand be any permanent card rather than a Dragon?
  • Simulation 10,000 runs of resource development, not of games — no opponent acts in any of them.

6 further questions about this engine are open and unsettled.

A-005 Sneak Attack ✓ cleared, 2 cycle(s) · 11 steps · 23 citations
Rules version 2026-06-19
The question put to the record
Two questions in one domain (what Sneak Attack's own text reaches). (a) Activate Sneak Attack paying {R}, putting Drakuseth onto the battlefield: walk the turn — is Drakuseth's attack trigger a cast trigger or an attack trigger, does it fire when Drakuseth attacks the turn it enters, when is the sacrifice trigger put on the stack and is it optional, and does the sacrificed Drakuseth's damage already dealt stand? (b) The strategic frame proposes Sneak Attack as a way to cheat The Ur-Dragon into play, dodging the nine-mana cast: can Sneak Attack ever put The Ur-Dragon onto the battlefield given the commander begins the game in the command zone and Sneak Attack reaches only 'a creature card from your hand'?
  1. Sneak Attack's activated ability resolves. Following its instructions in order, you choose to put Drakuseth, Maw of Flames from your hand onto the battlefield; it gains haste; and the resolution creates a delayed triggered ability — 'Sacrifice the creature at the beginning of the next end step.'
    Drakuseth is on the battlefield as a 7/7 flier with haste. A delayed triggered ability waiting on the next end step now exists. Drakuseth was never cast, so no cast-related events occur.
    CR 608.2c — “The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written.”
    CR 603.7a — “Delayed triggered abilities are created during the resolution of spells or abilities”
  2. You move to combat and declare Drakuseth as an attacker against the open opponent (the one with no board). Normally a creature that entered this turn could not attack, but haste overrides the summoning-sickness rule.
    Drakuseth legally attacks the turn it entered the battlefield.
    CR 302.6 — “A creature can’t attack unless it has been under its controller’s control continuously since their most recent turn began.”
    CR 702.10b — “If a creature has haste, it can attack even if it hasn’t been controlled by its controller continuously since their most recent turn began.”
  3. Drakuseth's 'Whenever Drakuseth attacks' ability triggers. It is an attack trigger, not a cast trigger: it cares only about Drakuseth being declared as an attacker, so it fires whether Drakuseth was cast or put onto the battlefield by Sneak Attack.
    The attack trigger fires the turn Drakuseth enters — cheating it in loses nothing from this ability.
    CR 508.3a — “An ability that reads “Whenever [a creature] attacks, . . .” triggers if that creature is declared as an attacker.”
    CR 508.2a — “Abilities that trigger on a creature attacking trigger only at the point the creature is declared as an attacker.”
  4. The trigger is put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority (still in the declare attackers step), and its targets are chosen then: 4 damage to the opponent with the 2/2, 3 damage to that opponent's 2/2, and 3 damage to the defending opponent ('up to two other targets').
    Drakuseth's trigger is on the stack with three distinct targets, before blockers are ever declared.
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority.”
    CR 115.1d — “The target(s) are chosen as the ability is put on the stack”
  5. All players pass priority and the trigger resolves during the declare attackers step: Drakuseth deals 4 damage to the second opponent, 3 to their 2/2, and 3 to the defending opponent. Damage dealt to a player makes them lose that much life.
    Second opponent: 40 → 36. Defending opponent: 40 → 37. The 2/2 has 3 damage marked on it.
    CR 508.2 — “Second, the active player gets priority.”
    CR 119.2 — “Damage dealt to a player normally causes that player to lose that much life.”
  6. State-based actions are checked before any player receives priority again: the 2/2 has damage marked on it greater than or equal to its toughness.
    The 2/2 has been dealt lethal damage and is destroyed. The battlefield's only potential blocker is gone (it could not have blocked the flier anyway).
    CR 704.5g — “If a creature has toughness greater than 0, it has damage marked on it, and the total damage marked on it is greater than or equal to its toughness, that creature has been dealt lethal damage and is destroyed.”
    CR 117.5 — “the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event”
  7. Combat continues. Drakuseth (7/7 flying) is unblocked; in the combat damage step it deals its combat damage to the defending opponent.
    Defending opponent: 37 → 30. One {R} activation delivered 17 total damage across the table.
    CR 510.1b — “An unblocked creature assigns its combat damage to the player, planeswalker, or battle it’s attacking.”
    CR 510.2 — “all combat damage that’s been assigned is dealt simultaneously”
  8. At the beginning of your end step, Sneak Attack's delayed triggered ability triggers and is put on the stack. Its instruction is 'Sacrifice the creature' — there is no 'may'; when it resolves you must follow it. It is not optional.
    The mandatory sacrifice trigger is on the stack. (Players may respond to it — e.g., a blink effect would exile Drakuseth first — but nothing in this scenario does.)
    CR 603.7b — “A delayed triggered ability will trigger only once—the next time its trigger event occurs”
    CR 513.1 — “Once it begins, the active player gets priority.”
    CR 608.2c — “The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written.”
  9. The sacrifice trigger resolves: you move Drakuseth from the battlefield to its owner's graveyard. The damage it dealt this turn was a set of one-shot effects that already happened; sacrificing the creature does not undo them.
    Drakuseth is in the graveyard. All 17 damage stands: opponents remain at 30 and 36.
    CR 701.21a — “To sacrifice a permanent, its controller moves it from the battlefield directly to its owner’s graveyard.”
    CR 610.1 — “A one-shot effect does something just once and doesn’t have a duration. Examples include dealing damage”
  10. (b) Zone check for The Ur-Dragon. As a commander it began the game in the command zone, and it has not been cast, so it is there now. The command zone and the hand are different zones — a card in the command zone is not 'a creature card from your hand.'
    Sneak Attack's ability has nothing legal to reach: The Ur-Dragon is not in your hand, and Sneak Attack's text reaches only your hand.
    CR 903.6 — “At the start of the game, each player puts their commander from their deck face up into the command zone.”
    CR 402.1 — “The hand is where a player holds cards that have been drawn.”
  11. (b) conclusion — REFUTATION. From the command zone, the only thing the rules let you do with The Ur-Dragon is cast it (nine mana, plus commander tax on later casts). Sneak Attack cannot put it onto the battlefield from there. The narrow exception: if The Ur-Dragon were genuinely in your hand — you drew it after it was shuffled away, or you declined the replacement that returns it to the command zone — Sneak Attack could then cheat it in, but that is not the commander's normal zone and not what the strategic frame described.
    The strategic frame's 'commander-cheat vector' claim is refuted: Sneak Attack does not dodge the nine-mana cast for a commander sitting in the command zone. (The deck's actual commander-cheat is Hellkite Courser, whose oracle text reads 'put a commander you own from the command zone onto the battlefield.')
    CR 903.8 — “A commander cast from the command zone costs an additional {2} for each previous time the player casting it has cast it from the command zone that game.”
    CR 903.9b — “If a commander would be put into its owner’s hand or library from anywhere, its owner may put it into the command zone instead.”
