THE PLAN

CAST EVERYTHING BUT HIM

What is the strongest card in a deck that begins every game with it face up and never pays a mana for it? Edgar Markov makes a Vampire for every Vampire you cast — from the command zone, from turn 1, for free — and the whole game is deciding which turn he finally sends the bill.

Start with the number that defines this deck: zero. That is what the engine costs. Edgar Markov's eminence triggers from the command zone — every other Vampire spell you cast makes a 1/1 black Vampire token while he sits at home, untaxed and untouchable. The goldfish run (seed 42, 10,000 iterations, conservative — no interaction modeled) shows what that buys: 1.9 bodies by turn 3, 4.2 by turn 6, on a curve that stacks 8 one-drops and 19 each at two and three. You are the beatdown by default, and unlike most beatdown decks you rebuild after a sweeper at the price of simply casting your next Vampire.

Going wide is not the whole plan, because roughly 120 combined life does not fall to 1/1s. The deck's honest reach is the each-opponent drain: Sanctum Seeker converts every attacking Vampire into 1 life from each opponent, and Bloodletter of Aclazotz doubles opponents' life loss on your turn. Stack 003 verified the arithmetic: seven attacking Vampires drain each opponent for 14 at declare-attackers — before blockers exist — and a 14-life opponent is simply eliminated by the trigger stack. A drain engine is drawn by turn 6 in 62.5% of games; a lord or Vampire payoff in 67.7%. Layer a token doubler (42.4% by turn 6) and stack 002's math kicks in: one two-mana Vampire cast becomes five bodies through Anointed Procession and Mondrak.

Edgar himself is the pivot, not the opener. His goldfish median cast is turn 6 (mean 6.613, 52.1% by turn 6), and the correct pivot is casting him the turn you alpha-strike: his attack trigger puts a +1/+1 counter on every Vampire you control, converting an accumulated token board into lethal width. Hold protection for exactly that turn — Akroma's Will, Boros Charm, Flawless Maneuver; the goldfish holds a protection piece by turn 6 in 57.1% of games — and remember the tension the strategic frame names: Deflecting Swat and Flawless Maneuver are free only while you control a commander, which is precisely what the eminence plan delays.

The infinites are insurance, not plan A. Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose plus either gain-on-loss half — Exquisite Blood or Bloodthirsty Conqueror — is a verified, deterministic table-kill (stacks 001 and 004, both checker-passed), but natural assembly is 2.8% by turn 6 and 4.9% by turn 10. What changes the math is the tutor suite: Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, and Diabolic Intent are not modeled by the goldfish, and at a bracket-4 floor the table will play as though the combo is coming regardless. Beat down, drain wide, and keep the knockout in your pocket for the game that goes long.

Deploy in waves under wrath-math: commit what wins before the near-certain sweeper, and let eminence be the rebuild — one token per cast, no recast tax, forever.

Cast Edgar (median cast turn 6) into a protected alpha strike — his attack trigger puts a +1/+1 counter on every Vampire you control, and resolving him turns Flawless Maneuver and Deflecting Swat free — or tutor the Vito half onto a gain-on-loss piece already in play and end the game on the stack instead.

Exquisite Blood + Vito: is the classic drain loop a table-kill?

The deck's bracket-4 backbone, verified end to end against the June 19, 2026 comprehensive rules over a 10-step resolution. The scene: Exquisite Blood and Vito on your battlefield, and Vampire Nighthawk's lifelink has just gained you 2 off combat damage. That 2-life seed is all the loop needs. Vito's trigger drains a target opponent, Exquisite Blood gains it back, Vito triggers again — and because each Vito trigger is a new object whose target you choose fresh as it goes on the stack, you walk the drain across the table in any order. The checker confirmed the parts people argue about: the loop is shortcut-legal, a targeted opponent dying mid-loop does not break the chain (their killing life loss still triggers your enchantment, which targets nothing), and from this board it takes exactly 38 Vito resolutions — 6, 13, and 19 — to clear opponents at 12, 25, and 38 life. You end at 105 life, having spent no mana and no cards, and win by rule 104.2a the moment the last opponent leaves.

The question
Resolve the loop from this 2-life seed. Is it deterministic and unbounded while an opponent survives to be targeted? Each Vito trigger targets ONE opponent — walk the targeting each iteration: can you retarget successive triggers at different opponents, what happens when the targeted opponent's life reaches zero mid-loop (state-based actions, when they lose, whether Exquisite Blood still triggers off the loss that killed them), and can the loop kill all three opponents from this position? What bounds it — and does the loop survive the death of the creature that seeded it?

Result. Deterministic table-kill, VERIFIED. From the 2-life seed, 38 Vito resolutions (6 + 13 + 19) drain opponent 3 (12), opponent 2 (25, one point of overkill), and opponent 1 (38) in any order you choose, retargeting each fresh trigger. Each opponent's killing loss still triggers Exquisite Blood, so the chain crosses deaths. You win via 104.2a when the last opponent leaves.

Verified line 002

Edgar's eminence through two doublers: how many bodies per cast?

The token-math stack, and the one to internalize because it runs every turn, not just combo turns. You cast Gifted Aetherborn for {B}{B} with Edgar still in the command zone and both doublers out. Eminence triggers once — one token event — and Anointed Procession and Mondrak each double it in whichever order you like (the checker confirmed the result is identical: 1 becomes 2 becomes 4). Those four 1/1s each enter carrying a Vampire Socialite counter because an opponent lost life this turn, and the Aetherborn itself arrives as a 3/4. Net: five new Vampire bodies off one two-mana spell. Just as important is what does not happen: eminence does not re-trigger on the tokens (they are created, not cast), and Cordial Vampire stays silent because nothing died. This is the multiplication table behind the goldfish's body counts — know it cold before you commit to a doubler turn.

The question
Resolve the eminence trigger and then the Gifted Aetherborn spell. How do the two token-doubling replacement effects stack on the single eminence token event (order, and the resulting token count)? Does the eminence trigger fire from the command zone? Do the tokens — and the resolved Gifted Aetherborn — each enter with Vampire Socialite's additional +1/+1 counter given an opponent lost life this turn? Final board: exactly how many new Vampire bodies, at what sizes, from this one two-mana cast?

Result. One {B}{B} cast yields five new Vampire bodies: eminence's single token event, doubled by Anointed Procession and Mondrak in an order you choose (result identical either way, 1→2→4), makes four 1/1 black Vampire tokens that each enter with a Vampire Socialite +1/+1 counter (2/2s), and the resolved Gifted Aetherborn also enters with a counter (3/4 deathtouch/lifelink). Eminence does not re-trigger on the tokens (created, not cast), and Cordial Vampire never triggers because nothing dies.

Verified line 003

Bloodletter + Sanctum Seeker: what does an attack actually drain?

The drain math that makes go-wide combat real against three opponents, verified at the moment of declare-attackers. Seven Vampires attack — Bloodletter, Sanctum Seeker, four eminence tokens, and a hasty Edgar — and Sanctum Seeker triggers once per attacking Vampire: seven triggers. It is your turn, so Bloodletter's replacement doubles each opponent's loss per trigger event: every opponent loses 2, seven times, for 14 total. Your gain is not doubled — Bloodletter reads opponents' life loss only — so you gain 7. The checker confirmed the brutal part: the opponent at 14 hits exactly 0 on the seventh trigger and is eliminated by state-based actions before blockers are ever declared, and because you control the remaining triggers, an opponent's death never strands them. Edgar's own attack trigger has already put a counter on all seven bodies. This is 14 damage per opponent for the cost of turning creatures sideways.

The question
One rules domain: the life-loss arithmetic of this attack declaration, before any blocks or combat damage. Seven Vampires attacked: how many Sanctum Seeker triggers fire, how much life does EACH opponent lose per trigger through Bloodletter's replacement effect (does doubling apply per trigger event), how much life do you gain (is your gain doubled — Bloodletter only doubles opponents' loss), does Edgar's counter trigger change the drain, and what are all three life totals after every trigger resolves? Does the trigger drain alone kill opponent 2 at 14, and do the deaths' timing (state-based actions between resolutions) stop remaining triggers?

Result. Attack declaration alone (before blocks or any combat damage): 7 Sanctum Seeker triggers, each draining every opponent for 2 (1 doubled once per event by Bloodletter — it is your turn) and gaining you 1 (NOT doubled; Bloodletter replaces only opponents' life loss). Edgar's trigger adds +1/+1 counters but changes nothing about the drain. Opponent 2 (14 life) hits exactly 0 on the seventh and final trigger and is eliminated by state-based actions. Final: you 33, opponent 1 at 7, opponent 2 eliminated — all before blockers are even declared.

Bloodthirsty Conqueror + Vito: the second infinite, verified on its own terms

The second infinite, verified on its own terms rather than by analogy — the strategic frame insisted, and it was right to. Bloodthirsty Conqueror's trigger matches Exquisite Blood's word for word, and the checker confirmed its '(Damage causes loss of life.)' parenthetical is reminder text, not a damage requirement: Vito's direct life loss triggers it just fine. From a 3-life seed the loop runs exactly as stack 001 did — deterministic, shortcut-legal, retargetable each iteration, unbroken by a mid-loop death — and kills a 9-life and a 30-life opponent in 13 Vito resolutions with zero overkill, leaving you at 60. The honest cost is the difference: Conqueror is a 5/5 creature, and every iteration's priority window admits instant-speed creature removal as a loop-breaker, making this pairing strictly more fragile than the enchantment version. Its flying and deathtouch contribute nothing to the loop — they are for the fair game this card also happens to dominate.

Is Conqueror + Vito deterministic and unbounded exactly as Exquisite Blood + Vito is, or does anything differ (Conqueror is a creature — what removal windows exist mid-loop; does its deathtouch/flying matter to the loop itself)? The one real difference from stack 001: Conqueror is a creature, so every iteration's priority window admits instant-speed creature removal as a loop-breaker — irrelevant here, where no opponent holds interaction. NO QUIET GAMES

Eminence banks a visible body every time you cast a spell — so 'I haven't done anything' is unsellable from turn 1. When your threat level is structural, what exactly is a deflection budget for, and what do you buy with the last of it?

You do not get a quiet game. Edgar Markov is one of the most famous commanders in the format, and a bracket-4 table reads your deck from the moment your commander is announced: eminence tokens from turn 1 make 'I haven't done anything' unsellable, because every cheap Vampire you cast visibly banks a body (goldfish: 1.9 bodies by turn 3, 4.2 by turn 6 with zero mana spent on the commander). Your perceived threat updates every cast, so your deflection budget spends down fast — plan the pivot before it's gone, not after.

What gets answered first, in order: Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose dies on sight. Both of the deck's contained infinites route through him (Exquisite Blood + Vito, popularity 107,037; Bloodthirsty Conqueror + Vito, 64,824), and at a bracket-4 table everyone knows it — the strategic frame is blunt that opponents will play against the loop even though natural assembly is only 2.8% by turn 6. Second: the drain engines (Sanctum Seeker, Bloodletter of Aclazotz) once anyone does the stack-003 math — seven attackers is 14 life from each opponent at declare attackers, before a point of combat damage. Third: Grave Pact and the doublers draw enchantment removal from anyone who has it. The lords mostly survive; they're the wrong things to protect anyway.

Sequencing to protect the two-card kills: the tutors are your concealment. Demonic Tutor and Vampiric Tutor put the missing half into hidden zones — the table knows what a tutor with Vito on board means contextually, but they cannot see the card, and Exquisite Blood in hand cannot be answered. So never present the pair in the same open-mana window: land the half the table undervalues first (Exquisite Blood alone reads as a lifegain trinket; Vito alone is a 3-mana creature they may not want to spend real removal on), and complete it the turn the answer-holder is tapped out or has just spent the wrath. Prefer the enchantment half when interaction is up — stack 004's honest finding is that the Conqueror loop offers a removal window every iteration because the gain half is a creature, while stack 001's loop only needed Vito to survive one window.

Edgar himself: keep him in the zone. The engine is free from there (his eminence ability states its zones and functions from the command zone — CR 113.6b), and goldfish says the honest cast is late anyway: mean 6.613, median 6, only 52.1% castable by turn 6 on 5.36 mean mana. Cast him the turn he converts the board — haste plus the attack trigger (+1/+1 counter on every Vampire) turns an accumulated token board into an alpha strike — and note that resolving him flips on your free protection suite: Flawless Maneuver and Deflecting Swat cost {0} only while you control a commander. Cast Edgar = swing wide with a free indestructible effect held. That is the turn to embrace archenemy: you are already the threat by construction, so take the label on the turn it kills a player, not a turn earlier.

Case A-001
Exquisite Blood + Vito: is the classic drain loop a table-kill?
Rules version 2026-06-19 · status: cleared in 1 review cycle(s)
Verified
  1. Seed: Vampire Nighthawk's lifelink combat damage to opponent 3 caused you to gain 2 life (you: 31 as given). Vito's ability ('Whenever you gain life, target opponent loses that much life') triggered on that gain and is on the stack. Its target — opponent 3, at 12 — was locked in when the trigger was put on the stack.
    One Vito trigger on the stack targeting opponent 3 for 2. Targets are per-instance choices made at put-on-stack time, not at resolution.
    CR 702.15b — “Damage dealt by a source with lifelink causes that source’s controller, or its owner if it has no controller, to gain that much life (in addition to any other results that damage causes).”
    CR 115.1d — “A triggered ability is targeted if it identifies something it will affect by using the phrase “target [something],” where the “something” is a phrase that describes an object and/or player. The target(s) are chosen as the ability is put on the stack; see rule 603.3d.”
  2. Vito's trigger resolves (no responses; opponents hold no instant-speed interaction). Opponent 3 loses 2 life: 12 -> 10. That life loss matches Exquisite Blood's trigger condition ('Whenever an opponent loses life, you gain that much life'), so Exquisite Blood triggers during resolution — but nothing happens yet.
    Opponent 3 at 10. Exquisite Blood has triggered and is waiting to be put on the stack.
    CR 117.2a — “Triggered abilities can trigger at any time, including while a spell is being cast, an ability is being activated, or a spell or ability is resolving. (See rule 603, “Handling Triggered Abilities.”) However, nothing actually happens at the time an ability triggers.”
  3. The next time a player would receive priority, state-based actions are checked (none apply — opponent 3 is at 10), then Exquisite Blood's trigger is put on the stack. It has no targets. It resolves: you gain 2 (31 -> 33). That life gain triggers Vito again.
    You gain 2 per iteration; each Exquisite Blood resolution re-triggers Vito. The engine is now self-sustaining.
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority.”
    CR 117.5 — “Each time a player would get priority, the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event (see rule 704, “State-Based Actions”), then repeats this process until no state-based actions are performed. Then triggered abilities are put on the stack”
  4. The new Vito trigger is put on the stack, and a NEW target is chosen for this new instance. You may keep targeting opponent 3 or retarget any other opponent — each iteration is an independent targeting decision, since target choice is part of putting each triggered ability on the stack.
    Retargeting between iterations is legal and free. You keep opponent 3 as the target while they live.
    CR 603.3d — “The remainder of the process for putting a triggered ability on the stack is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2c–d.”
    CR 115.1d — “The target(s) are chosen as the ability is put on the stack; see rule 603.3d.”
  5. Loop legality: each iteration (Vito resolves -> opponent loses 2 -> Exquisite Blood triggers -> resolves -> you gain 2 -> Vito triggers -> new target) is fully determined once you name the target, so the whole sequence may be proposed as a shortcut for a stated number of iterations rather than physically performed.
    The loop is deterministic and repeatable indefinitely while both permanents remain and a legal target exists. It transfers exactly 2 life per iteration from the targeted opponent to you.
    CR 732.1b — “Occasionally the game gets into a state in which a set of actions could be repeated indefinitely (thus creating a “loop”). In that case, the shortcut rules can be used to determine how many times those actions are repeated without having to actually perform them, and how the loop is broken.”
    CR 732.2a — “At any point in the game, the player with priority may suggest a shortcut by describing a sequence of game choices, for all players, that may be legally taken based on the current game state and the predictable results of the sequence of choices.”
  6. Killing opponent 3: after 6 total Vito resolutions targeting them (6 x 2 = 12), opponent 3 hits 0 life. Before any player next receives priority, state-based actions are performed: opponent 3 loses the game and leaves it.
    Opponent 3 is out. Their owned objects leave the game with them (none here).
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 104.5 — “If a player loses the game, that player leaves the game.”
    CR 800.4a — “When a player leaves the game, all objects (see rule 109) owned by that player leave the game”
  7. Does Exquisite Blood trigger off the loss that killed them? Yes. The life-loss event occurred while opponent 3 was still in the game, so Exquisite Blood triggered at that moment (though nothing happened yet); the ability is then put on the stack after state-based actions remove opponent 3, and it resolves normally — it targets nothing and belongs to YOUR enchantment. You gain 2 and Vito triggers again.
    The chain is not broken by a targeted opponent's death: the final loss still feeds a life-gain iteration, and the next Vito trigger simply chooses a new target among the survivors (603.3d would remove a Vito trigger only if NO legal target existed).
    CR 117.2a — “However, nothing actually happens at the time an ability triggers. Each time a player would receive priority, each ability that has triggered but hasn’t yet been put on the stack is put on the stack.”
    CR 117.5 — “the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event (see rule 704, “State-Based Actions”), then repeats this process until no state-based actions are performed. Then triggered abilities are put on the stack”
    CR 603.3d — “If a choice is required when the triggered ability goes on the stack but no legal choices can be made for it, or if a rule or a continuous effect otherwise makes the ability illegal, the ability is simply removed from the stack.”
  8. Edge case for completeness: if a Vito trigger were somehow already on the stack targeting a player who then left the game, that instance would not resolve (illegal target). In this loop it never happens — exactly one trigger resolves at a time and each new one is targeted fresh.
    No iteration is wasted; the loop loses no life to fizzled triggers under correct sequencing.
    CR 608.2b — “If the spell or ability specifies targets, it checks whether the targets are still legal. A target that’s no longer in the zone it was in when it was targeted is illegal.”
  9. Continue the shortcut against the survivors: 13 iterations targeting opponent 2 (25 -> -1; the 13th iteration takes them from 1 to -1) and 19 iterations targeting opponent 1 (38 -> 0). Each dies to the same state-based action and leaves the game; opponent 1's 'irrelevant permanents' leave with them. The loop does NOT depend on Vampire Nighthawk: it only seeded the first gain, and even the seed trigger exists on the stack independently of Nighthawk once it triggered — the Vito/Exquisite Blood engine runs on the two named permanents alone.
    All three opponents reach 0 or less. The loop survives the seed creature's death (or its absence entirely — any future life gain restarts it).
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 800.4a — “When a player leaves the game, all objects (see rule 109) owned by that player leave the game”
    CR 113.7a — “Once activated or triggered, an ability exists on the stack independently of its source. Destruction or removal of the source after that time won’t affect the ability.”
  10. The 38th and final Vito resolution puts opponent 1 at 0. State-based actions: opponent 1 loses and leaves. You are the only player remaining, so you win the game immediately — the last Exquisite Blood trigger (off opponent 1's killing loss) never resolves because the game has already ended.
    GAME OVER: you win. Final Exquisite Blood resolutions: 37 of 38 (the last is mooted by the win). Your life at game end: 31 + 37 x 2 = 105.
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 104.2a — “A player still in the game wins the game if that player’s opponents have all left the game. This happens immediately and overrides all effects that would preclude that player from winning the game.”

  7. State-based action check after trigger #7 resolves (before any player receives priority): opponent 2 has 0 life and loses the game, then leaves the game.
    Opponent 2 is eliminated on exactly the SEVENTH Sanctum Seeker trigger — the last one (14 life ÷ 2 per trigger = 7 triggers). The kill is not 'partway': the drain uses opponent 2's last point of life on the final trigger.
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 104.3b — “If a player’s life total is 0 or less, that player loses the game the next time a player would receive priority.”
    CR 104.5 — “If a player loses the game, that player leaves the game.”
  8. Would remaining triggers survive an opponent's death? Here none remain (Edgar's trigger already resolved first and opponent 2 died on the final drain). But in general: when a player leaves the game, only THAT player's objects leave — you control the Sanctum Seeker triggers, so any still on the stack would remain and resolve, and their 'each opponent' would simply drain only the opponents still in the game.
    Opponent 2's departure removes their own objects only; your triggers are unaffected. Combat continues against opponent 1 only.
    CR 800.4a — “When a player leaves the game, all objects (see rule 109) owned by that player leave the game and any effects which give that player control of any objects or players end.”
Case A-004
Bloodthirsty Conqueror + Vito: the second infinite, verified on its own terms
Rules version 2026-06-19 · status: cleared in 1 review cycle(s)
Verified
  1. Seed: you gained 3 life from some source (your life 24 as given already includes it). Vito's ability ('Whenever you gain life, target opponent loses that much life') triggered on that gain and is on the stack. Its target — opponent 2, at 9 — was locked in when the trigger was put on the stack.
    One Vito trigger on the stack targeting opponent 2 for 3. Targets are per-instance choices made at put-on-stack time, not at resolution.
    CR 115.1d — “A triggered ability is targeted if it identifies something it will affect by using the phrase “target [something],” where the “something” is a phrase that describes an object and/or player. The target(s) are chosen as the ability is put on the stack; see rule 603.3d.”
  2. Vito's trigger resolves (no responses; opponents hold no instant-speed interaction). Opponent 2 loses 3 life: 9 -> 6. That direct life loss matches Bloodthirsty Conqueror's trigger condition ('Whenever an opponent loses life, you gain that much life') — Vito's effect is life loss, not damage, and Conqueror's '(Damage causes loss of life.)' parenthetical is reminder text clarifying that damage ALSO counts, not a restriction to damage. Conqueror triggers during resolution — but nothing happens yet.
    Opponent 2 at 6. Conqueror has triggered and is waiting to be put on the stack.
    CR 117.2a — “Triggered abilities can trigger at any time, including while a spell is being cast, an ability is being activated, or a spell or ability is resolving. (See rule 603, “Handling Triggered Abilities.”) However, nothing actually happens at the time an ability triggers.”
    CR 207.2a — “Reminder text is italicized text within parentheses that summarizes a rule that applies to that card.”
  3. The next time a player would receive priority, state-based actions are checked (none apply — opponent 2 is at 6, Conqueror and Vito are undamaged), then Conqueror's trigger is put on the stack. It has no targets. It resolves: you gain 3 (24 -> 27). That life gain triggers Vito again.
    You gain 3 per iteration; each Conqueror resolution re-triggers Vito. The engine is self-sustaining, exactly as in stack 001 — the trigger condition is identical to Exquisite Blood's.
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority.”
    CR 117.5 — “Each time a player would get priority, the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event (see rule 704, “State-Based Actions”), then repeats this process until no state-based actions are performed. Then triggered abilities are put on the stack”
  4. The new Vito trigger is put on the stack, and a NEW target is chosen for this new instance. You may keep targeting opponent 2 or retarget opponent 1 — each iteration is an independent targeting decision, since target choice is part of putting each triggered ability on the stack.
    Retargeting between iterations is legal and free. You keep opponent 2 as the target while they live.
    CR 603.3d — “The remainder of the process for putting a triggered ability on the stack is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2c–d.”
    CR 115.1d — “The target(s) are chosen as the ability is put on the stack; see rule 603.3d.”
  5. Loop legality: each iteration (Vito resolves -> opponent loses 3 -> Conqueror triggers -> resolves -> you gain 3 -> Vito triggers -> new target) is fully determined once you name the target, so the whole sequence may be proposed as a shortcut for a stated number of iterations rather than physically performed. The amount is fixed at the seed's 3 for every iteration.
    The loop is deterministic and repeatable indefinitely while both permanents remain and a legal target exists. It transfers exactly 3 life per iteration from the targeted opponent to you — same structure as stack 001, different constant.
    CR 732.1b — “Occasionally the game gets into a state in which a set of actions could be repeated indefinitely (thus creating a “loop”). In that case, the shortcut rules can be used to determine how many times those actions are repeated without having to actually perform them, and how the loop is broken.”
    CR 732.2a — “At any point in the game, the player with priority may suggest a shortcut by describing a sequence of game choices, for all players, that may be legally taken based on the current game state and the predictable results of the sequence of choices.”
  6. THE difference from stack 001: Conqueror is a 5/5 creature, not an enchantment. Every iteration passes priority while each trigger is on the stack, and any player with priority may cast an instant — so each iteration is a real window for instant-speed creature removal on Conqueror (or Vito, a vulnerability 001 also had). If either dies mid-loop, a trigger already on the stack still resolves (abilities exist independently of their sources), but the engine stops after it. Formally, an opponent responds to a proposed shortcut by shortening it: naming the iteration where they will act differently. Here neither opponent holds instant-speed interaction, so the shortcut stands in full.
    Conqueror + Vito is mechanically identical to Exquisite Blood + Vito but strictly more fragile: it adds every creature-removal spell to the list of loop-breakers. In THIS scenario, no window is exploitable.
    CR 117.1a — “A player may cast an instant spell any time they have priority.”
    CR 113.7a — “Once activated or triggered, an ability exists on the stack independently of its source. Destruction or removal of the source after that time won’t affect the ability.”
    CR 732.2b — “may either accept the proposed sequence, or shorten it by naming a place where they will make a game choice that’s different than what’s been proposed.”
  7. Flying and deathtouch do NOT matter to the loop itself. Flying only restricts blocking in combat; deathtouch's destruction effect applies only to creatures dealt damage by the deathtouch source. The loop involves no combat and Conqueror deals no damage — the life loss comes from Vito's effect and the gain from Conqueror's trigger. Both keywords are combat-relevant extras, nothing more.
    The loop's operation is unchanged by Conqueror's keywords; they neither speed it up nor protect it.
    CR 702.9b — “A creature with flying can’t be blocked except by creatures with flying and/or reach.”
    CR 702.2b — “A creature with toughness greater than 0 that’s been dealt damage by a source with deathtouch since the last time state-based actions were checked is destroyed as a state-based action.”
  8. Killing opponent 2: after 3 total Vito resolutions targeting them (3 x 3 = 9), opponent 2 hits exactly 0 life — no overkill, the 3-point granularity divides 9 evenly. Before any player next receives priority, state-based actions are performed: opponent 2 loses the game and leaves it.
    Opponent 2 dies on iteration 3. Their owned objects leave the game with them (none here).
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 104.5 — “If a player loses the game, that player leaves the game.”
    CR 800.4a — “When a player leaves the game, all objects (see rule 109) owned by that player leave the game”
  9. Does Conqueror trigger off the loss that killed opponent 2? Yes. The life-loss event occurred while opponent 2 was still in the game, so Conqueror triggered at that moment (though nothing happened yet); the ability is then put on the stack after state-based actions remove opponent 2, and it resolves normally — it targets nothing and belongs to YOUR creature, which is still on the battlefield. You gain 3 (24 + 3x3 = 33 after Conqueror resolution 3) and Vito triggers again. The new Vito trigger is put on the stack and targeted fresh — at opponent 1 (30), the only remaining opponent. A Vito trigger would be removed from the stack only if NO legal target existed.
    The chain crosses opponent 2's death exactly as it crossed deaths in stack 001: the killing loss still feeds a gain iteration, and the next trigger retargets the survivor.
    CR 117.2a — “However, nothing actually happens at the time an ability triggers. Each time a player would receive priority, each ability that has triggered but hasn’t yet been put on the stack is put on the stack.”
    CR 117.5 — “the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event (see rule 704, “State-Based Actions”), then repeats this process until no state-based actions are performed. Then triggered abilities are put on the stack”
    CR 603.3d — “If a choice is required when the triggered ability goes on the stack but no legal choices can be made for it, or if a rule or a continuous effect otherwise makes the ability illegal, the ability is simply removed from the stack.”
  10. Continue the shortcut against opponent 1: iterations 4 through 13 — ten Vito resolutions at 3 each (30 -> 0, exactly zero again). The 13th and final Vito resolution puts opponent 1 at 0. State-based actions: opponent 1 loses and leaves; their permanents leave with them. You are the only player remaining, so you win the game immediately — the 13th Conqueror trigger (off opponent 1's killing loss) never resolves because the game has already ended.

    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
