Which card do you always have? Not the one you drew — the one in the command zone. CR 903.6 puts Edgar Markov there face up before anybody has a hand, and that guarantee is the only one this format hands you.

Eminence is why it matters here. It reads “Whenever you cast another Vampire spell, if Edgar is in the command zone or on the battlefield, create a 1/1 black Vampire creature token.” An ability that states which zones it functions in functions only from those zones — CR 113.6b — and this one names the command zone out loud. Stack 002 confirms it firing from there.

So the engine is live on turn one and it costs nothing. Two riders travel with it. The tokens are created, not cast, so they never trigger eminence a second time. And casting Edgar is not casting another Vampire spell — he is the one Vampire in your deck that mints nothing.

Now price the man himself. Six, then eight, then ten, then twelve: CR 903.8 adds {2} for every previous command-zone cast, and that cost compounds for the rest of the game. The ladder beside this column is the whole argument for leaving him home.

Colour identity is why the 99 looks the way it does. CR 903.4 reads mana symbols in the cost and in the rules text, so {3}{R}{W}{B} buys white's exile and board-wide protection, black's tutors and drains, and red's damage payoffs. It also shuts the door on blue counterspells and green ramp: this deck answers a permanent after it resolves, or it does not answer it.

Is the 21-damage clock real? CR 903.10a ends a player dealt 21 combat damage by one commander, and Edgar is a 4/4 with first strike and haste who puts a +1/+1 counter on every Vampire he attacks alongside, himself included. Treat it as a coincidence you can cash. Commander damage points at one seat; this deck's kill points at three.

★ Coach's read. The table re-prices you the moment the card is read out, so stop buying anonymity you cannot afford. Spend the budget on order instead: land the two-mana payoffs while they still look like durdle, and let the six-mana commander arrive last, to a board he makes lethal rather than one he has to build.

★ And be honest about protection. Your engine does not route through Edgar — eminence keeps minting whatever happens to him — so insurance on him is insurance on the wrong thing. What his presence does buy is a discount: Flawless Maneuver costs {2}{W} while he waits in the zone and {0} the moment he is on the battlefield.

THE PLAN

NOBODY HAS TO BLOCK

Every Vampire you cast is two bodies, and every body that enters costs all three opponents life at once. Build the layer that charges for it and the combat step becomes optional.

Your commander does his best work without ever showing up. Eminence functions from the command zone, so every other Vampire spell you cast mints a free 1/1 from turn one — stack 002 put that on the record, and stack 009 leaned on it again. Here is the trap, and it is the one that actually costs games: waiting for Edgar. His median cast is turn six and the simulation only lands him by then in 52.3% of games, so deploy into an empty board and let the table price you as a creature deck.

The kill is what happens when those free bodies land. Purphoros, God of the Forge, Impact Tremors, Warleader's Call and Corpse Knight each turn a creature entering into life gone from every seat at once — no attack, no blockers, no evasion. Stack 009 counts the whole sequence: one two-mana Vampire, one Anointed Procession, three of those payoffs on the table, nine triggers, twelve damage to each opponent. You have a converter in hand in 52.5% of games at a mean turn of 3.28, and a converter plus a multiplier in 40.2%. Build every turn toward that pairing.

Combat is your other kill and it shows up sooner than anything else you own — a lord or anthem in 83.2% of games, mean turn 2.60. Stack 003 prices what that buys: seven attacking Vampires under Sanctum Seeker with Bloodletter of Aclazotz out is fourteen out of every opponent at declare-attackers, before a single blocker exists. Attack early and attack wide; the drain happens whether or not the creatures get through.

You also own a deterministic table-kill, twice over, and you should hold it lightly. Bloodthirsty Conqueror with Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose or with Sanguine Bond is a free, unbounded loop, both verified (stacks 004 and 006) — and you draw a complete pair in 4.6% of games. That is a ticket you happen to be holding, not a plan. Every one of those three cards pays rent as a body or a drain the other nineteen games in twenty, so present the loop when a window opens and never slow the board down to protect it.

ROLL CALL

SIXTY-THREE SPELLS, ONE EVENT

Sorted by what each card does when a creature enters the battlefield, because that is the moment this deck is built around. The four converters lead; almost everything else exists to feed them.

The conversion layer: every entry is damage

Purphoros, God of the Forge

Aura + ProtectionTokens + Anthem

Two damage to each opponent for every other creature that enters — the largest converter in the deck. He is usually not a creature at your devotion, which costs you a body and costs the trigger nothing (stack 009).

Impact Tremors

One damage to each opponent per creature entering, for two mana. Cheap enough to land before the doublers do, which is exactly when you want the conversion layer online.

Warleader's Call

Tokens + Anthem

Impact Tremors with an anthem attached: the +1/+1 makes your tokens 2/2 while the trigger drains all three seats. It is the payoff that improves both of your kills at once.

Corpse Knight

A fourth converter for two mana, and the only one that causes life loss rather than damage. Not a Vampire, so casting it makes no eminence token — cast it anyway, and early.

The multipliers: one token becomes four

Anointed Procession

Doubles every token event you generate, which means it doubles eminence, Mavren Fein, Legion's Landing and Charismatic Conqueror all at once. Four mana that turns a two-drop into a lethal turn under the damage payoffs (stack 009).

Mondrak, Glory Dominus

Aura + Protection

A second doubler on a 4/4, and it stacks with the first because each replacement effect gets exactly one bite at the event (stack 002). The indestructible counter is genuine sweeper insurance — pay it on a turn you have bodies to spare.

Elspeth, Storm Slayer

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

A third token doubler that also makes a token every turn and destroys a creature with mana value 3 or greater. Her 0 is usually the better button: a counter on every creature plus flying is an attack the table did not price.

Ocelot Pride

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

A one-drop that makes a Cat at every end step you gained life, and with the city's blessing copies every token that entered this turn. Not a Vampire, so no eminence trigger — the copies more than cover it.

Caretaker's Talent

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Draws a card the first time tokens enter each turn, which here is every turn. Level it with spare mana: level 3 makes every creature token +2/+2 permanently.

Bodies that print bodies, for free

Charismatic Conqueror

Taxes every untapped creature or artifact your opponents play: they tap it, or you get a lifelinking Vampire token. Two mana that makes tokens on other people's turns, which the converters are delighted to see.

A one-mana Vampire token that flips into a land the moment you attack with three creatures. Play it on turn one; the back face is a mana source and a token factory nobody can two-for-one you on.

Mavren Fein, Dusk Apostle

Attack with any nontoken Vampire and he makes a lifelinking token — doubled by your doublers, converted by your converters. He rewards the attack you were making regardless.

A 2/2 flier every turn with no mana attached, and once you control five Vampires he flips into a lord that keeps making them. One of the few token engines that never asks for a card from your hand.

Elenda, the Dusk Rose

Counters + TokensLifegain + Death Trigger

Grows on every creature death and cashes out into that many lifelinking Vampire tokens when she dies. Let her die to the sweeper — that is the best version of this card, not a loss.

Strefan, Maurer Progenitor

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Blood tokens for every player who lost life this turn — which your converters guarantee — and sacrificing two puts a Vampire from hand onto the battlefield attacking. Cheating a five-drop into combat is the mode that ends games.

Preacher of the Schism

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Deathtouch on a 2/4 that either makes a token or draws a card every time it attacks, depending on who is at the top. Attack the life leader; the token is worth more to you than the card.

Kalitas, Traitor of Ghet

Exiles the nontoken creatures your opponents lose and hands you a 2/2 Zombie for each, so a wipe you cast leaves you a board. His lifelink also switches on Vito and Sanguine Bond.

Searches up any Vampire and puts it on top, then pumps a creature every time another Vampire enters. It is a body, an eminence trigger and the most reliable way to find Bloodthirsty Conqueror.

The attack-and-tap drains

Sanctum Seeker

Attack Trigger + TokensEquipment + Attack Trigger

Every attacking Vampire drains each opponent for 1 and gains you 1, so seven attackers is seven triggers (stack 003). Blockers do not stop a single point of it.

Bloodletter of Aclazotz

Flying + Damage Trigger

Doubles each opponent's life loss on your turn — Sanctum Seeker's drain, Corpse Knight, Throne of the God-Pharaoh (stacks 003 and 007). Your own gain is never doubled; the clause speaks only to an opponent's loss.

Throne of the God-Pharaoh

Two mana: each opponent loses life equal to your tapped creatures at your end step, so a wide attack drains twice over. Stack 007 prices it honestly — that is reach, not a kill.

Twilight Prophet

Flying + Damage Trigger

Once you control ten permanents — which happens quickly here — every upkeep reveals a card and drains each opponent for its mana value while you gain it. A 2/4 flier that wins the long games unassisted.

The deterministic kill, and the rent it pays

Bloodthirsty Conqueror

Flying + Damage Trigger

A 5/5 flying deathtouch body that gains you life whenever an opponent loses any, which with Vito or Sanguine Bond is a free deterministic table-kill (stacks 004 and 006). Even alone it turns every drain into a lifegain engine.

Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose

Lifegain + Death Trigger

Every point of life you gain becomes a point an opponent loses, and with Bloodthirsty Conqueror that is a verified infinite (stack 004). Do not forget his lifelink activation — on a wide board it is a real button.

Sanguine Bond

Lifegain + Death Trigger

Vito's trigger on an enchantment, which is one fewer creature for a removal spell to answer (stack 006). Five mana is the price of being harder to break.

Knight of the Ebon Legion

Counters + Tokens

A one-mana threat with a pump that makes it a 4/5 deathtouch, and it grows itself whenever a player lost 4 or more life this turn, which your converters do routinely. Excellent on turn one, still excellent on turn ten.

Vampire of the Dire Moon

Lifegain + Death Trigger

One mana, deathtouch, lifelink: the cheapest eminence trigger in the deck and a blocker nobody attacks into. The lifelink also feeds Vito.

Welcoming Vampire

Cemetery Gatekeeper

Exiles a card from a graveyard on entry, then pings whoever plays a card sharing that type. Choose the type deliberately: exiling a land taxes the whole table every turn.

Interaction, and insurance sized for a board

Olivia's Wrath

Toxic Deluge

Boros Charm

Swiftfoot Boots

THE ONLY TEN

BUY THE RATE, NOT THE CEILING

Ten cards, ranked, and three of them do the single thing this deck is short of. Five copies of an effect is 31% of opening sevens, seven copies is 41%, ten is 54% — that is what a slot actually purchases.

Price everything here against one rate. A card that turns a creature entering the battlefield into life gone from every seat is in your hand in 52.5% of games, 42.3% of them by turn six. Pair one with a multiplier and you are at 40.2%, mean turn 4.53. The intuition underneath those numbers: the bodies are never the shortage — a multiplier or extra token-maker turns up 78.2% of the time at a mean turn of 2.68 — and roughly three games in five reach turn six with the conversion layer still in the library.

That is why the cheapest thing this deck can buy is another rung on a ladder it already owns. The corpus arithmetic is blunt about it: five copies of an effect is 31% of opening sevens, seven copies is 41%, ten is 54% (strategy:deckbuilding.redundancy-vs-tutors). One more converter moves the number that decides the game further than any single more powerful card does, because it moves the number you see every game rather than the ceiling you see occasionally.

There is a second figure that prices a search effect exactly. The surviving infinite assembles in 4.6% of games on its own and 25.0% once unrestricted tutors count as wildcards. Twenty points is what a tutor is worth here, and it is the same twenty points that argue against buying a third copy of a two-card kill you see one game in twenty. Tutor the irreplaceable, stack the classes you want every game.

One thin place is worth a slot rather than a shrug: an answer to a noncreature permanent is in hand in 30.8% of games, off two cards, at a mean turn of 3.59. Against three opponents that is the axis that binds, and it is cheaper to widen than any ceiling on the board.

And one number to stop worrying about while you shop. The bracket floor is 4, set by four Game Changers — Ancient Tomb, Demonic Tutor, Teferi's Protection, Vampiric Tutor — rather than by combo content. Nothing on this list moves the tier, so the tier should not shape the list. Buy rate: another converter, another source of entries, or the mana to deploy two of them in one turn.

Edgar mints a Vampire on every Vampire cast; the deck's job is to make that entry hurt three seats at once. The measurement says the multiplier half is finished and the payoff half is not, so three of the ten are entry-trigger payoffs and none is a doubler. The bench is twenty-nine entries and it is a box of small Vampires: two of them duplicate cards already in the 99, one is outside the colour identity, and most of the rest convert Blood tokens or eat a creature. Three bench cards clear the bar on their own terms — Vampire Nocturnus, Akroma's Memorial and Markov Baron — and the tail loses its slots to the pool because nothing in the box converts a creature entry into damage, answers a two-mana permanent, or produces a black source. The floor is 4 before and after every one of the ten.

Case A-002 Edgar's eminence through two doublers: how many bodies per cast?
Rules version 2026-06-19 · status: cleared in 1 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
The question put to the record
Resolve the eminence trigger and then the Gifted Aetherborn spell. How do the two token-doubling replacement effects stack on the single eminence token event (order, and the resulting token count)? Does the eminence trigger fire from the command zone? Do the tokens — and the resolved Gifted Aetherborn — each enter with Vampire Socialite's additional +1/+1 counter given an opponent lost life this turn? Final board: exactly how many new Vampire bodies, at what sizes, from this one two-mana cast?
  1. All players pass priority; the stack resolves top-down. Edgar Markov's eminence trigger (top) resolves before the Gifted Aetherborn spell (bottom).
    The eminence triggered ability begins resolving first; Gifted Aetherborn waits on the stack.
    CR 405.5 — “When all players pass in succession, the top (last-added) spell or ability on the stack resolves.”
  2. The eminence trigger resolves. Its intervening 'if' clause — 'if Edgar is in the command zone or on the battlefield' — is checked again on resolution; Edgar is still in the command zone, and the ability functions there because it states the zones it functions in.
    The trigger resolves from the command zone and instructs you to create a 1/1 black Vampire creature token: a single token-creation event for one token.
    CR 113.6b — “An ability that states which zones it functions in functions only from those zones.”
    CR 603.4 — “If the ability triggers, it checks the stated condition again as it resolves. If the condition isn’t true at that time, the ability is removed from the stack and does nothing.”
  3. Two replacement effects — Anointed Procession ('If an effect would create one or more tokens under your control, it creates twice that many of those tokens instead') and Mondrak, Glory Dominus ('If one or more tokens would be created under your control, twice that many of those tokens are created instead') — both want to modify this one token-creation event. You, the affected player, choose which applies first.
    You pick an order (it is symmetrical here); the chosen doubler modifies the event, then the other applies to the modified event.
    CR 616.1 — “If two or more replacement and/or prevention effects are attempting to modify the way an event affects an object or player, the affected object’s controller (or its owner if it has no controller) or the affected player chooses one to apply”
  4. Each doubler gets exactly one application to the event, so the math is 1 token → 2 (first doubler) → 4 (second doubler), identical in either order.
    The modified event creates four 1/1 black Vampire creature tokens; neither doubler applies a second time.
    CR 614.5 — “A replacement effect doesn’t invoke itself repeatedly; it gets only one opportunity to affect an event or any modified events that may replace that event.”
  5. The four tokens are put onto the battlefield. They are created, not cast, so no game event matches eminence's 'you cast another Vampire spell' trigger condition — eminence does not fire again for the tokens.
    Four Vampire tokens enter the battlefield under your control; no new eminence triggers.
    CR 701.7a — “To create one or more tokens with certain characteristics, put the specified number of tokens with the specified characteristics onto the battlefield.”
    CR 603.2 — “Whenever a game event or game state matches a triggered ability’s trigger event, that ability automatically triggers.”
  6. Vampire Socialite's static ability — 'As long as an opponent lost life this turn, each other Vampire you control enters with an additional +1/+1 counter on it' — is an 'enters with' replacement effect. An opponent lost 3 life earlier this turn, and each token is another Vampire you control, so each token enters with a +1/+1 counter.
    Each 1/1 token enters with one +1/+1 counter and is a 2/2.
    CR 614.1d — “Continuous effects that read “[This permanent] enters . . .” or “[Objects] enter [the battlefield] . . .” are replacement effects.”
    CR 122.1a — “A +X/+Y counter on a creature or on a creature card in a zone other than the battlefield, where X and Y are numbers, adds X to that object’s power and Y to that object’s toughness.”
  7. Before any player gets priority, state-based actions are checked: no creature has 0 or less toughness or lethal damage, so nothing happens and nothing dies. Cordial Vampire ('Whenever this creature or another creature dies, put a +1/+1 counter on each Vampire you control') is scenery here — no death event occurred, so it never triggers. All players pass again.
    No state-based actions, no Cordial Vampire triggers; the Gifted Aetherborn spell (now top of stack) resolves next.
    CR 704.3 — “Whenever a player would get priority (see rule 117, “Timing and Priority”), the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event.”
    CR 603.2g — “An ability triggers only if its trigger event actually occurs.”
  8. Gifted Aetherborn (a 2/3 Aetherborn Vampire with deathtouch and lifelink, no targets) resolves and enters the battlefield under your control. It is another Vampire you control entering while an opponent lost life this turn, so Vampire Socialite's replacement effect applies to it as well. It is not a token, so Anointed Procession and Mondrak are irrelevant to it.
    Gifted Aetherborn enters with an additional +1/+1 counter: a 3/4 deathtouch lifelink Vampire.
    CR 608.3a — “If the object that’s resolving has no targets, it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under the control of the spell’s controller.”
    CR 614.1d — “Continuous effects that read “[This permanent] enters . . .” or “[Objects] enter [the battlefield] . . .” are replacement effects.”
    CR 122.1a — “A +X/+Y counter on a creature or on a creature card in a zone other than the battlefield, where X and Y are numbers, adds X to that object’s power and Y to that object’s toughness.”
  9. Final state-based-action check before priority: all creatures have positive toughness and no damage marked; the stack is empty.
    Nothing dies; no further triggers; the game proceeds with five new Vampire bodies on your battlefield.
    CR 704.3 — “Whenever a player would get priority (see rule 117, “Timing and Priority”), the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event.”

Case A-003 Bloodletter + Sanctum Seeker: what does an attack actually drain?
Rules version 2026-06-19 · status: cleared in 2 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
The question put to the record
One rules domain: the life-loss arithmetic of this attack declaration, before any blocks or combat damage. Seven Vampires attacked: how many Sanctum Seeker triggers fire, how much life does EACH opponent lose per trigger through Bloodletter's replacement effect (does doubling apply per trigger event), how much life do you gain (is your gain doubled — Bloodletter only doubles opponents' loss), does Edgar's counter trigger change the drain, and what are all three life totals after every trigger resolves? Does the trigger drain alone kill opponent 2 at 14, and do the deaths' timing (state-based actions between resolutions) stop remaining triggers?
  1. Declare attackers: all seven Vampires (Bloodletter of Aclazotz, Sanctum Seeker, four 1/1 Vampire tokens, Edgar Markov) are declared as attackers. Sanctum Seeker's ability ('Whenever a Vampire you control attacks, each opponent loses 1 life and you gain 1 life') triggers once for each attacking Vampire — seven separate trigger events, so SEVEN Sanctum Seeker triggers. Edgar Markov's ability ('Whenever Edgar attacks, put a +1/+1 counter on each Vampire you control') triggers once.
    Eight triggered abilities are waiting to go on the stack: 7 Sanctum Seeker drains + 1 Edgar counter trigger.
    CR 508.3a — “An ability that reads “Whenever [a creature] attacks, . . .” triggers if that creature is declared as an attacker.”
    CR 603.2c — “An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs. However, it can trigger repeatedly if one event contains multiple occurrences.”
  2. All eight triggers are put on the stack. You control all of them, so you choose their relative order. Chosen order here: Edgar's counter trigger is placed last (on top), so it resolves first; the seven Sanctum Seeker triggers resolve after it, one at a time.
    Stack (top to bottom): Edgar counter trigger, then Sanctum Seeker triggers #1–#7.
    CR 603.3b — “each player, in APNAP order, puts each triggered ability they control with a trigger condition that isn’t another ability triggering on the stack in any order they choose”
  3. Edgar Markov's trigger resolves: put a +1/+1 counter on each Vampire you control. All seven attackers get a counter (tokens become 2/2, Sanctum Seeker 4/5, Bloodletter 3/5, Edgar 5/5).
    Power and toughness change ONLY. Sanctum Seeker's drain is a fixed 'each opponent loses 1 life' — it does not reference power — so Edgar's counters do NOT change the trigger drain at all (they only matter for later combat damage). Active player would then receive priority, so the game checks state-based actions; no life totals changed, nothing happens.
    CR 117.3b — “The active player receives priority after a spell or ability (other than a mana ability) resolves.”
  4. Sanctum Seeker trigger #1 resolves: each opponent would lose 1 life and you would gain 1 life. Bloodletter of Aclazotz ('If an opponent would lose life during your turn, they lose twice that much life instead') is a replacement effect — it is your turn, so each opponent's 1 life loss is replaced with 2. It applies once to each opponent's loss event and does not re-apply to its own result. Your life GAIN is untouched: Bloodletter's text only replaces an OPPONENT'S life LOSS, and your gain is neither an opponent's event nor a loss.
    Opponent 1: 21 → 19. Opponent 2: 14 → 12. You: 26 → 27 (gain 1, not doubled). Life totals adjust accordingly.
    CR 614.1a — “Effects that use the word “instead” are replacement effects.”
    CR 614.5 — “A replacement effect doesn’t invoke itself repeatedly; it gets only one opportunity to affect an event or any modified events that may replace that event.”
    CR 119.3 — “If an effect causes a player to gain life or lose life, that player’s life total is adjusted accordingly.”
  5. Between each trigger resolution the active player would receive priority, so state-based actions are checked each time. Sanctum Seeker triggers #2 through #6 resolve identically: each opponent loses 2 (1 doubled by Bloodletter — the replacement applies anew to each separate life-loss event, since each trigger's resolution is its own event), you gain 1 each time.
    Running totals after trigger #6: Opponent 1: 21 → 9 (six drains × 2). Opponent 2: 14 → 2. You: 26 → 32. At every SBA check through trigger #6, opponent 2 is at 12/10/8/6/4/2 — above 0 — so no one has lost the game yet and all triggers keep resolving.
    CR 704.3 — “Whenever a player would get priority (see rule 117, “Timing and Priority”), the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event.”
    CR 117.2d — “State-based actions happen automatically when certain conditions are met. See rule 704. They’re dealt with before a player would receive priority.”
  6. Sanctum Seeker trigger #7 (the last drain) resolves: each opponent loses 1, doubled to 2 by Bloodletter; you gain 1.
    Opponent 1: 9 → 7. Opponent 2: 2 → 0. You: 32 → 33. Total drain across all seven triggers: 14 to each opponent, 7 gained by you.
    CR 614.1a — “replacement effects use the word “instead” to indicate what events will be replaced with other events”
    CR 119.3 — “If an effect causes a player to gain life or lose life, that player’s life total is adjusted accordingly.”

Nothing else on this board interacts with this loss — the damage source is Purphoros, so Gifted Aetherborn's lifelink (which is its own ability) is not involved, and no other permanent here triggers on damage or life loss.
    Opponent 1 at 14; opponent 2 at 0. Total from the {B}{B} cast: 5 triggers x 2 = 10 damage to EACH opponent.
    CR 120.3a — “Damage dealt to a player by a source without infect causes that player to lose that much life.”
    CR 119.3 — “If an effect causes a player to gain life or lose life, that player’s life total is adjusted accordingly.”
  9. State-based actions are checked before any player would receive priority: opponent 2 has 0 life and loses the game — on the resolution of the FIFTH trigger (the Gifted Aetherborn entry), the last one. Opponent 2 leaves the game owning no objects (empty board), so nothing else changes. No Purphoros triggers remain after this one; had any remained (i.e., had opponent 2 died on an earlier trigger), your triggers are not among the leaving player's objects — they would remain on the stack, still resolve, and still deal 2 to opponent 1, the remaining opponent.
    Opponent 2 is out of the game; opponent 1 remains at 14; the stack is empty and the game continues.
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 104.3b — “If a player’s life total is 0 or less, that player loses the game the next time a player would receive priority. (This is a state-based action. See rule 704.)”
    CR 800.4a — “When a player leaves the game, all objects (see rule 109) owned by that player leave the game and any effects which give that player control of any objects or players end.”

Case A-009 The primary plan, composing: three token-to-damage payoffs on one two-mana Vampire
Rules version 2026-06-19 · status: cleared in 3 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
The question put to the record
The eminence trigger resolves first and creates a Vampire token, doubled by Anointed Procession; then the Cordial Vampire spell resolves. Counting every creature-entry event: how many separate triggered abilities do Purphoros, Impact Tremors and Warleader's Call put on the stack in total, how much damage does each opponent take, and does this kill a three-opponent pod at 12 life each?
  1. Fix the board and compute devotion to red before anything resolves.
    You control four noncreature permanents (Purphoros, God of the Forge; Impact Tremors; Warleader's Call; Anointed Procession), five lands, and NO creature bodies. Devotion to red counts red mana symbols in the mana costs of permanents you control: Purphoros {3}{R} = 1, Impact Tremors {1}{R} = 1, Warleader's Call {1}{R}{W} = 1, Anointed Procession {3}{W} = 0. Devotion to red = 3, which is less than five, so Purphoros's own static ability ('As long as your devotion to red is less than five, Purphoros isn't a creature') applies in layer 4 and Purphoros is a Legendary Enchantment God, not a creature.
    CR 700.5 — “devotion to [color] is equal to the number of mana symbols of that color among the mana costs of permanents that player controls”
    CR 613.1d — “Layer 4: Type-changing effects are applied.”
  2. State exactly what Purphoros not being a creature does and does not change.
    It does NOT switch off 'Whenever another creature you control enters, Purphoros deals 2 damage to each opponent.' A permanent's abilities function because the permanent is on the battlefield, not because it is a creature, so the triggered ability of a non-creature Purphoros functions normally and triggers on every creature entry below. What it DOES change: Purphoros is not a body (it cannot attack or block, and it is not counted among creature bodies on this board), and it is not itself a creature that could ever satisfy Impact Tremors or Warleader's Call by entering. The word 'another' in Purphoros's trigger condition excludes only Purphoros itself; the two tokens and Cordial Vampire are all other objects, so each satisfies it regardless of Purphoros's own type. Devotion also cannot rise during this sequence: Cordial Vampire's mana cost is {B}{B} (no red symbols) and the Vampire tokens have no mana cost at all, since a token has only the characteristics the creating ability defines. Purphoros stays a non-creature throughout, and the trigger count would be identical either way. The same rule pair is what lets Edgar Markov operate this board at all: 113.6's battlefield-only default is a default, and an ability that states which zones it functions in functions only from those zones -- eminence names the command zone, so it functions from there while Edgar sits in it.
    CR 113.6 — “Abilities of all other objects usually function only while that object is on the battlefield.”
    CR 111.3 — “A token doesn’t have any characteristics not defined by the spell or ability that created it.”
    CR 113.6b — “An ability that states which zones it functions in functions only from those zones.”
  3. Before the eminence trigger resolves, recheck its intervening 'if' clause.
    Eminence reads 'Whenever you cast another Vampire spell, if Edgar is in the command zone or on the battlefield, create a 1/1 black Vampire creature token.' The 'if' immediately follows the trigger condition, so it is an intervening 'if': the condition was checked when you cast Cordial Vampire (a Vampire spell other than Edgar) and is checked AGAIN as the ability resolves. If it were false at that time the ability would be removed from the stack and do nothing -- and with no token created there would be no token entry event and none of the six triggers that come from it. On this board it is true in fact: Edgar Markov is in the command zone and nothing in this sequence moves him, so the condition holds on resolution and the trigger goes on to create its token.
    CR 603.4 — “If the ability triggers, it checks the stated condition again as it resolves. If the condition isn’t true at that time, the ability is removed from the stack and does nothing.”
    CR 113.6b — “An ability that states which zones it functions in functions only from those zones.”
  4. Read the stack top-down, then resolve the eminence trigger. Anointed Procession replaces the token creation.
    Cordial Vampire (the spell) is at pos 0 (bottom); Edgar Markov's eminence trigger sits above it, so the eminence trigger resolves first while the spell waits beneath it. No opponent holds instant-speed interaction, so every priority round passes in succession. On resolution the eminence ability would create one 1/1 black Vampire creature token. Anointed Procession is a replacement effect that applies to an effect that would create one or more tokens under your control and doubles it, so the event that actually happens is the creation of TWO 1/1 black Vampire creature tokens. Creating them puts both onto the battlefield as a single action: one event, two permanents. Only one doubler is on the board, so no further replacement applies.
    CR 608.1 — “Each time all players pass in succession, the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves.”
    CR 117.4 — “the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves”
    CR 614.1 — “Such effects watch for a particular event that would happen and completely or partially replace that event with a different event.”
    CR 614.16 — “These replacement effects apply if the effect of a resolving spell or ability creates a token”
    CR 701.7a — “To create one or more tokens with certain characteristics, put the specified number of tokens with the specified characteristics onto the battlefield.”
  5. Count the triggers from the two-token entry -- the arithmetic the scenario asks for.
    The two tokens enter the battlefield as ONE event containing TWO occurrences. When that event puts permanents onto the battlefield, every permanent on the battlefield is checked for enters-the-battlefield triggers matching the event; and an ability triggers once per occurrence, not once per event. So each of the three payoffs triggers TWICE, once for each token -- this is precisely why two simultaneously created tokens produce two instances of each trigger rather than one shared instance. Impact Tremors ('Whenever a creature you control enters') x2, Warleader's Call ('Whenever a creature you control enters') x2, Purphoros ('Whenever another creature you control enters') x2. Total from this wave: 6 separate triggered abilities.
    CR 603.6a — “Each time an event puts one or more permanents onto the battlefield, all permanents on the battlefield (including the newcomers) are checked for any enters-the-battlefield triggers that match the event.”
    CR 603.2c — “An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs. However, it can trigger repeatedly if one event contains multiple occurrences.”
  6. Put the 6 triggers on the stack.
    They triggered during the eminence trigger's resolution, so they are put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority -- after that resolution finishes. State-based actions are performed first (none apply: no player is at 0 or less life, no creature has lethal damage), then the triggers go on the stack. All six trigger on a creature entering -- not on another ability triggering -- so they are placed in the FIRST part of the two-part process, and all six are controlled by you, so you choose their relative order among themselves; all sit ABOVE the Cordial Vampire spell, which still has not resolved. Order does not change the total: each deals a fixed amount of damage to each opponent and none of them targets.
    CR 603.3 — “The ability becomes the topmost object on the stack.”
    CR 117.5 — “Each time a player would get priority, the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event”
    CR 603.3b — “First, each player, in APNAP order, puts each triggered ability they control with a trigger condition that isn’t another ability triggering on the stack in any order they choose.”
  7. Resolve the 6 triggers one at a time. Per-opponent arithmetic for the token wave.
    Purphoros deals 2 damage to each opponent, twice = 4 per opponent. Impact Tremors deals 1 damage to each opponent, twice = 2 per opponent. Warleader's Call deals 1 damage to each opponent, twice = 2 per opponent. Total 8 damage PER OPPONENT from the two tokens. Damage dealt to a player causes that player to lose that much life, so each opponent goes 12 -> 4. Across the three-seat pod that is 24 life removed in total; the per-seat figure is 8 and the two figures are not interchangeable.
    CR 608.1 — “Each time all players pass in succession, the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves.”
    CR 119.2 — “Damage dealt to a player normally causes that player to lose that much life.”
    CR 119.3 — “If an effect causes a player to gain life or lose life, that player’s life total is adjusted accordingly.”
  8. Check state-based actions between each of those six resolutions.
    A player at 0 or less life loses the game as a state-based action, checked whenever a player would get priority -- i.e. after each of the six resolutions, and again before each player would act. The lowest any opponent reaches during this wave, in any ordering, is 4 (12 -> 4). No opponent is at 0 or less at any point in the token wave, so no player-loss state-based action is performed and all three opponents are still in the game when the wave finishes.
    CR 704.3 — “the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event.”
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
  9. The stack is now down to the Cordial Vampire spell. All players pass; it resolves.
    Cordial Vampire is a creature spell, i.e. a permanent spell, so its resolution puts it onto the battlefield under your control. That is a THIRD creature-entry event -- one permanent this time, so one occurrence.
    CR 110.4b — “a spell that will enter the battlefield as a permanent as part of its resolution”
    CR 608.3 — “If the object that’s resolving is a permanent spell, its resolution may involve several steps.”
  10. Count the triggers from Cordial Vampire's entry, and state the total.
    All permanents on the battlefield are checked against this entry event: Impact Tremors triggers once, Warleader's Call triggers once, Purphoros triggers once (Cordial Vampire is 'another creature you control' -- an object other than Purphoros, and Purphoros's non-creature status is irrelevant to that reading). 3 more triggered abilities go on the stack above nothing else. GRAND TOTAL for the sequence: 6 + 3 = NINE separate triggered abilities -- three instances of Purphoros's ability, three of Impact Tremors's, three of Warleader's Call's.
    CR 603.6a — “Each time an event puts one or more permanents onto the battlefield, all permanents on the battlefield (including the newcomers) are checked for any enters-the-battlefield triggers that match the event.”
    CR 603.3 — “The ability becomes the topmost object on the stack.”
  11. Two things on this board that contribute nothing: Cordial Vampire's own ability, and Warleader's Call's +1/+1.
    Cordial Vampire reads 'Whenever this creature or another creature dies, put a +1/+1 counter on each Vampire you control.' Dies means put into a graveyard from the battlefield. In this sequence no creature is put into a graveyard from the battlefield -- no combat, no removal, no sacrifice -- so the ability never triggers. In particular an opponent losing the game is NOT a creature dying: a player leaving the game causes objects that player owns to leave the game, which is not a graveyard event. Cordial Vampire contributes zero triggers and zero damage here, and would add no damage even if it did trigger, since it only places +1/+1 counters. Separately, Warleader's Call's other half -- 'Creatures you control get +1/+1' -- is a static ability that modifies power and toughness only. It makes each Vampire token a 2/2 and Cordial Vampire a 2/2. No combat occurs in this sequence and no damage in this sequence is dealt by a creature: every point comes from the fixed numbers printed in the three triggered abilities (2, 1 and 1). The +1/+1 therefore changes no damage total in this scenario.
    CR 700.4 — “is put into a graveyard from the battlefield”
    CR 800.4a — “When a player leaves the game, all objects (see rule 109) owned by that player leave the game”
    CR 302.4c — “To determine a creature’s power and toughness, start with the numbers printed in its lower right corner, then apply any applicable continuous effects.”
  12. Resolve the final 3 triggers and step the life totals down.
    Per opponent: Purphoros 2, Impact Tremors 1, Warleader's Call 1 = 4 more damage each, taking each opponent 4 -> 0. Stepping down one resolution at a time (order is yours; Purphoros first): 4 -> 2 -> 1 -> 0. State-based actions are checked after each; after the first two no opponent is at 0 or less, so nothing is performed. GRAND TOTAL PER OPPONENT: Purphoros 3 x 2 = 6, Impact Tremors 3 x 1 = 3, Warleader's Call 3 x 1 = 3, i.e. TWELVE damage per opponent. Pod total across three seats: 36. Against 12 life per seat this is EXACTLY lethal, with zero margin -- one fewer trigger and every opponent survives at 1 or 2.
    CR 119.2 — “Damage dealt to a player normally causes that player to lose that much life.”
    CR 608.1 — “Each time all players pass in succession, the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves.”
  13. The final state-based-action check, and what happens to anything left on the stack.
    After the ninth trigger resolves, the next state-based-action check finds all three opponents at 0 life. A player at 0 or less life loses the game as a state-based action, and all applicable state-based actions are performed simultaneously as a single event, so all three opponents lose at the same time rather than one at a time. Each player who loses the game leaves the game. No opponent reached 0 earlier in the sequence, so no trigger is ever orphaned mid-sequence on this board and the stack is already empty when the last damage is dealt. For completeness: the moment the last opponent leaves, you are a player still in the game whose opponents have all left, so you win immediately, and any object still on the stack at that moment would never resolve because the game has ended.
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 704.3 — “the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event.”
    CR 104.3b — “If a player’s life total is 0 or less, that player loses the game the next time a player would receive priority.”
    CR 104.5 — “If a player loses the game, that player leaves the game.”
    CR 104.2a — “A player still in the game wins the game if that player’s opponents have all left the game. This happens immediately”
