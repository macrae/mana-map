FROM THE EDITOR

THE DECK WITH NO BIG TURN

Turn two, you cast a 1/1 that nobody at the table reads, and three life totals move at once. That is the whole deck, and it is either the best hour you will spend or the longest.

MANA MAP
From the Editor
Vol. 6 · EDGAR MARKOV

The card that runs this deck is the one card you will never draw.

Edgar Markov mints a Vampire every time you cast a Vampire, and he does it from the command zone — so the engine is switched on in game one, on turn one, whether or not you get around to paying six mana for the body. Everything else in the 99 feeds that fact: a curve made almost entirely of one- and two-mana creatures, doublers that turn one token into four, and payoffs that bill all three opponents for the privilege of a body existing.

Coach Brightside opens the log with what follows from that, and it sounds like a misplay out loud. The Counselor agrees with him on the rule and then takes about a third of the claim away, which is the best exchange in the issue. Ledger has the rates for the two lines nobody should plan around.

You will have a good time here if you want your whole turn to be a two-mana creature and you want that to be enough — if you like watching three life totals move at once off a card nobody bothered to read. You will not, if you are waiting for a big turn. This deck does not have one. It has a great many small ones, and it wants all of them.

One warning, from the Counselor's file: the kill that is on the record is exactly lethal. Not comfortably. Exactly.

Which card do you always have? Not the one you drew — the one in the command zone. CR 903.6 puts Edgar Markov there face up before anybody has a hand, and that guarantee is the only one this format hands you.

Eminence is why it matters here. It reads “Whenever you cast another Vampire spell, if Edgar is in the command zone or on the battlefield, create a 1/1 black Vampire creature token.” An ability that names the zones it functions in functions from those zones — CR 113.6b — and this one names the command zone out loud. Stack 002 has it firing from there, through two doublers, four tokens on one cast.

Two riders travel with it. The tokens are created, not cast, so they never trigger eminence a second time — the record closes that door. And casting Edgar is not casting another Vampire spell: he is the one Vampire in your deck that mints nothing.

Now price the man himself. Six, then eight, then ten, then twelve: CR 903.8 adds {2} for every previous cast from the zone, and the surcharge compounds for the rest of the game. The ladder beside this column is the whole argument for leaving him home. The simulation agrees: he is down by turn six in 52.1% of games, mean cast turn 6.6 — and the tokens have been arriving since turn one.

Colour identity is why the 99 looks the way it does. CR 903.4 reads mana symbols in the cost and in the rules text, so {3}{R}{W}{B} buys white's exile and board-wide protection, black's tutors and drains, and red's damage payoffs. It also shuts the door on blue counterspells and green ramp: this deck answers a permanent after it resolves, or it does not answer it.

Is the 21-damage clock real? CR 903.10a ends a player dealt 21 combat damage by one commander, and Edgar is a 4/4 with first strike and haste who puts a +1/+1 counter on every attacking Vampire, himself included. Treat it as a coincidence you can cash. Commander damage points at one seat; this deck's kill points at three.

One more zone fact, and it wins games against tax decks. Eminence is a triggered ability, not a cast. A tax on spells reaches the Vampire spell and never touches the token it mints.

★ Coach's read. The table re-prices you the moment your commander is read out loud, so stop buying anonymity you cannot afford. Spend the budget on order instead: land the two-mana payoffs while they still look like durdle, and let the six-drop arrive last, to a board he makes lethal rather than one he has to build.

★ And be honest about insurance. Your engine does not route through Edgar — eminence keeps minting whatever happens to him — so protecting him protects the wrong thing. What his presence buys is a discount: Flawless Maneuver costs {2}{W} while he waits in the zone and {0} the moment he is on the battlefield, and Deflecting Swat obeys the same clause.

Pilot TipBudget three mana for Deflecting Swat until Edgar is on the battlefield — the free mode reads 'if you control your commander,' and in half your games he is still home on turn six.

THE PLAN

EVERY CAST BILLS THREE SEATS

The table sees a six-mana commander and shields up for combat. You are not playing combat — you are casting cheap Vampires into a layer that charges every seat for each body that arrives.

The table sees Edgar Markov and shields up for combat. Let them. This deck kills with triggers, and the ignition never has to be drawn: every Vampire spell you cast mints a token straight from the command zone. No setup card to protect, no key turn to telegraph. You cast cheap Vampires — eleven of them cost two or less — and the machine runs itself.

The kill is conversion. Five cards turn every arriving body into table-wide damage: Purphoros, God of the Forge, Impact Tremors, Warleader's Call, Corpse Knight and Witty Roastmaster. Stack 009 is the whole plan in one exchange — one two-mana Vampire, three payoffs and a doubler on board, twelve damage to every opponent, table dead. You draw a converter by turn 6 in 62.6% of games. So your early turns have one job: land a converter and a multiplier, then keep casting Vampires into them.

The wipe is coming — plan to get paid for it. Blood Artist and Cruel Celebrant drain as your board dies, and eminence rebuilds from a zone no sweeper touches. So commit in waves: deploy the cheap bodies, hold one real threat back, and keep a protection spell — you run five that are free or flash — for the turn you go all-in, not the turn after.

Combat is your second kill: the lords, Shared Animosity and Sanctum Seeker turn the same width into lethal attacks. The infinites are found money, not the plan — Bloodthirsty Conqueror with Vito or Sanguine Bond ends the game on the spot (stacks 004 and 006 prove it), but it assembles unassisted in 4.8% of games. Don't chase it. Tutors move the main kill from 41.7% to 57.7% by turn 6; spend them on the missing converter or doubler, and take the loop only when it's dealt to you.

What this does not model

  • Stack 009's kill is exactly lethal: twelve to each of three seats sitting at twelve. One trigger fewer and all three opponents are alive at one or two.
  • Stacks 004 and 006 both open on three life already gained from an unnamed source. Everything above that seed is proved; the seed itself is verified by nothing.
  • Every checked board is a pod already low — 12 a seat in stack 009, 15 and 9 in 007, 24 and 10 in 008. The go-wide plan is on the record as a finisher, not yet as a clock from 40.
  • Unsettled With a free sacrifice outlet (Viscera Seer or Ashnod's Altar), Crossway Troublemakers AND High-Society Hunter on the battlefield, sacrifice N eminence tokens in one turn: how many cards are drawn, at what life cost, and does High-Society Hunter see ANY of them given its 'another nontoken creature' clause? Also: is a 1/1 black Vampire token 'a Vampire you control' for Crossway Troublemakers, and can the 2-life payment be declined per trigger?
  • Unsettled Stack 002 records eminence tokens entering WITH Vampire Socialite's additional +1/+1 counter (an opponent had lost life earlier that turn); stack 011 records them entering WITHOUT it, because the only life loss that turn came from a trigger that had not yet resolved. What is the practical sequencing rule - does the pilot cast the Vampire before or after the turn's first drain, and does an attack-step Sanctum Seeker trigger resolving beat a post-combat cast?
  • Unsettled Nothing measures whether a body is actually CONVERTED. The declaration prices 'a free sacrifice outlet drawn' at 77.6% and 'a drain that fires when a creature DIES' at 37.2% independently, but not the pair, and Forge cannot help: its evaluator activated Ashnod's Altar 0 times in 59 castings under both AI profiles and cast Viscera Seer 0 times in 500 games (both bench measurements on sacrifice decks, not taken on this list). Can the goldfish measure an outlet AND a death payoff together, and if not, what is the honest statement of what this stage's rate means?
  • Simulation 10,000 runs of resource development, not of games — no opponent acts in any of them.

1 further question about this engine is open and unsettled.

What this model does and does not do. These runs simulate resource development, not full games. Every assumption is stated:
  • Simulates resource development, not full games (no interaction, no removal).
  • Draw every turn including turn 1 (multiplayer Commander).
  • Mulligan: keep 7-card hands with 2-5 lands; up to 2 fresh redraws, keep the last.
  • One land drop per turn when available.
  • LANDS ENTER UNTAPPED, ALWAYS, and the one in hand longest is the one played. A tapland costs nothing here and no land is ever chosen over another, so this model CANNOT rank two lands that make the same colours — `mana-analysis` and `mana-fit` are what answer a land question, and they are deterministic for exactly this reason.
  • Mana rocks ('{T}: Add') contribute from the turn after they are cast.
  • Commander cast on first affordable turn (highest spending priority).
  • Bodies count = creatures cast + tokens parsed from 'create ... token' text.
  • Target assembly counts cards DRAWN by a turn (cast cards still count).
  • CARD DRAW IS ONE A TURN UNLESS `model_draw` IS SET. With the flag on, four channels are modelled — a card's own ETB draw, an instant or sorcery that draws, an upkeep trigger, and an ARRIVAL trigger that draws when other creatures or tokens enter (Welcoming Vampire, Caretaker's Talent), which rides the same single door the ETB damage payoffs use. Activated draw (needs a spending policy this model has no opinion on), X-based draw (board-dependent), and death- or attack-triggered draw (no deaths, and attacks only under `model_combat`) are NOT modelled and the cards are named in `meta.card_advantage.draw_not_modelled`. A conditional trigger — 'if you control an artifact', 'you may draw' — is treated as unmodelled rather than assumed to be on: 39 of the 348 ETB-draw cards in the corpus carry one.
  • AN ARRIVAL-DRAW ENGINE NEVER SEES ITS OWN ARRIVAL. Welcoming Vampire is a 2/3 that draws 'whenever one or more OTHER creatures you control with power 2 or less enter'; registered before its own entry it passed its own gate and drew a card it does not draw, overstating edgar-vampires by 22% at turn eight. The deferral applies to every card and not only the ones worded 'other', which understates Tocasia's Welcome and two relatives by one draw on the turn they land.
  • ARRIVAL DRAW IS SMALLER WITHOUT `model_combat`, and legitimately so. The channel rides the one door onto the battlefield, which `model_combat` also uses to spawn token copies and ETB-payoff tokens — those are real additional arrivals that do not exist in the resource-only model. Measured on edgar-vampires: 1.264 extra cards by turn ten with both flags against 1.106 with draw alone. Before 2026-08-28 the gap was 1.264 against 0.323, because every call to that door sat inside `if model_combat:` and a draw-only deck silently lost three quarters of its arrival draws.
  • HELD-UP INTERACTION is reported as two series and both are FLOORS. This model casts everything it can afford every turn, so `interaction_castable` is what a pilot developing at full speed has left over, not what a pilot choosing to hold up two mana would have. A low figure is therefore a real finding and a high one is unambiguous good news.
  • Tutors are modeled as wildcards: a CAST tutor that fetches to hand or the top — including a TYPED one (Worldly Tutor, Sarkhan's Triumph), which the old literal 'a card' match missed entirely — fills ONE missing any_of group. Consumed once, mana paid, and a tutor that puts the card on top of the library costs a turn. Every arrival fires them — cast, token or copy — and a copy is itself an arrival, so they compound, which is the deck working rather than a bug. A payoff worded 'another NONTOKEN' does not re-trigger on its own tokens, which is the brake the rules already had. A chain is capped at 12 deep. X counts the whole board rather than one subtype: exact where the creatures are Dragons, generous otherwise.

  • Rituals are not modeled (conservative).
  • Extra card draw is NOT modeled: one card per turn, always. That understates every deck, but by an amount proportional to how much draw it runs — so it is conservative WITHIN a deck and not neutral BETWEEN them. `meta.card_advantage` reports how much of each list this hides. Card advantage is measured nowhere in this suite.
  • COMBAT: one opponent at 40 life who does nothing — no blockers, no removal, no interaction. This is a goldfish in the literal sense, so `kill_turn` is the turn an UNOPPOSED board would finish ONE seat, not a win rate.
  • Attacks with every creature that is not summoning-sick; haste is read from the ORACLE TEXT (this line said 'type line' and was simply wrong). There is nothing to block, so nothing is ever held back — in a real four-player game you would keep blockers, which makes this an optimistic clock and a pessimistic board.

Three wordings are read — a replacement effect on damage you deal (Twinflame Tyrant), a granted double strike (Atarka) and a power doubling (Thrakkus). A grant worded 'each Dragon you control' is treated as applying to the WHOLE team: exact in a deck whose attackers are Dragons, generous in one where they are not. A creature's own double strike multiplies only itself. Board power is unaffected — a double-striker is not a bigger creature.
