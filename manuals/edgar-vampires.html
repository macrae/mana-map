Start with the question the ladder beside this paragraph is really asking: what does it cost this deck when Edgar Markov dies? The answer is unusual, and it is the reason you may play him the way you do.

Eminence is a triggered ability that functions from the command zone, because the ability itself says which zones it works in — CR 113.6b, and stack 002 put it on the record. Your commander is the only card you are guaranteed to have access to (CR 903.4). In most decks that guarantee is a finisher. Here it is the engine, and it is running from turn one whether or not you ever pay six mana.

So read the tax ladder as a price you are usually right to refuse. The printed cost is {3}{R}{W}{B}; a second cast adds {2}, a third adds {2} again (CR 903.8). The simulation puts him at a median cast turn of six and never reaches a mean of eight available mana inside ten turns — 7.258 on turn ten is as high as it gets. A second Edgar is a whole turn you paid for. A third is a fantasy. Which reframes the protection question: your insurance is not covering an engine, it is covering one alpha strike.

Colour identity is what a Mardu commander charges you (CR 903.4). Three colours of Vampire — every lord, every drain, every entry-damage enchantment — and no green ramp and no blue counterspell for the life of the deck. That is why the ramp here is seven copies of rocks and rebates rather than a stack of land tutors, and why your answers are white exile and black sweepers.

Now the clock nobody at this table is on. Twenty-one commander damage (CR 903.10a) asks for six connections from a 4/4, and this deck does not attack with Edgar six times. Commander damage is a coincidence here, not a plan — his attack trigger, a +1/+1 counter on each Vampire you control, is worth more in one swing than his own damage is all game.

And the political read, which starts before you draw a card. The table sees a Vampire commander and prices you as a creature deck. They are wrong in the direction that helps you: the machine is made of enchantments, and eminence rebuilds the bodies for free. Let them hold the wrath. Cast the second Vampire.

THE PLAN

TOKENS AS BURN SPELLS

Every Vampire you cast is two bodies before it does anything else. Three cards decide whether that second body is a chump blocker or a burn spell aimed at all three seats — and which one you find first is the game.

Your commander never has to leave the command zone, and that is the best news in the deck. Eminence reads “Whenever you cast another Vampire spell, if Edgar is in the command zone or on the battlefield, create a 1/1 black Vampire creature token” — free, untaxed, from turn one. So every cheap Vampire you cast is two bodies before it does anything else. Put Anointed Procession, Mondrak, Glory Dominus or Elspeth, Storm Slayer underneath it and one trigger makes four: stack 002 verifies the arithmetic, and a multiplier or extra token-maker is drawn in 79.0% of simulated games at a mean turn of 2.68.

Then comes the layer that makes width lethal against three seats at once. Purphoros, God of the Forge, Impact Tremors and Warleader's Call each turn a creature entering the battlefield into damage to EACH opponent — no attack step, no blockers, no evasion needed. Stack 008 counts it exactly: one two-mana Vampire through two doublers is five entries and five Purphoros triggers, 10 damage to every opponent. That conversion layer is drawn in 43.2% of games at a mean turn of 3.45, and it arrives with a multiplier alongside it in 33.4%.

Combat is your second kill and it assembles faster than anything else you own — a Vampire lord or anthem shows up in 82.9% of games at a mean turn of 2.58. Stack 003 prices what that buys: seven attacking Vampires under Sanctum Seeker with Bloodletter of Aclazotz out drain each opponent for 14 at declare-attackers, before a single blocker exists. Edgar is the pivot rather than the opener — median cast turn 6, and his attack trigger puts a +1/+1 counter on every Vampire you control the turn he swings. Here is the trap: waiting for him. The token board is already a clock, and the damage triggers do not care whether anything survives to combat.

You also hold a deterministic kill, and you should hold it lightly. Bloodthirsty Conqueror with Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose or with Sanguine Bond is a zero-mana table-kill, both verified (stacks 004 and 006) — and you draw a complete pair in 4.8% of games, 24.0% counting all three unrestricted tutors as wildcards. That is a lottery ticket you happen to be holding, not a plan, and every one of those cards pays rent as a Vampire body or a drain on the turns the loop never happens. Race with the tokens, present the loop only when the window is open, and never hold back a board to protect a pairing that shows up one game in twenty.

THE ROSTER

EVERY CAST COUNTS TWICE

Sixty-three spells with one hidden common cost: every Vampire among them also prints a body from the command zone. The conversion layer leads, because it is the difference between a board and a clock.

The conversion layer: entries become damage

Purphoros, God of the Forge

Aura + ProtectionTokens + Anthem

The heaviest converter in the deck: 2 damage to each opponent for every other creature you control entering the battlefield. Stack 008 measures the ceiling at 10 damage to every opponent off one two-mana Vampire cast — and because devotion to red usually keeps him a noncreature, he is an indestructible enchantment a creature sweeper cannot touch.

Impact Tremors

The same conversion for two mana: 1 damage to each opponent every time a creature you control enters. It is an enchantment, so it survives everything aimed at your board and keeps charging rent while you rebuild.

Warleader's Call

Tokens + Anthem

Conversion and combat in one card — every creature you control gets +1/+1, and every creature entering deals 1 damage to each opponent. In a deck with 29 creature copies and a token engine, that anthem is quietly the largest single buff you can play.

The ignition and its multipliers

Anointed Procession

Doubler one, and the cheapest way to make every eminence trigger worth twice as much. Stack 002 verifies that it and Mondrak apply once each to the same token event, in either order, for four tokens off one trigger.

Mondrak, Glory Dominus

Aura + Protection

Doubler two, on a 4/4 body that can protect itself by sacrificing two other permanents for an indestructible counter — and eminence tokens are exactly the permanents you can spare. It is a Phyrexian Horror, not a Vampire, so casting it makes no token of its own.

Elspeth, Storm Slayer

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Doubler three, with a static ability that needs no combat and a +1 that makes a Soldier every turn to feed it. Her 0 puts a +1/+1 counter on each creature you control and gives them flying until your next turn, which turns a token board into an evasive one for a turn.

Caretaker's Talent

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Draws a card the first time tokens enter each turn, which is a card on nearly every turn you cast a Vampire. Level 2 copies a token you control; level 3 gives every creature token +2/+2 and turns the swarm into a real combat force.

Ocelot Pride

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

A one-mana first-strike lifelink body that pays off the deck's constant incidental lifegain: gain any life and it makes a Cat at your end step. With the city's blessing it copies every token that entered this turn, which on a doubled eminence turn is a second wave.

The bodies that print bodies

Charismatic Conqueror

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

A tax that makes tokens on other people’s turns: whenever an artifact or creature an opponent controls enters untapped, they either tap it themselves or hand you a 1/1 white lifelink Vampire. Against three seats both branches are good for you, and it is a two-mana Vampire, so casting it is already two bodies.

Legion's Landing // Adanto, the First Fort

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

A one-mana lifelink Vampire that transforms the first time you attack with three or more creatures — a threshold this deck crosses routinely. The back face is a white source that makes another lifelink Vampire for {2}{W}, so it is a land that keeps feeding the entry triggers.

Mavren Fein, Dusk Apostle

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Every attack with one or more nontoken Vampires makes a 1/1 white lifelink Vampire — a token per combat, doubled by any doubler, and another entry for the damage payoffs.

Bloodline Keeper // Lord of Lineage

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

A repeatable 2/2 flying Vampire every turn with no mana required, which is the shape token doublers like best. Once you control five Vampires, flip it for {B} and the whole team gets +2/+2.

Elenda, the Dusk Rose

Counters + TokensLifegain + Death Trigger

A lifelink Vampire that grows on every other creature's death and cashes out into that many 1/1 white lifelink Vampires when she dies — a token wave that a sweeper triggers rather than prevents, and one your doublers double.

Strefan, Maurer Progenitor

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

A flier that turns a turn in which people lost life into Blood tokens — which, in this deck, is most turns. Sacrifice two on an attack to put a Vampire from hand onto the battlefield tapped, attacking and indestructible.

Preacher of the Schism

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Deathtouch on a three-mana Vampire, plus a choice the table cannot make comfortable: attack the player with the most life and you get a 1/1 lifelink Vampire; attack while you have the most life and you draw.

Kalitas, Traitor of Ghet

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Turns opponents' removal into a punishment: their nontoken creatures are exiled instead of dying and you get a 2/2 Zombie each time. Its sacrifice ability gives you a place to put spare tokens when Kalitas needs to be bigger.

Lords, anthems and the alpha strike

Legion Lieutenant

Two mana, other Vampires get +1/+1. In a deck averaging 4.12 bodies by turn six, that anthem is paid many times over — and it is a Vampire spell, so it brings its own token.

Stromkirk Captain

The lord that changes combat math rather than just size: other Vampires get +1/+1 and first strike, which makes a wide board of small tokens miserable to block.

Captivating Vampire

Vampire Socialite

Menace plus a counter engine keyed to something you cause constantly: while an opponent has lost life this turn, every other Vampire enters with an extra +1/+1 counter. Stack 002 confirms the tokens get it too, which turns a doubled eminence trigger into four 2/2s.

Flying for every creature you control carrying a +1/+1 counter, which this deck distributes freely through Edgar's attack trigger, Vampire Socialite, Cordial Vampire and Elspeth. It is a Human Soldier, so it makes no eminence token — it is here for evasion, not width.

Akroma's Will

Aura + ProtectionFlying + Damage Trigger

The protection spell that also ends games: flying, vigilance and double strike, or lifelink, indestructible and protection from each colour — and with a commander out, both. On a wide board either half is a swing of many points.

The attack-turn drains

Sanctum Seeker

Attack Trigger + TokensEquipment + Attack Trigger

Each attacking Vampire costs every opponent 1 life and gains you 1 — a drain that fires at declare-attackers and cannot be blocked away. Stack 003 verifies seven attackers under Bloodletter as 14 out of each opponent before blockers.

Bloodletter of Aclazotz

Flying + Damage Trigger

The doubler on the other axis: every opponent's life loss during your turn is doubled. Stack 003 verifies it applies per loss event, not per turn — seven Sanctum Seeker triggers become 14 out of each seat.

Throne of the God-Pharaoh

Two mana that converts a tapped-out attack into a second drain at your end step, equal to the number of tapped creatures you control. Stack 007 verifies the reach and bounds it honestly: seven tapped creatures under Bloodletter is 14 per opponent, which is not lethal by default.

Twilight Prophet

Flying + Damage Trigger

A four-mana flier that, once you control ten permanents, drains every opponent for a card's mana value each upkeep and refills your hand with it. A wide token board reaches ten permanents without trying.

The deterministic kill, and its rent

Bloodthirsty Conqueror

Flying + Damage Trigger

A 5/5 flying deathtouch Vampire that gains you life whenever any opponent loses it — which the entry triggers, Sanctum Seeker attacks and Throne end steps all cause anyway. With Vito or Sanguine Bond it is a verified zero-mana table-kill (stacks 004 and 006); without either, it is still one of the best blockers you can present.

Vito, Thorn of the Dusk Rose

Lifegain + Death Trigger

Every point of life you gain becomes targeted life loss, on a three-mana Vampire body that pays rent as an eminence trigger and a lord target. Stack 004 verifies the loop with Bloodthirsty Conqueror; its {3}{B}{B} ability gives the whole team lifelink for one drain-everything combat.

Sanguine Bond

Lifegain + Death Trigger

Vito's trigger word for word, on an enchantment — which is the point, because it gives the loop a half no creature removal can answer. Stack 006 verifies the kill with Bloodthirsty Conqueror at zero mana and one exposed creature instead of two.

Cheap bodies that bank tokens

Knight of the Ebon Legion

Counters + Tokens

A one-drop that never stops scaling: {2}{B} makes it a 4/4 deathtouch, and it grows permanently at your end step whenever a player lost 4 or more life this turn — a condition the entry-damage payoffs satisfy on their own.

Vampire of the Dire Moon

Lifegain + Death Trigger

One mana, deathtouch and lifelink. It blanks an attacker all game, banks an eminence token on the way in, and its lifelink triggers anything keyed to your life gain.

Welcoming Vampire

Counterspell + DrawDraw + Discard

A flier that draws a card the first time other small creatures enter each turn — and a doubled eminence trigger is exactly that. Board-gated draw is the right kind here, because a board is what this deck has.

Henrika Domnathi // Henrika, Infernal Seer

Draw + DiscardFlying + Damage Trigger

A flier that offers a different mode each combat — an edict at the table, a card, or a transform into a 3/3 flying deathtouch lifelink threat that pumps your evasive creatures. Three uses out of one four-mana Vampire.

Cemetery Gatekeeper

Bounce + ETBCopy + ETB

A two-mana first-striking Vampire that exiles a card from a graveyard on arrival and then taxes every player who plays a land or casts a spell sharing that card's type for 2 damage. Choose the exile with the table's decks in mind — it is a repeatable clock nobody removes early.

BEST IN THE COLOUR YOU NEED LEAST

Black carries 53.4% of your pips on 41.5% of your sources; white carries the lightest bill and always shows up. Here is exactly what that skew costs you on turn two.

Thirty-seven lands across 34 entries, and the figure to sit with first is 16 — the number that can enter tapped. That is 43% of the base against the one-in-three budget the deck builder allows itself, which sounds worse than it plays: only three of them (Nomad Outpost, Savai Triome, Midgar, City of Mako) enter tapped unconditionally. The rest are shocks, verges, checks and fastlands that come in untapped when you meet their condition. Land drops hit 99.8% on turn two and 92.1% on turn three, with mean available mana of 2.149 and 3.176 on those turns.

The interesting disagreement is between pips and sources, and it is entirely black. Black carries 53.4% of the deck's pips on 41.5% of its sources — 39 pips across 29 cards, up to three at a time, standing on 22 land sources. Red is the mirror image at 15.1% of pips on 26.4% of sources, and white is the one colour where the two agree, 31.5% against 32.1%. You are, in other words, best supported in the colour you need least.

On-curve probability prices that skew exactly. From lands alone, white arrives 83.1% of the time and red 76.2%; black arrives 63.9%. Your tightest leg is your heaviest colour, and that is the whole shape of this manabase in one number.

The rocks buy a real slice of it back. Arcane Signet, Patchwork Banner, Talisman of Indulgence and the back face of Legion's Landing take black from 22 sources to 25 and 63.9% to 71.8%, white to 88.1%, red to 83.1%. Sol Ring adds speed, not colour. Patchwork Banner is the standout of the group because it is a lord that taps for any colour, and the two cost reducers — Herald's Horn and Bontu's Monument — lower the bill rather than raising the sources, which is the same thing on a curve this cheap. Nothing in the deck produces restricted mana, so every source can pay for an activated ability.

About the 90% yardstick: it asks for 36 black sources against 22 red and 22 white. Thirty-six black sources in a 37-land deck is arithmetic that does not exist, and the audit says so itself — a yardstick, not a possibility. So read the gap as posture rather than as a defect. Sequence black first, keep the untapped black-white lands for one-drop starts, and let Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth do its trick: with it out, every land you control is a Swamp and the whole base taps for the colour the audit flags.
