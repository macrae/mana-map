Begin with the zone itself. CR 903.4 sets a commander's colour identity from every mana symbol in its mana cost and rules text — and Sisay's activation, {W}{U}{B}{R}{G}, puts all five colours in hers. A {2}{W} card is your licence to sleeve ninety-nine cards in five colours. That is not a loophole; it is the rule working exactly as written, and it is the entire reason this deck exists.

The zone guarantee is worth more here than in most decks. CR 903.8: the command zone gives you guaranteed access at a rising price — {2} more for each previous cast from the zone. Sisay runs 3, then 5, then 7, then 9. Note what the ladder beside this column does not tax: her power is recomputed from the battlefield, so a recast Sisay returns to a surviving board at full strength. Spot removal costs you the tax and a turn. A sweeper costs you the tax and the ladder. Those are different injuries; insure against the second.

The 21-damage clock — CR 903.10a — is on the record but rarely the plan. At four colours she is a 6/6, and three connections is a kill in theory. In practice her power is a gate, not a weapon: the deck's lethal output is the section titled The Kill. Treat commander damage as a bonus the scoreboard occasionally pays out.

Now the Coach's half: protect, don't budget for recasts. The goldfish holds a protection effect by turn six in 79.8% of games — Shalai, Flowering of the White Tree, Plaza of Heroes, and two free instants. A recast costs more than mana; it costs an activation window at a table that has now seen the engine. And the political read is fixed from the pregame: a five-colour commander who tutors on activation is announced as bracket 4 before your first land drop. Don't fight the label — schedule it.

THE PLAN

COUNT COLOURS, NOT CARDS

Why do good Sisay pilots pass with all five pips available and no activation? Because the number that decides what she can find isn't printed on her card — it's the count of distinct colours sitting beside her.

The table looks at five colours and files you under greedy. Let them. What you actually brought is a fair tutor that never taps, never says once per turn, and gets better every time it fires — and access is the only currency this deck spends.

Read her power, not her mana cost. Sisay is a 2/2 plus one for every colour among your other legendary permanents, and she finds any legendary permanent cheaper than that power. Three legal targets at power 2, ten at power 3, twenty-three at power 4. So your first move is never "cast Sisay" — it is "put a colour-dense legend down before you activate." Derevi buys three rungs for three mana. Katilda, Kinnan, Lotho, Saffi, Kambal and Kutzil each buy two.

Stop climbing at four colours. Nothing in this list costs more than five mana, so power 6 already opens all 33 targets and the fifth colour buys stats and Jodah's anthem and zero new access. Spend that turn holding up protection instead.

Here is the trap that eats new pilots. Most of what looks like acceleration cannot pay her: Sol Ring makes colourless, Delighted Halfling's any-colour mana is for legendary spells only and an activation is not a spell, Unclaimed Territory is creature spells only. Use those to deploy her, then count the six cards that genuinely foot the bill — Bloom Tender, Faeburrow Elder, Jegantha, Esika, Relic of Legends, Selvala — alongside Command Tower, Arcane Signet, Plaza of Heroes and Secluded Courtyard named on Human.

She is on the board by turn three in 95 games out of 100, so never plan around finding her. Plan around the turn she and a five-colour source arrive together, which happens by turn six a little over half the time. That is the turn your deck starts existing.

Then go shopping for the answer instead of holding it. This list runs one unconditional creature removal spell and one sweeper, and it does not need more, because Kutzil and Teferi take away the windows in which opponents may act, Thalia makes their board and their lands arrive tapped, and Kambal and Sheoldred bill them for their own turns. All of them are legendary at mana value 2 to 4 — pick the tax that hurts the seat that is actually beating you and go get it.

Watch which colour finishes your ladder. Among your other 33 legendary permanents, white appears on 19 and green on 14, but blue on 8, red on 6 and black on 5 — and the only red legend at three mana or less is Najeela. The card that completes your fourth colour and the card that makes you the archenemy are frequently the same card, so fetch her with the mana up to use her.

The kill is that Najeela, and it is the one line here that has been walked against the Comprehensive Rules and cleared an adversarial check: a deterministic, unbounded combat loop with Derevi that ends the table. Nine other lines the combo data flags for this deck are unresolved. Treat them as board states you might stumble into, never as the plan you keep a hand for.

Know your one real weakness and play around it on purpose. Twenty-six of your nonland cards are not legendary and are invisible to her forever — three of the six cards that pay her bill, every counterspell, both free protection instants, and all four Game Changers. She finds payoffs. You have to draw the fuel.

ROLL CALL

EVERY LEGEND EARNS ITS SEAT

Fifty-nine spells, one question each: does it climb the ladder, pay the bill, or hold the window open? The roster is ranked by load — the six cards that can pay her lead, and the lands report to Sources Say.

The pip battery — the only six that can pay her

Bloom Tender

Ramp + Cost Reduction

One mana per colour on your board, scaling off exactly the same colours the ladder counts, so it grows as you climb. It is non-legendary, so you will never fetch it — keep it when it shows up in an opener.

Faeburrow Elder

Ramp + Cost Reduction

It pays a pip of every colour on your board and grows as the ladder does, so treat it as a second Bloom Tender. Non-legendary again — draw it, or do without it.

Jegantha, the Wellspring

Ramp + Cost Reduction

One tap for {W}{U}{B}{R}{G}, which is exactly the bill and nothing else, since that mana cannot pay generic costs. Once it can tap, a single card funds a whole activation.

Relic of Legends

Three mana that turns any untapped legendary creature into a coloured pip, and it taps for one itself. Non-legendary, so it is another piece of fuel you have to draw.

One green mana and a tap for as many mana as your biggest creature's power, in any combination of colours — often the whole activation in one go. Get a fat body down first and she pays for everything.

Untap two other legendary creatures and the battery pays twice in one turn — that is a second activation out of a board you already have. Point it at whichever two produce the most colours.

Seedborn Muse

Thalia, Heretic Cathar

THE DRILL

MULLIGAN FOR ENABLERS

Which seven do you keep in a deck whose payoffs fetch themselves? The Coach wants fuel and a cheap legend; Ledger's ten thousand hands say you keep 84.2% of first sevens — so the judgement is all colour and fuel.

Keepable first sevens (simulated)84%

KEEP

Command Tower, Temple Garden, Forest, Bloom Tender, Katilda, Dawnhart Prime, Derevi, Empyrial Tactician, Rhystic Study

Two rungs on fair bodies, the best battery piece in the deck, unrestricted colour. Sisay arrives on curve and the ladder hits three colours by turn five — this is the enabler hand the whole section is asking for.

SHIP

Jodah, the Unifier, Kenrith, the Returned King, Surrak Dragonclaw, Jegantha, the Wellspring, Samut, Voice of Dissent, Plains, Island

Five payoffs, two lands, no fixing. Every legend here is a fetch target — this hand holds cards Sisay could have found, and none of the fuel she can't.

SHIP

Sol Ring, Delighted Halfling, Unclaimed Territory, Plains, Mountain, Najeela, the Blade-Blossom, Fierce Guardianship

The trap keep. It curves out fast and pays nothing: Sol Ring is colourless, the Halfling's mana is legendary-spells-only, the Territory is creature-spells-only. Sisay lands early and stares at a five-pip bill this hand cannot cover.

KEEP

City of Brass, Flooded Strand, Swamp, Arcane Signet, Kutzil, Malamet Exemplar, Esper Sentinel, Path to Exile

Three lands with real colour, a rock that pays any pip, a two-rung legend, and interaction to hold. Not flashy — exactly the hand the 84.2% keep rate is made of.

Everyone mulligans this deck hunting for the commander. She is the one card you do not need to find — the model casts her by turn three in 95 games out of 100. Keep for the things she cannot go and get.

You have seen how the 99 splits: six cards can pay a five-pip activation and three of those six are non-legendary, so they only ever arrive by draw. That is the whole judgement in a keep. Fuel and colours, not payoffs.

Three things make a seven. Two to four lands including at least one source that makes any colour — Command Tower, City of Brass, Mana Confluence, Exotic Orchard, Forbidden Orchard, Plaza of Heroes, a fetch, or Secluded Courtyard on Human. Something that accelerates or pays: Birds of Paradise, Bloom Tender, Faeburrow Elder, Arcane Signet, Fellwar Stone, Relic of Legends. And best of all, a cheap multicolour legend, because that hand casts Sisay and immediately climbs.

An early accelerant shows up by turn six in 64 games out of 100. So a seven without one is not unlucky, it is under the curve you are expected to hit — ship it and draw a normal hand instead.

Can thirty-nine lands pay a bill with no generic in it? Flat sources against unflat pips is the whole story — this base is priced for the activation, not the cast costs, and the audit zeroes every restricted source before it counts a thing.

The interesting disagreement is between pip share and source share. White carries 38.3% of the deck's pips on 23.1% of its sources; red carries 8.0% of the pips on 18.5%. Case A-002 Whether Najeela plus Derevi is genuinely a two-card infinite
Rules version June 19, 2026 · status: cleared in 3 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
The question put to the record

Do the tokens Najeela creates, which enter tapped and attacking, themselves trigger Najeela? (b) How many separate Derevi untap triggers do you get from first-combat damage, and can they untap enough lands to pay Najeela's {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} a second time? (c) Track the number of attacking creatures and the number of Derevi triggers through the second and third combats. Does the loop become self-sustaining, and if so on which combat? (d) Is the claim that these two cards alone form an infinite combo accurate, or does the loop depend on resources outside those two cards? State precisely what is required.
  1. Combat phase 1 begins. You control Najeela, the Blade-Blossom (3/2 Human Warrior) and Derevi, Empyrial Tactician (2/3 Bird Wizard), both untapped and controlled since before this turn, plus five untapped lands. Both opponents are at 40 with empty boards.
    The combat phase runs beginning of combat -> declare attackers -> declare blockers -> combat damage -> end of combat. Nothing is on the stack yet; the first thing that happens is the beginning of combat step.
    CR 506.1 — “The combat phase has five steps, which proceed in order: beginning of combat, declare attackers, declare blockers, combat damage, and end of combat.”
    CR 500.1 — “The beginning, combat, and ending phases are further broken down into steps, which proceed in order.”
  2. COMBAT 1 - beginning of combat step: establish who is being attacked. This is a three-player game, so a defending player must be determined before attackers are declared; the scenario stipulates you attack the first opponent (opponent A).
    Commander's default multiplayer setup is Free-for-All with the attack multiple players option, so unless the table has agreed otherwise that is the configuration in force here. A Free-for-All table must use exactly one of attack left, attack right, or attack multiple players. Under attack multiple players — the Commander default — NO turn-based choice is made in this step: every opponent is already a defending player, and you choose which player each creature attacks individually as you declare it. 506.2a's turn-based choice applies only to the other branch, and even there it is scoped: 506.2a covers the case where the attacking player chooses, and it expressly notes that the choice may instead be DICTATED by the variant or option in use — which is what attack left and attack right do (they hand you a fixed defending player, you do not pick one). So it is wrong to say 'either way the choice is a turn-based action you make'; under attack multiple players there is no such choice, and under attack left/right the choice is not yours. What IS true on every branch is that the determination is made fresh in EVERY combat phase, because each additional combat phase Najeela creates has its own beginning of combat step and its own declare attackers step. Under the Commander default that is what later licenses pointing the army at opponent B (step 22); under attack left/right the redirect in step 22 would NOT be available, because the defending player would be fixed by seating.

Both were legal choices: untapped and controlled since the turn began. Each attacker's defending player is chosen here, per creature.

  6. KEY CHECK (question a): token A does NOT trigger Najeela, even though it is a Warrior and is attacking.
    A creature put onto the battlefield attacking was never declared as an attacker, so for trigger purposes it never “attacked.” Najeela's “Whenever a Warrior attacks” therefore does not see it. It IS an attacking creature for every other purpose — it deals combat damage (step 8), it is untapped by Najeela's activated ability (step 12) and it can be declared again next combat (step 15). The tokens simply do not chain within a single combat: 1 Warrior declared -> 1 trigger -> 1 token, full stop. (This is what keeps the loop finite per combat instead of infinite inside combat 1.)

Every attacker therefore stays unblocked, and each unblocked attacker assigns its combat damage to the player it is attacking. This is the standing assumption for every combat in this line (the scenario stipulates the opponents have no blockers).

A player may activate mana abilities while paying that cost — that is precisely when a mana payment is being asked for — and those mana abilities do not use the stack, so the five mana appear in your pool and the {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} is paid in full. The important consequence of taking line L2 is that you do NOT have priority at the moment the lands are tapped: priority is only handed back after a mana ability when the player HAD priority before activating it, and during cost payment you did not. Under L2 you were paying a cost when City of Brass became tapped, so you did not have priority; 117.3c (which returns priority to a player who HAD priority when they activated an ability) applies to your activation of NAJEELA's ability, not to the land taps, and 405.6c does not hand priority back after the mana abilities either. A triggered ability waits and is put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority — here, the moment you receive priority after finishing the activation — and it becomes the topmost object on the stack. Najeela's ability is already on the stack by then, so the City of Brass trigger sits ABOVE it and resolves FIRST, dealing you 1 damage (you: 40 → 39); Najeela's ability resolves next (step 12), then the three Derevi triggers (step 13). (The alternative line L1 — tap the lands while holding priority, float the mana, then activate Najeela — is equally legal but produces the OPPOSITE stack order: the City of Brass trigger would be put on the stack beneath Najeela's ability and Najeela's ability would resolve first. Either way 1 damage is dealt and nothing downstream changes, but this resolution commits to L2 so the step order 11 → 12 → 13 is the true resolution order.) The trigger is NOT a mana ability: it cannot add mana to a mana pool, so it uses the stack like any other trigger. Every tap of City of Brass puts another copy of this trigger on the stack, including the repeat taps inside a single damage step described in step 17. Mana Confluence is the contrast case: its life payment is a cost, so it produces no stack object.

Your tokens are yours, so 800.4b's “no token is created” clause never bites. COMBAT 5: after combat 4's end of combat step every creature is removed from combat, the added combat phase begins with a fresh beginning of combat step, and at declare attackers you DECLARE 17 attackers — Najeela, Derevi and the 15 tokens, the same 17 creatures step 21 kept in combat — each attacking opponent B.

  23. Loop status and the TRUE life cost. Both triggers are optional (“you may”), so this is a loop you choose to iterate rather than a mandatory one; you shortcut it to lethal. CORRECTION TO THE NAIVE LINE: the minimum life paid per combat is 0, not 2.
    The set of actions can be repeated indefinitely and each iteration strictly increases attackers, damage and untaps, so no external resource is consumed faster than it is replenished. The only candidate drains are life: 1 damage per City of Brass tap (a triggered ability that uses the stack, once per tap — step 11) and 1 life per Mana Confluence tap (a cost). BOTH ARE AVOIDABLE ENTIRELY on the audited board of step 24. City of Brass's ability triggers only when that land becomes tapped, and Mana Confluence's life is paid only when its mana ability is activated — and activating a mana ability is never mandatory. So if you never tap those two lands, you never pay anything. You never have to: Command Tower produces one mana of any color in your commander's color identity, and this deck's commander is Sisay, Weatherlight Captain, whose color identity is all five colors, so Command Tower alone covers {B} — the one colour Plaza of Heroes cannot make. Plaza of Heroes' third mode makes one mana of any color among legendary permanents you control; the legendary permanents you control are Najeela (red) and Derevi (green/white/blue), an object being the colour of the mana symbols in its mana cost, so Plaza covers W, U, R and G with no spend restriction. The line: with the Derevi triggers on the stack, tap Plaza for {W}; pass; a Derevi trigger untaps Plaza; tap it for {U}; pass; a second trigger untaps it; tap it for {R}; pass; a third trigger untaps it; tap it for {G}; then tap Command Tower for {B}. Five taps, three untaps, {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} floating, all inside one combat damage step so the pool never empties — and neither City of Brass nor Mana Confluence was ever tapped. Combat 1 supplies EXACTLY the three Derevi triggers this needs; every later combat supplies five or more. The floor is therefore 0 life per combat, in every combat of the loop. The 2 life spent in steps 10–13 is the price of the naive tap-all-five line, not a lower bound; and even that is dwarfed by lifelink gains of 6, 8, 12 ... from combat 2 on. End state: Plaza and Command Tower tapped, City of Brass, Mana Confluence and Exotic Orchard untapped — the same 3 untapped lands the naive line leaves.

    Precise requirements: (1) five mana of five DIFFERENT colors every single combat, re-produced each time because mana pools empty each step; (2) mana permanents that Derevi's untaps can recycle — Derevi untaps one target permanent per trigger and only tapped permanents can be untapped, so the untaps are worth mana only if you control sources covering W, U, B, R and G; (3) at least 5 of your creatures connecting with a player each combat (combat 1 yields only 3 triggers, so the first activation must be pre-funded); (4) Najeela on the battlefield at each declare-attackers and Derevi on the battlefield at each combat damage step.
