FROM THE EDITOR

THE SHOPPING LICENCE

Kinnan is in your opening seven, and he is not here to double your mana. He is two colours, and two colours is thirteen more cards she can go and get.

MANA MAP
From the Editor
Vol. 3 · SISAY

Most commanders cost mana and hand you a body. This one costs mana and hands you a shopping licence.

Sisay is a 2/2 who grows by one for every colour among the other legendary permanents you control, and her ability goes and gets a legendary permanent cheaper than she is strong. So the currency this deck runs on is not cards and it is not mana. It is access — how much of your own library you are allowed to reach right now — and every other card in the 99 is either widening that number or spending it. A two-mana legend in two colours is worth more here than a mana rock, and the reason is printed on the commander rather than on the card in your hand.

Two things make this one unusual and both belong at the top. The first is the bill: her ability costs five different coloured pips with no generic in it at all, so a good deal of the acceleration in this deck cannot pay for the thing the deck is about. Ledger has what that does to a turn-four board. The second is how little of it is settled. One resolution survived the checker, and Vera spends most of the Pilot's Log marking which claims are readings rather than rulings — including one of the Coach's, which he takes well.

Play this if you enjoy a deck that plays like a search problem, where you are not drawing answers so much as buying the right to go and fetch them, and where the price of the next one falls every time you pay. Play something else if you want a commander who ends games by attacking.

Margot Stet

Start with the zone itself. CR 903.4 builds a commander's colour identity from every mana symbol in its mana cost and its rules text, and Sisay's activation — {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} — puts all five colours in hers. A {2}{W} card is your licence to sleeve ninety-nine cards in five colours. That is not a loophole. It is the rule working exactly as printed, and it is the whole reason this deck exists.

The zone's guarantee matters more here than in most decks, because the card it guarantees is a search engine rather than a threat. CR 903.8 gives you that access at a rising price: {2} more for every previous cast from the zone. Sisay runs 3, then 5, then 7, then 9 — the ladder beside this column. Note what the tax does not touch. Her power is recomputed from the battlefield, so a recast Sisay walks back onto a surviving board at full strength. Spot removal costs you the tax and a turn. A sweeper costs you the tax and every rung at once. Those are different injuries, and only the second one is worth insuring against.

The 21-damage clock, CR 903.10a, is on the record and rarely on the plan. At four colours she is a 6/6 and three connections is lethal in theory. In practice her power is a gate rather than a weapon: the deck's actual lethal output is the section called The Kill. Treat commander damage as a bonus the scoreboard occasionally pays.

Now the Coach's half of the page. Protect her; do not budget for recasting her. The simulation holds a protection effect by turn six in 79.8% of games — Shalai, Flowering of the White Tree, Plaza of Heroes' exile mode, and two free instants. A recast costs you more than mana. It costs an activation window at a table that has already seen what she does.

And the political read is fixed before your first land drop. A five-colour commander who tutors on activation gets filed as the archenemy in the pregame, and four Game Changers in the list confirm it out loud. You are not going to argue the table out of that label. You are going to schedule when you deserve it.

THE PLAN

COUNT COLOURS, NOT CARDS

Good Sisay pilots pass with all five pips available and no activation, and it looks like a misplay. The number that decides what she can find is not printed on her card — it is the count of distinct colours sitting beside her.

Five colours, a tutor commander, forty lands: the table files you as a greedy pile. Let them. She is a {2}{W} creature with no tap symbol and no once-per-turn clause; this deck's currency is access.

Read her power, never her cost: a 2/2 plus one per colour among your OTHER legendary permanents, fetching any legend cheaper than that. Zero colours, three legal targets; one, ten; two, twenty-three; four, all 33 — nothing costs more than five. The rung that matters is one to two: +13 targets, Najeela legal. Colour-dense legend first, then activate. Stop at four; the fifth buys stats, not access.

Then the bill: four cards demand {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} and only Jegantha adds it. Sol Ring is colourless, Delighted Halfling legendary spells only, Unclaimed Territory creature spells only — deploy her early off those three, then pay with the six that can: Bloom Tender, Faeburrow Elder, Jegantha, Esika, Relic of Legends, Selvala.

Stop mulliganing for the captain: turn three in 8,028 of 10,000 seeded games, turn six in 99 of 100. The gate is Sisay plus a multi-colour mana body: a little over half by six. Keep the fuel.

Removal is a shopping list, not a card in hand: one unconditional creature answer, one sweeper. The rest is tax, legendary, mana value 2 to 4: Thalia (creatures and nonbasics enter tapped), Teferi and Kutzil (turns), Kambal and Sheoldred (spells and draws). Fetch the tax that hurts the seat beating you.

Close with Najeela. She and Derevi are the one line cleared against the Comprehensive Rules — an unbounded combat loop, no life cost. It re-buys five colours a combat and wants five creatures connecting; present it when the base pays that toll.

Know the hole. Twenty-six nonland cards are not legendary, so she never sees them: three of the six that pay her, every counterspell, both free protection spells, all four Game Changers. She finds payoffs; you draw fuel.

What this does not model

  • Stack 002 measures the kill on a board where Najeela and Derevi are already down and untapped, five lands that each make any colour are open, and two opponents sit at 40 with empty boards and no blockers. That is the only board this deck's kill has ever been measured on.
  • Combat one is not free. The loop's first activation is paid out of five untapped lands held back before attackers were declared, so a turn you tapped out to fetch does not start it.
  • Three of the engine's eleven lines are verified and all three come off that one board. Every arrow out of the dial — the deck's central interaction — is drawn dashed.
  • Unsettled Re-run stack 001 (Sisay activated the turn she lands, and the escalating fetch chain) with the single -X/-X clause the checker flagged. Does the deck's central line — pay five pips, fetch a legend, recalculate power mid-resolution, fetch again — pass?
  • Unsettled Re-run stack 003 (Relic of Legends versus Esika's granted ability on a legend Sisay put onto the battlefield this turn), fixing the step-13 Esika vigilance attribution and the step-14 ceiling the checker refuted. Can a freshly fetched legend help pay for the NEXT activation, and what is the real per-turn mana ceiling?
  • Unsettled Add a win-line target and measure it: how often are Najeela plus a mana base that can produce all five colours both available, and by which turn?
  • Simulation 10,000 runs of resource development, not of games — no opponent acts in any of them.

The question put to the record
You attack with Najeela and Derevi. (a) In the first combat, how many Warriors are declared as attackers, and how many Najeela triggers result? Do the tokens Najeela creates, which enter tapped and attacking, themselves trigger Najeela? (b) How many separate Derevi untap triggers do you get from first-combat damage, and can they untap enough lands to pay Najeela's {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} a second time? (c) Track the number of attacking creatures and the number of Derevi triggers through the second and third combats. Does the loop become self-sustaining, and if so on which combat? (d) Is the claim that these two cards alone form an infinite combo accurate, or does the loop depend on resources outside those two cards? State precisely what is required.
  1. Combat phase 1 begins. You control Najeela, the Blade-Blossom (3/2 Human Warrior) and Derevi, Empyrial Tactician (2/3 Bird Wizard), both untapped and controlled since before this turn, plus five untapped lands. Both opponents are at 40 with empty boards.
    The combat phase runs beginning of combat -> declare attackers -> declare blockers -> combat damage -> end of combat. Nothing is on the stack yet; the first thing that happens is the beginning of combat step.
    CR 506.1 — “The combat phase has five steps, which proceed in order: beginning of combat, declare attackers, declare blockers, combat damage, and end of combat.”
    CR 500.1 — “The beginning, combat, and ending phases are further broken down into steps, which proceed in order.”
  2. COMBAT 1 - beginning of combat step: establish who is being attacked. This is a three-player game, so a defending player must be determined before attackers are declared; the scenario stipulates you attack the first opponent (opponent A).
    Commander's default multiplayer setup is Free-for-All with the attack multiple players option, so unless the table has agreed otherwise that is the configuration in force here. A Free-for-All table must use exactly one of attack left, attack right, or attack multiple players. Under attack multiple players — the Commander default — NO turn-based choice is made in this step: every opponent is already a defending player, and you choose which player each creature attacks individually as you declare it. 506.2a's turn-based choice applies only to the other branch, and even there it is scoped: 506.2a covers the case where the attacking player chooses, and it expressly notes that the choice may instead be DICTATED by the variant or option in use — which is what attack left and attack right do (they hand you a fixed defending player, you do not pick one). So it is wrong to say 'either way the choice is a turn-based action you make'; under attack multiple players there is no such choice, and under attack left/right the choice is not yours. What IS true on every branch is that the determination is made fresh in EVERY combat phase, because each additional combat phase Najeela creates has its own beginning of combat step and its own declare attackers step. Under the Commander default that is what later licenses pointing the army at opponent B (step 22); under attack left/right the redirect in step 22 would NOT be available, because the defending player would be fixed by seating.

    CR 806.2b — “Exactly one of the attack left, attack right, and attack multiple players options must be used.”
    CR 802.2 — “As the combat phase starts, the attacking player doesn’t choose an opponent to become the defending player. Instead, all the attacking player’s opponents are defending players during the combat phase.”
    CR 506.2a — “Unless all the attacking player’s opponents automatically become defending players during the combat phase, the attacking player chooses one of their opponents as a turn-based action during the beginning of combat step.     CR 803.1 — “Some multiplayer games use the optional attack left or attack right rules.”
    CR 506.1 — “The combat phase has five steps, which proceed in order: beginning of combat, declare attackers, declare blockers, combat damage, and end of combat.”

    Two creatures are declared as attackers. Exactly ONE of them is a Warrior: Najeela is a Human Warrior; Derevi is a Bird Wizard. Declaring them taps them (neither has vigilance). Both were legal choices: untapped and controlled since the turn began. Each attacker's defending player is chosen here, per creature.

    CR 802.3 — “As the attacking player declares each attacking creature, they choose a defending player, a planeswalker controlled by a defending player, or a battle protected by a defending player for it to attack.”

    CR 603.2c — “An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs.”
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority.”

    Token A is put onto the battlefield already tapped and attacking; since Najeela's ability does not name a defending player, you choose one as it enters — opponent A. Attacking creatures in combat 1 = 3 (Najeela, Derevi, token A). Total power on the table = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6.

    A creature put onto the battlefield attacking was never declared as an attacker, so for trigger purposes it never “attacked.” Najeela's “Whenever a Warrior attacks” therefore does not see it. It IS an attacking creature for every other purpose — it deals combat damage (step 8), it is untapped by Najeela's activated ability (step 12) and it can be declared again next combat (step 15).
