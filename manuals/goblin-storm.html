FROM THE EDITOR

TWO DECKS, TAKING TURNS

Volume one, and the deck on the cover is two decks sharing a mana base. One of them is built entirely around the commander; the other has never once asked for her.

MANA MAP
From the Editor
Vol. 1 · GOBLIN STORM

The deck on this cover is a Goblin deck the way a bonfire is a woodpile.

Zada, Hedron Grinder does exactly one thing. A spell that points at her and at nothing else is copied once for every other creature you control — so the Goblins are not a board, they are an exchange rate, and every cheap red spell in the ninety-nine is priced in them. The other half of the deck is a storm deck that never asked for her at all, and the two halves take turns being the game.

You will have a good time here if you enjoy arithmetic done fast and in public: counting casts, counting bodies, counting how much damage three separate seats are going to take, and living with the fact that getting one of those counts wrong ends your turn tapped out with everybody alive. You will not, if you want a board that frightens people. This is a pile of 1/1s until the turn it is not, and trying to look dangerous before then is how you lose the turn you were saving them for.

Two arguments in the log are worth the cover price. Coach Brightside opens by naming the two cards he thinks this deck is really bounded by, and Ledger has to tell him nobody has ever measured them. And there is a famous infinite combo inside these ninety-nine that is not infinite here — the Counselor has the reason, and it turns out she has two.

Read the log before you shuffle.

Start with the guarantee, because Commander gives you exactly one. CR 903.6 puts your commander face up in the command zone before a card is shuffled, and CR 903.8 lets you cast her from there — so Zada, Hedron Grinder is the only card in this issue you are never required to find. The schedule holds up: mean cast turn 4.352, median 4, exactly turn four in 7,002 of 10,000 goldfish runs, 90% by turn six. That is why your opening seven is allowed to be all fuel and no finisher.

Now read the bill. CR 903.8 charges an additional {2} for each previous cast from the command zone: four, then six, then eight. In a deck whose modal spell costs two and whose mean available mana on turn five is 4.589, a six-mana recast is three spells you did not cast — and spells cast is the only currency a storm count trades in. So the Counselor's finding and the Coach's instruction land on the same square. Buy protection on a quiet turn, where Swiftfoot Boots equips for {1}. Stop buying her back on a loud one.

Here is the part that makes this deck unusual at a Commander table: guaranteed access is not the same as required access. One of the three assembly targets the simulator tracks is gated on her, and of the five case files at the back of the book only A-001 and A-003 put her on the battlefield — A-002 kills through Impact Tremors, A-005 loops off Storm-Kiln Artist, and A-004 records her sitting in the zone for the whole turn. Colour identity keeps that honest: CR 903.4 reads only red off her cost and text, so the 99 holds no counterspell and exactly one answer to a resolved enchantment (Chaos Warp), and every slot the identity refuses buys another one-mana spell instead. As for CR 903.10a's 21-damage clock, a 3/3 needs seven clean connections — a coincidence, never a plan. Your clock is the wide board and the each-opponent effects. The table's clock is your turn-four window, and every opponent who can count Mountains has it circled. Let them watch the window. The deck does not live in it.

THE PLAN

CHUMP BLOCKERS, ALLEGEDLY

A table of veterans will look at your 1/1s and point its removal somewhere else. Take the gift — here's the game you're actually playing, in five moves.

The table counts your 1/1s, files you under aggro-that-runs-out-of-gas, and points its removal somewhere else. Take the gift. Every body you land is a card Zada, Hedron Grinder cashes later: an instant or sorcery targeting only her is copied once for each other creature you control, so one red mana across four Goblins is five cards and a hasted team (stack 001). Build the board first. Hold the cantrip second.

She is not the deck, and most pilots get that backwards. Of the five verified lines here only two put her on the battlefield: 002 runs off Impact Tremors, 005 off Storm-Kiln Artist, and 004 states outright that she is in the command zone while the turn happens. One of the three engine targets wants her; the other two do not. So when she eats a removal spell, do not rebuy her and do not pass the turn sulking. Keep casting.

Your ramp is the swarm, not your lands. Battle Hymn adds {R} for each creature you control, Brightstone Ritual adds {R} for each Goblin on the battlefield, and Skirk Prospector turns any spare token into red at instant speed. The goldfish has you at 5.182 mana on turn six — an ordinary curve. On the big turn, count Goblins, not Mountains.

Pick your exit before you cast the first spell; you have three and they are not interchangeable. Impact Tremors turns eight storm tokens into 8 damage to each opponent with no attack step (stack 002). Fists of Flame turns the same board into trample and takes a seat off 30 in one swing (stack 003). Haze of Rage with Storm-Kiln Artist simply does not stop (stack 005) — and it hits nobody by itself, so it is fuel, not a kill. A storm payoff and a ritual arrive together in 26.5% of games at a mean turn of 4.91, so when that hand shows up, know which of the first two ends the game before you tap anything.

What this does not model

  • Stack 003 is the deck's only measured kill and it kills ONE seat: 32 trample power into a single opponent at 30, with a lone 2/2 absorbing two of it. Exactly 30 into 30, and no margin in it.
  • Stack 002's '8 to each opponent' is measured against two seats at 32 and 28 with empty boards. A full pod is three players on 40 — 120 life against a turn that deals 8.
  • Neither board holds a blocker that matters, an instant, or an opponent who takes an action for a full turn cycle.
  • Unsettled Can a stack scenario verify the Empty the Warrens -> Impact Tremors arrow under the citation contract? Stack 002 resolves it (eight tokens, eight damage to each opponent) but names Impact Tremors only on the battlefield, and `line_cards` skips the board when the stack, hand and graveyard already name two or more cards — so the deck's headline kill has no citable link between the token flood and the damage.
  • Unsettled With Goblin chosen, does Roaming Throne's 'triggers an additional time' clause apply to Zada's copy trigger — and if it does, how many copies result: 2N, or N doubled with each set choosing targets separately?
  • Unsettled Does Frontline Heroism's copy clause behave as the strategic frame describes it — a second copy hub? Read literally it creates one Soldier and copies the spell once, at that token, regardless of board width, which is one extra copy rather than one per creature.
  • Simulation 10,000 runs of resource development, not of games — no opponent acts in any of them.

  • LANDS ENTER UNTAPPED, ALWAYS, and the one in hand longest is the one played.   • Mana rocks ('{T}: Add') contribute from the turn after they are cast.
  • Commander cast on first affordable turn (highest spending priority).
  • Bodies count = creatures cast + tokens parsed from 'create ... token' text.
  • Target assembly counts cards DRAWN by a turn (cast cards still count).
  • CARD DRAW IS ONE A TURN UNLESS `model_draw` IS SET. With the flag on, four channels are modelled — a card's own ETB draw, an instant or sorcery that draws, an upkeep trigger, and an ARRIVAL trigger that draws when other creatures or tokens enter (Welcoming Vampire, Caretaker's Talent), which rides the same single door the ETB damage payoffs use. Activated draw (needs a spending policy this model has no opinion on), X-based draw (board-dependent), and death- or attack-triggered draw (no deaths, and attacks only under `model_combat`) are NOT modelled and the cards are named in `meta.card_advantage.draw_not_modelled`. A conditional trigger — 'if you control an artifact', 'you may draw' — is treated as unmodelled rather than assumed to be on: 39 of the 348 ETB-draw cards in the corpus carry one.

Welcoming Vampire is a 2/3 that draws 'whenever one or more OTHER creatures you control with power 2 or less enter'; registered before its own entry it passed its own gate and drew a card it does not draw, overstating edgar-vampires by 22% at turn eight. The deferral applies to every card and not only the ones worded 'other', which understates Tocasia's Welcome and two relatives by one draw on the turn they land.

The channel rides the one door onto the battlefield, which `model_combat` also uses to spawn token copies and ETB-payoff tokens — those are real additional arrivals that do not exist in the resource-only model. Measured on edgar-vampires: 1.264 extra cards by turn ten with both flags against 1.106 with draw alone. Before 2026-08-28 the gap was 1.264 against 0.323, because every call to that door sat inside `if model_combat:` and a draw-only deck silently lost three quarters of its arrival draws.
  • HELD-UP INTERACTION is reported as two series and both are FLOORS. This model casts everything it can afford every turn, so `interaction_castable` is what a pilot developing at full speed has left over, not what a pilot choosing to hold up two mana would have. A low figure is therefore a real finding and a high one is unambiguous good news.
  • Tutors are modeled as wildcards: a CAST tutor that fetches to hand or the top — including a TYPED one (Worldly Tutor, Sarkhan's Triumph), which the old literal 'a card' match missed entirely — fills ONE missing any_of group. Consumed once, mana paid, and a tutor that puts the card on top of the library costs a turn. Reported as the *_assisted figures; the unassisted figures beside them exclude tutors entirely.

Every arrival fires them — cast, token or copy — and a copy is itself an arrival, so they compound, which is the deck working rather than a bug. A payoff worded 'another NONTOKEN' does not re-trigger on its own tokens, which is the brake the rules already had. A chain is capped at 12 deep. X counts the whole board rather than one subtype: exact where the creatures are Dragons, generous otherwise.

That understates every deck, but by an amount proportional to how much draw it runs — so it is conservative WITHIN a deck and not neutral BETWEEN them. `meta.card_advantage` reports how much of each list this hides.
