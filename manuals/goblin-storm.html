THE PLAN

CHUMP BLOCKERS, ALLEGEDLY

Why does a table full of veterans ignore a board of 1/1 Goblins? Because they haven't met the one that's a library in a trench coat — here's the game you're actually playing.

Everything starts with bodies, and you are never short of bodies. Dragon Fodder, Krenko's Command, Mogg War Marshal, Empty the Warrens, Siege-Gang Commander, and the attack-trigger crew — General Kreat, Searslicer Goblin, Howlsquad Heavy — flood the table fast, and Krenko, Mob Boss doubles the count every time he taps. Know exactly what Krenko is: verified line 004 shows that under Goblin Warchief he costs {1}{R}{R}, activates the turn he lands, and turns five Goblins into ten — but the database's promised infinite Krenko/Prospector loop does not exist in this deck, because Krenko is not your commander and stays tapped after his one activation. He is a burst, and a very good one — play him like it.

A wide board is not just an army here; it is the multiplier on everything else, and Zada, Hedron Grinder is the machine doing the multiplying. Line 001 shows the verified floor: one Crimson Wisps with four Goblin tokens out draws you five cards and hastes the team for one red mana. Expedite, Renegade Tactics, Ancestors' Aid, and Fists of Flame all fit the same slot, and line 003 proves that slot's ceiling — Fists of Flame copies resolving in sequence, each drawing before it buffs, add up to 32 power of trample off a board of 1/1s. Storm-Kiln Artist gets paid a Treasure for every spell you cast or copy, and Reliquary Tower lets you keep everything you drew.

The mana comes from rituals that scale with your board and the table: Battle Hymn, Brightstone Ritual, Skirk Prospector (verified in line 004 as a true mana ability — no stack, no responses, usable mid-payment), Mana Geyser, and Seething Song, discounted by Ruby Medallion and Goblin Warchief. Line 002 gives you the storm math to spend it on: copies are put on the stack, not cast, so an Empty the Warrens as your fourth spell makes eight Goblins and eight Impact Tremors damage to each opponent, while a later Grapeshot counts only your real casts. Count your casts, not your resolutions.

The ceiling is a verified true infinite. Line 005 proves that Haze of Rage with buyback plus Storm-Kiln Artist goes mana-positive once three spells have been cast — each recast returns the card to hand, mints more Treasures than the next cast costs, and pumps the whole team another +1/+0 per copy, without bound; two casts already leave nine Treasures and a 20/2 Storm-Kiln Artist. From there you assemble the kill yourself: trample via Fists of Flame, tokens-into-Tremors via Empty the Warrens off the Treasure mana, or a Grapeshot riding the storm count the loop keeps raising. And if the first wave breaks early, you rebuild — Past in Flames and Faithless Looting recycle the chain, Goblin Dark-Dwellers rebuys a cheap spell, and Goblin Matron and Moggcatcher find the missing piece.

NOBODY FEARS A 1/1

Your commander telegraphs turn four to anyone who can count Mountains. Your board tells a different story — here's how to keep three opponents reading the wrong one.

The table reads your clock, so read it better. Zada, Hedron Grinder lands on turn 4 in 70% of games — 7,002 of 10,000 goldfish runs cast her exactly on 4 (mean 4.35, median 4, 90% by turn 6). Anyone who has seen this deck counts your Mountains and circles the turn-4 window. Good — let them stare at the window while you build behind it.

What they can't read is your board. On turn 3 you average just 2.36 bodies — a Skirk Prospector and a couple of 1/1 tokens look like chump blockers, not the fuel tank of a draw engine. That gap between what your board says and what your hand is building is your entire early game. Protect it.

The flip from ignored to archenemy happens at two exact thresholds, and you know both cold. One: Zada untapped, three-plus other creatures, one red mana open — verified stack 001 turns a single {R} Crimson Wisps into five cards and a hasted board. Two: the verified infinite.

Stack 005 proves Haze of Rage with buyback plus Storm-Kiln Artist goes mana-positive once about three spells have been cast that turn. Iteration k costs four mana and pays k+3 Treasures plus a team-wide +1/+0 — the loop strictly improves. In the verified run, two casts made 9 Treasures, three 10/1 Goblins, and a 20/2 Storm-Kiln.

So the moment Storm-Kiln Artist sits on your battlefield with four mana up and Haze in hand, informed opponents must respect lethal. Their counterplay is scripted: kill Storm-Kiln in response to the first magecraft trigger, or hit the Treasures with instant-speed artifact hate. Treat the first Haze cast as the commitment point. Fire when the table's interaction is tapped or spent — that's not caution, that's timing.

Your other tells are simple. Skullclamp screams engine deck. Conspicuous Snoop shows the table your library top all game. Price both in before you play them.

Sequencing under the radar is a script, and you own it. Deploy token makers early — Dragon Fodder and Krenko's Command read as defense, and the goldfish puts your early token maker at turn 3.18 on average, comfortably before Zada. Cast Zada as 'just a 3/3' and pass.

The full engine assembles at turn 5.27 on average and only 49.6% of the time by turn 6, so you usually get one quiet turn. Spend it wisely. Never fire a cantrip at Zada for two copies of value.

Know what Krenko, Mob Boss is here, because the table won't. The combo databases call Krenko plus Prospector plus a haste lord infinite; stack 004 verifies it is not a loop in this deck. Krenko is not your commander, he stays tapped after one activation, and a Krenko sacrificed to Prospector is simply gone.

Play him as a one-activation payoff: {1}{R}{R} under Warchief, five tokens on a modest board, ten Goblins and 13 hasted power. Never sacrifice him expecting recursion, and never burn protection defending a loop you don't have. When an opponent panic-removes Krenko expecting the Spellbook infinite, they've paid a real card for a one-shot — sometimes you let them.

Embrace being the threat only when the turn converts. Pace for the median game — the Zada draw engine plus wide pressure — and hold the ceiling as opportunism. The go-decision is arithmetic done before the first spell: count the mana and castable spells in your hand and on your board only; expected draws are a bonus, not a plan.

The go signs: Impact Tremors or Boggart Shenanigans down, Krenko untapped with his partners, a Spinerock Knoll set, or the Haze loop live. The goldfish assembles 'storm payoff plus ritual' in only 15.2% of games, so let the table's fear sit on cards you haven't drawn. The turn you draw five cards in front of three opponents, you are the archenemy — make it the turn the table has to answer you or start dying.

Then triggered abilities are put on the stack (see rule 603, “Handling Triggered Abilities”).”
  5. The 3 magecraft triggers resolve one at a time (top of stack first), each creating a Treasure token.
    Treasures 1-3 enter the battlefield. Each is a colorless artifact token, immediately increasing Storm-Kiln Artist's power via its static +1/+0-per-artifact ability.
    CR 405.5 — “When all players pass in succession, the top (last-added) spell or ability on the stack resolves.”
    CR 111.10a — “A Treasure token is a colorless Treasure artifact token with “{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color.””
  6. The 3 storm copies resolve one at a time. Each gives your creatures +1/+0 until end of turn; each copy's buyback replacement redirects it toward your hand instead of the graveyard, but as a copy it ceases to exist by state-based action before any player receives priority.
    Three separate +1/+0-until-end-of-turn effects apply to all creatures you control. No copy ever becomes a card in your hand — only the original card can return. Running pump total: +3/+0 per creature.
    CR 702.27a — “If the buyback cost was paid, put this spell into its owner’s hand instead of into that player’s graveyard as it resolves.”
    CR 707.10 — “A copy of a spell is itself a spell, even though it has no spell card associated with it.”
    CR 704.5e — “If a copy of a spell is in a zone other than the stack, it ceases to exist.”
    CR 405.6f — “They are dealt with before a player would receive priority.”
  7. The cast's own magecraft trigger resolves: Treasure 4 is created.
    You now control 4 Treasures. Cast #1 magecraft accounting: 1 (cast) + 3 (copies) = 4 Treasures.
    CR 111.10a — “A Treasure token is a colorless Treasure artifact token with “{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color.””
  8. The original Haze of Rage resolves: creatures you control get +1/+0 until end of turn (4th pump), and buyback puts the actual card into your hand instead of your graveyard.
    Cast #1 complete: your creatures have +4/+0 from four independent until-end-of-turn effects, 4 Treasures on the battlefield, Haze of Rage back in hand. Each pump lasts until end of turn as a continuous effect from a resolved spell.
    CR 702.27a — “If the buyback cost was paid, put this spell into its owner’s hand instead of into that player’s graveyard as it resolves.”
    CR 611.2a — “A continuous effect generated by the resolution of a spell or ability lasts as long as stated by the spell or ability creating it (such as “until end of turn”).”
  9. Cast #2: you tap all 4 Mountains for {R}{R}{R}{R} and cast Haze of Rage from your hand paying {1}{R} plus the buyback additional cost {2} (4 mana total). This is the 5th spell cast this turn.
    Haze of Rage is on the stack again with buyback paid. Storm and magecraft trigger again. Storm counts actual casts only: 4 other spells were cast before it (the 3 earlier spells + cast #1); the 3 storm copies from cast #1 were never cast and add nothing.
    CR 702.27a — “You may pay an additional [cost] as you cast this spell”
    CR 707.10 — “a copy of a spell isn’t cast and a copy of an activated ability isn’t activated”
  10. Cast #2 resolves exactly as cast #1 did: storm makes 4 copies; magecraft fires 5 times (1 cast + 4 copies) creating Treasures 5-9; the 4 copies and the original each give +1/+0 until end of turn (5 more pumps); the copies cease to exist as state-based actions; the original card returns to your hand via buyback.
    Cast #2 totals: 4 storm copies, 5 Treasures, +5/+0 per creature. Combined with cast #1: 9 pump instances (+9/+0 per creature), 9 Treasures, Haze of Rage in hand, all 4 Mountains tapped.
    CR 702.40a — “copy it for each other spell that was cast before it this turn”
    CR 704.5e — “If a copy of a spell is in a zone other than the stack, it ceases to exist.”
    CR 702.27a — “If the buyback cost was paid, put this spell into its owner’s hand instead of into that player’s graveyard as it resolves.”
  11. Mana/loop accounting: each Treasure carries the activated mana ability '{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color', which resolves immediately without using the stack, so the next cast (#3) is fully payable by sacrificing 4 of your 9 Treasures.
    The loop is mana-positive and strictly improving: iteration k is the (k+3)th spell of the turn, costs 4 mana, and yields k+3 Treasures (net k-1) plus k+2 copies and 1 cast worth of pumps. Cast #3 nets +2 Treasures, cast #4 nets +3, and so on — repeatable arbitrarily many times with unbounded Treasures, mana, storm count, magecraft triggers, and team power, confirming the combo-graph claim.
    CR 111.10a — “A Treasure token is a colorless Treasure artifact token with “{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color.””
    CR 605.3b — “An activated mana ability doesn’t go on the stack, so it can’t be targeted, countered, or otherwise responded to. Rather, it resolves immediately after it is activated.”
  12. Final power calculation: the nine +1/+0 effects and Storm-Kiln Artist's per-artifact bonus all apply cumulatively in layer 7c (none sets power to a value, so they simply sum).
    Each Goblin token: 1 + 9 = 10/1. Storm-Kiln Artist: base 2, +9 from Haze pumps, +9 from its static '+1/+0 for each artifact you control' (9 Treasures) = 20/2. Team power 50, up from 5 — a total gain of +45 (+36 from the nine pumps across 4 creatures, +9 from the artifact bonus). Note the artifact bonus is a static ability that recounts artifacts continuously: sacrificing Treasures for cast #3 would lower Storm-Kiln's power by 1 per Treasure spent.
