Begin with the clause the whole deck is drafted around, CR 903.4: your commander is the only card you always have. In most decks that guarantee is a luxury; here it is load-bearing. Every cantrip, every ritual count, every mulligan decision in this issue assumes Zada, Hedron Grinder arrives on schedule — and the record supports the assumption: mean cast turn 4.352, median 4, cast exactly on turn 4 in 7,002 of 10,000 goldfish runs. The guarantee inverts your mulligan logic: an opening hand never needs a payoff, because the payoff is filed in the command zone.

Now the tax schedule, CR 903.8: two generic more for each previous cast from the command zone. First cast four; second, six; third, eight. Read those numbers against this deck's curve and the ruling writes itself — in a 99 where almost everything costs one or two, a six-mana recast is three storm counts that never happened, on a turn when mean available mana is 4.589. Recasting is a compounding cost, not a plan, so protection is the better spend: Swiftfoot Boots equips for one, and one mana is a fifth of a storm turn. The Coach's corollary: boot her up the turn before you fire, not the turn you fire.

Color identity, CR 903.4 again: Zada's cost and text carry only red, so the 99 is mono-red — no counterspells, and exactly one clean answer to a resolved enchantment (Chaos Warp, which shuffles anything). What the identity buys in exchange is the entire engine: the ritual suite, the copy cantrips, and a commander whose text turns both into card advantage. As for the parallel clock, CR 903.10a's 21 commander damage would take seven clean connections from a 3/3 — in this deck it is a coincidence, never a plan. Your real clock is the wide board and the each-opponent effects; the table's is your turn-4 window. Everyone at the pod can count Mountains. Let them watch the window while you build behind it.

THE PLAN

CHUMP BLOCKERS, ALLEGEDLY

Why does a table full of veterans ignore a board of 1/1 Goblins? Because they haven't met the one that's a library in a trench coat — here's the game you're actually playing.

The table looks at a pile of 1/1 Goblins and files you under "aggro that runs out of gas." Wrong file. Every body you land is a card Zada, Hedron Grinder cashes later: a spell that targets only her gets copied once for each other creature you control, so one red mana on four bodies is five cards (stack 001). Build the board first, hold the cantrip second.

Bodies are the one thing you are never short of. Dragon Fodder, Krenko's Command, Mogg War Marshal, Empty the Warrens and Siege-Gang Commander flood the table, and General Kreat, Searslicer Goblin and Howlsquad Heavy keep adding a token every combat. Krenko, Mob Boss doubles whatever you have — once. His ability taps, nothing here untaps him, and he is not your commander, so a Krenko in the graveyard stays there (stack 004): play him for the burst and never feed him to Skirk Prospector.

Your ramp is the swarm. Battle Hymn pays {R} per creature you control, Brightstone Ritual pays {R} per Goblin on the battlefield, and Skirk Prospector converts any spare token into red mana with nothing on the stack for anyone to answer. The goldfish has you at 5.18 mana on turn six, which is a fine ordinary curve and would never storm anybody out — so stop waiting on lands for your big turn and start counting Goblins.

Then pick your exit before you fire, because you have three and they are not interchangeable. Impact Tremors turns the swarm into damage no blocker touches: eight tokens is 8 to each opponent before you declare an attack (stack 002). Fists of Flame turns the same board into trample and takes a player off 30 in one swing (stack 003). And Haze of Rage with Storm-Kiln Artist simply does not stop (stack 005) — but the loop hits nobody on its own, so decide which of the other two ends the game before you start it.

The engine — Zada and her ammunition

Zada, Hedron Grinder

Copy + ETB

Every table reads her as a four-mana 3/3 and lets her live — take the gift. Any instant or sorcery aimed at only her is copied once per other creature you control, so cast your cantrip on the turn you have bodies, not the turn you draw it.

Crimson Wisps

One mana, one target, one card — and on four bodies it is five cards and a hasted team (stack 001). Hold it until the board is wide; it costs the same either way.

The same one-mana blueprint: haste plus a card. Point it at Zada and everything you made this turn can attack this turn.

Renegade Tactics

A one-mana cantrip that also says "can't block." Copied across the board it strips the table's blockers and refills your hand at once — fire it precombat, every time.

Discard a card, draw two, and hang a Wicked Role on one of your creatures. It is your cheapest way to bin Past in Flames or Wild Ride for a later flashback — treat whether Zada copies it as an open question, not a plan.

Fists of Flame

Counterspell + DrawDraw + Discard

Your combat finisher: each copy draws, then locks in +1/+0 per card drawn this turn, and everything gains trample (stack 003). Do the arithmetic before you cast — the buffs land ascending and a lone blocker absorbs almost nothing.

    CR 903.9a — “If a commander is in a graveyard or in exile and that object was put into that zone since the last time state-based actions were checked, its owner may put it into the command zone.”
    CR 701.26a — “Only untapped permanents can be tapped.”
    CR 701.21a — “To sacrifice a permanent, its controller moves it from the battlefield directly to its owner’s graveyard.”

Case A-005 Haze of Rage buyback + Storm-Kiln Artist: the self-sustaining storm loop
Rules version June 19, 2026 · status: cleared in 1 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
The question put to the record
Resolve TWO consecutive buyback casts of Haze of Rage: (a) per cast, how many storm copies go on the stack and how many Treasures does Storm-Kiln Artist's magecraft create (count casts vs copies precisely), (b) does buyback return the storm copies to hand or only the original card, and what happens to the copies, (c) track the mana: is the loop mana-positive after this second cast, and does each further iteration get strictly better — i.e. is it repeatable arbitrarily many times, (d) how much power has your team gained in total after both casts resolve, and what is Storm-Kiln Artist's own power given its artifact bonus and the Treasures on board?
  1. Haze of Rage (cast #1, buyback paid, 4th spell this turn) has been cast; its storm ability and Storm-Kiln Artist's magecraft ability both trigger.
    Two triggered abilities you control await placement: storm (from the spell) and magecraft (from the cast event). Magecraft is an ability word with no rules meaning; the trigger is exactly its printed text 'Whenever you cast or copy an instant or sorcery spell, create a Treasure token.'
    CR 702.40a — “Storm is a triggered ability that functions on the stack.”
    CR 207.2c — “Ability words are similar to keywords in that they tie together cards that have similar functionality, but they have no special rules meaning and no individual entries in the Comprehensive Rules.”
  2. You put both triggers on the stack in an order you choose: magecraft first, storm on top.
    Storm will resolve before the cast's magecraft trigger. Either order reaches the same end state since neither trigger affects the other.
    CR 603.3b — “each player, in APNAP order, puts each triggered ability they control with a trigger condition that isn’t another ability triggering on the stack in any order they choose”
  3. Storm trigger resolves: 3 spells were cast before Haze of Rage this turn, so 3 copies are put on the stack.
    Three copies of Haze of Rage go on the stack. Each copy retains the buyback choice (additional costs are among the copied decisions). The copies are NOT cast, so they will not themselves trigger storm and will not raise the storm count of later casts.
    CR 702.40a — “copy it for each other spell that was cast before it this turn”
    CR 707.10 — “a copy of a spell isn’t cast and a copy of an activated ability isn’t activated”
    CR 707.10 — “A copy of a spell or ability copies both the characteristics of the spell or ability and all decisions made for it, including modes, targets, the value of X, and additional or alternative costs.”
  4. Creating the 3 copies triggers Storm-Kiln Artist's magecraft 3 more times ('cast or copy'); before you receive priority, those triggers are put on the stack above the copies.
    Three magecraft triggers sit on top of the three copies. Each copy is a real spell even without a card, satisfying 'copy an instant or sorcery spell.'
    CR 112.1a — “A copy of a spell is also a spell, even if it has no card associated with it.”
    CR 117.5 — “Each time a player would get priority, the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event (see rule 704, “State-Based Actions”), then repeats this process until no state-based actions are performed. Then triggered abilities are put on the stack (see rule 603, “Handling Triggered Abilities”).”
  5. The 3 magecraft triggers resolve one at a time (top of stack first), each creating a Treasure token.
    Treasures 1-3 enter the battlefield. Each is a colorless artifact token, immediately increasing Storm-Kiln Artist's power via its static +1/+0-per-artifact ability.
    CR 405.5 — “When all players pass in succession, the top (last-added) spell or ability on the stack resolves.”
    CR 111.10a — “A Treasure token is a colorless Treasure artifact token with “{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color.””
  6. The 3 storm copies resolve one at a time. Each gives your creatures +1/+0 until end of turn; each copy's buyback replacement redirects it toward your hand instead of the graveyard, but as a copy it ceases to exist by state-based action before any player receives priority.
    Three separate +1/+0-until-end-of-turn effects apply to all creatures you control. No copy ever becomes a card in your hand — only the original card can return. Running pump total: +3/+0 per creature.
    CR 702.27a — “If the buyback cost was paid, put this spell into its owner’s hand instead of into that player’s graveyard as it resolves.”
    CR 707.10 — “A copy of a spell is itself a spell, even though it has no spell card associated with it.”
    CR 704.5e — “If a copy of a spell is in a zone other than the stack, it ceases to exist.”
    CR 405.6f — “They are dealt with before a player would receive priority.”
  7. The cast's own magecraft trigger resolves: Treasure 4 is created.
    You now control 4 Treasures. Cast #1 magecraft accounting: 1 (cast) + 3 (copies) = 4 Treasures.
    CR 111.10a — “A Treasure token is a colorless Treasure artifact token with “{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color.””
  8. The original Haze of Rage resolves: creatures you control get +1/+0 until end of turn (4th pump), and buyback puts the actual card into your hand instead of your graveyard.
    Cast #1 complete: your creatures have +4/+0 from four independent until-end-of-turn effects, 4 Treasures on the battlefield, Haze of Rage back in hand. Each pump lasts until end of turn as a continuous effect from a resolved spell.
    CR 702.27a — “If the buyback cost was paid, put this spell into its owner’s hand instead of into that player’s graveyard as it resolves.”
    CR 611.2a — “A continuous effect generated by the resolution of a spell or ability lasts as long as stated by the spell or ability creating it (such as “until end of turn”).”
  9. Cast #2: you tap all 4 Mountains for {R}{R}{R}{R} and cast Haze of Rage from your hand paying {1}{R} plus the buyback additional cost {2} (4 mana total). This is the 5th spell cast this turn.
    Haze of Rage is on the stack again with buyback paid. Storm and magecraft trigger again. Storm counts actual casts only: 4 other spells were cast before it (the 3 earlier spells + cast #1); the 3 storm copies from cast #1 were never cast and add nothing.
    CR 702.27a — “You may pay an additional [cost] as you cast this spell”
    CR 707.10 — “a copy of a spell isn’t cast and a copy of an activated ability isn’t activated”
  10. Cast #2 resolves exactly as cast #1 did: storm makes 4 copies; magecraft fires 5 times (1 cast + 4 copies) creating Treasures 5-9; the 4 copies and the original each give +1/+0 until end of turn (5 more pumps); the copies cease to exist as state-based actions; the original card returns to your hand via buyback.
    Cast #2 totals: 4 storm copies, 5 Treasures, +5/+0 per creature. Combined with cast #1: 9 pump instances (+9/+0 per creature), 9 Treasures, Haze of Rage in hand, all 4 Mountains tapped.
    CR 702.40a — “copy it for each other spell that was cast before it this turn”
    CR 704.5e — “If a copy of a spell is in a zone other than the stack, it ceases to exist.”
    CR 702.27a — “If the buyback cost was paid, put this spell into its owner’s hand instead of into that player’s graveyard as it resolves.”
  11. Mana/loop accounting: each Treasure carries the activated mana ability '{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color', which resolves immediately without using the stack, so the next cast (#3) is fully payable by sacrificing 4 of your 9 Treasures.
    The loop is mana-positive and strictly improving: iteration k is the (k+3)th spell of the turn, costs 4 mana, and yields k+3 Treasures (net k-1) plus k+2 copies and 1 cast worth of pumps. Cast #3 nets +2 Treasures, cast #4 nets +3, and so on — repeatable arbitrarily many times with unbounded Treasures, mana, storm count, magecraft triggers, and team power, confirming the combo-graph claim.
    CR 111.10a — “A Treasure token is a colorless Treasure artifact token with “{T}, Sacrifice this token: Add one mana of any color.””
    CR 605.3b — “An activated mana ability doesn’t go on the stack, so it can’t be targeted, countered, or otherwise responded to. Rather, it resolves immediately after it is activated.”
  12. Final power calculation: the nine +1/+0 effects and Storm-Kiln Artist's per-artifact bonus all apply cumulatively in layer 7c (none sets power to a value, so they simply sum).
    Each Goblin token: 1 + 9 = 10/1. Storm-Kiln Artist: base 2, +9 from Haze pumps, +9 from its static '+1/+0 for each artifact you control' (9 Treasures) = 20/2. Team power 50, up from 5 — a total gain of +45 (+36 from the nine pumps across 4 creatures, +9 from the artifact bonus). Note the artifact bonus is a static ability that recounts artifacts continuously: sacrificing Treasures for cast #3 would lower Storm-Kiln's power by 1 per Treasure spent.
    CR 613.4c — “Layer 7c: Effects and counters that modify power and/or toughness (but don’t set power and/or toughness to a specific number or value) are applied.”
    CR 611.2a — “A continuous effect generated by the resolution of a spell or ability lasts as long as stated by the spell or ability creating it (such as “until end of turn”).”
