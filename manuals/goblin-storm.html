Zada, Hedron Grinder

One spell in, five spells out — a goblin horde that casts itself.

Zada, Hedron Grinder turns every cheap spell that targets only her into a copy for each other creature you control — so a one-mana cantrip aimed at your commander becomes a fistful of cards when the Goblins are out in force. You flood the board with token makers, fuel monster turns with rituals like Battle Hymn and Brightstone Ritual, and convert the frenzy into damage through Impact Tremors, storm payoffs like Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens, and sacrifice engines like Goblin Bombardment. It plays like a tribal aggro deck that suddenly combusts into a storm deck.

How the Deck Wins

Everything starts with bodies. Dragon Fodder, Krenko's Command, Mogg War Marshal, Empty the Warrens, Siege-Gang Commander, and the attack-trigger crew (General Kreat, Searslicer Goblin, Howlsquad Heavy) put Goblins on the table fast, and Krenko, Mob Boss doubles the count every time he taps. A wide board is not just an army here — it is the multiplier on everything else you do.

With the horde assembled, Zada, Hedron Grinder converts your cheapest spells into avalanches. Her ability copies any instant or sorcery that targets only her, once for each other creature you control, with each copy aimed at a different creature. The verified line in this manual shows the shape of it: a single Crimson Wisps with four Goblin tokens out draws you five cards and gives the whole team haste for one red mana. Expedite, Renegade Tactics, Ancestors' Aid, and Fists of Flame all fit the same slot — one target, so they all qualify for Zada's trigger. Storm-Kiln Artist gets paid for every spell you cast or copy, so a Zada turn also mints a pile of Treasures, and Reliquary Tower lets you keep everything you drew.

The mana to chain it all comes from rituals that scale with your board and the table: Battle Hymn (a red per creature you control), Brightstone Ritual (a red per Goblin on the battlefield), Skirk Prospector (Goblins are mana), Mana Geyser, and Seething Song, discounted along the way by Ruby Medallion and Goblin Warchief. The turn ends in one of a few ways: a storm payoff — Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens grow with every spell cast before them in the turn — a mass-haste alpha strike sharpened by Goblin Chieftain, Quest for the Goblin Lord, and Goblin Bushwhacker, or attrition damage as Impact Tremors, General Kreat, and Boggart Shenanigans bleed opponents for every body that enters or dies. Goblin Bombardment and Pashalik Mons make sure even a board wipe costs your opponents life.

If the first wave fails, the deck rebuilds. Past in Flames and Faithless Looting recycle your spell chain, Goblin Dark-Dwellers rebuys a cheap sorcery or instant from the graveyard, and Goblin Matron and Moggcatcher find exactly the Goblin the situation calls for.

Crimson Wisps on Zada with four Goblins out ✓ verified · 1 iteration(s)

This is the deck's signature trick, verified step by step against the comprehensive rules (June 19, 2026 update). The scene: Zada, Hedron Grinder and four Goblin tokens on your battlefield, one red mana open, Crimson Wisps in hand. You cast Crimson Wisps targeting only Zada. Her ability triggers and, on resolution, creates one copy of the spell for each other creature you control — all four tokens are legal targets, so four copies hit the stack, each aimed at a different Goblin. Two rules details matter and are worth memorizing: the copies are put on the stack, not cast, so Zada does not re-trigger and they add nothing to your storm count; and each copy resolves in full, delivering the draw as well as the buff. The final tally for one mana and one card: five cards drawn, and all five of your creatures are red with haste until end of turn. Every one-target cantrip in the deck follows this same blueprint.

Where you end up

Zada's trigger created 4 copies of Crimson Wisps (one per Goblin token, each copy targeting a different one; copies were put on the stack, not cast, so Zada did not re-trigger). All 4 copies and then the original resolved: all 5 of your creatures (Zada + 4 Goblin tokens) are red and have haste until end of turn, and you drew 5 cards total (4 from copies + 1 from the original). Mulligan Guide ★ COACHING

Keep hands that can do two things: make Goblins early and cast spells cheaply. The gold standard is three lands (or two plus Sol Ring, Skirk Prospector, or Ruby Medallion), a one-or-two-mana token maker — Dragon Fodder, Krenko's Command, Mogg War Marshal, Searslicer Goblin — and a cantrip like Crimson Wisps or Expedite banked for the turn Zada comes down. Zada is always waiting in the command zone at four mana, so your opening hand doesn't need a payoff; it needs bodies and mana. Ship hands that are all crescendo and no verse — Blasphemous Act, Mana Geyser, and Empty the Warrens with two lands is a mulligan, because rituals and storm cards do nothing without a board and a spell count. Faithless Looting and Witch's Mark upgrade marginal keeps by trading dead cards for live ones. One table tip: the sideboard's Red Mana and Storm Counter cards are Secret Lair table aids with no rules text — put them in front of you on big turns to track floating ritual mana and your storm count, because a ten-spell turn is exactly when bookkeeping errors happen.

Upgrade Paths ◆ DATA-DERIVED

The obsolescence index flags a number of cards in this list with strictly-better replacements — treat these as prompts, not orders, since the index compares raw function and can't see that this deck cares about the Goblin creature type and about spells that target only one creature. The funniest finding first: Spreading Insurrection is flagged as obsoleted by Grapeshot (lower CMC, similarity 0.98) — a card you already run, which makes Insurrection a natural cut when you find something you like better. The deepest upgrade wells: Gempalm Incinerator is outclassed by Misty Knight, Hero for Hire (cheaper, bigger, similarity 0.996), Spider-Gwen, Free Spirit, and Hawkeye, Master Marksman; Rundvelt Hordemaster by Unlucky Witness (cheaper, 0.997) among several others; and Past in Flames by a cluster of cheaper options led by Firebolt and Kaleidoscorch (0.997) — though weigh what you'd lose there carefully. Elsewhere: Pashalik Mons is bettered by Krenko, Baron of Tin Street (bigger body plus counters, 0.91); Goblin Chieftain by Hero of the Games or the cheaper Hexing Squelcher; Goblin Warchief by Marauding Raptor or Foundry Inspector; Mana Geyser by the cheaper Desolation of Smaug or Dragon's Desire; Idol of Oblivion and Throne of Eldraine both by Daily Bugle Newspaper; Krenko's Command by Cindercone Smite; Battle Hymn and Brightstone Ritual by Guild Pact; Crimson Wisps and Expedite by Academic Dispute; Goblin Bushwhacker by Artillery Enthusiast; Goblin Lackey by Kellan, Planar Trailblazer; Searslicer Goblin by Prompto Argentum; General Kreat by Molten Lavamancer or Ultron, Artificial Malevolence; Broadside Bombardiers by Skittering Precursor; and Siege-Gang Commander, Siege-Gang Lieutenant, and Storm-Kiln Artist all by Contract Hero. Before any swap, check the replacement's creature type and targeting text against the deck's two pillars — Goblin tribal counts and Zada's 'targets only' clause. Finally, Sazacap's Brew is already sitting in your sideboard: a cheap instant that trades a discard for two fresh cards, worth testing in a flex slot.
