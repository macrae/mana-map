THE PLAN

GOBLINS ALL THE WAY DOWN

Why would a deck full of 1/1 Goblins bother with a one-mana spell that just says 'draw a card'? Because with Zada, Hedron Grinder on the battlefield, that spell doesn't say that anymore.

Everything starts with bodies. Dragon Fodder, Krenko's Command, Mogg War Marshal, Empty the Warrens, Siege-Gang Commander, and the attack-trigger crew (General Kreat, Searslicer Goblin, Howlsquad Heavy) put Goblins on the table fast, and Krenko, Mob Boss doubles the count every time he taps. A wide board is not just an army here — it is the multiplier on everything else you do. And the manual can now tell you exactly what Krenko is and is not: verified line 004 shows that under Goblin Warchief he costs {1}{R}{R}, activates the turn he lands, and turns five Goblins into ten — but the database's promised infinite Krenko/Prospector loop does not exist in this deck, because Krenko is not your commander and stays tapped after his one activation. He is a burst, and a very good one.

With the horde assembled, Zada, Hedron Grinder converts your cheapest spells into avalanches — this is the deck's verified floor. Line 001 shows the shape of it: a single Crimson Wisps with four Goblin tokens out draws you five cards and hastes the team for one red mana. Expedite, Renegade Tactics, Ancestors' Aid, and Fists of Flame all fit the same slot, and line 003 proves the ceiling of that slot: Fists of Flame copies resolving in sequence, each drawing before it buffs, add up to 32 power of trample off a board of 1/1s. Storm-Kiln Artist gets paid a Treasure for every spell you cast or copy, and Reliquary Tower lets you keep everything you drew.

The mana comes from rituals that scale with your board and the table — Battle Hymn, Brightstone Ritual, Skirk Prospector (verified in line 004 as a true mana ability: no stack, no responses, usable mid-payment), Mana Geyser, and Seething Song, discounted by Ruby Medallion and Goblin Warchief. Line 002 gives you the storm math to spend it on: copies are put on the stack, not cast, so an Empty the Warrens as your fourth spell makes eight Goblins and eight Impact Tremors damage to each opponent, while a later Grapeshot counts only your real casts. Count your casts, not your resolutions.

And now the deck has a verified true infinite as its ceiling: line 005 proves that Haze of Rage with buyback plus Storm-Kiln Artist goes mana-positive once three spells have been cast — each recast returns the card to hand, mints more Treasures than the next cast costs, and pumps the whole team another +1/+0 per copy, without bound. Two casts already leave nine Treasures and a 20/2 Storm-Kiln Artist. From there you assemble the kill yourself: trample via Fists of Flame, tokens-into-Tremors via Empty the Warrens off the Treasure mana, or a Grapeshot riding the storm count the loop keeps raising. If the first wave fails, the deck rebuilds: Past in Flames and Faithless Looting recycle the chain, Goblin Dark-Dwellers rebuys a cheap spell, and Goblin Matron and Moggcatcher find the missing piece.

THE READ

WHEN THEY DECIDE IT'S YOU

Nobody kills the Goblin player for having Goblins. So what exactly are three opponents looking at on the turn they all turn around at once?

The table reads your clock before you do. Zada, Hedron Grinder lands on turn 4 in 70% of games (7,002 of 10,000 goldfish iterations cast her exactly on 4; mean 4.35, median 4, 90% by turn 6). Any opponent who has seen this deck once will count your Mountains and know the turn-4 window is coming. What they can't read is your board before that: on turn 3 you average just 2.36 bodies — a Skirk Prospector and a couple of 1/1 tokens look like chump blockers, not the fuel tank for a draw engine. That gap between what your board says and what your hand is building is your entire early game.

The flip from ignored to archenemy happens at two precise thresholds now, and you should know both cold. The first is Zada untapped, three-plus other creatures, one red mana open: verified stack 001 shows one {R} Crimson Wisps becoming five cards and a hasted board. The second is the deck's true verified infinite — stack 005 proves that Haze of Rage with buyback plus Storm-Kiln Artist, once about three spells have been cast that turn, is a mana-positive loop that strictly improves: iteration k costs four mana and yields k+3 Treasures plus a team-wide +1/+0, and in the verified run two casts made 9 Treasures, three 10/1 Goblins, and a 20/2 Storm-Kiln. The moment Storm-Kiln Artist is on your battlefield with four mana up and Haze in hand, informed opponents must respect lethal — and their counterplay is scripted: kill Storm-Kiln in response to the first magecraft trigger, or hit the Treasures with instant-speed artifact hate. Treat the first Haze cast as the commitment point, and prefer to fire when the table's interaction is tapped or spent. Your other known-card signals stay the same: Skullclamp screams engine deck, and Conspicuous Snoop shows your library top all game.

Sequencing under the radar: deploy token makers early — Dragon Fodder and Krenko's Command read as defense, and the goldfish says your early token maker arrives on turn 3.18 on average, comfortably before Zada. Cast Zada as 'just a 3/3' and pass; the full engine assembles by turn 5.27 on average and only 49.6% of the time by turn 6, so you usually get one quiet turn. Never fire a cantrip at Zada for two copies of value. And know what Krenko, Mob Boss actually is here: the combo databases call Krenko plus Prospector plus a haste lord infinite, but stack 004 verifies it is not a loop in this deck — Krenko is not your commander, he stays tapped after one activation, and a Krenko sacrificed to Prospector is simply gone. Play him as a one-activation payoff ({1}{R}{R} under Warchief, five tokens on a modest board, ten Goblins and 13 hasted power), never sacrifice him expecting recursion, and don't burn protection defending a loop you don't have. When an opponent panic-removes Krenko expecting the Spellbook infinite, they've paid a real card for a one-shot — sometimes you let them.

Embrace being the threat only when the turn converts: pace for the median game — the Zada draw engine plus wide pressure — and hold the ceiling as opportunism. The go-decision is arithmetic done before the first spell: count the mana and castable spells in your hand and on your board only; expected draws are a bonus, not a plan. Impact Tremors or Boggart Shenanigans down, Krenko untapped with his partners, a Spinerock Knoll set, or the Haze loop live — those are go signs. The goldfish assembles 'storm payoff plus ritual' in only 15.2% of games, so let the table's fear sit on cards you haven't drawn. The turn you draw five cards in front of three opponents, you are the archenemy — so that turn should also be the turn the table has to answer you or start dying.
