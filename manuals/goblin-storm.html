Zada, Hedron Grinder

One spell in, five spells out — a goblin horde that casts itself.

Zada, Hedron Grinder turns every cheap spell that targets only her into a copy for each other creature you control — so a one-mana cantrip aimed at your commander becomes a fistful of cards when the Goblins are out in force. You flood the board with token makers, fuel monster turns with rituals like Battle Hymn and Brightstone Ritual, and convert the frenzy into damage through Impact Tremors, storm payoffs like Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens, and sacrifice engines like Goblin Bombardment. It plays like a tribal aggro deck that suddenly combusts into a storm deck.

How the Deck Wins

Everything starts with bodies. Dragon Fodder, Krenko's Command, Mogg War Marshal, Empty the Warrens, Siege-Gang Commander, and the attack-trigger crew (General Kreat, Searslicer Goblin, Howlsquad Heavy) put Goblins on the table fast, and Krenko, Mob Boss doubles the count every time he taps. A wide board is not just an army here — it is the multiplier on everything else you do.

With the horde assembled, Zada, Hedron Grinder converts your cheapest spells into avalanches. Her ability copies any instant or sorcery that targets only her, once for each other creature you control, with each copy aimed at a different creature. The verified line in this manual shows the shape of it: a single Crimson Wisps with four Goblin tokens out draws you five cards and gives the whole team haste for one red mana. Expedite, Renegade Tactics, Ancestors' Aid, and Fists of Flame all fit the same slot — one target, so they all qualify for Zada's trigger. Storm-Kiln Artist gets paid for every spell you cast or copy, so a Zada turn also mints a pile of Treasures, and Reliquary Tower lets you keep everything you drew.

The mana to chain it all comes from rituals that scale with your board and the table: Battle Hymn (a red per creature you control), Brightstone Ritual (a red per Goblin on the battlefield), Skirk Prospector (Goblins are mana), Mana Geyser, and Seething Song, discounted along the way by Ruby Medallion and Goblin Warchief. The turn ends in one of a few ways: a storm payoff — Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens grow with every spell cast before them in the turn — a mass-haste alpha strike sharpened by Goblin Chieftain, Quest for the Goblin Lord, and Goblin Bushwhacker, or attrition damage as Impact Tremors, General Kreat, and Boggart Shenanigans bleed opponents for every body that enters or dies. Goblin Bombardment and Pashalik Mons make sure even a board wipe costs your opponents life.

If the first wave fails, the deck rebuilds. Past in Flames and Faithless Looting recycle your spell chain, Goblin Dark-Dwellers rebuys a cheap sorcery or instant from the graveyard, and Goblin Matron and Moggcatcher find exactly the Goblin the situation calls for.

Fists of Flame on Zada: the sequential-draw lethal ✓ verified · 2 iteration(s)

This is the go-tall kill, verified step by step against the comprehensive rules (June 19, 2026 update) — and it took the checker two passes to bless it, for a reason worth learning from. The scene: Zada, Hedron Grinder and four Goblin tokens, one opponent at 30 life behind a lone untapped 2/2, two cards already drawn this turn, and Fists of Flame cast for {1}{R} targeting only Zada. Her trigger makes four copies — not cast, so she doesn't re-trigger — and they resolve before the original, each one drawing a card first and then applying its buff. The crucial rule: each copy's +1/+0-per-card value is determined once, on its own resolution, and never grows retroactively when later draws happen. So the buffs lock in ascending — +3, +4, +5, +6 on the tokens, +7 on Zada herself — leaving you five cards deeper and staring at 32 power of trample. The blocker absorbs at most 2, at least 30 tramples through: exactly lethal. And that exactness is why the checker demanded a revision — the first draft skipped the state-based action check. Combat damage doesn't kill anyone by itself; the game checks state-based actions before anyone gets priority, sees a player at 0 or less life, and only then is the game over. When your math comes out exactly lethal, walk that last step every time.

Playing the Table ★ COACHING

Archetypal board states and the political reads that go with them. These are coaching calls — grounded in the numbers above, decided by judgment.

Threat assessment

The table reads your clock before you do. Zada, Hedron Grinder lands on turn 4 in 70% of games (7,002 of 10,000 goldfish iterations cast her exactly on 4; mean 4.35, median 4, 90% by turn 6). Any opponent who has seen this deck once will count your Mountains and know the turn-4 window is coming. What they can't read is your board before that: on turn 3 you average just 2.36 bodies — a Skirk Prospector and a couple of 1/1 tokens look like chump blockers, not the fuel tank for a draw engine. That gap between what your board says and what your hand is building is your entire early game.

The flip from ignored to archenemy happens at one precise threshold: Zada untapped, three-plus other creatures, and a single red mana open. Verified stack 001 shows exactly why — one {R} Crimson Wisps targeting only Zada with four Goblin tokens out becomes five cards drawn and your whole board gaining haste, off one mana and one card. The first time the table watches that resolve, every future turn you pass with {R} up gets treated like a loaded gun. Your known-card signals are Skullclamp (a clamp on an empty-ish board still screams 'engine deck'), Storm-Kiln Artist (combo-graph partner with Zada — the table's spikes know the Treasure loop), and Conspicuous Snoop revealing your library top all game.

Sequencing under the radar: deploy token makers early — Dragon Fodder and Krenko's Command read as defense, and the goldfish says your early token maker arrives on turn 3.18 on average, comfortably before Zada. Cast Zada as 'just a 3/3' on turn 4 and pass; the full engine ('Zada online with a cantrip drawn') assembles by turn 5.27 on average and only 49.6% of the time by turn 6, so you usually get one quiet turn where Zada looks underwhelming. Never fire a cantrip at Zada for two or three copies of value — you reveal the engine for a fraction of its ceiling and eat the removal that was pointed elsewhere. And don't posture a storm kill you don't have: the goldfish assembles 'storm payoff plus ritual' in only 15.2% of games (mean turn 5.22). Matchups ★ COACHING

Sweeper control (Azorius/Esper wraths): your engine is your wrath insurance, not your victim. Stack 001 proves one Crimson Wisps with four bodies banks five cards into your hand — a zone the sweeper can't touch — and Storm-Kiln Artist's magecraft ('Whenever you cast or copy an instant or sorcery spell, create a Treasure token') turns those same copies into Treasures that survive a creature wrath. Hold one token maker in hand at all times against these decks, keep Past in Flames as your rebuild (flashback recasts your whole graveyard of rituals and cantrips), and remember Pashalik Mons and Goblin Bombardment convert a wrath into a burst of damage on the way down: Mons pings for each Goblin that dies, and Bombardment lets you sacrifice the board in response for value. Mogg War Marshal even dies into another body.

Stax and tax pieces: your outs are narrow but real, so know them by name. Vandalblast (overload {4}{R}) and Goblin Trashmaster ('Sacrifice a Goblin: Destroy target artifact') answer artifact stax — Trashmaster is instant-speed and repeatable, which is why the combo graph pairs him with Krenko. Chaos Warp is your only answer to a resolved enchantment or problem commander; do not spend it on anything an attack step could handle. Against Thalia-style taxes, Ruby Medallion and Goblin Warchief ('Goblin spells you cast cost {1} less') pay the toll for you, and Battle Hymn and Brightstone Ritual scale with your board rather than your lands — mana denial hurts you less than the table assumes, since your goldfish land-drop rate falls to 54% by turn 6 anyway and the deck is built to run on bodies.

Aggro and token mirrors: you win these on asymmetry, not size. Gempalm Incinerator cycles to kill their lord for X equal to all Goblins on the battlefield — theirs count too. Blasphemous Act costs {1} less per creature, so in a token mirror it's often {1}{R}; hold it as the reset button you're uniquely able to rebuild from (mean 4.07 bodies by turn 5 even with rituals unmodeled). Your anthems — Goblin Chieftain, Rundvelt Hordemaster, Quest for the Goblin Lord — break board stalls, and Impact Tremors plus General Kreat drain every opponent without ever attacking into their blockers. Let the other token deck be the archenemy; you out-grind them.

Combo tables: you are the pseudo-combo deck that must race, and the key mechanical fact is that Zada's copies are not cast (rule 707.10), so they add nothing to storm count — Grapeshot and Empty the Warrens only count real casts of rituals and cantrips. The goldfish confirms you should not build your plan around the storm line: payoff-plus-ritual assembles in 15.2% of games. Your realistic clock is wide haste attacks (Goblin Warchief, Goblin Chieftain) plus Impact Tremors drain, so apply pressure early and force the combo player to hold interaction. Bank Chaos Warp exclusively for their key permanent, and use Grenzo, Havoc Raiser combat triggers to goad their defenses apart. If the table asks whether you're 'the storm deck,' the honest answer that also serves you: usually you're a Goblin aggro deck with a very good draw engine.

Mulligan Guide ★ COACHING

Keep hands that can do two things: make Goblins early and cast spells cheaply. The gold standard is three lands (or two plus Sol Ring, Skirk Prospector, or Ruby Medallion), a one-or-two-mana token maker — Dragon Fodder, Krenko's Command, Mogg War Marshal, Searslicer Goblin — and a cantrip like Crimson Wisps or Expedite banked for the turn Zada comes down. Zada is always waiting in the command zone at four mana, so your opening hand doesn't need a payoff; it needs bodies and mana. Ship hands that are all crescendo and no verse — Blasphemous Act, Mana Geyser, and Empty the Warrens with two lands is a mulligan, because rituals and storm cards do nothing without a board and a spell count. Faithless Looting and Witch's Mark upgrade marginal keeps by trading dead cards for live ones. One table tip: the sideboard's Red Mana and Storm Counter cards are Secret Lair table aids with no rules text — put them in front of you on big turns to track floating ritual mana and your storm count, because a ten-spell turn is exactly when bookkeeping errors happen.
