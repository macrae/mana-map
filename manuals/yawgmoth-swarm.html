Begin with the clause that makes this format unlike every other one. CR 903.8: "A player may cast a commander they own from the command zone." Yawgmoth is therefore not a card you draw. He is a card you have. The simulation puts him on the battlefield in 9,846 of 10,000 games, mean turn 4.294, median 4, with 7,220 of those casting him on turn four exactly.

The price of that guarantee is in the same rule, and it compounds: "an additional {2} for each previous time the player casting it has cast it from the command zone that game." Four mana, then six, then eight, then ten. Set that schedule beside mean available mana of 3.911 on turn four, 5.223 on turn six and 7.1 on turn ten. The holding is short. The first recast costs you a turn. The second costs you the whole turn. The third is a turn nobody is going to give you.

Colour identity is CR 903.4, and it is the rule that built the other ninety-eight cards. One black symbol in the cost, one colour in the deck — and the audit comes back with nothing to reconcile: black is 100% of the pip load and 100% of the sources, on curve at 95.5% once ramp is counted. What it costs is breadth. This library holds no counterspell and no artifact removal, and none of the five tutors can find one, because there is none to find. That is the format's bargain stated as a rule rather than as a complaint.

Commander damage next, because the record disposes of it quickly. CR 903.10a removes a player who has been dealt 21 or more combat damage by the same commander. Yawgmoth is a 2/4 who would much rather be sacrificing than attacking, and 21 damage is eleven connections. Commander damage is a coincidence in this deck, not a clock.

Now the clause to memorise, because it cuts both ways. CR 702.16b: "A permanent or player with protection can't be targeted by spells with the stated quality and can't be targeted by abilities from a source with the stated quality." Yawgmoth has protection from Humans and is himself a Human Cleric, so his own ability can never target him — the 2/4 who could comfortably absorb four counters is not allowed to take one. Five checker-passed files turn on that clause: once an opponent's creature is gone, every legal target is one of your own tokens, which is what bounds the grinding board at five to eight activations. In the other direction it is free armour in a format thick with Human commanders.

One last fact about the zone. Four opponents can see it. They cannot see your hand. What they price when this commander resolves is a reputation rather than a board — and the bracket sheet agrees with them, landing at floor 4 on four Game Changers and eleven two-card infinite records.

COACH'S READ. Here is the part that will feel wrong and is right: do not buy the insurance. Three of your four verified kills name him nowhere. He is a draw engine, a second free outlet and a removal magnet, and you should be pleased to trade him — cast him on curve, draw four or five cards, and let somebody spend a card on him. Do not hold mana to protect him and do not mulligan toward him. When you do want him back, notice which routes are not recasts at all: Malakir Rebirth and Supernatural Stamina return him to the battlefield, so the tax never advances, and Animate Dead and Victimize reach him in the graveyard. Price the second trip before you make the first — six mana spent getting him back is six mana not spent assembling.

Pilot TipThe best reason to have him on the battlefield is this card — free removal, exile rather than destroy, on any turn you spent assembling.

THE PLAN

YOUR COMMANDER IS NOT THE PLAN

Three of the four kills a checker has signed never name Yawgmoth, and one of them costs you nothing at all. So what is he actually for on turns one through five?

You have four ways to end the game, and a checker has signed all four. Two of them are unbounded. Stack 009 loops Warren Soultrader, Gravecrawler and Bastion of Remembrance, and each activation's Treasure funds the next cast exactly. Stack 011 runs the same recursion through Phyrexian Altar, pays nothing at all, and gains you a life every time round.

The other two are lethal and they stop — for different reasons, and the difference is worth carrying to the table. Stack 012 is Exquisite Blood beside a body that reads "Whenever you gain life, each opponent loses 1 life." Once lit it is mandatory, and it is not a draw precisely because every cycle takes a life from every seat: drain #40 empties the pod. That one is lethal because it ends.

Stack 010 ends for a different reason — your own library. It needs 39 activations to zero the table against the 59 the mandatory draw permits, a margin of 20. All four kills take four opponents from 40 to 0 at the same moment. You are not winning a seat. You are winning the table.

Here is the part most pilots get backwards, so get it right from turn one: your commander is not the plan. Three of the four verified kills never name him. Yawgmoth is a mana-free instant-speed sacrifice outlet stapled to a draw engine, and that is a magnificent job — he arrives on turn 4 in 7,220 of 10,000 goldfished games, mean 4.294, 91.1% by turn 6. Use him to draw for three or four turns. Let the table spend removal on him. Then win with something he was never part of.

The enabler layers are deep and the kills are thin, and that split is the whole pilot's manual. By turn 10, unassisted, you have drawn a drain 82.4% of the time, a free repeatable sacrifice outlet 77.9%, renewable fodder 73.6%, an ignition source 73.1%, engine mana 61.9%, one of five tutors 60.6%. The specific kills read much smaller — KILL A 1.1% unassisted and 18.0% with a tutor, KILL B 0.5% / 12.3%, KILL C 6.9% / 42.5%, KILL D 4.6% / 33.7% (goldfish_metrics.json, 10,000 iterations, seed 42). Read those one at a time and never add them together; the simulator scores each requirement group on its own and there is no figure in it that means "any kill assembled." What the split tells you is simple: you will almost always have the machine running. Your job is to find the one missing piece, which is exactly what five tutors are for.

What beats you is not a wrath. A wrath is a payoff here — Bastion of Remembrance, Zulaport Cutthroat, Blood Artist and The Meathook Massacre all drain when your creatures die, and 28 creature copies plus Gravecrawler and Reassembling Skeleton rebuild cheaper than anyone at the table. What beats you is a permanent you cannot remove. deck-audit measures interaction-breadth at 2 answered classes against a target of 5, with no detected answer to artifacts, graveyards or lands, while every kill you have is a small pile of permanents sitting in the open. So deploy in the order that costs you least when it is answered, keep the drain in hand until the turn it converts, and present the assembly when the table's mana is spent. Bracket floor is 4 on four Game Changers and eleven two-card infinite records; say that out loud at Rule 0 and then go play the deck it describes.

ROLL CALL

ELEVEN GROUPS, IN ORDER OF MATTERING

Read the first two groups and you know how this deck ends a game. Read the rest and you know why it keeps working after somebody answers one of them.

The four kills — the cards that actually end games

Gravecrawler

Mill + Graveyard

The card two unbounded kills both route through, and the slot with no substitute. Its own ability modifies the zone it can be cast from (CR 113.6f), so with any Zombie out it recasts itself forever. You see it 16.5% of the time by turn 10 unassisted, 66.5% with a tutor.

Bastion of Remembrance

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

The single most load-bearing noncreature permanent you own: three of the four verified kills route their drain through it (stacks 009, 010, 011), and its enters trigger hands you a free Human Soldier to sacrifice. You draw it 16.7% of the time by turn 10 unassisted — hence the tutors.

Warren Soultrader

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Stack 009's engine: 'Pay 1 life, Sacrifice another creature: Create a Treasure token,' and the Treasure exactly funds Gravecrawler's next cast. It is also a Zombie, so it satisfies Gravecrawler's condition itself and the loop never has to eat it.

Phyrexian Altar

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

Sacrifice a creature for any colour, as a mana ability that never touches the stack — which is what makes stack 011 free, uncounterable and unbounded. It is the best engine-mana card in the deck by some distance.

Exquisite Blood

One half of the fastest kill you have — mean draw turn 5.301, 6.9% assembled unassisted by turn 10 and 42.5% with a tutor. It needs no sacrifice outlet and touches no graveyard, which makes it the shape to reach for when someone lands graveyard hate.

South Wind Avatar

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Half of stack 012's mandatory two-card kill, and a deathtouch 3/4 whose toughness buys four extra activations on the grinding board (stack 005). Its second ability, 'Whenever you gain life, each opponent loses 1 life,' is the exact text Exquisite Blood needs.

Marauding Blight-Priest

Lifegain + Death Trigger

Reads 'Whenever you gain life, each opponent loses 1 life' verbatim — a second body that pairs with Exquisite Blood, and a member of the nine-card drain class the goldfish measures at 82.4% by turn 10.

Starscape Cleric

Flying + Damage TriggerLifegain + Death Trigger

The same each-opponent lifegain trigger on a flier that can't block, with Offspring {2}{B} when you have the mana to buy a second copy of the trigger. Cheap, evasive, and a partner for Exquisite Blood.

Mikaeus, the Unhallowed

Flying + Damage TriggerLifegain + Death Trigger

Grants undying to every non-Human you control, which is the whole of stack 010 — the commander's -1/-1 counter annihilates the +1/+1 undying leaves and re-arms the body. Six mana, and worth it.

The other drains — each opponent, or one

Zulaport Cutthroat

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

An each-opponent drain on a body, and a legal substitute in the drain slot of every recursion kill. Stack 003 proves the useful thing about him: you are never forced to consume him, so he survives the whole engine and is spent last on purpose.

Ayara, First of Locthwain

Draw + DiscardLifegain + Death Trigger

A drain on every black creature entering plus a tap-to-draw outlet, and one of the three each-opponent drains the recursion kills accept. {B}{B}{B} is the real cost — she is a turn-3 card only when your first three lands are black, which they usually are.

The Meathook Massacre

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Does three jobs on one card: a scalable wipe, an each-opponent drain on your own creatures dying, and lifegain when theirs do. It is the wipe you cast when your board is the one that profits.

Mirkwood Bats

Flying + Damage TriggerSac + Death Trigger

The one drain that is paid on creation as well as sacrifice, which is why stack 008 gets two triggers from a single Yawgmoth activation paid with a token. Its 2/3 body is a genuine consideration on any line that wants small things to die.

Blood Artist

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Drains one target player per trigger rather than each opponent, and stack 004 measured what that costs on a bounded board — eight life removed from a pod of 160. He is still a fine body that pays you for every death; he is simply not the drain you tutor for.

Gray Merchant of Asphodel

Blink + ETBLifegain + Death Trigger

Devotion drain on a Zombie body, and in a mono-black deck full of {B} pips that number gets ugly fast. It is a drain, a Zombie for Gravecrawler and a reanimation target.

Free sacrifice outlets — 77.9% by turn ten

Carrion Feeder

Counters + TokensSac + Death Trigger

A one-mana free sacrifice outlet that is also a Zombie — that dual identity is precisely why stack 011's loop works, since it holds up Gravecrawler's cast permission while never being the thing sacrificed.

Viscera Seer

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

Free outlet, one mana, scry every time. Read stack 010 before you use her mid-loop: her sacrifice places no counter, and only Yawgmoth's counter can strip an undying return, so a spare activation costs a body for good.

Woe Strider

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Free outlet, a Goat to feed it on arrival, and escape from your own graveyard when the table wipes the board. Three functions on one three-mana card.

Yahenni, Undying Partisan

Counters + TokensLifegain + Death Trigger

Free outlet with indestructible attached — the one creature you keep through your own sweepers and through most of theirs, and a legal undying body under Mikaeus.

Blasting Station

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

Free outlet that also throws damage, and it untaps whenever a creature enters, which your token production does constantly. It is also a way to close from a board a drain cannot reach.

Ashnod's Altar

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

Free sacrifice outlet plus {C}{C}, which powers a lot of things — but note what it cannot do: colorless mana does not cast Gravecrawler for {B}. Use it for outlet and bulk mana, not to fund the recursion loop.

Phyrexian Tower

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

A land that turns a creature into {B}{B}. It is a sacrifice outlet, engine mana and a land drop at once, which is the cheapest kind of card in a deck this tight on slots.

Renewable fodder — the bodies that come back

Reassembling Skeleton

Mill + GraveyardReanimate + Death Trigger

Renewable fodder for {1}{B}, over and over, from the graveyard. It never runs out and never asks for anything but mana — the purest form of what this deck wants a body to be.

Bitterblossom

Flying + Damage Trigger

Free fodder every upkeep for 1 life — the cheapest engine in the deck and the one that keeps working while you hold up interaction. Two mana buys you a body a turn for the rest of the game.

Butcher Ghoul

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Undying fodder and a Zombie, so it turns on Gravecrawler and dies twice on its own. Under Mikaeus it is a legal alternation body for stack 010.

Stitcher's Supplier

Lifegain + Death TriggerMill + Graveyard

A one-mana Zombie that mills on the way in and on the way out, filling the graveyard your recursion suite wants. It also satisfies Gravecrawler's Zombie condition for a single black mana.

Geralf's Messenger

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Two life from one target opponent on arrival — a single seat, not the pod — plus undying, plus a Zombie for Gravecrawler. {B}{B}{B} is a real ask; the payoff is a body that has to be killed twice.

Sedgemoor Witch

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Ward-pay-3-life makes her hard to remove cheaply, and magecraft turns your instants and sorceries into fodder. Her Pest tokens gain you life when they die, which the lifegain drains notice.

Grave Titan

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Ten power across three bodies on arrival — a 6/6 deathtouch Giant plus two 2/2 Zombies — and two more Zombies every time it attacks. It is the card that makes an empty board into an engine again, and a fine thing to reanimate.

Black Market Connections

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

A Treasure, a card and a 3/2 body every first main phase, priced in life. Take what the board is short of — that flexibility is why it out-earns a fixed draw engine here.

Counters, and what they make

Nest of Scarabs

Turns your commander's removal mode into bodies. Stack 013 settles the arithmetic: the number of creatures you put counters on sets the number of triggers, and the counters on each of those creatures set that trigger's 'that many' — one proliferate across three counter-bearing creatures makes three Insects, not six.

Fume Spitter

-1/-1 + Death Trigger

One mana, sacrifices itself, places a -1/-1 counter. The cheapest ignition you own and a death trigger in the same card.

Skinrender

-1/-1 + Death TriggerBlink + ETB

Three -1/-1 counters on arrival — the deck's best single ignition, since it can kill an opposing creature outright or place three counters on your own body and hand Nest of Scarabs three Insects.

Soul Snuffers

-1/-1 + Death TriggerBlink + ETB

A counter on each creature, which sweeps small boards and lights the Nest at the same time. Remember it hits yours too; that is usually a feature, occasionally not.

Sinister Gnarlbark

-1/-1 + Death TriggerCounterspell + Draw

Draw a card and blight 1 at every end step — a free recurring -1/-1 counter, which means a free recurring Insect with Nest of Scarabs, plus a card. Recurring ignition on a three-mana body.

Massacre Girl, Known Killer

Draw + DiscardLifegain + Death Trigger

Gives your creatures wither, so combat damage becomes -1/-1 counters, and draws you a card whenever an opponent's creature dies with toughness below 1. Removal, ignition and card flow on one body.

Mana that is not a land

Sol Ring

Ramp + Cost Reduction

Two colorless on turn one, forever. It is the single biggest reason a turn-2 commander happens at all — the goldfish draws fast mana by turn 10 in 42.7% of games unassisted, and Sol Ring is the best card in that group.

Arcane Signet

Ramp + Cost Reduction

Two mana, taps for black, every turn. Unglamorous and correct — it is one of four persistent nonland producers that add black, and with the four rituals it lifts 34 land sources to 42.

Dark Ritual

Ramp + Cost Reduction

One black into three. The audit does count it — it is one of your eight nonland black sources — but it taps once and is gone. Treat it as a burst that buys one specific turn, and spend it on a turn that matters.

Pitiless Plunderer

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

A Treasure for every other creature you lose, which turns a board wipe into a mana pile and any sacrifice engine into a mana engine. Four mana is the price of admission; it earns it on the first activation.

Draw, priced in life

Necropotence

Draw + DiscardMill + Graveyard

Pay 1 life, exile a card, take it at your next end step. In a deck whose loops end at 80 life, life is the cheapest resource you own — spend it early and freely.

Skullclamp

Draw + DiscardEquipment + Attack Trigger

One mana, equip one, and any 1/1 you own becomes two cards. With Bitterblossom, Nest of Scarabs and Grave Titan making bodies, this is the deck's most efficient draw.

Deadly Dispute

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Sacrifice an artifact or creature at instant speed for two cards and a Treasure. It converts a creature in the removal crosshairs into cards and mana rather than nothing.

Plumb the Forbidden

Counterspell + DrawDraw + Discard

Instant-speed insurance: sacrifice as many creatures as you like in response to a wrath or a targeted exile, and copy the spell for each. The taxonomy has no pattern for it, which is not the same as having no function.

The five wishes

Demonic Tutor

The cleanest card in the format: two mana, any card, straight to hand. When one of your four kills is a piece short, this is the shortest route to it.

Vampiric Tutor

Instant-speed, one mana, two life, top of library. It costs a draw step, so cast it on the turn before the turn you need the card.

Diabolic Intent

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

A tutor whose additional cost is exactly what your deck was already going to do. Sacrificing a creature is a payment here and a trigger everywhere else on the board.

Beseech the Mirror

A tutor that can cast what it finds when bargained — sacrifice an artifact, an enchantment or a token you were going to sacrifice anyway, and the four-or-less piece arrives free.

Insatiable Avarice

Spree — {2}{B} in total puts any card on top of your library, {B}{B}{B} draws you three and costs you 3 life, and you may buy both modes on one cast. It is a tutor when you need a piece and raw cards when you need volume.

Interaction — and where it stops

Feed the Swarm

Removal + Death Trigger

The one card in the 99 that destroys a noncreature permanent an opponent controls — and it reads 'creature or enchantment', so an enchantment is the only noncreature it can reach. You draw it 17.3% of the time by turn 10 unassisted; hold it for the permanent that actually stops you.

Deadly Rollick

Removal + Death Trigger

Free while your commander is on the battlefield, and it exiles rather than destroys. The best reason to have Yawgmoth out is often this card.

Snuff Out

Removal + Death Trigger

Free with a Swamp for 4 life — instant-speed removal with no mana, which is how you interact on a turn you spent assembling. It cannot touch a black creature, and in this format that is a real gap.

Fatal Push

Removal + Death Trigger

One mana for mana value 2, or mana value 4 with revolt — and you turn on revolt every time you sacrifice anything, which is constantly. Cheap answers to cheap enablers.

Toxic Deluge

Three mana and X life clears almost anything, including indestructible. It applies -X/-X rather than counters, so the Nest stays quiet — this is your answer, not your engine.

Black Sun's Zenith

A scalable wipe that places actual counters, which is the only sweeper in the deck that Nest of Scarabs can see. Stack 013 explicitly declines to resolve what X greater than 1 does to the Nest's token count — that one needs a stack scenario before you count on the bigger number.

Grave Pact

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Your only repeatable edict, and the answer to a creature you cannot target. Every sacrifice that feeds a drain also makes each of your four opponents give a creature up — one activation, four boards shrinking at once. They pick which one; you just keep sacrificing.

The rebuild package

Persist

Animate Dead

Two mana for the best creature in any graveyard, yours or theirs. The -1/-0 rarely matters when the body's job is to be sacrificed.

Phyrexian Reclamation

Mill + Graveyard

Supernatural Stamina

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Protects a creature from removal and hands you an extra death trigger in the bargain, since the returned body triggers everything again. The role taxonomy has no pattern for it; the function is plain in the text.

Malakir Rebirth // Malakir Mire

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

A land on the turns you are short and a two-life save on the turns your key creature is targeted — and the return is another death trigger for everything on your board.

Lightning Greaves

The mana — 36 copies, one colour

Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth

Makes every land a Swamp, which produces no mana of its own — it counts for zero sources — and turns every land you have into fuel for Cabal Coffers and a Swamp for Snuff Out.

THE READING: the scenario's board line for Insect token A is annotated 'already sacrificed to pay the cost of the ability now on the stack', which matches stack[0] ('1 life paid (you are at 39) and Insect token A sacrificed') and the stated life total of 39.     CR 603.3b — “each player, in APNAP order, puts each triggered ability they control”
  7. The loop's shape. Resolving ONE Exquisite Blood trigger gains you 1 life; that life gain triggers South Wind Avatar's second ability, which is put on the stack ON TOP of the remaining Exquisite Blood triggers and therefore resolves next. So the sequence alternates strictly: one Exquisite Blood resolution, then one drain, then one Exquisite Blood resolution, then one drain. Each cycle nets +3 unresolved Exquisite Blood triggers on the stack, and neither card says 'may', so no player may decline any of it.
    Exactly one drain resolves per cycle. Each drain removes 1 life per seat (4 across the pod) and each cycle gains you exactly 1 life -- not 4, because the other three Exquisite Blood triggers from that drain are still waiting under the newer objects.
    CR 603.3 — “The ability becomes the topmost object on the stack.”
    CR 117.5 — “Then triggered abilities are put on the stack”
  8. The loop is mandatory but it is NOT a draw, and the limiter has a name. A 'loop' in the rules is a state in which a set of actions could be repeated INDEFINITELY, and the draw rule applies only to a loop 'repeating a sequence of events with no way to stop'. Neither condition is met here: every iteration changes the game state irreversibly -- each drain lowers every opponent's life by 1, adjusting four life totals that never rise again -- so the sequence cannot be repeated indefinitely and there IS a way to stop. It terminates after a finite, computable number of iterations when a state-based action ends the game. 732.4's bare 'only mandatory actions' sentence therefore does not reach this sequence, both because it imports 104.4b by its own cross-reference and because 732.1b's definition of a loop is unsatisfied.
    No draw. The loop runs to a forced conclusion instead.
    CR 732.1b — “a set of actions could be repeated indefinitely”
    CR 104.4b — “repeating a sequence of events with no way to stop, the game is a draw”
    CR 732.4 — “If a loop contains only mandatory actions, the game is a draw.”
    CR 119.3 — “If an effect causes a player to gain life or lose life”
  9. Count the iterations. Opponents start at 40 and each drain removes exactly 1 from each seat, so drain #40 is the one that brings all four seats to 0 simultaneously. Drain #1 followed the death trigger's life gain; every later drain #k (k >= 2) is preceded by exactly one Exquisite Blood resolution, so 39 Exquisite Blood triggers resolve before drain #40.
    Your life at the moment drain #40 resolves: 40 + 1 (death trigger) + 39 (Exquisite Blood resolutions) = 80. Per opponent: 40 - 40 = 0. Pod total: 160 - 160 = 0. 160 life removed across the pod, 40 per seat -- the two figures are not interchangeable.
    CR 119.3 — “If an effect causes a player to gain life or lose life”
  10. Drain #40 finishes resolving and a player would receive priority. State-based actions are checked FIRST, before the four new Exquisite Blood triggers from that drain are put on the stack (704.3, the same ordering cited at step 2). All four opponents have 0 life, so all four lose the game as a single simultaneous state-based action; because state-based actions were performed, the check repeats, and the waiting triggers never get put on the stack.
    Opponents 1-4 each at 0 life and each loses the game. Those four pending Exquisite Blood triggers never reach the stack, so your life stops at 80.
    CR 704.3 — “Whenever a player would get priority (see rule 117, “Timing and Priority”), the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event.”
    CR 704.3 — “otherwise all triggered abilities that are waiting to be put on the stack are put on the stack”
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
  11. Each opponent that lost the game leaves the game. You are the only player left, so you win immediately and the game ends immediately. Nothing still on the stack resolves.
    You win. Carrion Feeder's activated ability (pos 0) never resolves, so Carrion Feeder never gets its +1/+1 counter and stays a 1/1. The 117 Exquisite Blood triggers still on the stack never resolve. The opponents controlled no objects, so 800.4a removes nothing.
    CR 104.5 — “If a player loses the game, that player leaves the game.”
    CR 104.2a — “This happens immediately and overrides all effects that would preclude that player from winning the game.”
    CR 104.1 — “A game ends immediately when a player wins, when the game is a draw, or when the game is restarted.”
    CR 800.4a — “When a player leaves the game, all objects (see rule 109) owned by that player leave the game”
  12. Robustness note, and the one figure that is reading-dependent. Even if one insisted Exquisite Blood saw the drain as a single event and triggered only once, the alternation in step 7 is unchanged -- one Exquisite Blood resolution between successive drains -- so the drain count (40), the final per-seat total (0) and the pod total (0) are identical under every reading. YOUR final life is not: under the four-triggers reading and under a once-for-1 reading it is 80, but under a once-for-the-full-4-life-lost reading it would be 40 + 1 + 39x4 = 197. The kill does not depend on the reading; your life total does.
    Opponent figures and the win are reading-independent. Your final life is 80 on the reading resolved in step 6, and only that figure would move under an alternative reading.
    CR 603.2c — “An ability triggers only once each time its trigger event occurs. However, it can trigger repeatedly if one event contains multiple occurrences.”
    CR 700.1 — “Anything that happens in a game is an event. Multiple events may take place during the resolution of a spell or ability.”

EIGHTY-THREE CARDS

SEVENTY-ONE HANDS, NO HEADLINER

Nobody painted more than two cards in this deck and twelve artists are tied at exactly that, so there is no standout and we are not going to invent one. The feature goes to the pair whose two cards happen to include the commander's own tomb.

Start with the number that decides this section. Eighty-three cards counted, seventy-one different signatures, and the deepest single credit is two cards — twelve artists are tied there. Nobody dominates this deck, so nobody is going to be told they did.

John Avon is one of those twelve, and his pair is the one worth stopping on: Urborg, Tomb of Yawgmoth and Cabal Pit. Two black landscapes, thirty years of the same eye, and one of them is named after the man on the cover. Urborg makes every land in the game a Swamp and produces no mana of its own; Cabal Pit charges you a life for its black and then sacrifices itself to shrink something. Both of them are places that cost you something to stand in, which is the correct read of a mana base for this deck.

One figure needs saying out loud because it is the one that lies. Jorge Jacinto's Swamp appears twenty-two times here and counts once. Authorship is per card, never per copy — count it the other way and a basic land wins this section every single issue. Copies across the whole box: 104.

The printings cluster where recent Commander product does. Twelve cards from Lorwyn Eclipsed Commander, eight from Secrets of Strixhaven Commander, seven from Tarkir: Dragonstorm Commander, six from Commander Masters. Nobody curated that, and the analysis is specific about it: there is no contiguous collector-number run anywhere in the deck, so nothing here was bought whole as a sealed product and shuffled in. It is simply where the cardboard comes from, and it is most of why no single hand gets ahead.

And nothing here is foil and nothing is borderless. Eighty black borders and three white ones left over from Fifth and Sixth Edition. This is a working deck, sleeved at working prices, and it looks like one on purpose.
