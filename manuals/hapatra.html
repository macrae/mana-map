Begin with the exhibit everyone skips: the zone itself. Rule 903.8 lets you cast Hapatra from the command zone any time you could cast a sorcery — no draw required, no tutor spent. At two mana, that right gets exercised on turn 2 in 9,970 of 10,000 seeded runs. The holding follows: your opening seven never needs her, so all seven cards are free to hunt the thing the zone cannot promise — a repeatable counter source.

The tax ladder above is the schedule: two more generic mana on each return trip (903.8). Read it against this deck's ledger — mean available mana 4.71 on turn 4, 6.15 on turn 6 — and a second or even third cast stays on schedule. So the court's spending advice: for Hapatra, pay the tax. Save Heroic Intervention and the boots for Yawgmoth and Mikaeus, the pieces the zone does not resurrect.

Colour identity (903.4) is the clause with teeth, and it counts mana symbols in rules text. Black-green admits every -1/-1 counter card on record and forbids the counterspell outright. Note what it does not forbid: green owns artifact and enchantment removal, and this 99 carries none. That hole is a signed trade, not a legal constraint — say so plainly in your Rule 0 conversation.

On the 21-point commander clock (903.10a), the record is short. Hapatra is an unevasive 2/2; eleven successful combats against a single defender is not a plan. Stack 001, step 11, classifies her combat trigger as a bonus iteration, never an engine. The clock exists. It is not yours.

Coach's corner (★): the political read. A two-mana commander behind a spread of 1/1 Snakes is the quietest opening in the format — the table files you under 'later.' That filing is an asset. Spend it on engine pieces, and expect it revoked the moment a tutor resolves.

READ HER RIGHT

RUN THE MINT

Why does a 2/2 that never wants to attack keep winning long games? Because Hapatra is a mint, not an engine — other cards place the counters, she pays you a deathtouch Snake per placement, and every Snake becomes a card or a drain.

The table sees a snake deck with a combo sheet stapled to it and treats you like a fuse. Here is what is actually true, and it is better news: Hapatra is a mint. Her combat trigger is a bonus on an unevasive 2/2 — never a plan. The half you build around is the payout: every time you put one or more -1/-1 counters on a creature, she hands you a 1/1 deathtouch Snake, one per placement event.

So stop hunting payoffs and start hunting sources. Of the eight sacrifice outlets here that cost no mana, exactly one — Yawgmoth, Thran Physician — also places a counter, and Devoted Druid only becomes a repeatable source with Mikaeus out to reset it. You draw a tutor in 78.0% of games: point the first one at a repeatable counter source, not at a drain. Blowfly Infestation, Serrated Arrows, Contagion Clasp and Black Sun's Zenith are the same job on different bodies — take whichever one your hand can cast this turn.

Most games you win by out-carding three people and then draining them, and that game assembles 67.2% of the time on a mean turn of 3.62. Clamp a Snake for two cards. Cash a spare body to Deadly Dispute or Village Rites at instant speed. Let Grim Haruspex collect your real creatures and Morbid Opportunist collect everyone else's. Every Snake you leave home also blanks an attacker of any size, so the board that draws you cards is the board nobody wants to swing into.

The ceiling is narrower than the sheet claims and you will cash it more often for knowing that. The kill is one repeatable source plus one converter that hits all three seats — Zulaport Cutthroat, Bastion of Remembrance, or best of all Obelisk Spider, which drains on counter placement instead of on death and so runs roughly four times faster on a loop. You have a converter in hand in 51.9% of games, so when the second half arrives, pivot: deploy the source, count the table's spent interaction, and go. Blood Artist drains one player at a time — take the value, do not mistake him for a clock.

ROLL CALL

RANK AND VILE

Sixty-four spells sort into one scarce class and a lot of abundance. The sources lead this roster because they have to — everything behind them is bodies becoming cards, and cards becoming a clock on every seat.

The mint — counters become bodies

Hapatra, Vizier of Poisons

-1/-1 + Death TriggerAttack Trigger + Tokens

Cast her turn two and forget the combat trigger — an unevasive 2/2 rarely connects, and that half is a bonus. Build around the other half: one 1/1 deathtouch Snake for every placement event where you put -1/-1 counters on a creature.

Nest of Scarabs

The best exchange rate you own: the -1/-1 counters you place come back as that many 1/1 Insects, so a two-counter placement is two bodies. Watch the templating — it pays only when you are the one placing them.

Flourishing Defenses

Five mana, and it triggers on any -1/-1 counter placed on any creature by anyone. Slower than Nest of Scarabs and broader; take it when the table is also spraying counters, and do not lead your curve with it.

The source — the class that is two deep

Yawgmoth, Thran Physician

-1/-1 + Death TriggerAura + Protection

Your default tutor target. Of the eight sacrifice outlets here that cost no mana he is the only one that also places a counter, so he is outlet, Snake factory and draw engine on one body. Have a second creature before you start: the counter needs a target that is not the thing you just ate.

Devoted Druid

-1/-1 + Death TriggerRamp + Cost Reduction

A mana dork whose untap ability pays a -1/-1 counter — a free repeatable counter source hiding in the ramp slot. With Mikaeus out it resets itself forever; without him, run it down for two green, two Snakes and a body in the graveyard whenever the turn needs it.

Blowfly Infestation

-1/-1 + Death TriggerLifegain + Death Trigger

Every creature that dies with a -1/-1 counter puts another counter on something — that is a repeatable source, and repeatable sources are the scarce class. Play it for exactly that. The two-card kill it is famous for is unresolved here; do not build a turn around it.

Fume Spitter

-1/-1 + Death Trigger

One mana, sacrifices itself, places a counter. That is a Snake, a trigger and a dead one-toughness creature for the cheapest price in the deck — do not hold it hoping for a better target.

Grim Affliction

-1/-1 + Death Trigger

A counter plus proliferate at instant speed. Best used when two or more of your counters are already sitting on the board waiting to be finished.

Contagion Clasp

-1/-1 + Death TriggerBlink + ETB

A counter on arrival, then proliferate for four whenever you have nothing better. Late, it turns one counter on an opponent's creature into a kill and a Snake at the same time.

Serrated Arrows

-1/-1 + Death Trigger

Three counters, three activations, three Snakes, spread over three turns. Slow and repeatable, which is exactly the class this deck is short of.

Black Sun's Zenith

Not a reset — a rebuild. X counters on each creature leaves your counter payoffs a board to work with and hands Necroskitter every opposing creature that dies with a counter on it. Cast it for the number that kills their side and leaves yours breathing.

The converters — a clock on every seat

Zulaport Cutthroat

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Each opponent loses one for every creature you control that dies. That is your table clock; deploy it the turn before you go off, not four turns early where it invites removal.

Bastion of Remembrance

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

The same drain in enchantment form, which means a wrath cannot answer it, and it brings its own Soldier along. Against a table with sweepers, lead with this converter over the creatures.

Obelisk Spider

-1/-1 + Death TriggerFlying + Damage Trigger

The converter worth tutoring for. It drains each opponent on counter placement rather than on death, so it prices any loop several times faster than a dies-trigger does — and reach plus a combat-damage counter makes it a real blocker while you set up.

Blood Artist

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

One target player per death, not each — a wrath tax and a value engine, never your clock. Point the drains at whoever is closest to dead, and take the incidental life.

The outlets — free to feed

Ashnod's Altar

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

A free outlet that turns every spare body into two colourless mana, which is genuine acceleration in a deck that makes bodies for nothing. Its famous Mikaeus pairing is refuted — use it as a mana engine and a sacrifice engine, not a combo piece.

Phyrexian Altar

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

The coloured version: any body, any colour, no mana in the cost. The colour matters more than you think when your black pips run to triple.

Carrion Feeder

Counters + TokensSac + Death Trigger

One mana, sacrifices at will, and it grows. Just know that it puts +1/+1 counters on itself, which is precisely the wrong direction under Mikaeus.

Viscera Seer

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

A one-mana free outlet that smooths your draws. Sacrifice in response to removal every single time; the scry is small, and free is free.

Yahenni, Undying Partisan

Counters + TokensLifegain + Death Trigger

A free outlet with haste that shrugs off wraths by going indestructible. Sacrifice a Snake in response to the sweeper and keep your three-drop.

Blasting Station

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

Untaps whenever a creature enters, so a stream of tokens becomes repeatable damage with no drain payoff needed at all. This is your out when the table has hosed lifeloss.

Bodies to cards

Skullclamp

Draw + DiscardEquipment + Attack Trigger

Equip a 1/1 Snake, watch it die, draw two for one mana. Do it every turn you have spare mana and a spare body; this is the card that turns your tokens into a deck.

Grim Haruspex

-1/-1 + Death TriggerDraw + Discard

Draws off your nontoken deaths only, so he pays for the real creatures — Snakes and Insects do nothing for him. Three mana, and he replaces himself the moment a wrath lands.

Morbid Opportunist

Counterspell + DrawDraw + Discard

Once a turn, any creature dying anywhere hands you a card. That means he draws on every opponent's turn too — leave him back and let three other players work for you.

Deadly Dispute

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Two cards and a Treasure at instant speed for a body that was already fodder. Fire it in response to removal on the creature you were about to sacrifice anyway.

Village Rites

One black, two cards, one dead Snake. The cheapest way to turn a wrath or a targeted kill into card advantage — hold it for that turn rather than spending it on curve.

Night's Whisper

Counterspell + DrawDraw + Discard

Two cards for two mana and two life. Unremarkable, and exactly what a hand with an engine and no fuel needs on turn two.

Pitiless Plunderer

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Every other creature you control that dies leaves a Treasure, tokens included. That is what pays for the expensive half of your turn after a sweeper resolves.

Sephiroth, Fabled SOLDIER // Sephiroth, One-Winged Angel

Attack Trigger + TokensDraw + Discard

Sacrifices a creature and draws when it enters or attacks, and drains an opponent on every other creature's death. Four drain resolutions in one turn flips it — count them on the turn you go wide, because that transformation is free value you can actually plan for.

Bitterblossom

Flying + Damage Trigger

A free flying body every upkeep for one life. Fodder, chump blocker, Skullclamp target, and the reason you survive a wrath — deploy it turn two and never think about it again.

The combo shelf — two lines verified, the rest pending

Mikaeus, the Unhallowed

Flying + Damage TriggerLifegain + Death Trigger

Six mana, and he pays for it only with a partner — Devoted Druid or Walking Ballista plus an outlet. Know two things before you cast him: he does nothing for Hapatra or Yawgmoth, who are both Human, and he makes your Snakes 2/2, which nothing here has settled the token loops' opinion of.

Walking Ballista

Counters + Tokens

Removal you can hold at any size, and the cleanest verified kill in the deck when Mikaeus and any free outlet join it. Cast it for X=1 early if it buys a body; save it if the table is presenting nothing worth shooting.

Scurry Oak

Attack Trigger + TokensCounters + Tokens

Evolves, and makes a Squirrel every time it gains a +1/+1 counter. A fine body that makes bodies; the combo it is named for has not been settled here.

Ivy Lane Denizen

Counters + Tokens

Your Snakes are green, so every one of them puts a +1/+1 counter on a creature of your choice. Use that to save a creature from your own -1/-1 counters, and treat the Scurry Oak line as unresolved rather than as a plan.

Host of the Hereafter

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

A four-mana body that arrives with two +1/+1 counters and moves counters off your dying creatures onto a survivor. The loop it is listed for is unverified; play it as a value body that keeps your counters on the board.

Necroskitter

Lifegain + Death TriggerReanimate + Death Trigger

Every creature an opponent controls that dies with a -1/-1 counter comes back under your control. Against a creature table, place counters on their board rather than yours and let the sweeper do the rest.

The eight tutors

Demonic Tutor

Two mana, any card, no strings. Fetch a repeatable counter source first and a converter second — that ordering is the whole tutor policy of this deck.

Vampiric Tutor

Instant speed, top of library, two life. Use it on an opponent's end step so the card you fetch arrives with a full turn of mana behind it.

Imperial Seal

The sorcery-speed version, and it costs one black. Fire it on a turn you were not going to use all your mana, because the card does not arrive until your next draw.

Worldly Tutor

Instant speed for a creature, on top. Grab Yawgmoth or Obelisk Spider at end of turn and untap into it.

Survival of the Fittest

Repeatable creature tutoring for one green, discarding a creature you would rather have in the graveyard anyway with Phyrexian Reclamation out. Left alone it finds every creature in your deck.

Green Sun's Zenith

Puts a green creature straight onto the battlefield, which beats drawing one. X=2 is Devoted Druid; X=0 or 1 is a dork on the turn you need the mana more.

Diabolic Intent

Sac + Death TriggerSac + ETB

Any card to hand, and the sacrifice is nearly free — the body was fodder, and its death is a trigger for half your board. Sequence it after you have a Snake to spare.

Grim Tutor

Demonic Tutor for a mana and three life more. Your life total is a resource here anyway; spend it and get the source.

SEVEN CARDS

ROUTE OR RERACK

The keep test is one question: is there a route to a repeatable counter source? Ledger's receipts say you keep 79.9% of first sevens — so be picky. You can afford it.

Keepable first sevens (simulated)80%

KEEP — the shape you want

Swamp, Forest, Bayou, Llanowar Elves, Yawgmoth, Thran Physician, Skullclamp, Night's Whisper

Three lands, a turn-1 dork, the one free repeatable source in the 99, and two ways to cash it. Hapatra waits in the zone — this hand holds the card the zone can't promise.

KEEP — two lands, engine attached

Swamp, Command Tower, Elvish Mystic, Demonic Tutor, Blood Artist, Village Rites, Blowfly Infestation

A two-lander carries with a dork attached, and Demonic Tutor is any card — the census answer is one fetch away. Keep Blowfly as what it reliably is, a counter source for the grind, never half of an unverified kill.

SHIP — payoffs without a source

Swamp, Forest, City of Brass, Mana Confluence, Zulaport Cutthroat, Blood Artist, Bastion of Remembrance

Four lands, three converters, nothing placing counters. The goldfish keeps this hand — its rule only counts lands — and you are smarter than the goldfish: these are blanks waiting for a card you haven't drawn yet.

SHIP — one land and a prayer

Forest, Lotus Petal, Mikaeus, the Unhallowed, Nest of Scarabs, Grim Affliction, Heroic Intervention, Morbid Opportunist

A Petal that finds no second land loses the commander, and Mikaeus is your only six-drop — a combo piece for two named partners, not a plan. Mean mulligans run 0.243; shipping this is what the average is made of.

Everyone keeps the hand with Nest of Scarabs and Blood Artist in it. Do not be everyone. Those cards are exchange rates, and an exchange rate with nothing to exchange is a blank. The keep test is one question: does this hand reach a repeatable -1/-1 source?

You can afford to be strict, because the deck is forgiving. You keep the first seven 79.9% of the time and average 0.243 mulligans; Hapatra hits the table on turn two in 9,970 of 10,000 runs. A keep does not have to contain the engine — it has to cast the commander on schedule and find one.

Keep on sight: two to four lands with Hapatra castable on two, plus any of Yawgmoth, Devoted Druid, Blowfly Infestation or a tutor. Keep the two-lander that has Birds of Paradise, Llanowar Elves, Elvish Mystic, Sol Ring, Lotus Petal or Dark Ritual attached — that hand casts the commander a turn early with a play left over. Keep Skullclamp with any token maker, and keep Bitterblossom alone, because it is a body every turn and it rebuilds through a wrath by itself.

Ship the five-lander with no tutor and no dork. You have seen the curve: four zero-drops, sixteen ones, nineteen twos, and two cards above four mana. That hand is holding two cards it will never spend. Ship converters with no source — Blood Artist, Zulaport Cutthroat, Bastion of Remembrance are the classic trap keep. Ship hands built on Mikaeus alone; he is your only six-drop, he places no counters, and he needs a specific partner to do anything at all. Ship one-landers even with a mox, because a mox that finds no second land loses your commander.

Treat Scurry Oak, Ivy Lane Denizen and Host of the Hereafter as bodies rather than as routes — the lines they are famous for are unsettled here, and you do not mulligan toward something this manual will not assert. How lopsided can a pip load get before the mana breaks? Black carries 71.4% of the pips on an even 50% source share and pays nothing for it: 29 black sources and 29 green against a target of 22, on curve 96.2% of the time on lands alone — and not one of the 36 lands arrives tapped.

Twenty-nine black sources, twenty-nine green, against a hypergeometric target of twenty-two apiece. That is not comfortable, it is a surplus of seven on each side — and it comes out of thirty-six lands across twenty-four distinct entries, of which exactly zero enter tapped.

Twenty-two of those thirty-six make both colours; the other fourteen are basics. That is why lands alone put both pips at 96.2% on curve. The 90% yardstick is usually the thing ramp exists to rescue, and here it is cleared before a single rock is counted.

Now the disagreement the audit exists to find. Black carries 71.4% of the pip load — 40.0 pips spread over 34 cards, running as deep as triple-black — while holding a 50% source share; green carries 28.6% of the load, 16.0 pips over 15 cards, on the same 50%. Normally a gap that size costs something. It costs nothing here, because when 22 of your lands answer to either question, there is no split to get wrong.

Sixteen nonland producers finish the job: nine rocks, five dorks, two rituals, lifting on-curve to 98.8% black and 99.1% green and tilting the nonland source count green, 10 to 8, since dorks are Forests that block. Five of the rocks — Sol Ring, Mana Vault, Grim Monolith, Ashnod's Altar and Chrome Mox — score zero coloured sources under the audit's unconditional-mana rule. Nothing is free. City of Brass, Mana Confluence and Tarnished Citadel bill you in life, and Forbidden Orchard hands an opponent a Spirit every time you tap it. Restricted mana: none — not one source arrives with a spend-this-only-on clause attached. Zero tapped lands is an invoice written on a life total, and this list is built to pay it.

Case A-001 Does Hapatra's -1/-1 counter reset undying under Mikaeus?
Rules version June 19, 2026 · status: cleared in 4 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
The question put to the record
Resolve the full sequence. (1) Does Mikaeus grant undying to a Snake *token*, and what happens to a token that undying tries to return? (2) When a creature with a +1/+1 counter later receives a -1/-1 counter from Hapatra, do the counters annihilate, and under which rule? (3) Does a creature whose +1/+1 counter was annihilated this way satisfy undying's 'if it had no +1/+1 counters on it' check the next time it dies? (4) Given all of that, is the Mikaeus + Ashnod's Altar line actually unbounded in this deck, and if so what is the loop's per-iteration output — or does it terminate, and where?
  1. You activate Ashnod's Altar, sacrificing the 1/1 green Snake token as the activation cost.
    Correction to the scenario file: this is a mana ability — it requires no target and could add mana — so it never uses the stack. It resolves immediately and cannot be responded to; the listed stack object does not exist. Choices are locked first, then the cost is paid: the Snake moves from the battlefield directly to your graveyard. You have {C}{C} floating.
    CR 605.1a — “An activated ability is a mana ability if it meets all of the following criteria: it doesn’t require a target”
    CR 605.3b — “An activated mana ability doesn’t go on the stack, so it can’t be targeted, countered, or otherwise responded to. Rather, it resolves immediately after it is activated.”
    CR 602.2b — “The remainder of the process for activating an ability is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2b–i.”
    CR 701.21a — “To sacrifice a permanent, its controller moves it from the battlefield directly to its owner’s graveyard.”
  2. Sub-question 1a — does Mikaeus grant undying to a token? Yes.
    The Snake is a non-Human creature you control, so Mikaeus's static ability applies: a 2/2 with deathtouch and undying. There is no token exemption in the grant. It had no +1/+1 counters, so undying's trigger condition and its intervening 'if' are both satisfied by its death.
    CR 111.6 — “A token is subject to anything that affects permanents in general or that affects the token’s card type or subtype.”
    CR 702.93a — “if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, return it to the battlefield under its owner’s control with a +1/+1 counter on it.”
  3. Both death triggers fire: the Snake's undying and Blood Artist.
    Undying is a leaves-the-battlefield ability, so it looks back in time — the game uses the Snake as it appeared immediately before the event, when it still had undying and no +1/+1 counters. Both fire before the token ceases to exist.
    CR 603.10a — “Some zone-change triggers look back in time. These are leaves-the-battlefield abilities, abilities that trigger when a player sacrifices a permanent”
    CR 111.7 — “applicable triggered abilities will trigger before the token ceases to exist”
  4. Before either trigger goes on the stack, state-based actions are checked — and the token ceases to exist.
    The decisive ordering point. SBAs are performed first as a single event, and only when a check performs none do waiting triggers get put on the stack. The token is in a zone other than the battlefield, so it ceases to exist in that first check. By the time undying is placed on the stack, the object it wants to return no longer exists in any zone.
    CR 704.3 — “the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event”
    CR 704.3 — “otherwise all triggered abilities that are waiting to be put on the stack are put on the stack, then the check is repeated”
    CR 704.5d — “If a token is in a zone other than the battlefield, it ceases to exist.”
  5. Sub-question 1b — the undying trigger resolves and does nothing.
    A token that has left the battlefield cannot come back. The return instruction is impossible, so the ability does only as much as possible, which is nothing. Undying on a token is a dead grant. Blood Artist targets a player, so it is unaffected.
    CR 111.8 — “A token that has left the battlefield can’t move to another zone or come back onto the battlefield. If such a token would change zones, it remains in its current zone instead.”
    CR 609.3 — “If an effect attempts to do something impossible, it does only as much as possible.”
  6. Blood Artist's trigger resolves.
    Its target is still legal: an opponent loses 1 life, you gain 1. Net for the scenario: {C}{C}, one drain, and the Snake gone permanently. No loop has begun.
    CR 608.2b — “If the spell or ability specifies targets, it checks whether the targets are still legal.”
    CR 117.3b — “The active player receives priority after a spell or ability (other than a mana ability) resolves.”
  7. Sub-question 2 — counter annihilation is a state-based action.
    As soon as a player would receive priority, if a permanent has both a +1/+1 and a -1/-1 counter, N of each are removed. One of each leaves zero. There is no window in which the creature holds both while a player has priority.
    CR 704.5q — “If a permanent has both a +1/+1 counter and a -1/-1 counter on it, N +1/+1 and N -1/-1 counters are removed from it, where N is the smaller of the number of +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters on it.”
    CR 122.3 — “If a permanent has both a +1/+1 counter and a -1/-1 counter on it, N +1/+1 and N -1/-1 counters are removed from it as a state-based action”
  8. Sub-question 3 — annihilation does reset undying, with one sharp exception.
    If the annihilation happened in an earlier SBA check and the creature survived, then at its later death it genuinely has zero +1/+1 counters and undying triggers. THE TRAP: if the -1/-1 counters both annihilate and take toughness to 0 in the SAME check, last known information is read from before any of those SBAs — the +1/+1 counter is still visible and undying does NOT trigger. The CR's own example is exactly this case. Strip the counter first, let SBAs run, kill it separately.
    CR 603.4 — “If the ability triggers, it checks the stated condition again as it resolves. If the condition isn’t true at that time, the ability is removed from the stack and does nothing.”
    CR 704.8 — “If a state-based action results in a permanent leaving the battlefield at the same time other state-based actions were performed, that permanent’s last known information is derived from the game state before any of those state-based actions were performed.”
    CR 704.8 — “When it was last on the battlefield, it had a +1/+1 counter on it, so undying will not trigger.”
  9. Sub-question 4a — for Snake tokens the loop produces ZERO iterations.
    Mikaeus plus an outlet fed by Hapatra's Snakes is not a loop. Each Snake gives {C}{C} and one drain, then ceases to exist. Any line assuming 'sacrifice a token, get it back' is invalid on its face.
    CR 704.5d — “If a token is in a zone other than the battlefield, it ceases to exist.”
    CR 111.8 — “A token that has left the battlefield can’t move to another zone or come back onto the battlefield. If such a token would change zones, it remains in its current zone instead.”
  10. Sub-question 4b — for a non-token creature with a counter-neutral outlet, exactly ONE extra death.
    Iteration 1: sacrifice it with no counters, undying returns it as a new object WITH a +1/+1 counter. Iteration 2: sacrifice again — it dies carrying that counter, the intervening 'if' is false, undying never triggers. Total: 4 colorless and 2 drains. This is the verdict for the six two-card entries whose outlet places no -1/-1 counter: Ashnod's Altar, Phyrexian Altar, Carrion Feeder, Viscera Seer, Yahenni, Blasting Station. Carrion Feeder is actively anti-synergistic — it ADDS +1/+1 counters. FULL OUTLET CENSUS, since my earlier counts were wrong twice: eight of this deck's sacrifice outlets have no mana in their activation cost — Ashnod's Altar, Phyrexian Altar, Carrion Feeder, Viscera Seer, Yahenni and Yawgmoth (all activatable without tapping), plus Blasting Station ({T}, but it untaps itself whenever a creature enters, which an undying loop supplies) and Lazotep Quarry ({T}, no untapper, so once per turn and unable to loop). Exactly one of the eight — Yawgmoth — also places a -1/-1 counter.
    CR 702.93a — “if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, return it to the battlefield under its owner’s control with a +1/+1 counter on it.”
    CR 603.4 — “If the ability triggers, it checks the stated condition again as it resolves. If the condition isn’t true at that time, the ability is removed from the stack and does nothing.”
    CR 400.7 — “An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence.”
  11. Sub-question 4c — Hapatra does NOT supply the reset. The scenario's claim fails here.
    Her ability is a combat-damage trigger: it goes on the stack only when Hapatra connects. It is not an at-will repeatable placement and cannot be interleaved between sacrifices. At best it buys ONE extra iteration per combat.
    CR 113.3c — “Whenever the trigger event occurs, the ability is put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority”
  12. SUB-QUESTION 4, part C-bis — Yawgmoth, Thran Physician is one of the deck's EIGHT mana-free sacrifice outlets and the only one that is also a repeatable reset. It needs a SECOND body.
    "Pay 1 life, Sacrifice another creature: Put a -1/-1 counter on up to one target creature and draw a card." That counter annihilates an undying +1/+1 counter under 704.5q, so this outlet does not hit the step-10 wall. ON THE TARGETING, corrected: 601.2c (reached for activated abilities through 602.2b) locks targets BEFORE costs are paid at 601.2h, so you may perfectly legally choose the creature you are about to sacrifice. What stops it is resolution, not announcement — a target no longer in the zone it was in when targeted is illegal, and since this ability has only one instance of the word 'target', that single illegal target means it does not resolve at all. You would not even draw the card. Hence a second body is mandatory. That is a FOUR-CARD assembly — Mikaeus + Yawgmoth + two non-Human creatures — collapsing to three cards plus the commander's Snakes when Hapatra is on the battlefield, since each -1/-1 counter she sees placed generates fresh fodder. Loop with two non-Human creatures A and B: activate targeting B and sacrificing A → A returns with a +1/+1 counter, the ability resolves putting a -1/-1 counter on B. Next activation targets A (annihilating its counter) and sacrifices B. Symmetric, and it repeats. BUT each activation costs 1 life and the draw is mandatory, so the loop is LIBRARY-BOUNDED — run it out and you lose to an empty draw. A strong engine, not a true infinite. With Hapatra out, the counters generate Snakes, which supply the fodder half.
    CR 704.5q — “If a permanent has both a +1/+1 counter and a -1/-1 counter on it, N +1/+1 and N -1/-1 counters are removed from it, where N is the smaller of the number of +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters on it.”
    CR 602.2b — “The remainder of the process for activating an ability is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2b–i.”
    CR 601.2c — “The player announces their choice of an appropriate object or player for each target the spell requires.”
    CR 601.2h — “The player pays the total cost.”
    CR 608.2b — “A target that’s no longer in the zone it was in when it was targeted is illegal.”
    CR 702.93a — “if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, return it to the battlefield under its owner’s control with a +1/+1 counter on it.”
    CR 104.3c — “If a player is required to draw more cards than are left in their library, they draw the remaining cards and then lose the game the next time a player would receive priority.”
  13. Mechanism — Devoted Druid's untap ability uses the stack, and its counter is a cost.
    It cannot add mana, so it is an ordinary activated ability created on the stack. Its cost, the -1/-1 counter, is paid on activation, not resolution. On the activation that brings the Druid to 0 toughness the counter is already on it while the untap ability still sits on the stack, so the next SBA check puts the Druid in the graveyard before the untap resolves. Critically it goes there with three -1/-1 counters and ZERO +1/+1 counters, and no other SBA fires alongside, so 704.8's trap does not apply and undying triggers cleanly.
    CR 605.1a — “it could add mana to a player’s mana pool when it resolves”
    CR 113.3b — “A player may activate such an ability whenever they have priority. Doing so puts it on the stack, where it remains until it’s countered, it resolves, or it otherwise leaves the stack.”
    CR 602.2a — “That ability is created on the stack as an object that’s not a card. It becomes the topmost object on the stack.”
    CR 602.2b — “The remainder of the process for activating an ability is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2b–i.”
    CR 601.2h — “The player pays the total cost.”
    CR 704.5f — “If a creature has toughness 0 or less, it’s put into its owner’s graveyard.”
    CR 608.2h — “If the effect requires information from a specific object, including the source of the ability itself, the effect uses the current information of that object if it’s in the public zone it was expected to be in; if it’s no longer in that zone, or if the effect has moved it from a public zone to a hidden zone, the effect uses the object’s last known information.”
    CR 609.3 — “If an effect attempts to do something impossible, it does only as much as possible.”
  14. Sub-question 4d — Mikaeus + Devoted Druid IS a true two-card infinite, but NOT a mana engine.
    Devoted Druid (Elf Druid, non-Human, 0/2 → 1/3 under Mikaeus) resets itself and needs no outlet. FIRST PASS, with the Druid not summoning-sick: three taps for {G} interleaved with three untaps takes it to 0 toughness; it dies counterless and undying returns it with a +1/+1 counter. That is THREE green, not four. EVERY PASS AFTER PRODUCES ZERO MANA — the returned Druid is a new object that has not been under your control since your most recent turn began, so 302.6 forbids its {T} ability. The loop still runs forever because the untap cost carries no tap or untap SYMBOL and is outside 302.6 entirely. STEADY STATE (no haste): one activation annihilates the +1/+1 counter, three more take it to 0 toughness and it dies counterless; undying returns it. Per cycle: FOUR -1/-1 placements, ONE death, ZERO mana. On the scenario's actual board that is four Hapatra Snakes and one Blood Artist drain per cycle; Nest of Scarabs and Obelisk Spider would add four Insects and four drains, but NEITHER IS ON THIS BOARD, so those outputs are conditional. WITH HASTE the returned Druid can tap again and the cycle becomes: (1) re-equip the haste source; (2) tap {G}; (3) untap-activate, annihilating the counter; (4) tap {G}; (5) untap; (6) tap {G}; (7) untap; (8) tap {G}; (9) untap → dies → undying returns it. Four green produced per cycle. Lightning Greaves is equip {0}, so it NETS 4 GREEN; Swiftfoot Boots is equip {1}, so it NETS 3. Step (1) is not optional: the Equipment unattaches the moment the Druid dies — it is then attached to a nonexistent permanent, a state-based unattachment — and the returned Druid is a different object, so it must be re-equipped every cycle. Equip can only be activated as a sorcery, and an ability with that restriction requires that you have priority, that it be the main phase of your turn, and that the stack be empty — so the three-card mana engine is main-phase-only and cannot run at instant speed. Greaves' shroud does not affect the loop (neither Druid ability targets) but does shut off Hapatra and Yawgmoth targeting the Druid.
    CR 302.6 — “A creature’s activated ability with the tap symbol or the untap symbol in its activation cost can’t be activated unless the creature has been under its controller’s control continuously since their most recent turn began.”
    CR 400.7 — “An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence.”
    CR 702.10c — “If a creature has haste, its controller can activate its activated abilities whose cost includes the tap symbol or the untap symbol even if that creature hasn’t been controlled by that player continuously since their most recent turn began.”
    CR 704.5q — “If a permanent has both a +1/+1 counter and a -1/-1 counter on it, N +1/+1 and N -1/-1 counters are removed from it, where N is the smaller of the number of +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters on it.”
    CR 122.2 — “Counters on an object are not retained if that object moves from one zone to another.”
    CR 704.5n — “If an Equipment or Fortification is attached to an illegal permanent or to a player, it becomes unattached from that permanent or player. It remains on the battlefield.”
    CR 301.5c — “An Equipment that equips an illegal or nonexistent permanent becomes unattached from that permanent but remains on the battlefield. (This is a state-based action. See rule 704.)”
    CR 702.6a — “Attach this permanent to target creature you control. Activate only as a sorcery.”
    CR 602.5d — “Activated abilities that read “Activate only as a sorcery” mean the player must follow the timing rules for casting a sorcery spell, though the ability isn’t actually a sorcery.”
    CR 307.5 — “it means only that the player must have priority, it must be during the main phase of their turn, and the stack must be empty”
  15. Mikaeus + Walking Ballista is a THREE-card line, and here is why.
    Ballista is printed 0/0. Mikaeus's +1/+1 is a layer-7c modification applied alongside counters, so a counterless Ballista is a 1/1 under Mikaeus, not a 0/0. Its toughness is above 0, so 704.5f never fires and the classic 'remove the last counter and it dies' pattern is switched off. An external free outlet is therefore mandatory. Per iteration: 1 damage plus whatever the outlet yields. Genuinely unbounded, but only as a three-card assembly.
    CR 613.4c — “Layer 7c: Effects and counters that modify power and/or toughness (but don’t set power and/or toughness to a specific number or value) are applied.”
    CR 704.5f — “If a creature has toughness 0 or less, it’s put into its owner’s graveyard.”
    CR 702.93a — “if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, return it to the battlefield under its owner’s control with a +1/+1 counter on it.”
  16. Loop bookkeeping for the lines that are genuinely unbounded.
    These loops contain mandatory components — Hapatra's Snake trigger and Blood Artist's drain fire whether you want them to or not, and undying is not a 'may'. But the DRIVER of every iteration is an optional action the controller chooses: activating the Druid's untap ability, or activating an outlet. Because the loop contains an optional action it is not a mandatory loop and the game is not a draw; you announce a number of iterations as a shortcut and end where a player has priority. CONVERSION, correcting my earlier claim that a payoff is still needed: Blood Artist is already on the scenario board, so the Druid loop converts directly — one life from a player of your choice per cycle, repeated until each opponent is dead. Obelisk Spider would convert four times faster but is not on this board. The Ballista line converts to damage without needing any drain at all.
    CR 104.4b — “Loops that contain an optional action don’t result in a draw.”
    CR 732.4 — “If a loop contains only mandatory actions, the game is a draw.”
    CR 732.2a — “The ending point of this sequence must be a place where a player has priority”
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 117.3b — “The active player receives priority after a spell or ability (other than a mana ability) resolves.”

THE PAINT

EIGHTY HANDS

Nobody painted this deck — eighty artists share its 88 cards, and no one holds more than three. So the credit goes to the whole field, led by the man who happened to paint both the signature sweeper and the verified kill piece.

Daniel Ljunggren tops this roster with three cards, and in an eighty-artist field that is not a signature — it is the crest of a perfectly flat wave. John Avon also has three. Nobody else clears two. The analysis says it plainly: no standout, so the honest feature is the breadth itself.

And the breadth has a cause worth stating out loud: nobody chose these printings. The decklist names cards, not editions, and each entry carries whatever printing the resolver returned first — which is why the largest single stamp is a reprint set holding twelve cards by twelve different artists, and why there is not one foil in the hundred. No curation happened here, and we won't invent taste where none was exercised.

What luck delivered is still worth the page. Ljunggren's three are Black Sun's Zenith, Forbidden Orchard and Walking Ballista — the deck's signature sweeper, its most mischievous land, and the verified kill piece. If one set of hands had to lead a flat field, the ledger could have done far worse.
