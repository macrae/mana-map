READ HER RIGHT

RUN THE MINT

Why does a 2/2 that never wants to attack keep winning long games? Because Hapatra is a mint, not an engine — other cards place the counters, she pays you a deathtouch Snake per placement, and every Snake becomes a card or a drain.

Here's your first winning move: read Hapatra as a mint, not an engine. Her second ability pays out one 1/1 deathtouch Snake per placement *event* — other cards place the counters, she prints the bodies — so Black Sun's Zenith for three on one creature mints one Snake, while Black Sun's Zenith for one across six creatures mints six. Her combat trigger is never the plan: verified stack 001 (step 11) classifies it as a combat-damage trigger on an unevasive 2/2, worth at most one extra iteration per combat. She is, however, the most reliable card you own — turn 2 in 9,970 of 10,000 goldfish runs, mean 2.007, 99.9% by turn 6.

Because she's a mint, the scarce class is the *counter source*, and this is the trap most pilots never see. Stack 001's adversarial census counted eight sacrifice outlets that cost no mana — Ashnod's Altar, Phyrexian Altar, Carrion Feeder, Viscera Seer, Yahenni, Yawgmoth, Blasting Station, and Lazotep Quarry once per turn — and exactly *one* of them, Yawgmoth, Thran Physician, also places a -1/-1 counter. The only other free repeatable source is Devoted Druid, and only while Mikaeus is out to reset it. Two cards deep — which is why your eight tutors are the engine room, not a luxury: one is drawn in 78.0% of games, 67.6% by turn 6.

The median game — most games — you win by out-carding three opponents and then draining them. Skullclamp turns every 1/1 into two cards for {1}; Grim Haruspex draws off each nontoken death; Deadly Dispute and Village Rites cash a spare body at instant speed; Morbid Opportunist collects once a turn no matter whose creatures die. And every deathtouch Snake blanks an attacker of any size, so the board that's drawing you cards is also the wall nobody wants to swing into. The goldfish names this game 'commander plus any -1/-1 payoff': 67.2% assembled, mean turn 3.62 — that's your real identity.

The ceiling is real, just narrower than the bracket sheet suggests — pilot it that way and you'll cash it more often, not less. The kill is a repeatable source feeding a converter that hits every seat: Zulaport Cutthroat, Bastion of Remembrance or Obelisk Spider, with Blood Artist one tier down because he drains a single target player per death. Obelisk Spider is the tutor target worth naming — it drains on counter *placement* rather than death, roughly four times faster on any loop — and a drain converter is drawn in 51.9% of games, 41.5% by turn 6. Pivot when both halves are live and you've counted the table's spent interaction, and hold the honest line on the bracket: the floor of 4 stands on the 11 Game Changers, not on the inflated '19 two-card infinites' — knowing that keeps you dangerous.

ROUTE OR RERACK

The keep test is one question: is there a route to a repeatable counter source? Ledger's receipts say you keep 79.9% of first sevens — so be picky. You can afford it.

KEEP — the shape you want

Swamp, Forest, Bayou, Llanowar Elves, Yawgmoth, Thran Physician, Skullclamp, Night's Whisper

Three lands, a turn-1 dork, the one free repeatable source in the 99, and two ways to cash it. Hapatra waits in the zone — this hand holds the card the zone can't promise.

KEEP — two lands, engine attached

Swamp, Command Tower, Elvish Mystic, Demonic Tutor, Blood Artist, Village Rites, Blowfly Infestation

A two-lander carries with a dork attached, and Demonic Tutor is any card — the census answer is one fetch away. Keep Blowfly as what it reliably is, a counter source for the grind, never half of an unverified kill.

SHIP — payoffs without a source

Swamp, Forest, City of Brass, Mana Confluence, Zulaport Cutthroat, Blood Artist, Bastion of Remembrance

Four lands, three converters, nothing placing counters. The goldfish keeps this hand — its rule only counts lands — and you are smarter than the goldfish: these are blanks waiting for a card you haven't drawn yet.

SHIP — one land and a prayer

Forest, Lotus Petal, Mikaeus, the Unhallowed, Nest of Scarabs, Grim Affliction, Heroic Intervention, Morbid Opportunist

A Petal that finds no second land loses the commander, and Mikaeus is your only six-drop — a combo piece for two named partners, not a plan. Mean mulligans run 0.243; shipping this is what the average is made of.

The keep test is not 'does this hand have a payoff'. It's: **is there a route to a repeatable counter source?** Hapatra mints Snakes from counters other cards place, so a hand of Nest of Scarabs, Blood Artist and Zulaport Cutthroat with nothing placing counters is a hand of blanks waiting for a card you haven't drawn yet.

The numbers say you can afford to be picky and rarely have to be. The goldfish keeps its first seven 79.9% of the time (mean 0.243 mulligans), casts Hapatra on turn 2 in 9,970 of 10,000 runs (mean 2.007, 99.9% by turn 6), and has a tutor in hand 67.6% of the time by turn 6. So a keep doesn't need to *contain* the engine — it needs lands, a turn-2 commander, and a card that finds one.

Keep on sight: two to four lands, Hapatra castable on turn 2, plus any of Yawgmoth, Devoted Druid, or any of the eight tutors. Keep two-landers with a dork or a mox — Birds of Paradise, Llanowar Elves, Elvish Mystic, Sol Ring, Lotus Petal, Dark Ritual — because that hand casts Hapatra on turn 1 or 2 with a play attached. Keep Skullclamp with any token maker; Bitterblossom alone is a card every other turn and rebuilds through wraths. And keep Blowfly Infestation as what it reliably is — a counter source — not as half of a two-card kill that has never been verified.

Ship these. Five or more lands with no tutor or dork: the curve's mean mana value is 2.19 and cannot use the seventh land. Drain converters (Blood Artist, Zulaport Cutthroat, Bastion of Remembrance) with no source and no tutor — those are the blanks the mint metaphor warns about. Hands built around Mikaeus alone: he's your only six-drop, he's not a source by himself, and he's a combo piece for two specific partners, not a general-purpose enabler.

Ship one-landers even with a mox — a mox that finds no second land loses the commander. And treat hands whose only route is Scurry Oak, Ivy Lane Denizen or Host of the Hereafter as hands with *no* route: those lines are unresolved, and you don't mulligan-plan around a combo this manual won't assert.

On a mulligan to six, the priorities compress to exactly two things: lands that produce black on turn 2, and one card that either places counters repeatedly or finds one that does.

TABLE READ

HARMLESS UNTIL PROVEN GUILTY

What does the table see? A two-mana 2/2 and a few 1/1 Snakes — the quietest board in the pod. How long they keep believing it is entirely a sequencing decision, and it's yours.

Start with the correction that reorganises everything else: Hapatra is a mint, not an engine. Her first ability is a combat-damage trigger on an unevasive 2/2 — stack 001 step 11 calls it "not an at-will repeatable placement," worth one extra iteration per combat at best. She does not make -1/-1 counters. She pays you a deathtouch Snake every time another card places them.

So the scarce resource is the counter SOURCE — not the outlet, not the payoff. Stack 001's census counted eight mana-free sacrifice outlets (Ashnod's Altar, Phyrexian Altar, Carrion Feeder, Viscera Seer, Yahenni, Yawgmoth, Blasting Station, Lazotep Quarry) and exactly one, Yawgmoth, also places a counter. Everything downstream is abundant; the enabler class is two deep. Read your hands and your tutors through that lens — strategy:mulligans.engine-hands says payoffs without the enabler are blanks until the engine exists — and read the table through it too.

Now the hardest fact to own, and the one that drives your threat ranking from the pre-game onward: your interaction covers exactly one of the five permanent classes. Eight removal slots, two sweepers and four protection slots all answer creatures. There is no artifact removal, no enchantment removal, no counterspell and no land answer. The only graveyard-touching effect in the list is Abstergo Entertainment's {3}, {T}, exile-itself ability — it returns a historic card, exiles graveyards, and destroys nothing.

strategy:deckbuilding.interaction-suite is blunt about the cost: breadth before depth, because "three opponents mean every permanent type appears." So invert the usual habit. A creature is never the threat you fear most — you have eight answers, two sweepers and a wall of deathtouch for creatures. The threats that beat you are the ones you will never remove: a Sphere effect, Winter Orb, Blood Moon, a Rest-in-Peace-class hoser, a Rule-of-Law effect, an artifact or enchantment combo assembling in front of you.

strategy:threat-assessment says it straight: "an answer only counts when it matches a threat that actually beats you." Rank by permanent class before you rank by size. If two seats can present prison permanents, your role is decided before the first land drop — you do not own the long game under an assembling lock, so you are the beatdown racing the lock's completion (strategy:threat-assessment.resource-denial). Your outs are speed, politics and someone else's removal; name them out loud on turn one and sequence to protect them (strategy:resource-hedging.playing-to-outs).

Here is your asset: what the table sees early is cheap and defensive. Hapatra costs two and lands on turn 2 in 9,970 of 10,000 goldfish runs (mean cast turn 2.007, median 2, 99.9% by turn 6), and 'commander plus any -1/-1 payoff' assembles 67.2% of the time at a mean turn of 3.623, 55.7% by turn 6. A 2/2 Cleric and a couple of 1/1 Snakes read as a defensive board — exactly the posture strategy:multiplayer.threat-deflection tells you to buy.

But this list spends that cover faster than most, and the 11 Game Changers are the tell: Chrome Mox, Mox Diamond, Mana Vault, Grim Monolith, Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Imperial Seal, Worldly Tutor, Survival of the Fittest, Orcish Bowmasters, The One Ring. The turn a Mox hits the battlefield or a Demonic Tutor resolves, the Snakes stop reading as chump blockers and start reading as a combo deck's early board. strategy:multiplayer.threat-deflection notes the table discounts your "I haven't done anything" precisely because it tracks mana and draw engines.

So sequence like a politician. Lead with the token half and the cheap counter sources. Deploy fast mana on the turn it buys you a threat, not on turn one for its own sake, and fire tutors on a turn when someone else has just done something louder. The One Ring is the loudest card you own and it costs escalating life every upkeep — cast it when you want the cards, not when you want the protection turn to look clever.

The flip from ignored to archenemy has three concrete triggers, and all three are the second permanent — never Hapatra herself. First: Yawgmoth plus a second body with Hapatra out. Stack 001 step 12 verifies the shape — pay 1 life, sacrifice another creature, put a -1/-1 counter on up to one target creature and draw — and the trap inside it: the ability has a single instance of "target," so an illegal target on resolution means it does not resolve at all and you do not even draw. Hence the mandatory second body, and hence Hapatra's Snakes filling that slot.

The A/B loop repeats for real but is library-bounded by its own mandatory draw — a strong engine, not a true infinite. Any competent opponent kills Yawgmoth on sight. Do not present him into open mana unless the turn converts.

Second: Mikaeus, the Unhallowed alongside Devoted Druid — your one verified true two-card infinite (stack 001 steps 13-14). Infinite deaths, -1/-1 placements, Snakes and drain, and explicitly NOT mana: three green on the first pass and zero on every pass after, because the returned Druid is a new object and 302.6 blocks its tap ability. Steady state is four counter placements and one death per cycle at zero mana. With Blood Artist already on board it converts directly at one life per cycle from a player of your choice (step 16).

Third: Mikaeus plus Walking Ballista plus any free outlet — genuinely unbounded, and it converts to damage with no drain payoff at all. It is a three-card assembly, because Mikaeus's +1/+1 is a layer-7c modification that makes a counterless Ballista a 1/1 and switches off the classic self-kill (step 15). Mikaeus costs six; goldfish mean available mana is 6.150 on turn 6. 'Mikaeus + a free sacrifice outlet' is drawn 9.5% of the time overall, 5.8% by turn 6, mean turn 5.092 — and that target overstates its own value, because stack 001 refuted six of the eight Mikaeus entries it was built to track.

The last piece of threat management is aimed at your own self-image. The bracket-4 floor is safe — the 11 Game Changers carry it by themselves — but the '19 two-card infinites' figure in the bracket report is inflated, and you will not repeat it in a Rule 0 conversation or believe it at the table. Two lines are verified: Mikaeus + Devoted Druid, and Mikaeus + Walking Ballista as a three-card assembly.

Six are refuted outright by stack 001, checker verdict pass over four iterations. Mikaeus with Ashnod's Altar, Phyrexian Altar, Carrion Feeder, Viscera Seer, Yahenni or Blasting Station each yields exactly one extra undying return. Snake tokens yield zero — a token in a zone other than the battlefield ceases to exist before its undying trigger is even placed on the stack.

Eleven remain unresolved: the three Blowfly Infestation entries, Scurry Oak + Ivy Lane Denizen, the six outlet-paired Ivy Lane entries, and Hapatra + Host of the Hereafter. None of the eleven may be spoken of as fact or tapped out for. The Blowfly line is the one the goldfish tracks (16.3% assembled, 12.5% by turn 6, mean turn 4.102), and it is exactly the one with no stack scenario behind it — treat it as a strong board, not a kill, until it is verified.

Separate ceiling from median and pace for the median (strategy:critical-mass, strategy:deckbuilding.threat-density). The median is genuinely strong — you out-card three opponents in most games without ever going off. And when you choose to be the archenemy, take one counted pivot window rather than the first loop that looks live: strategy:multiplayer.pivot-window says assume every held answer appears the moment you present lethal. Count the table's open mana first, and prefer the attempt that leaves Bitterblossom, Snakes and a drain converter behind if it fails.

Sweeper control first, because you are built for it — and you will still sequence like you are not. Your token half is nearly free to lose. Bitterblossom rebuilds every upkeep for 1 life, and Skullclamp turns a 1/1 Snake into two cards for {1} — it gives +1/-1, so the Snake dies on equip. Grim Haruspex draws off every nontoken death, and Blood Artist triggers on ANY creature dying, so a table wrath with Blood Artist out is a large drain aimed wherever you like. That resilience was bought at deckbuild time — exactly what strategy:resource-hedging.wrath-math means by buying resilience instead of restraint.

The asymmetry runs the other way on your combo half. Hapatra, Yawgmoth, Devoted Druid, Ivy Lane Denizen, Scurry Oak and Necroskitter are all creatures, so a wrath is close to one-sided against the half that wins and cheap against the half that grinds. Deploy in waves: tokens and cheap counter sources first, the enabler held in hand until the pivot window. Assume the wrath — over a four-player game one is near-certain.

Your own sweepers are not interchangeable, and this is the single most valuable sequencing fact in the matchup. Black Sun's Zenith puts X -1/-1 counters on each creature: it triggers Hapatra once per creature, feeds Nest of Scarabs, and hands Necroskitter every opposing creature that dies with a counter on it. That is a rebuild, not a reset. Toxic Deluge gives -X/-X until end of turn and places no counters — zero triggers for Hapatra, Nest and Necroskitter, with an additional cost of X life. It is a pure panic button; sequence it as one.

Heroic Intervention is the card that flips this matchup — hold it for the turn you go, not the turn you feel sad. One caution, stated plainly: on oracle text it grants hexproof and indestructible, which does not obviously answer a -X/-X effect that kills by toughness rather than destruction. That reading deserves its own stack scenario before you bet a game on it.

Against stax and tax the breadth gap gets its bill, and the honest coaching is that you cannot answer the archetype — you race it or you politick it. There is no artifact removal, no enchantment removal and no counterspell in the list, so a Sphere effect, a Winter Orb, a Blood Moon or a graveyard hoser sits there permanently. strategy:deckbuilding.interaction-suite names stax as "the hardest breadth test" and requires answers castable under the tax; you have none of the right class. Here is why I still like your seat — three reasons.

One: you are fast and cheap. 36 lands with zero entering tapped, 16 ramp producers — 9 rocks, 5 dorks and 2 rituals — including three zero-cost rocks, and a commander who lands on turn 2 in 9,970 of 10,000 goldfish runs (mean 2.007). You are frequently under the lock before the second and third pieces layer — and layering is the whole game here, since strategy:threat-assessment.resource-denial notes that one piece is an inconvenience while the compounding second and third are the lock.

Two: your engines are mana-free once assembled, which is the exact profile that survives a tax. Yawgmoth's activation costs 1 life and a body and no mana. The Devoted Druid loop's steady state produces four counter placements and one death per cycle at zero mana (verified stack 001 step 14), and Bitterblossom triggers on your upkeep for free. Recount your hand under the tax the way that section says — then notice the part of your deck that wins is the part the tax does not touch.

Three: you can tell the truth. strategy:multiplayer.politics warns that credibility is a multi-game resource, so say out loud that you are the seat with no artifact or enchantment answer, and ask the player holding the Krosan Grip to spend it. Keep the deal small, specific and expiring — one turn of your -1/-1 counters pointed elsewhere for one piece of enchantment removal.

And know your tutor default. Stack 001's final state names the target that matters: a free repeatable -1/-1 source, not any particular altar. A tutor is drawn in 78.0% of games, 67.6% by turn 6, mean turn 2.695 — under an assembling lock, fetch the source, then the converter, and take Green Sun's Zenith for Devoted Druid straight onto the battlefield when you need the tempo.

Against aggro you are favoured, and for a reason that never shows up in a removal count: a 1/1 deathtouch Snake is virtual interaction. It makes attacking into you a losing exchange regardless of the attacker's size, and a board of them turns an opposing creature deck's best cards into cards it cannot profitably use. That is strategy:card-advantage.virtual exactly — "a card that blanks several opposing cards generates advantage without drawing anything" — and the Commander correction is the part that matters: at a four-player table virtual advantage triples, because one effect can blank attack plans from three players at once, the cheapest way to answer roughly 120 life worth of aggression. Even trample trades up for you — you eat the overflow and their creature dies to a token that cost you nothing.

The goldfish puts mean bodies at 1.942 by turn 4 and 3.162 by turn 6, and that is a floor rather than a ceiling — the model simulates resource development rather than full games and counts bodies as creatures cast plus tokens parsed from "create ... token" text. Behind the Snakes, Black Sun's Zenith and Toxic Deluge are your catch-up buttons. Necroskitter converts their aggression into your board — it has wither, and any creature an opponent controls that dies with a -1/-1 counter on it comes back under your control.

One honesty flag: the frame's read that Necroskitter alongside Black Sun's Zenith is your single best card against a creature table is oracle reasoning only. Necroskitter is verified absent from the combo graph and the pairing has no stack scenario. Plan for it; do not promise it.

The real discipline here is life. This deck spends it constantly — Yawgmoth at 1 per activation, Phyrexian Reclamation at 2 per return, Bitterblossom every upkeep, The One Ring's burden counters, Toxic Deluge's additional cost, and City of Brass, Mana Confluence and Tarnished Citadel in the mana base — so your life total is a shared budget with your engine. Track which single opponent's clock is actually real and defend against that one rather than the table (strategy:multiplayer.pod-management). And do not convert a good matchup into a bad seat by attacking with Hapatra into open blockers for a marginal combat trigger.

Against combo you are thin in both directions — play it as a race, not an interaction game. You have no counterspell and no way to touch an artifact or enchantment kill, so the only combo decks you can interact with are creature-based. There you do hold real instant-speed answers: Grim Affliction, Fume Spitter's free sacrifice, Walking Ballista's counter-removal ping, Serrated Arrows' tap.

What survives, verified. **Mikaeus + Devoted Druid is a true two-card infinite** — of deaths, -1/-1 placements, Snakes and drain, and explicitly **not of mana**. The Druid's untap ability pays a -1/-1 counter as a cost, so it kills itself into a counterless graveyard and undying returns it cleanly. Now the clause everyone misreads: the returned Druid is a *new object* that has not been under your control since your turn began, so rule 302.6 forbids its {T} ability — three green on the first pass, **zero on every pass after**. Steady state without haste: four counter placements, one death, zero mana per cycle. With one, it is genuinely unbounded at 1 damage per iteration plus whatever the outlet yields, and it converts to a win with no drain payoff at all.

What is refuted. **Six of the listed infinites fail outright.** Mikaeus paired with Ashnod's Altar, Phyrexian Altar, Carrion Feeder, Viscera Seer, Yahenni or Blasting Station yields exactly *one* undying return: the creature comes back wearing a +1/+1 counter, and on the second sacrifice the intervening 'if' is false — undying never triggers.     CR 702.93a — “if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, return it to the battlefield under its owner’s control with a +1/+1 counter on it.”
  3. Both death triggers fire: the Snake's undying and Blood Artist.
    Undying is a leaves-the-battlefield ability, so it looks back in time — the game uses the Snake as it appeared immediately before the event, when it still had undying and no +1/+1 counters. Both fire before the token ceases to exist.
    CR 603.10a — “Some zone-change triggers look back in time. These are leaves-the-battlefield abilities, abilities that trigger when a player sacrifices a permanent”
    CR 111.7 — “applicable triggered abilities will trigger before the token ceases to exist”
  4. Before either trigger goes on the stack, state-based actions are checked — and the token ceases to exist.
    The decisive ordering point. SBAs are performed first as a single event, and only when a check performs none do waiting triggers get put on the stack. The token is in a zone other than the battlefield, so it ceases to exist in that first check. By the time undying is placed on the stack, the object it wants to return no longer exists in any zone.
    CR 704.3 — “the game checks for any of the listed conditions for state-based actions, then performs all applicable state-based actions simultaneously as a single event”
    CR 704.3 — “otherwise all triggered abilities that are waiting to be put on the stack are put on the stack, then the check is repeated”
    CR 704.5d — “If a token is in a zone other than the battlefield, it ceases to exist.”
  5. Sub-question 1b — the undying trigger resolves and does nothing.
    A token that has left the battlefield cannot come back. The return instruction is impossible, so the ability does only as much as possible, which is nothing. Undying on a token is a dead grant. Blood Artist targets a player, so it is unaffected.
    CR 111.8 — “A token that has left the battlefield can’t move to another zone or come back onto the battlefield. If such a token would change zones, it remains in its current zone instead.”
    CR 609.3 — “If an effect attempts to do something impossible, it does only as much as possible.”
  6. Blood Artist's trigger resolves.
    Its target is still legal: an opponent loses 1 life, you gain 1. Net for the scenario: {C}{C}, one drain, and the Snake gone permanently. No loop has begun.
    CR 608.2b — “If the spell or ability specifies targets, it checks whether the targets are still legal.”
    CR 117.3b — “The active player receives priority after a spell or ability (other than a mana ability) resolves.”
  7. Sub-question 2 — counter annihilation is a state-based action.
    As soon as a player would receive priority, if a permanent has both a +1/+1 and a -1/-1 counter, N of each are removed. One of each leaves zero. There is no window in which the creature holds both while a player has priority.
    CR 704.5q — “If a permanent has both a +1/+1 counter and a -1/-1 counter on it, N +1/+1 and N -1/-1 counters are removed from it, where N is the smaller of the number of +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters on it.”
    CR 122.3 — “If a permanent has both a +1/+1 counter and a -1/-1 counter on it, N +1/+1 and N -1/-1 counters are removed from it as a state-based action”
  8. Sub-question 3 — annihilation does reset undying, with one sharp exception.
    If the annihilation happened in an earlier SBA check and the creature survived, then at its later death it genuinely has zero +1/+1 counters and undying triggers. THE TRAP: if the -1/-1 counters both annihilate and take toughness to 0 in the SAME check, last known information is read from before any of those SBAs — the +1/+1 counter is still visible and undying does NOT trigger. The CR's own example is exactly this case. Strip the counter first, let SBAs run, kill it separately.
    CR 603.4 — “If the ability triggers, it checks the stated condition again as it resolves. If the condition isn’t true at that time, the ability is removed from the stack and does nothing.”
    CR 704.8 — “If a state-based action results in a permanent leaving the battlefield at the same time other state-based actions were performed, that permanent’s last known information is derived from the game state before any of those state-based actions were performed.”
    CR 704.8 — “When it was last on the battlefield, it had a +1/+1 counter on it, so undying will not trigger.”
  9. Sub-question 4a — for Snake tokens the loop produces ZERO iterations.
    Mikaeus plus an outlet fed by Hapatra's Snakes is not a loop. Each Snake gives {C}{C} and one drain, then ceases to exist. Any line assuming 'sacrifice a token, get it back' is invalid on its face.
    CR 704.5d — “If a token is in a zone other than the battlefield, it ceases to exist.”
    CR 111.8 — “A token that has left the battlefield can’t move to another zone or come back onto the battlefield. If such a token would change zones, it remains in its current zone instead.”
  10. Sub-question 4b — for a non-token creature with a counter-neutral outlet, exactly ONE extra death.
    Iteration 1: sacrifice it with no counters, undying returns it as a new object WITH a +1/+1 counter. Iteration 2: sacrifice again — it dies carrying that counter, the intervening 'if' is false, undying never triggers. Total: 4 colorless and 2 drains. This is the verdict for the six two-card entries whose outlet places no -1/-1 counter: Ashnod's Altar, Phyrexian Altar, Carrion Feeder, Viscera Seer, Yahenni, Blasting Station. Carrion Feeder is actively anti-synergistic — it ADDS +1/+1 counters. FULL OUTLET CENSUS, since my earlier counts were wrong twice: eight of this deck's sacrifice outlets have no mana in their activation cost — Ashnod's Altar, Phyrexian Altar, Carrion Feeder, Viscera Seer, Yahenni and Yawgmoth (all activatable without tapping), plus Blasting Station ({T}, but it untaps itself whenever a creature enters, which an undying loop supplies) and Lazotep Quarry ({T}, no untapper, so once per turn and unable to loop). Exactly one of the eight — Yawgmoth — also places a -1/-1 counter.
    CR 702.93a — “if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, return it to the battlefield under its owner’s control with a +1/+1 counter on it.”
    CR 603.4 — “If the ability triggers, it checks the stated condition again as it resolves. If the condition isn’t true at that time, the ability is removed from the stack and does nothing.”
    CR 400.7 — “An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence.”
  11. Sub-question 4c — Hapatra does NOT supply the reset. The scenario's claim fails here.
    Her ability is a combat-damage trigger: it goes on the stack only when Hapatra connects. It is not an at-will repeatable placement and cannot be interleaved between sacrifices. At best it buys ONE extra iteration per combat.
    CR 113.3c — “Whenever the trigger event occurs, the ability is put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority”
  12. SUB-QUESTION 4, part C-bis — Yawgmoth, Thran Physician is one of the deck's EIGHT mana-free sacrifice outlets and the only one that is also a repeatable reset. It needs a SECOND body.
    "Pay 1 life, Sacrifice another creature: Put a -1/-1 counter on up to one target creature and draw a card." That counter annihilates an undying +1/+1 counter under 704.5q, so this outlet does not hit the step-10 wall. ON THE TARGETING, corrected: 601.2c (reached for activated abilities through 602.2b) locks targets BEFORE costs are paid at 601.2h, so you may perfectly legally choose the creature you are about to sacrifice. What stops it is resolution, not announcement — a target no longer in the zone it was in when targeted is illegal, and since this ability has only one instance of the word 'target', that single illegal target means it does not resolve at all. You would not even draw the card. Hence a second body is mandatory. That is a FOUR-CARD assembly — Mikaeus + Yawgmoth + two non-Human creatures — collapsing to three cards plus the commander's Snakes when Hapatra is on the battlefield, since each -1/-1 counter she sees placed generates fresh fodder. Loop with two non-Human creatures A and B: activate targeting B and sacrificing A → A returns with a +1/+1 counter, the ability resolves putting a -1/-1 counter on B. Next activation targets A (annihilating its counter) and sacrifices B. Symmetric, and it repeats. BUT each activation costs 1 life and the draw is mandatory, so the loop is LIBRARY-BOUNDED — run it out and you lose to an empty draw. A strong engine, not a true infinite. With Hapatra out, the counters generate Snakes, which supply the fodder half.
    CR 704.5q — “If a permanent has both a +1/+1 counter and a -1/-1 counter on it, N +1/+1 and N -1/-1 counters are removed from it, where N is the smaller of the number of +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters on it.”
    CR 602.2b — “The remainder of the process for activating an ability is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2b–i.”
    CR 601.2c — “The player announces their choice of an appropriate object or player for each target the spell requires.”
    CR 601.2h — “The player pays the total cost.”
    CR 608.2b — “A target that’s no longer in the zone it was in when it was targeted is illegal.”
    CR 702.93a — “if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, return it to the battlefield under its owner’s control with a +1/+1 counter on it.”
    CR 104.3c — “If a player is required to draw more cards than are left in their library, they draw the remaining cards and then lose the game the next time a player would receive priority.”
  13. Mechanism — Devoted Druid's untap ability uses the stack, and its counter is a cost.
    It cannot add mana, so it is an ordinary activated ability created on the stack. Its cost, the -1/-1 counter, is paid on activation, not resolution. On the activation that brings the Druid to 0 toughness the counter is already on it while the untap ability still sits on the stack, so the next SBA check puts the Druid in the graveyard before the untap resolves. Critically it goes there with three -1/-1 counters and ZERO +1/+1 counters, and no other SBA fires alongside, so 704.8's trap does not apply and undying triggers cleanly.
    CR 605.1a — “it could add mana to a player’s mana pool when it resolves”
    CR 113.3b — “A player may activate such an ability whenever they have priority. Doing so puts it on the stack, where it remains until it’s countered, it resolves, or it otherwise leaves the stack.”
    CR 602.2a — “That ability is created on the stack as an object that’s not a card. It becomes the topmost object on the stack.”
    CR 602.2b — “The remainder of the process for activating an ability is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2b–i.”
    CR 601.2h — “The player pays the total cost.”
    CR 704.5f — “If a creature has toughness 0 or less, it’s put into its owner’s graveyard.”
    CR 608.2h — “If the effect requires information from a specific object, including the source of the ability itself, the effect uses the current information of that object if it’s in the public zone it was expected to be in; if it’s no longer in that zone, or if the effect has moved it from a public zone to a hidden zone, the effect uses the object’s last known information.”
    CR 609.3 — “If an effect attempts to do something impossible, it does only as much as possible.”
  14. Sub-question 4d — Mikaeus + Devoted Druid IS a true two-card infinite, but NOT a mana engine.
    Devoted Druid (Elf Druid, non-Human, 0/2 → 1/3 under Mikaeus) resets itself and needs no outlet. FIRST PASS, with the Druid not summoning-sick: three taps for {G} interleaved with three untaps takes it to 0 toughness; it dies counterless and undying returns it with a +1/+1 counter. That is THREE green, not four. EVERY PASS AFTER PRODUCES ZERO MANA — the returned Druid is a new object that has not been under your control since your most recent turn began, so 302.6 forbids its {T} ability. The loop still runs forever because the untap cost carries no tap or untap SYMBOL and is outside 302.6 entirely. STEADY STATE (no haste): one activation annihilates the +1/+1 counter, three more take it to 0 toughness and it dies counterless; undying returns it. Per cycle: FOUR -1/-1 placements, ONE death, ZERO mana. On the scenario's actual board that is four Hapatra Snakes and one Blood Artist drain per cycle; Nest of Scarabs and Obelisk Spider would add four Insects and four drains, but NEITHER IS ON THIS BOARD, so those outputs are conditional. WITH HASTE the returned Druid can tap again and the cycle becomes: (1) re-equip the haste source; (2) tap {G}; (3) untap-activate, annihilating the counter; (4) tap {G}; (5) untap; (6) tap {G}; (7) untap; (8) tap {G}; (9) untap → dies → undying returns it. Four green produced per cycle. Lightning Greaves is equip {0}, so it NETS 4 GREEN; Swiftfoot Boots is equip {1}, so it NETS 3. Step (1) is not optional: the Equipment unattaches the moment the Druid dies — it is then attached to a nonexistent permanent, a state-based unattachment — and the returned Druid is a different object, so it must be re-equipped every cycle. Equip can only be activated as a sorcery, and an ability with that restriction requires that you have priority, that it be the main phase of your turn, and that the stack be empty — so the three-card mana engine is main-phase-only and cannot run at instant speed. Greaves' shroud does not affect the loop (neither Druid ability targets) but does shut off Hapatra and Yawgmoth targeting the Druid.
    CR 302.6 — “A creature’s activated ability with the tap symbol or the untap symbol in its activation cost can’t be activated unless the creature has been under its controller’s control continuously since their most recent turn began.”
    CR 400.7 — “An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence.”
    CR 702.10c — “If a creature has haste, its controller can activate its activated abilities whose cost includes the tap symbol or the untap symbol even if that creature hasn’t been controlled by that player continuously since their most recent turn began.”
    CR 704.5q — “If a permanent has both a +1/+1 counter and a -1/-1 counter on it, N +1/+1 and N -1/-1 counters are removed from it, where N is the smaller of the number of +1/+1 and -1/-1 counters on it.”
    CR 122.2 — “Counters on an object are not retained if that object moves from one zone to another.”
    CR 704.5n — “If an Equipment or Fortification is attached to an illegal permanent or to a player, it becomes unattached from that permanent or player. It remains on the battlefield.”
    CR 301.5c — “An Equipment that equips an illegal or nonexistent permanent becomes unattached from that permanent but remains on the battlefield. (This is a state-based action. See rule 704.)”
    CR 702.6a — “Attach this permanent to target creature you control. Activate only as a sorcery.”
    CR 602.5d — “Activated abilities that read “Activate only as a sorcery” mean the player must follow the timing rules for casting a sorcery spell, though the ability isn’t actually a sorcery.”
    CR 307.5 — “it means only that the player must have priority, it must be during the main phase of their turn, and the stack must be empty”
  15. Mikaeus + Walking Ballista is a THREE-card line, and here is why.
    Ballista is printed 0/0. Mikaeus's +1/+1 is a layer-7c modification applied alongside counters, so a counterless Ballista is a 1/1 under Mikaeus, not a 0/0. Its toughness is above 0, so 704.5f never fires and the classic 'remove the last counter and it dies' pattern is switched off. An external free outlet is therefore mandatory. Per iteration: 1 damage plus whatever the outlet yields. Genuinely unbounded, but only as a three-card assembly.
    CR 613.4c — “Layer 7c: Effects and counters that modify power and/or toughness (but don’t set power and/or toughness to a specific number or value) are applied.”
    CR 704.5f — “If a creature has toughness 0 or less, it’s put into its owner’s graveyard.”
    CR 702.93a — “if it had no +1/+1 counters on it, return it to the battlefield under its owner’s control with a +1/+1 counter on it.”
  16. Loop bookkeeping for the lines that are genuinely unbounded.
    These loops contain mandatory components — Hapatra's Snake trigger and Blood Artist's drain fire whether you want them to or not, and undying is not a 'may'. But the DRIVER of every iteration is an optional action the controller chooses: activating the Druid's untap ability, or activating an outlet. Because the loop contains an optional action it is not a mandatory loop and the game is not a draw; you announce a number of iterations as a shortcut and end where a player has priority. CONVERSION, correcting my earlier claim that a payoff is still needed: Blood Artist is already on the scenario board, so the Druid loop converts directly — one life from a player of your choice per cycle, repeated until each opponent is dead. Obelisk Spider would convert four times faster but is not on this board. The Ballista line converts to damage without needing any drain at all.
    CR 104.4b — “Loops that contain an optional action don’t result in a draw.”
    CR 732.4 — “If a loop contains only mandatory actions, the game is a draw.”
    CR 732.2a — “The ending point of this sequence must be a place where a player has priority”
    CR 704.5a — “If a player has 0 or less life, that player loses the game.”
    CR 117.3b — “The active player receives priority after a spell or ability (other than a mana ability) resolves.”

THE PAINT

EIGHTY HANDS

Nobody painted this deck — eighty artists share its 88 cards, and no one holds more than three. So the credit goes to the whole field, led by the man who happened to paint both the signature sweeper and the verified kill piece.

Daniel Ljunggren tops this roster with three cards, and in an eighty-artist field that is not a signature — it is the crest of a perfectly flat wave. John Avon also has three. Nobody else clears two. The analysis says it plainly: no standout, so the honest feature is the breadth itself.

And the breadth has a cause worth stating out loud: nobody chose these printings. The decklist names cards, not editions, and each entry carries whatever printing the resolver returned first — which is why the largest single stamp is a reprint set holding twelve cards by twelve different artists, and why there is not one foil in the hundred. No curation happened here, and we won't invent taste where none was exercised.

What luck delivered is still worth the page. Ljunggren's three are Black Sun's Zenith, Forbidden Orchard and Walking Ballista — the deck's signature sweeper, its most mischievous land, and the verified kill piece. If one set of hands had to lead a flat field, the ledger could have done far worse.
