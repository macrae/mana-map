FROM THE EDITOR

LIKED AND UNBEATABLE

Three players thank you for the cards, and mean it. Then, on each of their upkeeps, a piece of cardboard nobody has thought about in years asks how many they are holding.

MANA MAP
From the Editor
Vol. 4 · HELIOD, THE WARPED ECLIPSE

The deck in this issue hands every player at the table free cards, and then bills them for holding the cards.

That is the whole idea, and it is worth an issue because of how strange the seat feels. You spend your turn making three other people richer, and some of them thank you and mean it. Then, on each of their upkeeps — the step nobody looks at — a piece of cardboard asks how many cards they are holding and takes the difference out of their life total. You will not attack anybody. Your damage arrives while you are doing nothing, which is the only time it can arrive, because your mana is spoken for by the counterspell in your hand.

There is also a spectacular one-turn kill in here, and its reputation is the wrong way round. The Counselor has walked that line end to end and it is real. Ledger has the rate at which it turns up, and it is small enough to change what you do on turn four.

You will have a good time here if your favourite thing in this game is being liked and being unbeatable in the same evening, and if you can enjoy a turn where you do nothing on purpose. You will not, if you want your damage to be your own idea.

Coach Brightside opens by telling you the best card in the plan deals no damage. He gets narrowed for it twice, and the second one is the best thing in the department.

Exhibit one. Heliod, the Radiant Dawn is a white card: {2}{W}{W}, a 4/4 God whose entry returns an enchantment from your graveyard. His colour identity is blue and white anyway. CR 903.4 counts every mana symbol anywhere on the card, and the transform ability reads {3}{U/P}. One hybrid Phyrexian pip in an activation cost is why forty cards in this deck are allowed to ask for blue mana.

Now the guarantee, which is the reason the 99 looks like this. Your commander begins the game in the command zone and is the only card you always have (CR 903.4). Every table-draw engine here was chosen because the back face converts opponents' draws into your discount and your flash. The goldfish casts him on a median turn 4 and has him available in 90.2% of games by turn six.

The tax is on the record: {2}{W}{W} the first time, and each recast from the zone costs {2} more than the last (CR 903.8). Four mana, then six, then eight. Heliod also owes a toll nobody prints — every trip home refunds the flip, so you pay {3}{U/P} again, at sorcery speed only, on a later turn.

Coach's ledger on that: insure, do not re-buy. The Phyrexian pip takes two life instead of blue mana, Teferi's Protection buys a whole turn of immunity, and a held Swan Song is cheaper than paying both tolls twice. In this deck protection is the better spend by a wide margin.

Is the 21-damage clock a plan here (CR 903.10a)? No — a coincidence. The back face is a 4/6 that would need six clean connections in a deck whose attack step is ceremonial. Your clocks are Iron Maiden, Ebony Owl Netsuke and a Reservoir ledger.

The political read is worth more than the combat math. The front face registers as a harmless enchantment rebuy, and the flip is your tell: the turn the Warped Eclipse shows up, three opponents learn you have instant-speed range and a discount that grows every time they draw. Spend that information on the turn you choose.

Pilot TipTransform only at sorcery speed, so plan the flip a full rotation ahead and pay the Phyrexian pip with life whenever your blue mana is holding up a counterspell.

THE PLAN

PUNISHER FIRST, GIFTS SECOND

You are about to hand three opponents free cards, on purpose, every turn. Ten cards in this 99 fill their hands and six charge for the result — the order is the whole skill.

The table has you filed as the nice one. You handed out cards, you never attacked, you said "sure, take one" three times. Then Iron Maiden invoices them for every card you gave away, on their own upkeep, before they get to spend it.

That is the deck, and you should play it in that order. Ten cards make the whole table draw. Six cards charge for the resulting hand. Both halves are on the battlefield in 56.7% of games and 41.2% of games by turn six — so more than half your games are the clock game, and you play toward the clock. Practical version: when you are holding a punisher and a donation engine, land the punisher first or on the same turn. Never after. A Howling Mine that goes down naked is you paying three opponents rent.

Close with Approach of the Second Sun. You reach a win condition in 61.2% of games and 50.5% by turn six, which is the single most reliable ending this deck has — cast it once, let it slot seventh from the top, gain the 7, and set the redraw with Mystical Tutor when you can. Stack 002 walks the second cast and finds it survivable behind held countermagic. So keep one blue up for that turn instead of spending it on the scary-looking spell that isn't going to kill you.

And then the famous one. Hullbreaker Horror plus a cheap rock plus Aetherflux Reservoir is a verified three-opponent kill — stack 006 does the whole ledger — and you will assemble it in 0.7% of games, 2.4% if a tutor helps, 0.3% by turn six. Read that as a gift, not a plan. Do not hold a hand together waiting for it, do not mulligan toward it, and do not tap out hunting the fourth piece. When all four show up on the same turn with Grand Abolisher available, take it. Every other game, you are the punisher deck, and the punisher deck wins by being boring on schedule.

What this does not model

  • The Reservoir kill assembles in 0.7% of games, 2.4% with the tutor counted — and 0.7% is a ceiling rather than a rate. The requirement is satisfied by Sol Ring OR Arcane Signet, and stack 001 walked the one-rock board and found no loop at all.
  • Hullbreaker Horror is one card deep: 7.1% of opening sevens, 9.1% by turn three, and Long-Term Plans is the only search in the 99 that reaches a creature.
  • The third exit is claimed and unpriced. Jace, Wielder of Mysteries turns drawing from an empty library into a win, and nothing here says which turn this deck empties itself — or whether the Jace is on the battlefield when it does.
  • Unsettled How many upkeeps does the punisher clock actually need to kill one opponent, and how often does the deck get them? The pair (a donation engine plus a punisher) is measured at 56.7% assembled, 41.2% by turn six — but nothing measures the clock those two produce as a WIN, so the deck's median route to victory has no rate at all.
  • Unsettled Does the punisher rank need The Second Doctor or Folio of Fancies to be lethal at all? With a seven-card maximum hand size the cleanup discard caps Iron Maiden and Viseling at 3 damage per upkeep; those two cards remove the cap and nothing measures whether one of them is present.
  • Unsettled Can the Aetherflux Reservoir kill be executed through interaction? Stack 006 states every loop cast and every activation opens a fully open priority window on an unprotected board and names Grand Abolisher as the in-99 closer, but no stack has walked the kill with Abolisher deployed or with an opponent holding an instant.
  • Simulation 10,000 runs of resource development, not of games — no opponent acts in any of them.

3 further questions about this engine are open and unsettled.
