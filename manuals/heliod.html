THE PLAN

THE GENEROSITY TRAP

Why would a control deck hand every opponent an extra card, every turn, on purpose — and then add a rabbit who gives them life too? Because in this court the cards aren't a gift. They're evidence, and the sentence is read at each opponent's upkeep.

This deck hands the whole table a firehose of cards, then charges for the water. The engines — Howling Mine, Font of Mythos, Temple Bell, Dictate of Kruphix, Kami of the Crescent Moon, Folio of Fancies, and above all Forced Fruition — are perfectly symmetric on their face, and the new hug layer leans into it: Kwain offers everyone a card (and a life), The Second Doctor offers another at your end step. The punishers make the generosity anything but kind: Iron Maiden and Viseling deal upkeep damage equal to hand size minus 4, and Ebony Owl Netsuke deals a flat 4 to anyone at seven or more cards. Stack 003 verified the arithmetic that makes this a real clock: once Forced Fruition has filled a hand, cleanup trims the opponent to exactly 7 cards, and the next upkeep Owl hits for 4 and Maiden for 3 — 7 damage, from you, without tapping a single land. And that 7 is now the floor, not the ceiling: The Second Doctor's 'Players have no maximum hand size' means the cleanup trim never happens, so a ten-card hand takes 6 from the Maiden instead of 3 while the Owl keeps firing. Both artifacts trigger on each opponent's upkeep, so the tax runs three ways while you sit behind a counter suite the goldfish sim says you're holding in 73% of games — with Supreme Verdict, the uncounterable reset this deck previously lacked, waiting for the board states countermagic can't fix.

The clean kill is Approach of the Second Sun. The first cast gains 7 life — 14 with Alhammarret's Archive out — and buries the card seventh from the top: trivially shallow in a deck where everyone draws in bulk and two doublers (Archive, Teferi's Ageless Insight) accelerate your own digging, or Mystical Tutor and Long-Term Plans put it back on top directly. Stack 002 verified the second cast end to end: the win condition is checked during resolution, as a spell effect, and the first casting still counts because the game tracks the casting event in its history, not the card that got shuffled away. The opponents' only window is while the second Approach is on the stack. One honest cost of the rebuild: with White Sun's Zenith moved to the pool, win-condition access is drawn by turn 6 in only 42.6% of goldfish runs, down from 50% — the tutors carry more of the weight now, so commit on the turn you can protect it, because once resolution begins, nothing stops it.

The third piece is an engine, not a win: Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring + a two-mana rock (stack 004). Each rock's cast triggers the Horror to bounce the other rock — a legal target, because at that moment it is a permanent — and tapping each rock before it bounces makes the cycle exactly mana-neutral: 3 spent, 3 produced, unbounded casts in a single main phase. It nets zero mana, zero cards, zero damage on its own. The flagged lethal outlet is Aetherflux Reservoir — 1 life per spell cast this turn (doubled by the Archive), then Pay 50 life for 50 damage — but that line has not been stack-verified; treat it as a candidate, not a fact. Displacer Kitten's promise of making the loop mana-positive, by contrast, is settled: stack 005 refutes it — the loop stays exactly neutral, with a one-time +1-mana exit. And know what the deck does NOT do: the combo database's two-card Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring 'infinite' is refuted (stack 001). The pair alone produces nothing.

Heliod is the hinge that makes all of it asymmetric. The front face rebuys an enchantment on arrival; a sorcery-speed {3}{U/P} flips him into the Warped Eclipse, which grants everything flash and discounts your spells by {1} for each card your opponents drew this turn — and every card an opponent accepts from Kwain or the Second Doctor grows that discount on the turn you want to act. The goldfish sim has Heliod down by turn 6 in 90.8% of games (mean turn 4.1), the deck's most reliable single event, and the rebuild's engine additions lifted the core two-piece — table engine plus punisher — to 39.6% assembled by turn 6, up from 34.3%. Still not automatic: most games you play draw-go control while the pieces gather, and the deck is built to be good at exactly that.

Never pitch this deck to the table — or compute its bracket — on the strength of that database line.

THE READ

GUILTY OF LOOKING HARMLESS

At a bracket-4 table the gift bow fools nobody for long — professional pods have seen Kwain before, and they know the genre. So which misreads survive, and how do you spend the ones that do?

At a tier-4 table, forget the casual-room fantasy that hug reads as harmless. Professional pods have seen Kwain, Itinerant Meddler before, and they know the genre: symmetric draw plus hand-size punishers is a slug deck wearing a gift bow. Expect your 'I haven't done anything' to be discounted from turn two — experienced tables hit the quiet engine player precisely because combo and punisher decks advance in secret (strategy:multiplayer.threat-deflection). Your signal profile is loud on paper: 8 Game Changers force the bracket-4 floor, and three of them are read instantly. Rhystic Study on turn 2 is priced in at this tier — it draws groans, not removal. Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring: is the claimed infinite real?
Rules version June 19, 2026 · status: cleared in ? review cycle(s)
Verified
  1. You cast Sol Ring for {1}. The card moves from your hand to the stack and becomes a spell you control.
    Sol Ring is a spell on the stack (pos 0), controlled by you. While on the stack it is a spell, not a permanent.
    CR 601.2a — “To propose the casting of a spell, a player first moves that card (or that copy of a card) from where it is to the stack. It becomes the topmost object on the stack. It has all the characteristics of the card (or the copy of a card) associated with it, and that player becomes its controller.”
  2. Casting Sol Ring meets Hullbreaker Horror's trigger condition ('Whenever you cast a spell'). The triggered ability is put on the stack above Sol Ring the next time a player would receive priority.
    Stack, top to bottom: Hullbreaker Horror's trigger, then Sol Ring. The trigger will resolve (or leave the stack) before Sol Ring does.
    CR 113.3c — “Whenever the trigger event occurs, the ability is put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority and stays there until it’s countered, it resolves, or it otherwise leaves the stack.”
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority. See rule 117, “Timing and Priority.” The ability becomes the topmost object on the stack.”
  3. The trigger is modal ('choose up to one —'). You must announce the mode — and any target for that mode — now, as the ability is put on the stack, not when it resolves.
    Mode and target legality are locked in at this moment, while Sol Ring is still a spell on the stack.
    CR 603.3c — “If a triggered ability is modal, its controller announces the mode choice when putting the ability on the stack.”
    CR 700.2b — “The controller of a modal triggered ability chooses the mode(s) as part of putting that ability on the stack.”
    CR 603.3d — “The remainder of the process for putting a triggered ability on the stack is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2c–d.”
  4. Check mode 1 ('Return target spell you don't control to its owner's hand') against Sol Ring: the only spell on the stack is Sol Ring, and you control it.
    Sol Ring is not an 'appropriate object' for mode 1 — it fails the 'you don't control' requirement — so mode 1 cannot be chosen targeting Sol Ring. A mode whose only would-be target is illegal can't be chosen at all.
    CR 601.2c — “The player announces their choice of an appropriate object or player for each target the spell requires.”
    CR 603.3c — “If one of the modes would be illegal (due to an inability to choose legal targets, for example), that mode can’t be chosen.”
  5. Check mode 2 ('Return target nonland permanent to its owner's hand') against Sol Ring: while on the stack, Sol Ring is a spell, not a permanent.
    Mode 2 cannot target the Sol Ring being cast. Legal mode-2 targets at this moment are only existing nonland permanents — Hullbreaker Horror itself or the opponent's tapped creature — neither of which advances any loop.
    CR 110.1 — “A permanent is a card or token on the battlefield. A permanent remains on the battlefield indefinitely. A card or token becomes a permanent as it enters the battlefield and it stops being a permanent as it’s moved to another zone by an effect or rule.”
  6. You choose no mode ('up to one' permits zero). The ability is removed from the stack; players pass priority.
    The trigger leaves the stack with no effect. Sol Ring is now the top object on the stack.
    CR 603.3c — “If no mode is chosen, the ability is removed from the stack.”
    CR 608.1 — “Each time all players pass in succession, the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves.”
  7. Sol Ring resolves. It is a permanent spell with no targets, so it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under your control.
    Only now is Sol Ring a permanent — and Hullbreaker Horror's trigger from casting it is already gone. The trigger can never bounce the very spell that caused it: too early on the stack (illegal target for both modes), too late once it has resolved (the trigger no longer exists).
    CR 608.3 — “If the object that’s resolving is a permanent spell, its resolution may involve several steps.”
    CR 608.3a — “If the object that’s resolving has no targets, it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under the control of the spell’s controller.”
  8. You tap Sol Ring for {C}{C}. This is an activated mana ability: it does not use the stack and is not casting a spell.
    You add {C}{C}. Hullbreaker Horror does not trigger (nothing was cast), and no cast count / storm-relevant count increases. Activating Sol Ring any number of times per turn is impossible anyway — it taps.
    CR 605.3b — “An activated mana ability doesn’t go on the stack, so it can’t be targeted, countered, or otherwise responded to. Rather, it resolves immediately after it is activated.”
  9. Loop analysis for the pair alone: after step 7 there is no further spell to cast (only Counterspell, which is a one-shot, not a loop piece the claim relies on). Hullbreaker Horror only triggers on casts, and Sol Ring can only be re-bounced as a permanent — which requires some OTHER spell to be cast while Sol Ring is on the battlefield.

Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring alone produce neither unbounded colorless mana nor an unbounded storm count: the Horror trigger created by casting Sol Ring can never return that Sol Ring (illegal target under both modes while it is a spell; trigger already resolved once it is a permanent), and tapping Sol Ring is not a cast, so no new trigger ever arises. Even the {C}{C} produced does not accumulate across phases. The real loop needs a third card: with Sol Ring on the battlefield, casting any other recastable spell (cheapest with a {0}-cost artifact) lets the Horror bounce Sol Ring as a permanent, and recasting Sol Ring lets the Horror bounce the third piece — netting {C}{C} minus recast costs per iteration. Unbounded mana and unbounded casts exist only with that additional piece.
    CR 110.1 — “A card or token becomes a permanent as it enters the battlefield and it stops being a permanent as it’s moved to another zone by an effect or rule.”
    CR 113.3c — “Triggered abilities have a trigger condition and an effect.”
    CR 106.4 — “Each player’s mana pool empties at the end of each step and phase, and the player is said to lose this mana.”

    CR 115.6 — “A spell or ability that requires targets may allow zero targets to be chosen.”
    CR 608.2b — “If all its targets, for every instance of the word “target,” are now illegal, the spell or ability doesn’t resolve.”
  9. ORDERING B (blink-first): put Horror's trigger on the stack first, the Kitten trigger on top. The Kitten trigger resolves first: exile Sol Ring, then return it to the battlefield. It re-enters UNTAPPED as a new object, and you may immediately tap it for {C}{C} again (mana ability, no stack, no waiting).
    Sol Ring IS tapped for {C}{C} a second time within one cast — the blink-untap works exactly as the scenario hoped. Floating: {C}{C} extra.
    CR 400.7 — “An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence.”
    CR 110.5b — “Permanents enter the battlefield untapped, unflipped, face up, and phased in unless a spell or ability says otherwise.”
    CR 405.6c — “Mana abilities resolve immediately.”
  10. But Horror's trigger, targets locked at announcement on the PRE-blink Sol Ring, now resolves — and its target has changed zones (battlefield → exile → battlefield) and become a new object. A target no longer in the zone it was in when targeted is illegal; with its only target illegal, Horror's bounce does not resolve. Nothing is returned to your hand. After the Signet resolves (tap it for +1), your hand is EMPTY: no artifact can be recast, no further spells can be cast, no further triggers occur. The loop TERMINATES. (Having Horror target Kitten or Horror itself instead does not save it: recasting them costs {3}{U} / {5}{U}{U}, strictly mana-negative, and the {5}{U}{U} is unpayable from this engine's float.)
    The blink and the bounce are mutually exclusive per cast: whichever trigger resolves second loses its target to the new-object rule. Extra Sol Ring taps can only be bought at the price of ending the loop.
    CR 608.2b — “A target that’s no longer in the zone it was in when it was targeted is illegal.”
    CR 400.7 — “An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence.”
  11. Exact accounting of the one-shot blink exit (choose Ordering B on the FINAL iteration, starting from steady state: Sol Ring on battlefield untapped, Signet in hand, float 0): tap Sol Ring +{C}{C}; cast Signet −{2}; Kitten blinks Sol Ring, tap it again +{C}{C}; Horror's trigger fizzles; Signet resolves, tap +1 colored. Terminal float: 3 ({C}{C} plus one colored). Best exit WITHOUT using the Kitten: cast Signet (Sol Ring tap pays), Horror bounces Sol Ring, tap Signet +1, cast Sol Ring −1, Horror's modal trigger declines its mode ('up to one' — choosing no mode simply removes the ability), Sol Ring resolves, tap +{C}{C}: terminal float 2. The Kitten's blink is therefore worth one extra {C}{C} Sol Ring activation on exactly one cast, for a net gain of +1 mana over the best Kitten-less exit — ONCE per game state, not per cycle.
    Maximum achievable float after any number of loop iterations: 3 mana with the blink exit, 2 without. Bounded either way.
    CR 700.2b — “If no mode is chosen, the ability is removed from the stack.”
    CR 405.6c — “Mana abilities resolve immediately.”
  12. Verdict. The cycle is NOT mana-positive and total mana is NOT unbounded. The claim fails on two interlocking rules: targets are locked when each trigger is put on the stack, and an exiled-and-returned (or bounced) permanent is a new object — so the two triggers can never both act usefully on the same cast, and every cast offers exactly one useful battlefield target. What remains is precisely the stack-004 engine: unbounded CASTS at exactly zero net mana, now with a one-time +1 mana kicker on the final iteration (terminal float 3). Within these cards alone: an X spell could be paid for X no greater than the actual terminal float (at most 3 mana here — this is what the mana COULD pay for; no X spell is on this board), and a cast-count payoff such as Aetherflux Reservoir would profit from the unbounded cast count exactly as established in stack 004 — but no such card is on this board, and nothing among Hullbreaker Horror, Displacer Kitten, Sol Ring, and Arcane Signet converts casts into mana, damage, or cards.
    REFUTED: Displacer Kitten does not make the Hullbreaker Horror / Sol Ring / Arcane Signet loop mana-positive. It upgrades the loop's exit by exactly +1 mana, once.
