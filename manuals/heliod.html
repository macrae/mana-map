THE PLAN

THE GENEROSITY TRAP

Why would a control deck hand every opponent an extra card, every turn, on purpose? Because in this deck the cards aren't a gift — they're evidence, and the sentence is read at each opponent's upkeep.

This deck hands the whole table a firehose of cards, then charges for the water. The engines — Howling Mine, Font of Mythos, Temple Bell, Dictate of Kruphix, Kami of the Crescent Moon, Folio of Fancies, and above all Forced Fruition — are perfectly symmetric on their face. The punishers make them anything but: Iron Maiden and Viseling deal upkeep damage equal to hand size minus 4, and Ebony Owl Netsuke deals a flat 4 to anyone at seven or more cards. Stack 003 verified the arithmetic that makes this a real clock: once Forced Fruition has filled a hand, cleanup trims the opponent to exactly 7 cards, and the next upkeep Owl hits for 4 and Maiden for 3 — 7 damage, from you, without tapping a single land. Both artifacts trigger on each opponent's upkeep, so the tax runs three ways while you sit behind counterspells. That is the default win: attrition with inevitability, defended by a counter suite the goldfish sim says you're holding in 73% of games.

The clean kill is Approach of the Second Sun. The first cast gains 7 life and buries the card seventh from the top — trivially shallow in a deck where everyone draws in bulk and Teferi's Ageless Insight doubles your own draws — or Mystical Tutor and Long-Term Plans put it back on top directly. Stack 002 verified the second cast end to end: the win condition is checked during resolution, as a spell effect, and the first casting still counts because the game tracks the casting event in its history, not the card that got shuffled away. The opponents' only window is while the second Approach is on the stack. Commit on the turn you can protect it — Swan Song countering their counter was walked through explicitly — because once resolution begins, nothing stops it.

The third piece is an engine, not a win: Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring + a two-mana rock (stack 004). Each rock's cast triggers the Horror to bounce the other rock — a legal target, because at that moment it is a permanent — and tapping each rock before it bounces makes the cycle exactly mana-neutral: 3 spent, 3 produced, unbounded casts in a single main phase. It nets zero mana, zero cards, zero damage on its own. The flagged lethal outlet is Aetherflux Reservoir — 1 life per spell cast this turn, then Pay 50 life for 50 damage — but that four-card line has not been stack-verified; treat it as a candidate, not a fact. And know what the deck does NOT do: the combo database's two-card Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring 'infinite' is refuted (stack 001). The pair alone produces nothing.

Heliod is the hinge that makes all of it asymmetric. The front face rebuys an enchantment on arrival; a sorcery-speed {3}{U/P} flips him into the Warped Eclipse, which grants everything flash and discounts your spells by {1} for each card your opponents drew this turn. Your own engines pay your mana bill: on a Font of Mythos board an opponent turn cycle can make your next spell nearly free, cast at instant speed, with countermagic still up. The goldfish sim has Heliod down by turn 6 in 90% of games — the deck's most reliable single event.

Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring: is the claimed infinite real?

What this deck does NOT do. The combo database lists Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring as a two-card infinite (296,000 decks believe it); this walk refutes it. The Horror's trigger is modal, and mode and target lock in while the Sol Ring that caused it is still a spell on the stack — a spell you control is not a legal target for 'target spell you don't control,' and it is not a nonland permanent either. By the time Sol Ring is a permanent, the trigger is gone. The pair alone loops nothing: no mana, no storm count. Never pitch this deck to the table — or compute its bracket — on the strength of that database line.

Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring: is the claimed infinite real?
Rules version June 19, 2026 · status: cleared in ? review cycle(s)
Verified
  1. You cast Sol Ring for {1}. The card moves from your hand to the stack and becomes a spell you control.
    Sol Ring is a spell on the stack (pos 0), controlled by you. While on the stack it is a spell, not a permanent.
    CR 601.2a — “To propose the casting of a spell, a player first moves that card (or that copy of a card) from where it is to the stack. It becomes the topmost object on the stack.   2. Casting Sol Ring meets Hullbreaker Horror's trigger condition ('Whenever you cast a spell'). The triggered ability is put on the stack above Sol Ring the next time a player would receive priority.

  3. The trigger is modal ('choose up to one —'). You must announce the mode — and any target for that mode — now, as the ability is put on the stack, not when it resolves.
    Mode and target legality are locked in at this moment, while Sol Ring is still a spell on the stack.
    CR 603.3c — “If a triggered ability is modal, its controller announces the mode choice when putting the ability on the stack.”
    CR 700.2b — “The controller of a modal triggered ability chooses the mode(s) as part of putting that ability on the stack.”
    CR 603.3d — “The remainder of the process for putting a triggered ability on the stack is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2c–d.”
  4. Check mode 1 ('Return target spell you don't control to its owner's hand') against Sol Ring: the only spell on the stack is Sol Ring, and you control it.
    Sol Ring is not an 'appropriate object' for mode 1 — it fails the 'you don't control' requirement — so mode 1 cannot be chosen targeting Sol Ring. A mode whose only would-be target is illegal can't be chosen at all.
    CR 601.2c — “The player announces their choice of an appropriate object or player for each target the spell requires.”
    CR 603.3c — “If one of the modes would be illegal (due to an inability to choose legal targets, for example), that mode can’t be chosen.”
  5. Check mode 2 ('Return target nonland permanent to its owner's hand') against Sol Ring: while on the stack, Sol Ring is a spell, not a permanent.
    Mode 2 cannot target the Sol Ring being cast. Legal mode-2 targets at this moment are only existing nonland permanents — Hullbreaker Horror itself or the opponent's tapped creature — neither of which advances any loop.
    CR 110.1 — “A permanent is a card or token on the battlefield. A permanent remains on the battlefield indefinitely. A card or token becomes a permanent as it enters the battlefield and it stops being a permanent as it’s moved to another zone by an effect or rule.”
  6. You choose no mode ('up to one' permits zero). The ability is removed from the stack; players pass priority.
    The trigger leaves the stack with no effect. Sol Ring is now the top object on the stack.
    CR 603.3c — “If no mode is chosen, the ability is removed from the stack.”
    CR 608.1 — “Each time all players pass in succession, the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves.”
  7. Sol Ring resolves. It is a permanent spell with no targets, so it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under your control.
    Only now is Sol Ring a permanent — and Hullbreaker Horror's trigger from casting it is already gone. The trigger can never bounce the very spell that caused it: too early on the stack (illegal target for both modes), too late once it has resolved (the trigger no longer exists).
    CR 608.3 — “If the object that’s resolving is a permanent spell, its resolution may involve several steps.”
    CR 608.3a — “If the object that’s resolving has no targets, it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under the control of the spell’s controller.”
  8. You tap Sol Ring for {C}{C}. This is an activated mana ability: it does not use the stack and is not casting a spell.
    You add {C}{C}. Hullbreaker Horror does not trigger (nothing was cast), and no cast count / storm-relevant count increases. Activating Sol Ring any number of times per turn is impossible anyway — it taps.
    CR 605.3b — “An activated mana ability doesn’t go on the stack, so it can’t be targeted, countered, or otherwise responded to. Rather, it resolves immediately after it is activated.”
  9. Loop analysis for the pair alone: after step 7 there is no further spell to cast (only Counterspell, which is a one-shot, not a loop piece the claim relies on). Hullbreaker Horror only triggers on casts, and Sol Ring can only be re-bounced as a permanent — which requires some OTHER spell to be cast while Sol Ring is on the battlefield.

Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring alone produce neither unbounded colorless mana nor an unbounded storm count: the Horror trigger created by casting Sol Ring can never return that Sol Ring (illegal target under both modes while it is a spell; trigger already resolved once it is a permanent), and tapping Sol Ring is not a cast, so no new trigger ever arises. The real loop needs a third card: with Sol Ring on the battlefield, casting any other recastable spell (cheapest with a {0}-cost artifact) lets the Horror bounce Sol Ring as a permanent, and recasting Sol Ring lets the Horror bounce the third piece — netting {C}{C} minus recast costs per iteration. Unbounded mana and unbounded casts exist only with that additional piece.
    CR 110.1 — “A card or token becomes a permanent as it enters the battlefield and it stops being a permanent as it’s moved to another zone by an effect or rule.”
    CR 113.3c — “Triggered abilities have a trigger condition and an effect.”
    CR 106.4 — “Each player’s mana pool empties at the end of each step and phase, and the player is said to lose this mana.”

    CR 603.3c — “If a triggered ability is modal, its controller announces the mode choice when putting the ability on the stack.”
    CR 115.1d — “The target(s) are chosen as the ability is put on the stack”
    CR 110.1 — “A permanent is a card or token on the battlefield.”
  2. With the trigger on the stack and Sol Ring still on the battlefield, you (holding priority or when you receive it) tap Sol Ring for mana: '{T}: Add {C}{C}.'
    This is a mana ability — it does not use the stack and resolves immediately, so nothing can stop it. {C}{C} is now floating in your mana pool. Tapping Sol Ring before the bounce resolves is mandatory sequencing for the loop's mana math.
    CR 605.3a — “A player may activate an activated mana ability whenever they have priority”
    CR 605.3b — “An activated mana ability doesn’t go on the stack, so it can’t be targeted, countered, or otherwise responded to. Rather, it resolves immediately after it is activated.”
  3. The Hullbreaker Horror trigger (top of stack) resolves. Sol Ring is checked for target legality: it is still on the battlefield (tapped is irrelevant), so it is still legal. Sol Ring is returned to your hand.
    Sol Ring is in your hand; the {C}{C} it made remains in your pool.
    CR 608.2b — “If the spell or ability specifies targets, it checks whether the targets are still legal. A target that’s no longer in the zone it was in when it was targeted is illegal.”
    CR 405.5 — “When all players pass in succession, the top (last-added) spell or ability on the stack resolves.”
  4. The Arcane Signet spell (now top of stack) resolves and enters the battlefield untapped.
    Board: Hullbreaker Horror, Arcane Signet. Hand: Sol Ring. Floating: {C}{C} — floating mana survives between spells; it is only lost at the end of each step and phase, and we stay inside your main phase throughout.
    CR 405.5 — “When all players pass in succession, the top (last-added) spell or ability on the stack resolves.”
    CR 106.4 — “it can be used to pay costs immediately, or it can stay in the player’s mana pool as unspent mana”
  5. With the stack now empty in your main phase, cast Sol Ring from your hand for {1}, paying with one floating {C} (one {C} remains floating). Hullbreaker Horror triggers again; choose 'Return target nonland permanent' targeting Arcane Signet — which is now a permanent on the battlefield, not a spell.
    Note the timing constraint: artifacts are sorcery-speed, so the two casts cannot respond to each other — each cast must wait for the stack to empty. That is exactly why the bounces must ALTERNATE between two rocks rather than a rock bouncing itself. The trigger goes on the stack above the Sol Ring spell with Arcane Signet locked as its target.
    CR 301.1 — “A player who has priority may cast an artifact card from their hand during a main phase of their turn when the stack is empty.”
    CR 603.3c — “If a triggered ability is modal, its controller announces the mode choice when putting the ability on the stack.”
    CR 110.1 — “A permanent is a card or token on the battlefield.”
  6. With the trigger on the stack, tap Arcane Signet for one mana of your commander's color identity — Heliod, the Radiant Dawn is white/blue, so {W} or {U} (either works; the loop only needs it as generic mana).
    Legal even though the Signet just entered: the 'summoning sickness' tap restriction applies only to creatures, and Arcane Signet is a noncreature artifact. Floating mana is now {C} plus one {W} or {U}.
    CR 605.3a — “A player may activate an activated mana ability whenever they have priority”
    CR 302.6 — “A creature’s activated ability with the tap symbol or the untap symbol in its activation cost can’t be activated unless the creature has been under its controller’s control continuously since their most recent turn began.”
  7. The trigger resolves (Arcane Signet still on the battlefield, still a legal target): Arcane Signet returns to your hand. Then the Sol Ring spell resolves and enters the battlefield untapped.
    The game state now exactly matches the cycle's start: Hullbreaker Horror and untapped Sol Ring on the battlefield, the two-mana rock in hand, you holding priority in your main phase with an empty stack. The loop is closed.
    CR 608.2b — “If the spell or ability specifies targets, it checks whether the targets are still legal. A target that’s no longer in the zone it was in when it was targeted is illegal.”
    CR 405.5 — “When all players pass in succession, the top (last-added) spell or ability on the stack resolves.”
  8. Full-cycle mana ledger (one cycle = cast Signet, bounce+tap Sol Ring, cast Sol Ring, bounce+tap Signet).
    Spent: {2} (Arcane Signet) + {1} (Sol Ring) = 3 mana. Produced: {C}{C} (Sol Ring) + 1 (Signet) = 3 mana. The cycle is exactly MANA-NEUTRAL. The 2 mana floating at the end of each cycle ({C} + {W/U}) pays the next cycle's Signet cost of {2}, so after the initial {2} investment (the {U}{U} available here) no further outside mana is ever needed.
    CR 106.4 — “When an effect instructs a player to add mana, that mana goes into a player’s mana pool. From there, it can be used to pay costs immediately, or it can stay in the player’s mana pool as unspent mana.”
  9. Because each mana-neutral cycle returns to an identical game state with you holding priority, you may shortcut the loop for any chosen number of iterations: the cast/storm count IS unbounded with Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring + Arcane Signet plus {2} of starting mana. Substitutions for the two-mana rock: Thought Vessel substitutes exactly ({2} to cast, '{T}: Add {C}' — the same 3-spent/3-produced neutral ledger). Fellwar Stone substitutes only while an opponent controls a land that produces a color ('{T}: Add one mana of any color that a land an opponent controls could produce') — true in most real games, but false on this stated board, where both opponents' boards are empty: there Fellwar Stone taps for nothing and its cycle is mana-negative (3 spent, 2 produced), so the loop is NOT unbounded with it.
    Precise bounds, stated exactly: (1) the loop nets ZERO mana, zero cards, and zero damage — it is a pure cast-count engine (2 casts per cycle), so it only wins through a payoff that cares about casts (e.g., a storm or magecraft-style effect) or by generating unbounded Hullbreaker triggers; (2) every trigger in the loop is consumed bouncing your own rock — the moment you aim a trigger at an opponent's permanent instead, the chain breaks (your rock stays on the battlefield and cannot be recast); (3) the loop lives inside a single phase, since the pool empties at end of phase — but arbitrarily many iterations fit before that, so this does not bound the count.
    CR 732.1b — “Occasionally the game gets into a state in which a set of actions could be repeated indefinitely (thus creating a “loop”). In that case, the shortcut rules can be used to determine how many times those actions are repeated without having to actually perform them”
    CR 732.2a — “the player with priority may suggest a shortcut by describing a sequence of game choices, for all players, that may be legally taken based on the current game state and the predictable results of the sequence of choices”
    CR 106.4 — “Each player’s mana pool empties at the end of each step and phase”
  10. What Sapphire Medallion changes: nothing about this cycle's arithmetic. Its text is 'Blue spells you cast cost {1} less to cast' — but Sol Ring ({1}) and Arcane Signet ({2}) have no colored mana symbols in their mana costs, so both are colorless, and colorless is not a color. Neither cast in the cycle is a blue spell.
    The Medallion discounts ZERO of the loop's casts: the cycle stays exactly mana-neutral and does NOT become mana-positive — no infinite mana. (Pearl Medallion is identical reasoning for white.) A Medallion only discounts genuinely blue/white spells cast alongside the loop, e.g., recasting Hullbreaker Horror itself. The rock that would change the math is one whose cast-plus-tap is mana-positive or whose cost a discount can actually touch — no such piece is on this board, and this resolution makes no claim beyond it.
    CR 202.2b — “Objects with no colored mana symbols in their mana costs are colorless.”
    CR 202.2a — “an object with a mana cost of {2} is colorless”
    CR glossary:colorless — “An object with no color is colorless. Colorless is not a color.”
