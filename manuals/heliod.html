THE GENEROSITY TRAP

Why would a control deck hand every opponent an extra card, every turn, on purpose — and then add a rabbit who gives them life too? Because in this court the cards aren't a gift, they're evidence, the sentence is read at each opponent's upkeep — and as of this printing, the execution can be scheduled a turn in advance.

This deck hands the whole table a firehose of cards, then charges for the water. The engines — Howling Mine, Font of Mythos, Temple Bell, Dictate of Kruphix, Kami of the Crescent Moon, Folio of Fancies, and above all Forced Fruition — are perfectly symmetric on their face, and the hug layer leans into it: Kwain offers everyone a card (and a life), The Second Doctor offers another at your end step. Heliod is the hinge that makes all of it asymmetric: the front face rebuys an enchantment on arrival, and the {3}{U/P} flip into the Warped Eclipse grants everything flash and discounts your spells {1} for each card your opponents drew this turn — every card the table accepts pays your mana bill on the turn you want to act. The goldfish sim has Heliod down by turn 6 in 89.6% of games (median turn 4), and while the pieces gather you play draw-go control behind a counter suite held by turn 6 in 67.2% of games, with Supreme Verdict as the uncounterable reset your own planeswalkers now survive.

The primary win is verified end to end, and it is a storm kill: Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring + Arcane Signet + Aetherflux Reservoir (stack 006, checker-passed). The 004-verified loop — each rock's cast bounces the other, tapped for its mana before it leaves — is exactly mana-neutral, so casts are unbounded in one main phase, and the Reservoir converts the count into life: cast n nets +n, so k casts pay out k(k+1)/2. From 32 life you need that sum to reach 119, which makes 15 casts the proven minimum (14 casts pay only 105 and fail). The verified schedule: casts 1–6 lift you to 53, the first 'Pay 50 life: 50 damage' activation drops you to 3 and kills the 26-life opponent via state-based actions; casts 7–12 rebuild to 60, the second activation (60 to 10) kills the 40-life seat; casts 13–15 reach 52, the third (52 to 2) kills the last, and with every opponent gone you win on the spot — all in one main phase, ending at 2 life. The same artifact names the line's weakness honestly: every activation (and every loop cast) opens a fully open priority window. Activating an ability is not casting a spell, so Hullbreaker never triggers on it, and the loop's sorcery-speed artifact pieces can only be cast with the stack empty — the engine cannot respond to anything, ever. Grand Abolisher, now maindeck, is the named answer: during your turn opponents can't cast spells or activate artifact, creature, or enchantment abilities, closing exactly the windows the kill opens. Win-condition access is drawn by turn 6 in 49.6% of goldfish runs (61.2% of games ever), and the whole line needs {2} and the pieces, nothing more.

The second verified path is the clean one: Approach of the Second Sun. The first cast gains 7 life — 14 with Alhammarret's Archive out — and buries the card seventh from the top, trivially shallow when everyone draws in bulk and two doublers accelerate your digging; Mystical Tutor or Long-Term Plans puts it back on top directly. Stack 002 verified the second cast end to end: the win is checked during resolution, as a spell effect, and the first casting still counts because the game tracks the casting event, not the shuffled-away card. The opponents' one and only window is while the second Approach sits on the stack — commit behind held countermagic, because once resolution begins, nothing stops it.

The third verified path is attrition: the punisher clock. Iron Maiden and Viseling deal upkeep damage equal to hand size minus 4, and Ebony Owl Netsuke deals a flat 4 at seven-plus cards. Stack 003 verified the arithmetic: once Forced Fruition fills a hand, cleanup trims the opponent to exactly 7, and the next upkeep Owl hits for 4 and Maiden for 3 — 7 damage without you tapping a land, running on all three opponents' upkeeps. The Second Doctor makes 7 the floor, not the ceiling: with no maximum hand size the trim never happens, so a ten-card hand takes 6 from the Maiden while the Owl keeps firing. The core two-piece — engine plus punisher — assembles by turn 6 in 41.3% of games, so most games open as pure control; the deck is built to be good at exactly that, and the moment the four kill pieces line up behind an Abolisher, you stop playing fair.

Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring: is the claimed infinite real?

What this deck does NOT do. The combo database lists Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring as a two-card infinite (296,000 decks believe it); this walk refutes it. The Horror's trigger is modal, and mode and target lock in while the Sol Ring that caused it is still a spell on the stack — a spell you control is not a legal target for 'target spell you don't control,' and it is not a nonland permanent either. By the time Sol Ring is a permanent, the trigger is gone. The pair alone loops nothing: no mana, no storm count. Never pitch this deck to the table — or compute its bracket — on the strength of that database line.

Then determine: with only these two cards (plus ordinary lands), does repeating any sequence of casts and activations produce unbounded colorless mana or an unbounded storm count, or does the loop require an additional card? Result. Sol Ring resolves and is on the battlefield; the Horror trigger it created resolved first with no legal way to bounce it (no mode chosen, or a strictly non-loop bounce of an existing permanent). You may tap Sol Ring for {C}{C} once. No repeatable sequence exists with only these two cards: the 'Infinite colorless mana / Infinite storm count' claim requires at least one additional recastable spell (ideally a {0}-cost artifact) and should not, on its own, drive a bracket floor of 4 for this deck.

OVERRULED ON APPEAL

Last printing this page said zero swaps left, with pride. The pilot appealed, the review charter got de-biased, and the re-run found four — two applied, two benched with their pull-in conditions on the record, plus a tutor the pool never held. The verdict changed because the process improved. That's the whole point of the process.

Start with the paid debt: the v3 rebuild cashed the obsolescence index's two clearest checks — Worn Powerstone (superseded by the two-mana Talisman class) and Brass Infiniscope (flagged for Daily Bugle Newspaper) both left the 99 — and v4's changes came from a different source entirely: re-verification, not the index. The appetite-rule pass over the 60-card pool overturned the earlier 'zero swaps' verdict, and its two promotes are now applied. Teferi, Hero of Dominaria took Secure the Wastes' slot — a sweep-surviving clock that draws while untapping two counter-lands beats an X-spell that needs seven-plus mana and still has to cross three combat steps — and Archmage's Charm took Ghostly Prison's, never-dead modal interaction over a third attack tax at a draw-go table. Two more swaps stay benched as pod-conditional, pull-in conditions documented: Sphinx of the Final Word comes in for Propaganda at two or more heavy-permission seats (its static covers sorceries, making the second Approach cast counter-proof — though it is explicitly no Grand Abolisher substitute, since a Reservoir activation is not a cast), and No More Lies comes in for Font of Mythos at fast-combo pods, where the slowest symmetric engine is pure fuel for the storm seat; the pilot keeps Font maindeck by default. What remains in the index itself is nuance, not urgency. Azorius Signet's entry lists Mind Stone among its superseders — 'additional: draw' — a fair swap only if you value the late-game cash-in over colored fixing in a two-color deck. The index long flagged Fellwar Stone as obsoleted by Sol Ring on cost; v4 finally benched it, but for the verified demerit rather than the indexed one — stack 004 shows it sustains the Hullbreaker loop only while an opponent's land can actually make mana, and Thought Vessel substitutes exactly, so the conditional rock was the natural cut when Enlightened Tutor needed the slot. Long-Term Plans is indexed as obsoleted by Mystical Tutor, which you also run — but the supersession is narrower than it looks, since Plans fetches any card (a punisher, Aetherflux Reservoir, the missing loop rock) where Mystical only finds instants and sorceries; keep both. Counterspell draws entries for Syncopate and Spell Snare on cost alone — both conditional counters by their own text, a poor trade in a deck that plans to hold mana anyway. The incoming three were checked too: Teferi, Hero of Dominaria and Enlightened Tutor have no index entries at all, and Archmage's Charm's five — Spell Snare, Change the Equation, Pact of Negation and company, every one tagged 'lower CMC' — are conditional counters wearing the Counterspell verdict: similarity artifacts, because the modal never-dead text is the entire reason the Charm is here. The earlier re-checks stand unchanged — Grand Abolisher, Drannith Magistrate, Jace Beleren, and Teferi, Temporal Pilgrim have no entries; Jace, Wielder of Mysteries' lone entry (Jace, the Perfected Mind, 'additional: removal') fails the oracle-text test on the only clause that matters, the empty-library win static; and the Alhammarret's Archive, Supreme Verdict, The Second Doctor, and Kwain entries remain similarity artifacts — as does the standing negative result: the punisher core itself — Iron Maiden, Ebony Owl Netsuke, Viseling, Howling Mine, Font of Mythos — has no obsolescence entries. Nothing printed since does this job better; the old cards are the tech, not the debt. Outside the index, the deck-analyst gap study on two-piece consistency (41.3% assembled by turn 6) named three answers, and v4 bought the first: Enlightened Tutor is now IN the 99, a one-mana instant that puts any artifact or enchantment piece of the core on top, at the cost of a tenth Game Changer the bracket-4 floor already absorbs. Fabricate — any artifact piece straight to hand — and Trouble in Pairs — converting the second draw your engines guarantee every opponent every turn into cards for you instead of damage — remain the outside-pool asks: the consistency ceiling this list still hasn't bought, deck-analyst shortlist evidence rather than obsolescence data, so weigh them as recommendations, not supersessions.

Case A-001 Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring: is the claimed infinite real?
Rules version June 19, 2026 · status: cleared in ? review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
  1. You cast Sol Ring for {1}. The card moves from your hand to the stack and becomes a spell you control.
    Sol Ring is a spell on the stack (pos 0), controlled by you. While on the stack it is a spell, not a permanent.
    CR 601.2a — “To propose the casting of a spell, a player first moves that card (or that copy of a card) from where it is to the stack. It becomes the topmost object on the stack. It has all the characteristics of the card (or the copy of a card) associated with it, and that player becomes its controller.”
  2. Casting Sol Ring meets Hullbreaker Horror's trigger condition ('Whenever you cast a spell'). The triggered ability is put on the stack above Sol Ring the next time a player would receive priority.
    Stack, top to bottom: Hullbreaker Horror's trigger, then Sol Ring. The trigger will resolve (or leave the stack) before Sol Ring does.
    CR 113.3c — “Whenever the trigger event occurs, the ability is put on the stack the next time a player would receive priority and stays there until it’s countered, it resolves, or it otherwise leaves the stack.”
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority. See rule 117, “Timing and Priority.” The ability becomes the topmost object on the stack.”
  3. The trigger is modal ('choose up to one —'). You must announce the mode — and any target for that mode — now, as the ability is put on the stack, not when it resolves.
    Mode and target legality are locked in at this moment, while Sol Ring is still a spell on the stack.
    CR 603.3c — “If a triggered ability is modal, its controller announces the mode choice when putting the ability on the stack.”
    CR 700.2b — “The controller of a modal triggered ability chooses the mode(s) as part of putting that ability on the stack.”
    CR 603.3d — “The remainder of the process for putting a triggered ability on the stack is identical to the process for casting a spell listed in rules 601.2c–d.”
  4. Check mode 1 ('Return target spell you don't control to its owner's hand') against Sol Ring: the only spell on the stack is Sol Ring, and you control it.
    Sol Ring is not an 'appropriate object' for mode 1 — it fails the 'you don't control' requirement — so mode 1 cannot be chosen targeting Sol Ring. A mode whose only would-be target is illegal can't be chosen at all.
    CR 601.2c — “The player announces their choice of an appropriate object or player for each target the spell requires.”
    CR 603.3c — “If one of the modes would be illegal (due to an inability to choose legal targets, for example), that mode can’t be chosen.”
  5. Check mode 2 ('Return target nonland permanent to its owner's hand') against Sol Ring: while on the stack, Sol Ring is a spell, not a permanent.
    Mode 2 cannot target the Sol Ring being cast. Legal mode-2 targets at this moment are only existing nonland permanents — Hullbreaker Horror itself or the opponent's tapped creature — neither of which advances any loop.
    CR 110.1 — “A permanent is a card or token on the battlefield. A permanent remains on the battlefield indefinitely. A card or token becomes a permanent as it enters the battlefield and it stops being a permanent as it’s moved to another zone by an effect or rule.”
  6. You choose no mode ('up to one' permits zero). The ability is removed from the stack; players pass priority.
    The trigger leaves the stack with no effect. Sol Ring is now the top object on the stack.
    CR 603.3c — “If no mode is chosen, the ability is removed from the stack.”
    CR 608.1 — “Each time all players pass in succession, the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves.”
  7. Sol Ring resolves. It is a permanent spell with no targets, so it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under your control.
    Only now is Sol Ring a permanent — and Hullbreaker Horror's trigger from casting it is already gone. The trigger can never bounce the very spell that caused it: too early on the stack (illegal target for both modes), too late once it has resolved (the trigger no longer exists).
    CR 608.3 — “If the object that’s resolving is a permanent spell, its resolution may involve several steps.”
    CR 608.3a — “If the object that’s resolving has no targets, it becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under the control of the spell’s controller.”
  8. You tap Sol Ring for {C}{C}. This is an activated mana ability: it does not use the stack and is not casting a spell.
    You add {C}{C}. Hullbreaker Horror does not trigger (nothing was cast), and no cast count / storm-relevant count increases. Activating Sol Ring any number of times per turn is impossible anyway — it taps.
    CR 605.3b — “An activated mana ability doesn’t go on the stack, so it can’t be targeted, countered, or otherwise responded to. Rather, it resolves immediately after it is activated.”
  9. Loop analysis for the pair alone: after step 7 there is no further spell to cast (only Counterspell, which is a one-shot, not a loop piece the claim relies on). Hullbreaker Horror only triggers on casts, and Sol Ring can only be re-bounced as a permanent — which requires some OTHER spell to be cast while Sol Ring is on the battlefield.
    Verdict: the combo DB claim is REFUTED as a two-card line. Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring alone produce neither unbounded colorless mana nor an unbounded storm count: the Horror trigger created by casting Sol Ring can never return that Sol Ring (illegal target under both modes while it is a spell; trigger already resolved once it is a permanent), and tapping Sol Ring is not a cast, so no new trigger ever arises. Even the {C}{C} produced does not accumulate across phases. The real loop needs a third card: with Sol Ring on the battlefield, casting any other recastable spell (cheapest with a {0}-cost artifact) lets the Horror bounce Sol Ring as a permanent, and recasting Sol Ring lets the Horror bounce the third piece — netting {C}{C} minus recast costs per iteration. Unbounded mana and unbounded casts exist only with that additional piece.
    CR 110.1 — “A card or token becomes a permanent as it enters the battlefield and it stops being a permanent as it’s moved to another zone by an effect or rule.”
    CR 113.3c — “Triggered abilities have a trigger condition and an effect.”
    CR 106.4 — “Each player’s mana pool empties at the end of each step and phase, and the player is said to lose this mana.”

Case A-002 Approach of the Second Sun, second cast off Mystical Tutor
Rules version June 19, 2026 · status: cleared in ? review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
  1. You have cast Approach of the Second Sun from your hand for {6}{W}. It is the only object on the stack. The casting is already complete, so the spell 'was cast from your hand' for the purpose of its own first sentence, and you receive priority.
    Approach of the Second Sun is a sorcery spell on the stack, cast from your hand. It has no targets, so there is no targeting legality to check on resolution. You hold priority with {U} still available (Swan Song in hand).
    CR 601.2i — “Once the steps described in 601.2a–h are completed, effects that modify the characteristics of the spell as it’s cast are applied, then the spell becomes cast.”
    CR 117.3c — “If a player has priority when they cast a spell, activate an ability, or take a special action, that player receives priority afterward.”
  2. Before Approach can resolve, every player must pass priority in succession. This is the opponents' window — their only window — to interact with the win.
    The opponent known to hold an instant may cast it in response while Approach is on the stack. Anything cast in response goes on top of the stack and resolves before Approach does. If instead all players pass without acting, Approach will resolve (step 5).
    CR 117.4 — “If all players pass in succession (that is, if all players pass without taking any actions in between passing), the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves”
    CR 117.7 — “the new spell or ability has been cast or activated”
    CR 117.7 — “The new spell or ability will resolve first.”
  3. Contingency: the opponent's instant is a counterspell targeting Approach of the Second Sun, and it resolves.
    If that happens, Approach is removed from the stack, it does not resolve, and none of its effects occur — no win-condition check, no life gain, and it is put into your graveyard rather than back into your library. The win is fully stoppable at this point.
    CR 701.6a — “To counter a spell or ability means to cancel it, removing it from the stack. It doesn’t resolve and none of its effects occur. A countered spell is put into its owner’s graveyard.”
  4. Your answer: while the opponent's instant is on the stack, you respond with Swan Song for {U}, targeting that instant ('Counter target enchantment, instant, or sorcery spell. Its controller creates a 2/2 blue Bird creature token with flying.').
    Swan Song goes on top of the stack and resolves first. If it resolves, the opponent's instant is countered and that opponent creates a 2/2 blue Bird creature token with flying; Approach then remains on the stack and resolves as in step 5 (unless someone has further interaction). Honest limits: Swan Song can only counter an enchantment, instant, or sorcery SPELL — if the opponent interacts with an activated or triggered ability instead of a spell, Swan Song cannot stop it; and Swan Song itself can be responded to.
    CR 117.7 — “The new spell or ability will resolve first.”
    CR 701.6a — “To counter a spell or ability means to cancel it, removing it from the stack.”
    CR 113.9 — “Activated and triggered abilities on the stack aren’t spells, and therefore can’t be countered by anything that counters only spells.”
  5. All players pass priority in succession. Approach of the Second Sun begins to resolve, and you follow its instructions in the order written.
    Once resolution begins, no player has priority and nobody can respond any longer — the interaction window of steps 2–4 has closed. The first instruction to evaluate is the conditional first sentence of the card.
    CR 608.1 — “Each time all players pass in succession, the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves.”
    CR 608.2c — “The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written.”
    CR 117.2e — “Even if a player is doing so, no player has priority while a spell or ability is resolving.”
  6. The win condition is checked NOW — during resolution, as a resolving spell effect. Both halves of the condition are evaluated: (a) 'this spell was cast from your hand' — true; (b) 'you've cast another spell named Approach of the Second Sun this game' — true, per the game history.
    This is an effect that states a player wins the game (CR 104.2b) — it is not a state-based action and does not wait for any check or priority pass. The moment this instruction is followed, you win. Because the condition is met, the 'Otherwise' clause (put it seventh from the top of your library and gain 7 life) is never performed.
    CR 608.2c — “The controller of the spell or ability follows its instructions in the order written.”
    CR 104.2b — “An effect may state that a player wins the game.”
  7. Why the first casting still counts even though the card went back into the library: the condition asks whether YOU have CAST another spell with that name this game — it checks an event in the game's history, not a property of any current card.
    Casting is an action the player completed earlier this game (the spell 'becomes cast' when the casting steps finish). The physical card later becoming a new object as it was shuffled back into your library changes the card object's identity, but the condition never looks at that object — your casting of it remains a fact of game history. (For the same reason, even a first Approach that had been COUNTERED would satisfy this clause: it was still cast.)
    CR 701.5a — “To cast a spell is to take it from the zone it’s in (usually the hand), put it on the stack, and pay its costs, so that it will eventually resolve and have its effect.”
    CR 601.2i — “Once the steps described in 601.2a–h are completed, effects that modify the characteristics of the spell as it’s cast are applied, then the spell becomes cast.”
    CR 400.7 — “An object that moves from one zone to another becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence.”
  8. The game ends. You win, immediately, mid-resolution.
    The game ends immediately when a player wins — there are no further priority passes and no state-based-action checks (those only happen when a player would get priority, which never occurs again). Both opponents' life totals (25 and 31) are irrelevant; they do not 'lose to an SBA' — the game simply ends with you as the winner. Note: the limited range of influence option (under which a win instead makes opponents in range lose) applies only to games using that option; Commander does not, so the whole game ends.
    CR 104.1 — “A game ends immediately when a player wins, when the game is a draw, or when the game is restarted.”
    CR 117.5 — “Each time a player would get priority, the game first performs all applicable state-based actions as a single event”
    CR 104.3h — “In a multiplayer game using the limited range of influence option (see rule 801), an effect that states that a player wins the game instead causes all of that player’s opponents within the player’s range of influence to lose the game.”

Case A-003 Forced Fruition feeds Iron Maiden and Ebony Owl Netsuke
Rules version June 19, 2026 · status: cleared in 2 review cycle(s) · tap to open Verified
  1. Opponent 1 (hand size 2, on their own main phase) casts Naturalize targeting Forced Fruition. The card leaves their hand for the stack, leaving 1 card in hand. Casting the spell is the trigger event for Forced Fruition ('Whenever an opponent casts a spell, that player draws seven cards'); the trigger is put on the stack on top of Naturalize before any player gets priority.
    Stack, top-down: Forced Fruition's triggered ability (affects opponent 1), then Naturalize (targeting Forced Fruition). Opponent 1's hand: 1 card.
    CR 603.3 — “Once an ability has triggered, its controller puts it on the stack as an object that’s not a card the next time a player would receive priority. See rule 117, “Timing and Priority.” The ability becomes the topmost object on the stack.”
  2. All players pass priority. The top object of the stack resolves first: Forced Fruition's triggered ability. It resolves BEFORE Naturalize, because it sits on top of the spell that triggered it.
    Opponent 1 draws seven cards: 1 + 7 = 8 cards in hand. Yes — the draw happens even though Naturalize will destroy Forced Fruition, because the trigger resolves first.
    CR 608.1 — “Each time all players pass in succession, the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves.”
  3. Naturalize resolves next and destroys Forced Fruition (moved to its owner's graveyard). Note that even if the destruction had somehow happened before the trigger resolved, the trigger would still resolve — an ability on the stack is independent of its source.
    Forced Fruition is destroyed. Opponent 1 still has the 7 drawn cards (hand: 8). No further Fruition triggers will occur for future spells.
    CR 701.8a — “To destroy a permanent, move it from the battlefield to its owner’s graveyard.”
    CR 113.7a — “Once activated or triggered, an ability exists on the stack independently of its source. Destruction or removal of the source after that time won’t affect the ability.”
  4. Opponent 1 takes no further action this turn (per the scenario). At the cleanup step of their own turn, they are the active player holding 8 cards, one more than the normal maximum hand size of seven, so they must discard down to 7.

  4. Arithmetic bound for the kill: you must pay 50 life three times (150 total), can only pay 50 while at 50 or more life, and must end above 0 (a player at 0 or less loses as a state-based action). From 32 life: 32 + k(k+1)/2 - 150 >= 1 requires k(k+1)/2 >= 119, so k = 15 (120 life gained); k = 14 yields only 105 and fails.
    Minimum 15 casts this turn, with the three payments interleaved at points where your life is at least 50 (a legal schedule is given in steps 5, 9, and 10).

  6. Response window — honest assessment. The activation uses the stack, and every opponent receives priority before it resolves; nothing on this board forbids responses (Grand Abolisher is in this 99 but is NOT on this battlefield). Per the scenario, all three opponents' boards are empty, so any response must be castable without mana from permanents (e.g. a pitch-cost instant), but the window is rules-open.     CR 117.4 — “If all players pass in succession (that is, if all players pass without taking any actions in between passing), the spell or ability on top of the stack resolves”
  7. Hullbreaker Horror and the activation: no protection. Activating an ability is not casting a spell — an ability goes on the stack without a card, and only a card on the stack is a spell — so your Reservoir activation does not trigger Hullbreaker. Nor can you manufacture a trigger in response: the only castable cards in the engine are Sol Ring and Arcane Signet, sorcery-speed artifact spells that are legal to cast only during your main phase while the stack is empty — with the activation (or an opponent's answer) on the stack, no loop cast is legal. On this board, each activation window is fully open; Grand Abolisher (in the 99, not on this battlefield) is the card that would close it during your turn.
    The activation can be responded to, and Hullbreaker Horror offers NO protection during any activation window on this board.

  11. What the line requires beyond the three loop pieces + Reservoir: nothing. No extra mana (the loop is mana-neutral per stacks 004/005 and the activation cost is life only, paid at activation), and no particular starting life — any positive total works because per-cast gain grows without bound, though from 32 the floor is 15 casts. What interrupts it: any instant-speed answer at any priority window — each of the three activations, and each loop cast, passes priority to every opponent. On this board there is NO protection: the loop's castable pieces are sorcery-speed artifacts, so you can never cast into an occupied stack to trigger Hullbreaker, and Grand Abolisher is not on this battlefield to close the windows.
