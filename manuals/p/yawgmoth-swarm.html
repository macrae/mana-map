1General

What the machine is and what it is for.

A free, repeatable sacrifice outlet turns every body you control into three payments at once — a -1/-1 counter, a card, and one life off every seat — and the deck's real question is never how to go infinite but which of six kills the outlet is holding, because the three that ARE unbounded (stacks 009, 011, 012) need no commander and no counter, while the Nest of Scarabs line everyone names was measured by four passing stacks as a body-bounded rotation that grinds rather than wins.

What this model does not do (19 stated assumptions)
  • Simulates resource development, not full games (no interaction, no removal).
  • Draw every turn including turn 1 (multiplayer Commander).
  • Mulligan: keep 7-card hands with 2-5 lands; up to 2 fresh redraws, keep the last.
  • One land drop per turn when available.
  • LANDS ENTER UNTAPPED, ALWAYS, and the one in hand longest is the one played. A tapland costs nothing here and no land is ever chosen over another, so this model CANNOT rank two lands that make the same colours — `mana-analysis` and `mana-fit` are what answer a land question, and they are deterministic for exactly this reason.
  • Mana rocks ('{T}: Add') contribute from the turn after they are cast.
  • Commander cast on first affordable turn (highest spending priority).
  • Bodies count = creatures cast + tokens parsed from 'create ... token' text.
  • Target assembly counts cards DRAWN by a turn (cast cards still count).
  • CARD DRAW IS ONE A TURN UNLESS `model_draw` IS SET. With the flag on, four channels are modelled — a card's own ETB draw, an instant or sorcery that draws, an upkeep trigger, and an ARRIVAL trigger that draws when other creatures or tokens enter (Welcoming Vampire, Caretaker's Talent), which rides the same single door the ETB damage payoffs use. Activated draw (needs a spending policy this model has no opinion on), X-based draw (board-dependent), and death- or attack-triggered draw (no deaths, and attacks only under `model_combat`) are NOT modelled and the cards are named in `meta.card_advantage.draw_not_modelled`. A conditional trigger — 'if you control an artifact', 'you may draw' — is treated as unmodelled rather than assumed to be on: 39 of the 348 ETB-draw cards in the corpus carry one.
  • AN ARRIVAL-DRAW ENGINE NEVER SEES ITS OWN ARRIVAL. Welcoming Vampire is a 2/3 that draws 'whenever one or more OTHER creatures you control with power 2 or less enter'; registered before its own entry it passed its own gate and drew a card it does not draw, overstating edgar-vampires by 22% at turn eight. The deferral applies to every card and not only the ones worded 'other', which understates Tocasia's Welcome and two relatives by one draw on the turn they land.
  • ARRIVAL DRAW IS SMALLER WITHOUT `model_combat`, and legitimately so. The channel rides the one door onto the battlefield, which `model_combat` also uses to spawn token copies and ETB-payoff tokens — those are real additional arrivals that do not exist in the resource-only model. Measured on edgar-vampires: 1.264 extra cards by turn ten with both flags against 1.106 with draw alone. Before 2026-08-28 the gap was 1.264 against 0.323, because every call to that door sat inside `if model_combat:` and a draw-only deck silently lost three quarters of its arrival draws.
  • HELD-UP INTERACTION is reported as two series and both are FLOORS. This model casts everything it can afford every turn, so `interaction_castable` is what a pilot developing at full speed has left over, not what a pilot choosing to hold up two mana would have. A low figure is therefore a real finding and a high one is unambiguous good news.
  • Tutors are modeled as wildcards: a CAST tutor that fetches to hand or the top — including a TYPED one (Worldly Tutor, Sarkhan's Triumph), which the old literal 'a card' match missed entirely — fills ONE missing any_of group. Consumed once, mana paid, and a tutor that puts the card on top of the library costs a turn. An ACTIVATED, ETB or death-triggered tutor does not count, because this model only knows 'drawn and affordable'; nor does a land fetch, which is ramp and has no channel here yet. Reported as the *_assisted figures; the unassisted figures beside them exclude tutors entirely.
  • ENTERS-THE-BATTLEFIELD PAYOFFS are modelled: damage equal to the entering creature's power (Terror of the Peaks), X damage where X counts your board (Scourge of Valkas, Dragon Tempest), a token (Lathliss) and a token COPY of what entered (Miirym). Every arrival fires them — cast, token or copy — and a copy is itself an arrival, so they compound, which is the deck working rather than a bug. A payoff worded 'another NONTOKEN' does not re-trigger on its own tokens, which is the brake the rules already had. A chain is capped at 12 deep. X counts the whole board rather than one subtype: exact where the creatures are Dragons, generous otherwise.
  • The COMMANDER joins the battlefield when cast, attacks, and fires its own triggers. It used to be a mana sink and a flag — cast, then dropped — so a 10/10 flier contributed no power and never swung. Commander tax, death and recasting are still not modelled: it is cast once and it stays.
  • STATIC cost reduction IS modeled: a commander's eminence from the command zone (live from turn one, unremovable) and typed reducers once they are on the battlefield. It pays GENERIC only and is floored at the coloured pip count, because a discount can never pay a pip. A reduction that SCALES with a board state (Animar, Rakdos, Hamza) is refused rather than counted flat, and cost reduction on artifacts, noncreature spells or a colour is not modeled at all.
  • Rituals are not modeled (conservative).
  • Extra card draw is NOT modeled: one card per turn, always. That understates every deck, but by an amount proportional to how much draw it runs — so it is conservative WITHIN a deck and not neutral BETWEEN them. `meta.card_advantage` reports how much of each list this hides. Card advantage is measured nowhere in this suite.

6.1Mana

Nineteen cards: 36 land copies across 16 entries (the two MDFCs are counted elsewhere), plus Sol Ring, Dark Ritual and Jet Medallion. 41 black sources against a 36-source target — five clear, and deck-audit's colour-sources axis reads AT, not short. The 33 that are lands are only part of the count: the target budgets SOURCES, not lands, and eight more come off nonland permanents. 76 black pips spread over 55 cards; mean available mana 5.23 at turn 6, land drop hit rate 58.9% there. FAST mana is only three cards — Sol Ring, Ancient Tomb, Dark Ritual — at 43.6% drawn / 34.8% by turn 6, the smallest and lowest-rated multi-card component the audit prices. The mana is engine-shaped, not acceleration-shaped, and the commander lands on mean turn 4.301 (median 4, 91% by turn 6) rather than turn 2. The second currency is LIFE. Ancient Tomb, City of Brass, Cabal Pit, Ifnir Deadlands and Castle Locthwain all charge it, every card in `fuel` charges it, and the outlet charges 1 per activation; the drains in `output` are the refund, which is why stack 011's loop ends with the pilot at 80. Four lands hold second jobs the mana count cannot see: Phyrexian Tower is a free outlet paying {B}{B}, Cabal Pit shrinks a creature, Takenuma buys a body back, Bojuka Bog exiles a graveyard. NOTHING IN THIS STAGE APPEARS IN A PASSING STACK — the eleven scenarios name Swamps as furniture and the citation index filters them, so every claim here is deterministic arithmetic, not verified board state.

6.2Ignition

Nine cards, and the first thing that has to happen. Nothing this deck holds is a resource until one of them is down: a body is only fodder if you can eat it at will, for no mana, at instant speed. Yawgmoth is the best of them — mana-free, instant-speed, and he pays a card back — and the other eight are 78.5% drawn / 68.0% by turn 6 — the third-deepest layer here, behind the nine-card drain layer and the nine-card RENEWABLE FODDER class at 82.3% each. TEN of the eleven presentable passing stacks open from a board where a sacrifice has ALREADY been paid, which is how load-bearing this stage is: it is assumed, not argued. Stack 006 is the one exception and it cuts the other way — Nest of Scarabs and three Swamps, no outlet anywhere on the board, a counter source making a body with nothing to eat it — which makes 006 the single verified support for the DECLARATION's reading of ignition. I still disagree with that reading. goldfish_targets names 'an ignition source drawn (something that places the first -1/-1 counter)' — eight cards, 73.8% — and that is the ignition of one BRANCH. The three unbounded kills are stacks 009, 011 and 012, and the string '-1/-1' appears nowhere in any of them; neither does Yawgmoth. Two exclude the branch outright by assumption: 012 says 'Nest of Scarabs is NOT on the battlefield' and 009 says 'no Nest of Scarabs, no Blowfly Infestation, no commander'. An outlet is on the board in all three. The counter is what the commander's branch converts; the outlet is what every branch needs. Price of running it: 1 life and one body per Yawgmoth activation, and the draw is MANDATORY — there is no 'may' — which is what bounds two of the six kills (010 and 014 both terminate on the library).

6.3Fodder

Eleven cards: what the outlet eats, and what puts it back. Token engines that pay upkeep by upkeep (Bitterblossom, Ophiomancer, and Sedgemoor Witch off any instant or sorcery), a fat one-card board (Grave Titan), a body that recurs itself for {1}{B} (Reassembling Skeleton), and five ways to buy back a dead one — Animate Dead, Victimize, Persist, Agadeem's Awakening and Phyrexian Reclamation, the slow one because it returns to HAND rather than the battlefield — plus Stitcher's Supplier, which buys nothing back and mills three to fill the yard the other five draw from. Measured: mean bodies on board 4.239 at turn 6, and the audit's nine-card RENEWABLE FODDER class is 82.3% drawn / 72.2% by turn 6. That class is split across FOUR stages here on purpose, five of its nine landing in this one: Nest of Scarabs and Pawn of Ulamog make bodies only as the output of a death, so they are `conversion`; Gravecrawler is a kill half, so it is `wincon`; and Black Market Connections is bought for the cards, not the Demon, so it is `fuel`. THIS IS THE STAGE EVERY VERIFIED ROTATION RUNS OUT OF. Stacks 002, 003, 004 and 005 each end for the same reason and it is never life and never the library — it is bodies. 002 completes five activations from a three-body board; 005 gets eight because South Wind Avatar's 4 toughness absorbs four counters. One activation costs two bodies and returns one whenever the counter has to go on something of yours that dies to it, so the engine is body-NEGATIVE unless the deck keeps feeding it from here.

6.4Fuel

Seven cards, and five of them are tutors. Necropotence and Black Market Connections print cards for life directly; Demonic Tutor, Vampiric Tutor, Beseech the Mirror, Diabolic Intent and Insatiable Avarice convert one card into the exact missing half. The tutor class is 61.2% drawn / 50.3% by turn 6, and the goldfish models a drawn, affordable tutor as filling exactly ONE missing any_of group — that single substitution is the whole distance between the unassisted and assisted rates the rest of this file quotes: KILL E 13.5% → 58.8%, Gravecrawler 17.0% → 66.9%, the lone answer to a noncreature permanent 17.1% → 67.1%. Every tutor spent on defence is a tutor not spent on a kill half, and the simulation cannot model that choice. The bigger draw engine is not in this stage at all: it is the commander's own mandatory 'draw a card', and the goldfish's stated assumptions exclude extra card draw entirely, so every unassisted figure in this model is a FLOOR on games where Yawgmoth sticks and digs. No card in this stage appears in any of the eleven passing stacks — the verified tier covers 18 of the deck's 80 distinct cards and none of them is a tutor.

6.5Conversion

Seven cards, each turning a death into something that is NOT life loss: a token (Nest of Scarabs, Pawn of Ulamog), a Treasure (Pitiless Plunderer), two cards (Skullclamp, Deadly Dispute), one card per body sacrificed (Plumb the Forbidden), a sacrifice at every other seat (Grave Pact). Nest of Scarabs is the marquee — five of the eleven passing stacks name it, more than any card but the commander — and four of those five refute the line the card is famous for. From 002's three-body board the engine completes FIVE activations and stops on body exhaustion: seven deaths, 28 life off a pod of 160, pilot 39 → 42. 003 is the same board with Zulaport Cutthroat and settles that the pilot is never forced to eat his own drain (601.2h, 115.6). 004 is eight drains and its checker-passed resolution ends 'NOT A KILL, AND NOT EVEN CLOSE' — a verdict the adversarial checker did not merely accept: it 'wrote an exhaustive depth-first search over the whole move space' and reproduced every headline figure (8, 7, 28, 24) independently. 005 gets eight activations but only seven drains, because a body that dies at toughness 0 gains 0 life and CR 119.9 says that is not a lifegain event. 008 is the exception that pays double: Mirkwood Bats sees the sacrifice AND the creation, 13 triggers, each opponent 40 → 27. Rate for the declared kill built on this stage: 13.5% assembled / 58.8% assisted, mean turn 6.267. FOUR of the seven have no evidence at all — Grave Pact, Skullclamp, Deadly Dispute and Plumb the Forbidden are each absent from every passing stack, from every goldfish any_of and from all 39 contained lines. Only Nest of Scarabs, Pawn of Ulamog and Pitiless Plunderer carry any.

6.6Output

Sixteen cards, and the two halves are the same currency rather than two suites. The DRAIN half is nine deep — Bastion of Remembrance, Zulaport Cutthroat, Blood Artist, Mirkwood Bats, Ayara, South Wind Avatar, Marauding Blight-Priest, Starscape Cleric, The Meathook Massacre — at 82.3% drawn / 72.0% by turn 6, the deck's best-built layer; Gray Merchant is the tenth, a one-shot version of the same text. That nine-card rate is NOT every kill's drain rate, and the split is functional: only KILL E and KILL F draw their drain slot from all nine. KILL A, B and D need a DEATH-triggered drain and take a 3-deep group (Bastion, Zulaport, Ayara); KILL C needs a LIFEGAIN-triggered one and takes a disjoint 3-deep group (South Wind Avatar, Marauding Blight-Priest, Starscape Cleric). Four of the six kills have a three-card drain layer, not a nine-card one. Bastion of Remembrance alone is named in four passing stacks (002, 009, 010, 011). The SUBTRACTION half — Fume Spitter, Skinrender, Soul Snuffers, Black Sun's Zenith, Massacre Girl's wither, Sinister Gnarlbark's blight — is not a second removal suite: a -1/-1 counter placed on anything is a Nest of Scarabs trigger and a shrunken creature is still a body the outlet can eat, so the deck's removal and the deck's engine spend the same resource. Spot removal aimed at a permanent that is a PROBLEM — Fatal Push, Deadly Rollick, Feed the Swarm — lives in `protection` instead, because it answers the table rather than feeding the engine. Both halves feed backwards: the drains refund the life the outlet spends, and 002 measures the refund exactly — five activations against seven death triggers, ending 39 → 42, the board having arrived at 39 with the first activation's life already paid, so the ledger closes as 39 − 4 + 7.

6.7Protection

Six cards. Lightning Greaves puts shroud on a commander whose entire job is standing on the battlefield being activated. Malakir Rebirth and Supernatural Stamina answer a removal spell by letting the creature die and come straight back — and the death still pays every drain, which is the shape this deck wants from insurance. Deadly Rollick is free while you control a commander, Fatal Push kills the cheap thing, and Feed the Swarm is the card the audit prices as this stage's single point of failure: the deck's only answer to a noncreature permanent, 17.1% drawn / 67.1% if a tutor is spent on defence instead of a kill half. READ THAT FIGURE NARROWLY. Feed the Swarm destroys 'target creature or enchantment an opponent controls' — it cannot touch an artifact — and the format's cheap hosers for THIS deck are artifacts: Grafdigger's Cage on the graveyard casting and the undying returns, Cursed Totem on every creature outlet including the commander. The odds of answering either are not 17.1%; they are zero. The interaction axis reads 2 of 5 classes (creature 14, enchantment 1, artifact 0, graveyard 0, land 0), and no card among the 80 destroys, exiles or bounces an artifact. What binds here is therefore not a draw rate on one enchantment answer — it is a class covered zero times, which no draw improves. Nothing in this stage appears in any passing stack, so none of it is verified in play; it is the layer the eleven scenarios assume away when they state 'no player responds to anything'.

6.8Win condition

Five cards, each the named half of a declared kill: Gravecrawler (KILL A and KILL B), Exquisite Blood (C), Mikaeus (D), Butcher Ghoul with Geralf's Messenger (F). Three of the six kills are genuinely unbounded and all three are checker-verified. 009: Warren Soultrader plus Gravecrawler plus a drain — each iteration's Treasure exactly funds the next cast, 39 further iterations put all four opponents at 0 at once. 011: Phyrexian Altar instead, and it costs no life at all — the pilot ends at 80. 012: Exquisite Blood plus a you-gain-they-lose body, mandatory, and the checker settled that it is a win and not the CR 104.4b draw because every iteration lowers every opponent's life. The other two are library-bounded and still lethal: 010 needs 39 more activations against the 59 the library permits, 014 kills at ~30 activations inside a ~55 bound with ~25 draws of margin. The rules are not the problem — the rates are. A 1.3% / 18.2% assisted, B 0.6% / 12.0%, C 6.6% / 42.6%, D 4.6% / 34.3%, F 1.8% / 26.9%. The best-drawing line is KILL E at 13.5% / 58.8%, and four passing stacks say its board grinds rather than wins, which is why the card that names it sits in `conversion` and not here.
