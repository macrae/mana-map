1General

What the machine is and what it is for.

This is a mono-green deck that spends the three turns which are not yours: Radagast opens one discounted flash window per player turn, a creature walks through each one, a card-per-body engine pays for the creature, and the accumulated board is cashed in by a mass-pump finisher — whose verified kill threshold is FIVE other bodies beside the finisher, not the finisher itself.

What this model does not do (19 stated assumptions)
  • Simulates resource development, not full games (no interaction, no removal).
  • Draw every turn including turn 1 (multiplayer Commander).
  • Mulligan: keep 7-card hands with 2-5 lands; up to 2 fresh redraws, keep the last.
  • One land drop per turn when available.
  • LANDS ENTER UNTAPPED, ALWAYS, and the one in hand longest is the one played. A tapland costs nothing here and no land is ever chosen over another, so this model CANNOT rank two lands that make the same colours — `mana-analysis` and `mana-fit` are what answer a land question, and they are deterministic for exactly this reason.
  • Mana rocks ('{T}: Add') contribute from the turn after they are cast.
  • Commander cast on first affordable turn (highest spending priority).
  • Bodies count = creatures cast + tokens parsed from 'create ... token' text.
  • Target assembly counts cards DRAWN by a turn (cast cards still count).
  • CARD DRAW IS ONE A TURN UNLESS `model_draw` IS SET. With the flag on, four channels are modelled — a card's own ETB draw, an instant or sorcery that draws, an upkeep trigger, and an ARRIVAL trigger that draws when other creatures or tokens enter (Welcoming Vampire, Caretaker's Talent), which rides the same single door the ETB damage payoffs use. Activated draw (needs a spending policy this model has no opinion on), X-based draw (board-dependent), and death- or attack-triggered draw (no deaths, and attacks only under `model_combat`) are NOT modelled and the cards are named in `meta.card_advantage.draw_not_modelled`. A conditional trigger — 'if you control an artifact', 'you may draw' — is treated as unmodelled rather than assumed to be on: 39 of the 348 ETB-draw cards in the corpus carry one.
  • AN ARRIVAL-DRAW ENGINE NEVER SEES ITS OWN ARRIVAL. Welcoming Vampire is a 2/3 that draws 'whenever one or more OTHER creatures you control with power 2 or less enter'; registered before its own entry it passed its own gate and drew a card it does not draw, overstating edgar-vampires by 22% at turn eight. The deferral applies to every card and not only the ones worded 'other', which understates Tocasia's Welcome and two relatives by one draw on the turn they land.
  • ARRIVAL DRAW IS SMALLER WITHOUT `model_combat`, and legitimately so. The channel rides the one door onto the battlefield, which `model_combat` also uses to spawn token copies and ETB-payoff tokens — those are real additional arrivals that do not exist in the resource-only model. Measured on edgar-vampires: 1.264 extra cards by turn ten with both flags against 1.106 with draw alone. Before 2026-08-28 the gap was 1.264 against 0.323, because every call to that door sat inside `if model_combat:` and a draw-only deck silently lost three quarters of its arrival draws.
  • HELD-UP INTERACTION is reported as two series and both are FLOORS. This model casts everything it can afford every turn, so `interaction_castable` is what a pilot developing at full speed has left over, not what a pilot choosing to hold up two mana would have. A low figure is therefore a real finding and a high one is unambiguous good news.
  • Tutors are modeled as wildcards: a CAST tutor that fetches to hand or the top — including a TYPED one (Worldly Tutor, Sarkhan's Triumph), which the old literal 'a card' match missed entirely — fills ONE missing any_of group. Consumed once, mana paid, and a tutor that puts the card on top of the library costs a turn. An ACTIVATED, ETB or death-triggered tutor does not count, because this model only knows 'drawn and affordable'; nor does a land fetch, which is ramp and has no channel here yet. Reported as the *_assisted figures; the unassisted figures beside them exclude tutors entirely.
  • ENTERS-THE-BATTLEFIELD PAYOFFS are modelled: damage equal to the entering creature's power (Terror of the Peaks), X damage where X counts your board (Scourge of Valkas, Dragon Tempest), a token (Lathliss) and a token COPY of what entered (Miirym). Every arrival fires them — cast, token or copy — and a copy is itself an arrival, so they compound, which is the deck working rather than a bug. A payoff worded 'another NONTOKEN' does not re-trigger on its own tokens, which is the brake the rules already had. A chain is capped at 12 deep. X counts the whole board rather than one subtype: exact where the creatures are Dragons, generous otherwise.
  • The COMMANDER joins the battlefield when cast, attacks, and fires its own triggers. It used to be a mana sink and a flag — cast, then dropped — so a 10/10 flier contributed no power and never swung. Commander tax, death and recasting are still not modelled: it is cast once and it stays.
  • STATIC cost reduction IS modeled: a commander's eminence from the command zone (live from turn one, unremovable) and typed reducers once they are on the battlefield. It pays GENERIC only and is floored at the coloured pip count, because a discount can never pay a pip. A reduction that SCALES with a board state (Animar, Rakdos, Hamza) is refused rather than counted flat, and cost reduction on artifacts, noncreature spells or a colour is not modeled at all.
  • Rituals are not modeled (conservative).
  • Extra card draw is NOT modeled: one card per turn, always. That understates every deck, but by an amount proportional to how much draw it runs — so it is conservative WITHIN a deck and not neutral BETWEEN them. `meta.card_advantage` reports how much of each list this hides. Card advantage is measured nowhere in this suite.

6.1Mana

Thirty-six deliberately plain lands plus a two-humped ramp class (18 cards, RAMP drawn by t6 at 93.6%) — but the job is not casting bigger things sooner. The job is having something left over when it is somebody else's turn. A tapped-out Radagast deck has three dead windows a round, so this stage is measured by what stays UNSPENT. Castle Garenbrig is the emblem: its six {G} burst is spend-restricted to creature spells, which makes a liability elsewhere the best land here. It APPEARS in three passing stacks (003, 005, 007) and it PAYS in exactly one of them — stack 005, where four Forests buy the activation and all six restricted {G} land on a discounted Craterhoof. In 003 the six Forests pay for Craterhoof directly and Garenbrig is explicitly left untapped; in 007 it cannot pay for Chord at all. An appearance count is not a load-bearing count, and iterations 1 and 2 reported it as one. Sakura-Tribe Elder, Elvish Reclaimer and Wood Elves sit on both sides of the ramp/body line; Wood Elves is filed under fuel because it is the canonical window body.

6.2Ignition

Radagast has to resolve. His one ability — 'the first creature spell you cast each turn costs {2} less to cast and can be cast as though it had flash' — grants the turn's FIRST creature spell both prongs at once, and 'each turn' means every player's turn: four windows per round in a four-player pod, verified. The two prongs have different backups, and that is why this stage names NO single point of failure. On TIMING, Yeva reads 'you may cast green creature spells as though they had flash' with no per-turn limit and this deck is mono-green, so she is strictly broader than Radagast; and Radagast is the commander, the one card removal cannot answer for good — stack 004 step 5 moves him to the command zone under 903.9a, recastable at tax. On PRICE there is no backup at all: nothing else in the 71 reduces a creature spell by {2}, and Yeva is a single copy that no declared component prices. Answering Radagast therefore does not answer the window — it answers the discount, and the discount is what the one verified kill payment is made of.

6.3Fuel

The window consumes one creature per turn, and the declared METRONOME component — 19 cards, drawn by t6 in 94.5% of runs, mean turn 1.615 — is what it eats. The declaration labels that component '3-5 MV, >=2 generic — the full-value flash-in class', and four of its nineteen members fall outside the stated band: Satyr Wayfinder ({1}{G}, MV 2, one generic), Eternal Witness ({1}{G}{G}, MV 3, one generic), Whiptongue Hydra (MV 6) and Hornet Queen (MV 7). The membership is defensible — all four are still bodies the window can deploy — but the parenthetical does not describe it, and a goldfish edit is proposed for that. The ten cards listed HERE are the narrower set whose only job is to be a body at the right price. Mirkwood Pathmaker is the odd one and the important one: its power and toughness each equal the number of lands you control, which makes it the deck's greatest-power dial — see map_disagreements.

6.4Conversion

The engine proper: it turns bodies into cards, which become bodies. Eleven cards are filed here; ten of them are the declared GAS component (drawn by t6 at 75.9%, mean turn 2.467) and the eleventh, Fauna Shaman, belongs to the declared TUTOR component instead — it converts a spare creature in hand into the specific one. What the engine converts is a resource other decks throw away: mana still untapped when the turn passes. Glademuse is the purest statement of that ('whenever a player casts a spell, if it's not their turn, that player draws') and it is symmetric. There is no `fodder` stage in this deck — not because nothing sacrifices (Sakura-Tribe Elder, Caustic Caterpillar, Sylvan Safekeeper and Elvish Reclaimer all do, and Tireless Provisioner makes Food and Treasure to crack), but because none of it is a THEME: no card here eats a body for value on purpose, and the one board that gets consumed in a passing stack is consumed by an opponent's Day of Judgment. Selvala is the deck's only priced single point of failure — a one-card class at 12.3% by t6 — though stack 007 shows Chord finds her at instant speed.

6.5Output

What the window produces besides bodies: interaction on somebody else's turn, mostly wearing a creature. The declared FLASH TRAP component is 16 cards drawn by t6 at 91.3%, and it spans FIVE stages, 6/6/2/1/1: six here, six in protection, two in fuel (Hornet Nest, Dawn's Light Archer), one in conversion (Ohran Frostfang), one in ignition (Yeva). Two of the ten are native instants — Beast Within, and Chord of Calling, which stack 007 casts in opponent 3's end step under 304.1 while verifying that it takes no discount, consumes no window, cannot spend Garenbrig's restricted six and triggers no Beast Whisperer. One cannot be held at all: Pick Your Poison, a sorcery. The other seven split on WHEN THEY ANSWER, not on when they arrive. Three answer at instant speed from the battlefield with no grant and no window spent: Wickerbough Elder ('{G}, Remove a -1/-1 counter from this creature: Destroy target artifact or enchantment'), Caustic Caterpillar ('{1}{G}, Sacrifice this creature:' the same) and Nantuko Vigilante, whose morph reminder reads 'Turn it face up any time for its morph cost' and whose destroy trigger fires on the turn-up. Only four have the answer welded to the arrival — Indrik Stomphowler, Whiptongue Hydra and Ravenous Sailback on an ETB, Collector Ouphe on a static — and those four are the ones a flash grant times. For the other three a grant buys deployment, not timing. So on Radagast alone the stage answers with two native instants, one granted arrival a turn, and whatever of the three self-timers is already down; Yeva's grant has no per-turn limit.

6.6Protection

The deck is linear on Radagast and on one board, so this stage is what stops a single removal spell or a single wrath from being the whole game. Guardian Augmenter flashes in, pumps commander creatures +2/+2 and grants commanders hexproof; Saryth grants hexproof to OTHER untapped creatures you control and deathtouch to OTHER tapped ones — the 'other' matters twice, because Saryth protects neither herself nor a creature that has attacked, and an attacking Radagast is tapped and therefore uncovered. FIVE of the eight are instants held rather than cast: Heroic Intervention {1}{G}, Tyvar's Stand {X}{G}, Veil of Summer {G}, Inspiring Call {2}{G}, Warg Tactics {1}{G} — which costs nothing here because the deck was holding mana anyway. Sylvan Safekeeper is not the sixth and this model counted it as one: it is a {G} Human Wizard, so it has to be CAST, at sorcery speed unless it is that turn's first creature spell and spends the window on a 1/1 — and its protection is an activated ability paid in LAND, 'Sacrifice a land: Target creature you control gains shroud until end of turn'. It answers at instant speed only once it is already on the battlefield, and shroud is a targeting restriction (702.18a: 'can't be the target of spells or abilities'), so it does nothing against a wrath. Toski belongs functionally in this stage too and is filed under conversion; its indestructibility is verified in stack 004 and its draw in 002.

6.7Win condition

A mass-pump alpha strike over a wide board. Four finishers, drawn by t6 at 42.4%; the declared ASSEMBLED target measures 40.2% — and its second group is TWENTY cards, the metronome nineteen plus Scute Swarm, not the metronome component its label names. Two passing stacks bracket the real threshold and it is the most important arithmetic in the deck. Stack 003: Craterhoof over FOUR other bodies is X=5, five attackers, 36 unblocked into a 40-life seat — NOT lethal, the seat survives on 4. Stack 005: Craterhoof cast in your own precombat main over FIVE other bodies is X=6, exactly 40 trample power into one seat, lethal with zero margin and only per-seat, never the pod (one of the five, Hornet Nest, has defender and never attacks — it raises X without swinging). Stack 005 also names the one card the deck's signature verb is wrong for: flashed on the prior end step, Craterhoof's +6/+6 and trample expire at that turn's cleanup (514.2), the swing is 10 unpumped power and it kills nobody; the flash mode is a wrath-dodge only. The four finishers are not one class. Only Craterhoof scales on creature COUNT and brings its own haste and trample; Overwhelming Stampede and Pathbreaker Ibex multiply by the greatest power on your board; Return of the Wildspeaker is a flat +3/+3 for NON-HUMAN creatures with no trample, and its other mode is a draw spell. Radagast's discount is creature-spell-only, so Craterhoof and Ibex can ride the window and Stampede (sorcery) and Return (instant) never can. This stage names no single point of failure: no passing stack resolves any of the other three, so nothing establishes either that they work or that they do not, and an open question asks.
