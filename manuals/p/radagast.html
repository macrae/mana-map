The Plan

What this deck is trying to do, and what it is assuming away. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

How many turns a round does this deck actually get? The table says one. Radagast makes the first creature spell you cast each turn cost {2} less and lets you cast it at instant speed, and "each turn" means every player's turn — four windows a round at a four-player table, verified in stack 001, where Wood Elves costs a single {G} on an opponent's end step. Stop waiting for your own turn.

Here is the trade the deck makes. Hold the mana you were not going to spend, and buy a body and a card on a turn that is not yours: Beast Whisperer, Toski and Glademuse all pay you for a cast you were making anyway, and Glademuse pays only when it is not your turn — which is exactly where you are casting. The goldfish average board at turn six is 3.8 bodies and it counts no window at all, so every window you take is a body nobody modelled. Take all four.

The same open mana is your interaction, which is why holding it costs you nothing. Ohran Frostfang flashed in after blockers are declared hands your attackers deathtouch in time for damage and draws off it (stack 002); Fecundity turns their wrath into four cards while indestructible Toski just stands there (stack 004). Spend each window on whatever hurts the table most that turn — a body, an answer, a blowout. The board that survives all of that is the win condition; everything after it is collection.

So treat Craterhoof Behemoth as a button, not a plan. Press it from your own precombat main — flashed on an end step, the pump wears off before you get to swing with it (stack 005). Five other bodies beside the Hoof clears exactly one seat's forty and four bodies leaves that seat alive on 4 (stacks 005 and 003), so choose the seat first, then subtract every creature they can put in front of you. What else that count quietly assumes is printed beside this page — read it before you press.

What this does not model

  • Unsettled Whiptongue Hydra flashed in after blockers are declared: does its ETB sweep the fliers before combat damage, how many +1/+1 counters does it keep, and does it kill your own Hornet Nest tokens and Hornet Queen tokens in the process?
  • Unsettled Can Scute Swarm alone reach the fifth body BESIDE the finisher? With six or more lands its landfall creates a copy of itself per land entering; how many bodies does the board hold on the turn before Craterhoof, counting one land drop plus a fetch from Wood Elves or Sakura-Tribe Elder?
  • Unsettled Does Mirkwood Pathmaker function as this deck's greatest-power dial? With N lands, what is the X on Overwhelming Stampede and Pathbreaker Ibex, how many cards does Return of the Wildspeaker draw, and does Selvala's 'power greater than each other creature's power' gate then shut off for every subsequent body — including against opponents' creatures?

Keep or Ship

What a keepable hand looks like, and how often you get one. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

Keep two to four lands and one accelerant — that is the whole rule, and the sim backs it: 81.5% of first sevens are keeps, and kept hands cluster at two to four lands. A one-mana dork is turn-three Radagast; he lands on three in about a third of games and on four in more than half, nearly always off an Elves.

The second thing to want is something to do at instant speed: an Ohran Frostfang, a Heroic Intervention, a Wood Elves to flash into someone's end step. A hand that plays draw-go from turn three is a keep. A hand that must tap out on its own turn every turn is playing someone else's deck.

Ship three shapes. Two or more five-plus drops — Craterhoof Behemoth is the payoff, not the plan. All lands and dorks with nothing to hold up. And any hand you are keeping because Selvala, Heart of the Wilds is in it — she is a class of one, so she is a bonus, never a reason. Everything else, keep: this deck averages 0.22 mulligans, and it earns that by not needing much.

The metronome window

does Radagast's discount-plus-flash reset on every player's turn, and what does Wood Elves cost on an opponent's end step?

The case turns on two words: "each turn." The printed template "each of your turns" exists, and Radagast does not use it — so the count of creature spells you have cast zeroes at every untap, yours and theirs alike, a turn being a structural unit every player takes (CR 500.1, CR 102.1). One candour the record keeps and this column repeats: no single rule states that an each-turn count resets. The holding is derived from the ability's text plus the definition of a turn, and the checker judged the derivation sound exactly as hedged.

What resolves: Wood Elves for {G} on an opponent's end step, both halves of the ability — discount and as-though-flash — riding the same first spell (CR 601.3d, CR 601.2f), the fetched Forest entering untapped because it is put, not played (CR 305.4). Plain English: four windows per round in a four-player pod, one spell per window, and your second creature spell in any turn gets nothing.

The Frostfang trap

flashed in after blockers are declared, does deathtouch apply to damage already lined up, and how many cards does one unblocked 1/1 draw?

The clause that decides this is CR 604.2: a static ability functions from the moment its permanent is on the battlefield. Nothing asks whether Ohran Frostfang was present when attackers were declared — attacking status persists until the creature leaves combat or combat ends — so deathtouch attaches to damage already lined up. Toski's single point destroys the 6/6 as a state-based action (CR 704.5h); the 6 coming back is ignored outright, indestructible reading the lethal-damage check out of existence (CR 702.12b).
