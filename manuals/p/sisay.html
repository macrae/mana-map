1General

What the machine is and what it is for.

Sisay is a mana-to-battlefield converter whose exchange rate is her own power: five coloured pips buy one legendary permanent, every new colour she buys widens the next purchase, and the currency every other stage is paying for is ACCESS rather than cards or mana.

What this model does not do (19 stated assumptions)
  • Simulates resource development, not full games (no interaction, no removal).
  • Draw every turn including turn 1 (multiplayer Commander).
  • Mulligan: keep 7-card hands with 2-5 lands; up to 2 fresh redraws, keep the last.
  • One land drop per turn when available.
  • LANDS ENTER UNTAPPED, ALWAYS, and the one in hand longest is the one played. A tapland costs nothing here and no land is ever chosen over another, so this model CANNOT rank two lands that make the same colours — `mana-analysis` and `mana-fit` are what answer a land question, and they are deterministic for exactly this reason.
  • Mana rocks ('{T}: Add') contribute from the turn after they are cast.
  • Commander cast on first affordable turn (highest spending priority).
  • Bodies count = creatures cast + tokens parsed from 'create ... token' text.
  • Target assembly counts cards DRAWN by a turn (cast cards still count).
  • CARD DRAW IS ONE A TURN UNLESS `model_draw` IS SET. With the flag on, four channels are modelled — a card's own ETB draw, an instant or sorcery that draws, an upkeep trigger, and an ARRIVAL trigger that draws when other creatures or tokens enter (Welcoming Vampire, Caretaker's Talent), which rides the same single door the ETB damage payoffs use. Activated draw (needs a spending policy this model has no opinion on), X-based draw (board-dependent), and death- or attack-triggered draw (no deaths, and attacks only under `model_combat`) are NOT modelled and the cards are named in `meta.card_advantage.draw_not_modelled`. A conditional trigger — 'if you control an artifact', 'you may draw' — is treated as unmodelled rather than assumed to be on: 39 of the 348 ETB-draw cards in the corpus carry one.
  • AN ARRIVAL-DRAW ENGINE NEVER SEES ITS OWN ARRIVAL. Welcoming Vampire is a 2/3 that draws 'whenever one or more OTHER creatures you control with power 2 or less enter'; registered before its own entry it passed its own gate and drew a card it does not draw, overstating edgar-vampires by 22% at turn eight. The deferral applies to every card and not only the ones worded 'other', which understates Tocasia's Welcome and two relatives by one draw on the turn they land.
  • ARRIVAL DRAW IS SMALLER WITHOUT `model_combat`, and legitimately so. The channel rides the one door onto the battlefield, which `model_combat` also uses to spawn token copies and ETB-payoff tokens — those are real additional arrivals that do not exist in the resource-only model. Measured on edgar-vampires: 1.264 extra cards by turn ten with both flags against 1.106 with draw alone. Before 2026-08-28 the gap was 1.264 against 0.323, because every call to that door sat inside `if model_combat:` and a draw-only deck silently lost three quarters of its arrival draws.
  • HELD-UP INTERACTION is reported as two series and both are FLOORS. This model casts everything it can afford every turn, so `interaction_castable` is what a pilot developing at full speed has left over, not what a pilot choosing to hold up two mana would have. A low figure is therefore a real finding and a high one is unambiguous good news.
  • Tutors are modeled as wildcards: a CAST tutor that fetches to hand or the top — including a TYPED one (Worldly Tutor, Sarkhan's Triumph), which the old literal 'a card' match missed entirely — fills ONE missing any_of group. Consumed once, mana paid, and a tutor that puts the card on top of the library costs a turn. An ACTIVATED, ETB or death-triggered tutor does not count, because this model only knows 'drawn and affordable'; nor does a land fetch, which is ramp and has no channel here yet. Reported as the *_assisted figures; the unassisted figures beside them exclude tutors entirely.
  • ENTERS-THE-BATTLEFIELD PAYOFFS are modelled: damage equal to the entering creature's power (Terror of the Peaks), X damage where X counts your board (Scourge of Valkas, Dragon Tempest), a token (Lathliss) and a token COPY of what entered (Miirym). Every arrival fires them — cast, token or copy — and a copy is itself an arrival, so they compound, which is the deck working rather than a bug. A payoff worded 'another NONTOKEN' does not re-trigger on its own tokens, which is the brake the rules already had. A chain is capped at 12 deep. X counts the whole board rather than one subtype: exact where the creatures are Dragons, generous otherwise.
  • The COMMANDER joins the battlefield when cast, attacks, and fires its own triggers. It used to be a mana sink and a flag — cast, then dropped — so a 10/10 flier contributed no power and never swung. Commander tax, death and recasting are still not modelled: it is cast once and it stays.
  • STATIC cost reduction IS modeled: a commander's eminence from the command zone (live from turn one, unremovable) and typed reducers once they are on the battlefield. It pays GENERIC only and is floored at the coloured pip count, because a discount can never pay a pip. A reduction that SCALES with a board state (Animar, Rakdos, Hamza) is refused rather than counted flat, and cost reduction on artifacts, noncreature spells or a colour is not modeled at all.
  • Rituals are not modeled (conservative).
  • Extra card draw is NOT modeled: one card per turn, always. That understates every deck, but by an amount proportional to how much draw it runs — so it is conservative WITHIN a deck and not neutral BETWEEN them. `meta.card_advantage` reports how much of each list this hides. Card advantage is measured nowhere in this suite.

6.1Mana

The activation this deck exists to make costs {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} — five coloured pips with zero generic — so none of the deck's generic acceleration touches it. That is why 39 land entries carry the load and only four rocks come along. Black is the ceiling: mana_analysis reports W 26, G 27, U 22, R 21 and B 21 sources, against a Karsten target of 36 for black (Sheoldred is {2}{B}{B}) and 22 for the other four; on-curve probability for black is 0.299 on lands alone and 0.610 once rocks and dorks are counted, against 0.944 W and 0.951 G. Black carries 11.1% of the pip load and 18.5% of the sources, so the shortfall is not about casting Toxic Deluge on time — it is that the activation you want to make every turn is one black source away from not happening. The declared 'Early accelerant drawn' component is seven cards: 41.2% in the opener, 49.8% by turn three, 74.7% assembled, mean turn 2.836. Plaza of Heroes sits here because stack 002 taps and untaps it for four of the five pips, though mana_analysis credits it with zero colour sources: both coloured modes are conditional. It is NOT tutorable: its type line is Land, Sisay searches for a legendary permanent card, and no fetchland here finds it either — so its hexproof-and-indestructible mode is protection she can never reach. Delighted Halfling pays none of them: its any-colour mana is spend-only-to-cast-a-legendary-spell and these five pips are an ACTIVATION cost, so mana_analysis records it as producing nothing. Birds of Paradise, unrestricted, pays exactly one of the five. Mox Amber is here rather than at ignition because it is colourless: one of only three legendary permanents below mana value 2, it adds nothing to Sisay's power and produces nothing until a legendary creature or planeswalker is on the board.

6.2Ignition

Nothing in this deck happens on schedule until Sisay's ability reaches more than three cards, and what it reaches is set by the number of colours among OTHER legendary permanents you control. Counted over cards.json: 3 / 10 / 23 / 28 / 33 / 33 targets at 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 colours. The number to hold is TWO, where access more than doubles. These six are the cheapest way there — coloured legendary permanents at mana value 2 or less whose job is not the dial and not a shield, four of them carrying two colours on one card. They share no text at all: Kinnan doubles nonland mana, Lotho taxes second spells into Treasure, Rona loots and untaps on legendary casts, Saffi buys a creature back, Katilda gives Humans (Sisay included) a mana ability, Yoshimaru just grows itself. In this engine their first job is identical — be a coloured legendary permanent on turn two, so the first activation is not choosing between Mox Amber, Yoshimaru and Mikaeus. Sisay herself is filed at conversion, not here: she is the machine rather than the starting gun, and what has to be true before her first activation is worth making is a board state, not a card. Nothing in goldfish_targets measures this, which is the gap the frame's own reading points at — 'Sisay plus payoffs is a hand of payoffs'.

6.3Fuel

One activation per turn cycle is value; two is something else, and this stage buys the extras. The six declared multi-colour mana sources are not interchangeable. Only Jegantha adds all five off one tap, and its can't-pay-generic clause is irrelevant against a cost with no generic. Bloom Tender and Faeburrow Elder pay one mana per colour among ALL permanents you control — not the number printed on Sisay, which counts colours among OTHER LEGENDARY permanents. Ten nonlegendary coloured permanents in this 99 — including both Vivid dorks counting themselves — move the Vivid count, not the dial. Vivid is a superset: never behind the ladder, usually ahead. Esika grants every OTHER legendary creature '{T}: Add one mana of any color', turning each fetched body into a source. Selvala adds X in any combination for your greatest creature power, often Sisay. Relic of Legends, the sixth, taps a legendary creature as a COST, not that creature's own {T}. Measured: 36.4% in the opener, assembled 67.9%, 55.8% by turn 6, mean turn 4.219, six cards deep. The untap half — Ioreth, Samut, Derevi, Seedborn Muse — turns those sources over again, and only Derevi's is proved by a passing stack. Emiel does not untap: he blinks, and the returned body is summoning-sick, so its own {T} waits on Samut's haste — Relic's cost does not. The draw half (Rhystic Study, Esper Sentinel, Reki) refills a hand the tutor cannot reach: Sisay PUTS permanents onto the battlefield rather than casting them, so Reki does not trigger off her ability. This deck has no `fodder` stage: nothing is sacrificed, discarded or exiled as a cost to run the engine. Mikaeus converts one board resource into another, a counter off himself onto the team, but nothing consumes a resource to power it.

6.4Conversion

Sisay's gate reads POWER: 'search your library for a legendary permanent card with mana value less than Sisay's power'. Base 2/2, +1/+1 for each colour among other legendary permanents. Two routes move that number and the card does not care which produced it. Colour breadth: Dihada (three colours), Surrak (three), Jodah (five; his own cast cost is the same five pips, a bill The Prismatic Bridge — Esika's back face, {1}{G}{G} in hand under CR 712.8a and {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} if you cast that side — also pays; at mana value 5 he is legal only at power 6, and Surrak and Kenrith tie him there). Counters and anthems: Mikaeus taps to grow himself and then to grow the team, Bristly Bill puts a counter on every landfall, Heliod turns lifegain into counters, Kenrith buys counters and haste with spare mana, and Flowering of the White Tree gives legendary creatures +2/+1 permanently — two more mana value of reach, plus ward {1}. The declared 'three-colour legend to grow her' component is the thinnest in the deck: four cards, 25.8% in the opener, 41.9% by turn 6, assembled 52.5%. Where the ladder tops out is a fact about this 99 and not about the card — nothing here exceeds mana value 5, so power 6 buys everything and further power buys nothing. Sisay is the single point of failure for the entire model: she is the only card that converts mana into board, and losing her resets the recast tax and every rung already climbed. Her central line is NOT verified. Stack 001 walked exactly this chain and the checker exhausted its iteration bound on one clause, so every arrow leaving this stage is dashed.

6.5Output

Instead of ten removal spells the deck buys virtual advantage, and most of it sits on the battlefield where the tutor can reach it. Kutzil stops opponents casting during your turn; Teferi holds them to sorcery speed; Drannith Magistrate cuts them off from anywhere but hand; Thalia makes their creatures and nonbasic lands enter tapped; Kambal and Sheoldred convert their spells and their draws into loss; Dauthi Voidwalker exiles what would reach their graveyards. Loran and Path to Exile are the two targeted answers, Toxic Deluge the reset, Kodama the trample that makes a counter-laden board connect, and Raff the flash grant that lets you deploy legends with counterspell mana still up. The frame's reading of the tax shell holds: 'All six are legendary at mana value 2-4, so each sits inside the fetch pool from the second colour onward' — for those six the answer suite and the ladder's rungs are the same cards, which is exactly why access compounds rather than merely accumulates. It does not generalise to the stage: four of these twelve sit outside the fetch pool at any power, because Sisay searches for a legendary permanent card. Drannith Magistrate and Dauthi Voidwalker are nonlegendary creatures, Path to Exile is an instant and Toxic Deluge a sorcery; all four have to be drawn. deck-audit reads interaction at 8 copies (at target) and interaction-breadth at 3 of 5 classes. This is the one stage where the deck's own map agrees with the engine completely: all twelve sit in THE TOOLBOX, whose gloss — 'one answer at a time, chosen after you see the problem' — is a fair description of what a tutor is for.

6.6Protection

The declared component is eleven cards and it is the healthiest thing in the deck: 57.4% in the opener, 67.0% by turn three, 88.4% assembled, 79.8% by turn 6, mean turn 2.3. All three of Cullen's classes are present — a constant that is itself tutorable (Shalai grants you and your other creatures hexproof), free or cheap instants (Fierce Guardianship, Flawless Maneuver, Heroic Intervention), and a denial layer (Grand Abolisher, Silence, Swan Song, An Offer You Can't Refuse, Dispel, Negate, Arcane Denial), plus Boros Charm's indestructible mode against a wrath. The structural fact the map states by accident: not one of the five cards in THE HELD ANSWERS is legendary, and neither are Heroic Intervention, Flawless Maneuver, Silence, Grand Abolisher or Boros Charm. The commander cannot fetch her own insurance. That is the frame's blind spot made concrete — 26 of the 99 are nonland and not legendary, and that set holds every instant in the deck and all four Game Changers. Protection here is a DRAW problem rather than an access problem, which is the opposite of every other stage in this model. Why the recast tax bites harder than usual: losing Sisay costs {2} more per prior command-zone cast AND every accumulated rung of access, so the protection is not insuring a body, it is insuring a position.

6.7Win condition

Najeela. Stack 002 is this deck's only checker-passed artifact and it settles what the pair IS rather than anything about the bracket: with Derevi it is a deterministic, unbounded combat loop but NOT a self-contained two-card infinite. The floor does not rest on it — bracket_report reaches 4 twice over without Derevi, once from four Game Changers alone and once from a two-card-infinite driver listing four lines, three of which pair Najeela with Faeburrow Elder, Esika or Selvala. It produces no mana, so every iteration re-buys {W}{U}{B}{R}{G} from outside, and it needs at least five of your creatures connecting with a player. The arithmetic it establishes: attackers follow A' = 2A - 1 (3, 5, 9, 17, 33) and Derevi's untap triggers equal the attacker count while the cost stays fixed at five, so combat 1 is funded from pre-combat lands, COMBAT 2 IS BREAK-EVEN (5 triggers against 5 mana) and combat 3 onward runs a surplus. Damage per combat is A + 3 (6, 8, 12, 20, 36); the first opponent dies during combat 4, the second by combat 6, and the life cost floors at 0 per combat. The number to hold in your head is the break-even combat, not the card count. What this model will not claim is that the deck reliably arrives here: goldfish_targets declares four targets and not one of them measures a win line, so how often Najeela and a live five-colour base show up together has never been measured. deck-audit's threat-density counts Boros Charm, Kambal and Samut and not Najeela, so the taxonomy and this engine disagree about what a finisher is here. Everything else that could end a game is filed where its first job is — a board of legends under Jodah's anthem, with Kodama's trample and Kenrith's haste.
