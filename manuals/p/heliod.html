1General

What the machine is and what it is for.

Heliod hands the whole table free cards and then bills them for holding the cards: ten symmetric-draw permanents fill every hand, three punishers charge for hand size on each opponent's upkeep (Iron Maiden, Viseling, Ebony Owl Netsuke) while three more drain or mill per card drawn, and the same turbulence pays the commander's discount and the tax layer's rent — behind that median plan sits a rules-verified but very rare one-turn kill built on a single creature.

What this model does not do (19 stated assumptions)
  • Simulates resource development, not full games (no interaction, no removal).
  • Draw every turn including turn 1 (multiplayer Commander).
  • Mulligan: keep 7-card hands with 2-5 lands; up to 2 fresh redraws, keep the last.
  • One land drop per turn when available.
  • LANDS ENTER UNTAPPED, ALWAYS, and the one in hand longest is the one played. A tapland costs nothing here and no land is ever chosen over another, so this model CANNOT rank two lands that make the same colours — `mana-analysis` and `mana-fit` are what answer a land question, and they are deterministic for exactly this reason.
  • Mana rocks ('{T}: Add') contribute from the turn after they are cast.
  • Commander cast on first affordable turn (highest spending priority).
  • Bodies count = creatures cast + tokens parsed from 'create ... token' text.
  • Target assembly counts cards DRAWN by a turn (cast cards still count).
  • CARD DRAW IS ONE A TURN UNLESS `model_draw` IS SET. With the flag on, four channels are modelled — a card's own ETB draw, an instant or sorcery that draws, an upkeep trigger, and an ARRIVAL trigger that draws when other creatures or tokens enter (Welcoming Vampire, Caretaker's Talent), which rides the same single door the ETB damage payoffs use. Activated draw (needs a spending policy this model has no opinion on), X-based draw (board-dependent), and death- or attack-triggered draw (no deaths, and attacks only under `model_combat`) are NOT modelled and the cards are named in `meta.card_advantage.draw_not_modelled`. A conditional trigger — 'if you control an artifact', 'you may draw' — is treated as unmodelled rather than assumed to be on: 39 of the 348 ETB-draw cards in the corpus carry one.
  • AN ARRIVAL-DRAW ENGINE NEVER SEES ITS OWN ARRIVAL. Welcoming Vampire is a 2/3 that draws 'whenever one or more OTHER creatures you control with power 2 or less enter'; registered before its own entry it passed its own gate and drew a card it does not draw, overstating edgar-vampires by 22% at turn eight. The deferral applies to every card and not only the ones worded 'other', which understates Tocasia's Welcome and two relatives by one draw on the turn they land.
  • ARRIVAL DRAW IS SMALLER WITHOUT `model_combat`, and legitimately so. The channel rides the one door onto the battlefield, which `model_combat` also uses to spawn token copies and ETB-payoff tokens — those are real additional arrivals that do not exist in the resource-only model. Measured on edgar-vampires: 1.264 extra cards by turn ten with both flags against 1.106 with draw alone. Before 2026-08-28 the gap was 1.264 against 0.323, because every call to that door sat inside `if model_combat:` and a draw-only deck silently lost three quarters of its arrival draws.
  • HELD-UP INTERACTION is reported as two series and both are FLOORS. This model casts everything it can afford every turn, so `interaction_castable` is what a pilot developing at full speed has left over, not what a pilot choosing to hold up two mana would have. A low figure is therefore a real finding and a high one is unambiguous good news.
  • Tutors are modeled as wildcards: a CAST tutor that fetches to hand or the top — including a TYPED one (Worldly Tutor, Sarkhan's Triumph), which the old literal 'a card' match missed entirely — fills ONE missing any_of group. Consumed once, mana paid, and a tutor that puts the card on top of the library costs a turn. An ACTIVATED, ETB or death-triggered tutor does not count, because this model only knows 'drawn and affordable'; nor does a land fetch, which is ramp and has no channel here yet. Reported as the *_assisted figures; the unassisted figures beside them exclude tutors entirely.
  • ENTERS-THE-BATTLEFIELD PAYOFFS are modelled: damage equal to the entering creature's power (Terror of the Peaks), X damage where X counts your board (Scourge of Valkas, Dragon Tempest), a token (Lathliss) and a token COPY of what entered (Miirym). Every arrival fires them — cast, token or copy — and a copy is itself an arrival, so they compound, which is the deck working rather than a bug. A payoff worded 'another NONTOKEN' does not re-trigger on its own tokens, which is the brake the rules already had. A chain is capped at 12 deep. X counts the whole board rather than one subtype: exact where the creatures are Dragons, generous otherwise.
  • The COMMANDER joins the battlefield when cast, attacks, and fires its own triggers. It used to be a mana sink and a flag — cast, then dropped — so a 10/10 flier contributed no power and never swung. Commander tax, death and recasting are still not modelled: it is cast once and it stays.
  • STATIC cost reduction IS modeled: a commander's eminence from the command zone (live from turn one, unremovable) and typed reducers once they are on the battlefield. It pays GENERIC only and is floored at the coloured pip count, because a discount can never pay a pip. A reduction that SCALES with a board state (Animar, Rakdos, Hamza) is refused rather than counted flat, and cost reduction on artifacts, noncreature spells or a colour is not modeled at all.
  • Rituals are not modeled (conservative).
  • Extra card draw is NOT modeled: one card per turn, always. That understates every deck, but by an amount proportional to how much draw it runs — so it is conservative WITHIN a deck and not neutral BETWEEN them. `meta.card_advantage` reports how much of each list this hides. Card advantage is measured nowhere in this suite.

6.1Mana

Thirty-three land copies across 18 entries — 17 of them here, with Mikokoro read as ignition below — plus seven cheap artifacts. Two jobs, and only one of them is 'cast your spells'. The first is ordinary: four donation permanents cost two — Howling Mine {2}, Kami of the Crescent Moon {U}{U}, Folio of Fancies {1}{U}, Kwain {W}{U} — and the other six cost three or more, so what a turn-one Sol Ring buys is a three- or four-drop on turn two: Temple Bell, Jace Beleren, Dictate of Kruphix, The Second Doctor, Font of Mythos. The ignition component's mean turn of 2.507 is NOT that sequence and cannot be: it is the mean turn the component is DRAWN, and the simulator counts a card drawn whether or not it was ever cast. The second job decides games. This deck spends its own turn deploying and still holds counter mana on everyone else's — and the land count alone does not support calling the base draw-go. Deck-audit reads mana-base 33 copies against strategy:deckbuilding.ratios' 37-39 (verdict under, gap 4) and colour-sources under by 5. What makes the posture affordable is the other axis and the price of the spells: counting the six persistent producers, mana-sources is 39 against Burgess's 37, verdict 'at' — and the counter suite is cheap enough to be held rather than cast, at 45.6% of openers and 78.1% of games (67.2% by turn six). Two rocks are literal engine parts rather than ramp: Sol Ring and Arcane Signet are the artifacts stacks 004, 005 and 006 recast against each other, where they are the thing being cast for a cast count. Sapphire and Pearl Medallion discount colours; stack 004 states flatly that Sapphire Medallion changes nothing about the loop, because both loop pieces are colourless.

6.2Ignition

Nothing else in this deck has a resource to charge for until one symmetric-draw permanent resolves. That is the whole ignition: Howling Mine, Font of Mythos, Temple Bell, Dictate of Kruphix, Kami of the Crescent Moon, Folio of Fancies, Forced Fruition, Kwain, The Second Doctor, Jace Beleren — plus Mikokoro, a land that does the same job for {2}. Ten of the eleven are the priced component: 53.7% of opening sevens, 63.3% by turn three, drawn in 85.2% of games (75.4% by turn six) at a mean turn of 2.507 — a DRAWN-by figure, not a deploy turn. It is the most reliable thing the deck does and the cheapest to get wrong, because a punisher with nothing donating is a blank. Two of them do a second job the stage name hides. The Second Doctor and Folio of Fancies each read 'Players have no maximum hand size', which removes the cleanup discard — without one of those two, Iron Maiden and Viseling are capped at 3 damage per upkeep by a seven-card hand. That is a two-card sub-requirement inside an eleven-card stage and nothing measures it (see proposed_goldfish_edits). Heliod is deliberately NOT here. The deck ignites without the commander; what he does to a running engine is conversion.

6.3Fuel

Once cards are flowing, this is what keeps them flowing and makes your share asymmetric. Three kinds of card. The tax layer is two different taxes and only one of them touches the donated draws. Consecrated Sphinx ('whenever an opponent draws a card, you may draw two cards') and Faerie Mastermind ('whenever an opponent draws their second card each turn, you draw a card') charge for the draws the ignition stage manufactures. Rhystic Study and Mystic Remora charge for CASTS, and reach the donation engine only second-hand — a fat hand casts more spells, which is a real effect and a different sentence. With Heliod down that seven-card component (it also counts Smothering Tithe, Teferi's Ageless Insight and Alhammarret's Archive) assembles in 71.0% of games, 54.0% by turn six, mean turn 5.070, and it is the one component deck-audit marks commander-gated. The doublers, Alhammarret's Archive and Teferi's Ageless Insight, take every draw after your first each turn and make it two. The burst — Prosperity, Skyscribing, Mathemagics, Braingeyser, Stroke of Genius, Dig Through Time — fills hands in one shot instead of one card a turn, which is how the punishers turn on inside a single turn cycle rather than across three upkeeps. The three tutors sit here rather than with the win conditions, because what they do is convert a card into the missing piece. The measured lift is small and real: with the deck's one modelled unrestricted tutor, the core pair goes 56.7% to 63.5% and win-condition access 61.2% to 68.0%. The kill's 0.7% to 2.4% is quoted in the conversion stage with the caveat it needs.

6.4Conversion

This deck converts twice, on two completely different clocks, and both machines live here. The median machine is the punisher rank, and it is not one trigger but three. Iron Maiden and Viseling deal hand size minus 4 on each opponent's upkeep and Ebony Owl Netsuke deals 4 at seven or more cards — those three are the upkeep hand-size tax. Psychosis Crawler drains 1 per card YOU draw and Sphinx's Tutelage mills two per card you draw (no damage at all); Scrawling Crawler drains 1 per card an opponent draws. Smothering Tithe bills each opposing draw for {2} or a Treasure. Six form the priced punisher component: 36.4% of openers, drawn in 67.2% of games, 55.9% by turn six, mean turn 3.045. Assembled WITH a donation engine the pair sits at 56.7% (41.2% by turn six, mean turn 4.215) — the pair is a strict subset of the ignition component, so a real band of games reaches turn six donating with nothing charging for it. Heliod converts the table's draws into a discount and flash: mana and a window, not damage. The rare machine is one card. Hullbreaker Horror plus two recastable artifacts is an unbounded, exactly mana-neutral cast loop (stack 004); Displacer Kitten does NOT make it mana-positive (005) and the two-card version does not exist at all (001). Hullbreaker is deck-audit's only single point of failure at 7.1% of openers. The declared KILL target measures 0.7% assembled, 2.4% assisted, 0.3% by turn six, mean turn 6.809 — read those as a CEILING, not the kill's rate: its rock group is any_of [Sol Ring, Arcane Signet], satisfied by either alone, which is the configuration 001 refuted, while 004's loop needs two. See proposed_goldfish_edits[0].

6.5Output

What the deck produces other than the punisher clock: targeted answers and a small amount of board. Path to Exile, Swords to Plowshares and Teferi, Hero of Dominaria's -3 handle a resolved permanent; Supreme Verdict resets a board none of these stages needs; Cyclonic Rift is the one-sided reset that buys the commit turn; Loran of the Third Path answers an artifact or enchantment on entry. Torrential Gearhulk and Metallurgic Summonings turn the instants this deck spends anyway into a body — the draw-go turns are already happening, and these are what makes them produce something. The two planeswalkers here are the sweeper hedge: neither dies to your own Supreme Verdict, and both keep ticking behind an empty board. Note what this stage is NOT: it holds no attacker anyone fears. The deck's damage comes from the conversion stage's upkeep triggers, not from combat, so removal in here is bought to protect a clock rather than to clear a path.

6.6Protection

Eight counterspells, three lock or tax pieces, one fog for the whole turn, and a flash grant. The counter suite is the priced component: 45.6% of openers, held in 78.1% of games, 67.2% by turn six, mean turn 2.764 — the second-most reliable thing this deck has after ignition, and the reason a draw-go posture is affordable at all. It guards two different things. Against the table it guards the engine: a deck whose damage arrives on other players' upkeeps has to survive to those upkeeps, and Propaganda plus The Second Doctor's rider mean the survival is bought with taxes rather than blockers. On the commit turn it guards the win, and stack 006 is explicit that it has to: every loop cast and every Reservoir activation opens a fully open priority window, Hullbreaker's trigger provides no protection there, and Grand Abolisher is the in-99 card that closes exactly those windows. Leyline of Anticipation is here rather than in mana because what flash buys this deck is the ability to deploy without ever tapping out on its own turn. One taxonomy note: deck-audit's protection axis reads 1 copy, because card_roles tags neither Grand Abolisher nor any counterspell as protection. A role is a property, not a job in this 99.

6.7Win condition

Only three cards in the 99 end a game by themselves. Approach of the Second Sun wins during the resolution of its second cast, and stack 002 walks exactly when: the first casting counts even though the card was shuffled back, and the only window to stop it is while the spell is on the stack. Aetherflux Reservoir is the payoff the verified loop keys on — stack 006 walks a three-opponent kill in one main phase from 32 life, and 15 casts is the proven minimum. Jace, Wielder of Mysteries is the third and it is not a rider: this deck can deck ITSELF. Font of Mythos gives every player two extra cards each draw step, three more permanents give one, the two doublers double every draw after the first, and Braingeyser, Stroke of Genius and Mathemagics read 'target player' — you are a legal target. Self-decking is the archetype's characteristic loss; Jace converts it into a win. Life is what this stage eats, and it is why there is no `fodder` stage. The Reservoir manufactures life on the loop's own casts and spends it in the same main phase (stack 006: +120 across 15 casts, -150 across three activations, ending at 2), and the deck pays life for mana besides (Ancient Tomb, Mana Confluence, Talisman of Progress) and to flip Heliod. Nothing in the 99 converts a resource into fodder — no sacrifice outlet, no discard outlet — so life is conversion output and wincon input, and a fodder box would take the Reservoir out of the stage where the game ends. The declared 'Win condition access' component is 5 cards at 31.2% of openers and 61.2% of games (50.5% by turn six, 68.0% assisted, mean turn 3.083) — but it mixes finishers with two tutors, so it prices a finisher OR something that finds one. The deck's median win is in conversion, which nothing measures as a win.
