The Plan

What this deck is trying to do, and what it is assuming away. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

The table has you filed as the nice one. You handed out cards, you never attacked, you said "sure, take one" three times. Then Iron Maiden invoices them for every card you gave away, on their own upkeep, before they get to spend it.

That is the deck, and you should play it in that order. Ten cards make the whole table draw. Six cards charge for the resulting hand. Both halves are on the battlefield in 56.7% of games and 41.2% of games by turn six — so more than half your games are the clock game, and you play toward the clock. Practical version: when you are holding a punisher and a donation engine, land the punisher first or on the same turn. Never after. A Howling Mine that goes down naked is you paying three opponents rent.

Close with Approach of the Second Sun. You reach a win condition in 61.2% of games and 50.5% by turn six, which is the single most reliable ending this deck has — cast it once, let it slot seventh from the top, gain the 7, and set the redraw with Mystical Tutor when you can. Stack 002 walks the second cast and finds it survivable behind held countermagic. So keep one blue up for that turn instead of spending it on the scary-looking spell that isn't going to kill you.

And then the famous one. Hullbreaker Horror plus a cheap rock plus Aetherflux Reservoir is a verified three-opponent kill — stack 006 does the whole ledger — and you will assemble it in 0.7% of games, 2.4% if a tutor helps, 0.3% by turn six. Read that as a gift, not a plan. Do not hold a hand together waiting for it, do not mulligan toward it, and do not tap out hunting the fourth piece. When all four show up on the same turn with Grand Abolisher available, take it. Every other game, you are the punisher deck, and the punisher deck wins by being boring on schedule.

Keep or Ship

What a keepable hand looks like, and how often you get one. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

You keep 73.8% of opening sevens and average 0.329 mulligans, so the free first ship is cheap and you should use it without agonising. Keep two to five lands. Below two you are not casting anything on time; above five you are drawing lands in a deck that already hands you extras.

The keep you want is two or three lands, a mana rock, and one card that makes the table draw. Howling Mine, Font of Mythos, Kami of the Crescent Moon, Temple Bell, Kwain — any of them. That hand digs toward a punisher, and digging is the point: your turn-three land drop hits 86.6% of the time, so a two-land opener with a rock is a real hand, not a prayer.

Ship the mirror image. Iron Maiden, Ebony Owl Netsuke and Viseling with no donation engine is three blanks and a land — nobody's hand gets to seven cards on its own, and you will sit there holding a clock with nothing to wind it. One punisher plus one engine plus two lands is a snap keep. Two punishers and no engine is a mulligan even when the mana is perfect.

Mulligan to the class, not the card. There is no named card you are allowed to look for here — ten donation effects and six punishers exist so that you never have to. That goes double for the storm kill: it turns up in 0.7% of games at a mean turn of 6.809, so a hand is never a keep because it has Aetherflux Reservoir in it.

One more keep test: your commander lands on mean turn 4.133 and shows up by turn six in 90.2% of games, so you do not need him in the opener. A hand with white sources and no blue is the one to look hardest at — the deck's blue demand starts on turn one and Archmage's Charm wants three of them.

Hullbreaker Horror + Sol Ring

is the claimed infinite real?

The clause that decides this is "choose up to one." A modal triggered ability has its mode and its targets locked in when it is put on the stack, not when it resolves (CR 603.3c: "If a triggered ability is modal, its controller announces the mode choice when putting the ability on the stack"). At that moment the artifact whose casting caused the trigger is still a spell, and CR 110.1 is precise about the difference: "A permanent is a card or token on the battlefield." A spell on the stack is neither.

So the trigger cannot name the card that made it. Either no mode is chosen and the ability leaves the stack (CR 603.3c), or the mode bounces some other permanent, which does nothing repeatable.

The holding, plainly: the two-card line the combo database lists — infinite colourless mana, infinite storm count — does not exist. The pair produces one Sol Ring activation. Whatever else drives this deck's bracket floor, this line does not.

Approach of the Second Sun, second cast off Mystical Tutor

The operative words are "you've cast another spell named Approach of the Second Sun this game." The condition tracks a casting event in the game's history. It does not track the card, which matters, because CR 400.7 says an object that changes zones "becomes a new object with no memory of, or relation to, its previous existence" — and the card did change zones when it went seventh from the top. The event survives what the object forgets.

The check happens during resolution, as an instruction the spell's controller follows in written order (CR 608.2c). It is not a state-based action, and CR 117.2e forecloses the remaining question: "no player has priority while a spell or ability is resolving."

That fixes the window exactly. Interaction lives while Approach is on the stack and nowhere after it. A counterspell there ends the matter; Swan Song answers a counterspell but, per CR 113.9, answers no ability. Once resolution begins, the game ends under CR 104.1 — "immediately when a player wins" — and the opposing life totals are never consulted.

Forced Fruition feeds Iron Maiden and Ebony Owl Netsuke

Rule 113.7a is the whole case: "Once activated or triggered, an ability exists on the stack independently of its source. Destruction or removal of the source after that time won't affect the ability." Forced Fruition's trigger goes on the stack above the spell that triggered it, so it resolves first (CR 608.1), and killing the enchantment afterwards does not claw the seven cards back.

Two smaller clauses do the arithmetic. Ebony Owl Netsuke carries an intervening "if" — seven or more cards — which CR 608.2a checks at both trigger and resolution. Iron Maiden carries none, and computes hand size minus 4 on resolution. Both belong to you while an opponent is the active player, so CR 101.4 puts the ordering in your hands, and here the order changes nothing.

Holding: 4 from the Netsuke and 3 from the Maiden against a seven-card hand, 7 damage in one upkeep, 18 down to 11. The cleanup trim at CR 514.1 still applies on this board — the record contains no walkthrough of what happens when a "no maximum hand size" effect deletes it.
