The Plan

What this deck is trying to do, and what it is assuming away. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

They count to nine in your command zone, see five colours, and file you under ramp. Let them; it is the only free thing you get.

Eminence pays from the command zone on turn one: every other Dragon spell costs {1} less, with nothing to cast and nothing an opponent can remove. Four printed reducers stack on it — Urza's Incubator, Dragonspeaker Shaman, Herald's Horn, Dragonlord's Servant. One is in hand by turn six 41.9% of the time; ramp or fixing 71.2%.

Then you charge the table for every Dragon that enters. Dragon Tempest, Terror of the Peaks and Scourge of Valkas are the whole damage leg — three cards — and each pays only for arrivals after it. So the order is payoff, then widener, then Dragon. Never the other way round.

What that board does is proven twice. With Miirym, Panharmonicon, Terror of the Peaks and Dragon Tempest down, one Lathliss cast is 64 damage in twelve separately aimed instances, and every instance reads any target: pointed at one player the running total is 6, 12, 16, 20, 26, 32, 37, 42, so a seat at 40 dies (stack 002, settled by stack 007). Ancient Gold Dragon connecting is the other shape — fifteen entries on one instruction, 255 available (stack 001). Neither board kills two seats.

The commander is the name of the deck and not the plan. Mean cast turn 8.172, cast by turn six in 4.1%, never inside ten turns in 7,170 of 10,000 goldfish games. Cast the discounted Dragon instead.

What has to be true is mana. 31 lands, land drops 0.834 on turn three, 0.670 on four, 0.525 on five, against 26 maindeck spells at mana value five or more. In 100 seeded Forge games against your pod you won 12 — 0.12, ci95 [0.070, 0.198] — and made 5.48 land drops a game (ci95 [5.15, 5.81]); what separated the twelve wins from the 88 losses was land drops, 7.25 against 5.24, difference 2.01, ci95 [0.52, 3.50]. Every seat in that run is Forge's AI, including yours.

Keep or Ship

What a keepable hand looks like, and how often you get one. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

Keep two to five lands and one accelerant. Two lands and a seven-drop is not a keep, however it looks. You can afford to ship: the simulator keeps 70.3% of first sevens at 0.379 mean mulligans.

The accelerant that decides games is a cost reducer. Urza's Incubator, Dragonspeaker Shaman, Herald's Horn or Dragonlord's Servant in an opener — stop reading the hand and keep it. A reducer is in hand by turn six 41.9% of the time, ramp or fixing 71.2%, and both together — the hand that casts a turn-five Dragon nobody expected — 28.5%.

Mox Jasper is not acceleration in an opening hand. It is blank until a Dragon is on the battlefield.

Want a payoff too: Dragon Tempest, Terror of the Peaks, Scourge of Valkas, 61.7% by turn six.

Never mulligan toward the commander. Eminence works from the command zone, so a hand that can eventually pay nine is worth no more than one that cannot — 4.1% by turn six.

Ship two shapes on sight. Lands plus top end with nothing to do on turn three: land drops fall to 0.670 on four and 0.525 on five, so the bridge does not arrive later. And three taplands — 14 of the 31 lands enter tapped, five of them always, and the turn you spend tapped out is the turn the reducer was meant to be down.

Count black before you keep for it. Eight black land sources, 0.217 on curve from lands alone, and exactly three cards want black: the commander, Crux of Fate at {3}{B}{B}, and Dragonlord Kolaghan. A hand whose removal plan is Crux of Fate is a hand with no removal.

Protection is a keep bonus, not a keep. Teferi's Protection, Heroic Intervention, Eerie Interlude — one of the suite is there 65.5% by turn six, and it is what lets you commit the board later.

Dragon Tempest + Ancient Gold Dragon

the d20 damage burst

The word that decides this is nontoken. Fifteen Faerie Dragons arrive on one instruction and not one of them qualifies, so Miirym copies nothing at all. Dragon Tempest carries no such reservation: one event with fifteen occurrences is fifteen separate triggers (CR 603.2c), each choosing its target as it goes on the stack (CR 115.1d) and each counting your Dragons again at its own resolution (CR 608.2h). Seventeen damage, fifteen times, with priority passing between every instalment.

What it proves: 255 available at a single target, and the 30-life seat dead on the second trigger aimed there. What it does not prove is that you arrive here. The board is granted a connected Ancient Gold Dragon and the d20 is fixed at 15. Arithmetic on the printed card puts the real threshold lower — with N tokens each trigger deals N+2 and there are N of them, so three seats at 40 needs twelve tokens, which is a roll of 12 or better — but that is arithmetic, not a checked line. Aim carefully: a trigger still pointed at a player who has already lost is removed on resolution (CR 608.2b).

how many copies?
