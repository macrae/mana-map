The Plan

What this deck is trying to do, and what it is assuming away. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

The table sees Edgar Markov and shields up for combat. Let them. This deck kills with triggers, and the ignition never has to be drawn: every Vampire spell you cast mints a token straight from the command zone. No setup card to protect, no key turn to telegraph. You cast cheap Vampires — eleven of them cost two or less — and the machine runs itself.

The kill is conversion. Five cards turn every arriving body into table-wide damage: Purphoros, God of the Forge, Impact Tremors, Warleader's Call, Corpse Knight and Witty Roastmaster. Stack 009 is the whole plan in one exchange — one two-mana Vampire, three payoffs and a doubler on board, twelve damage to every opponent, table dead. You draw a converter by turn 6 in 62.6% of games. So your early turns have one job: land a converter and a multiplier, then keep casting Vampires into them.

The wipe is coming — plan to get paid for it. Blood Artist and Cruel Celebrant drain as your board dies, and eminence rebuilds from a zone no sweeper touches. So commit in waves: deploy the cheap bodies, hold one real threat back, and keep a protection spell — you run five that are free or flash — for the turn you go all-in, not the turn after.

Combat is your second kill: the lords, Shared Animosity and Sanctum Seeker turn the same width into lethal attacks. The infinites are found money, not the plan — Bloodthirsty Conqueror with Vito or Sanguine Bond ends the game on the spot (stacks 004 and 006 prove it), but it assembles unassisted in 4.8% of games. Don't chase it. Tutors move the main kill from 41.7% to 57.7% by turn 6; spend them on the missing converter or doubler, and take the loop only when it's dealt to you.

What this does not model

  • Unsettled Stack 011 settled the round-1 form of this: Mirkwood Bats triggers once per TOKEN, so N tokens created in one event is N triggers and N life per opponent, tracking the tokens and never the doublers. What 011 deliberately left counterfactual is the deck's own board — it holds ONE doubler and two opponents, and 010's eight-token board carries no Bats and ends with both opponents at 40. What does the real go-wide board put on the table: Bats plus two or three doublers into three untouched seats, and does Bloodletter of Aclazotz double it?
  • Unsettled Does Bloodletter of Aclazotz double DAMAGE, not only stated life loss? Its reminder text reads '(Damage causes loss of life.)' and stack 004's checker treated the identical parenthetical on Bloodthirsty Conqueror as reminder text under 207.2a — but stacks 003 and 007 only ever tested 'each opponent loses N life' from Sanctum Seeker and Throne. Nobody has run the other direction: does Purphoros dealing 2 to each opponent become 4?
  • Unsettled How many two-mana Vampire casts does the payoff suite need to take one seat from 40 to 0 with only ONE doubler on the battlefield? Stack 009 is exactly lethal at 12 life per seat and stack 008 measures 10 damage per cast with two doublers.
  • Simulation Simulates resource development, not full games (no interaction, no removal).

Keep or Ship

What a keepable hand looks like, and how often you get one. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

You keep 80.8% of opening sevens, so mulligan decisions here are about spotting the bad fifth, not agonizing over every hand. The frame is simple: two to four lands, at least one black source, and something to cast on turns one and two. Eminence means your engine is already in hand every game — what you're looking for is fuel.

KEEP: two-plus lands with a one- or two-drop Vampire (Indulgent Aristocrat, Vampire Cutthroat, Voldaren Epicure, Knight of the Ebon Legion, Cordial Vampire, Vampire of the Dire Moon) and any converter or draw piece. A hand with Impact Tremors or Skullclamp and cheap bodies is a snap keep — that's the 62.6% turn-6 converter figure showing up early, and it's the whole game plan in seven cards.

SHIP: any hand with no black source. Black carries 39 of the deck's 76 coloured pips and the drain payoffs live there — a hand of Plains and red duals casts your worst cards and none of your best. Ship five-land hands too: your curve tops at six with a modal cost of three, and the deck refills through Skullclamp, Champion of Dusk and Pact of the Serpent, not through extra land drops.

The close calls: a slow hand with a doubler (Anointed Procession, Mondrak) and lands is keepable — the multiplier is the half of the kill you draw less urgently, and eminence provides the bodies. A hand of all payoffs and no cheap Vampires is the trap: converters convert entries, and with nothing to cast, nothing enters. When in doubt, keep the hand that casts a Vampire on turn one or two. The land drop holds at 91.9% through turn 3 — trust it.

Edgar's eminence through two doublers

how many bodies per cast?

The clause that decides this is CR 616.1: when two replacement effects both want the same token-creation event, the affected player — you — chooses which applies first, and CR 614.5 grants each exactly one application. The choice is therefore yours and immaterial: one token becomes two becomes four in either order. The record then closes two doors. The tokens are created, not cast, so eminence does not trigger again; and Vampire Socialite's entry clause reads each of them onto the battlefield with a counter. The holding, in plain English: one two-mana Vampire spell yields five bodies, and the loop the reader might fear — tokens re-triggering eminence — does not exist.

Bloodletter + Sanctum Seeker

what does an attack actually drain?

Two words decide this case: 'during your turn.' Bloodletter of Aclazotz's replacement applies to each opponent's life loss, once per loss event — and the trigger count is fixed at declaration under CR 508.3a: seven attacking Vampires is seven Sanctum Seeker triggers, each its own event, each doubled separately to 2 per opponent. Fourteen total. Your gain is not doubled; the text reaches only opponents' loss. Note what Edgar's counter trigger does not do: Sanctum Seeker drains a fixed 1, so the counters change the combat to come, never the drain. The holding: the 14-life opponent reaches exactly 0 on the seventh trigger and is removed by state-based actions — before a single blocker is declared.

Bloodthirsty Conqueror + Vito

the second infinite, verified on its own terms

The controlling fact is CR 603.3d: each new Vito trigger is a fresh instance, and a fresh instance chooses its own target. That is what lets the loop walk the table one opponent at a time. The record runs thirteen Vito resolutions: nine points end opponent 2 exactly, thirty end opponent 1 exactly, and the killing loss still triggers Bloodthirsty Conqueror — the chain crosses a death without breaking, and the win arrives via CR 104.2a. One honest cost is on the record: Conqueror is a creature, and every iteration passes priority, so instant-speed creature removal is a loop-breaker at any point. Where an opponent holds such an answer, the record is silent and so am I; here none was held, and the game ends with you at 60.
