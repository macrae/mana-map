1General

What the machine is and what it is for.

Edgar mints a free Vampire on every Vampire cast and three replacement doublers turn one mint into eight - but v1.0.1 stopped asking those bodies to connect and started asking them to DIE: nine cards convert them into cards, counters and life loss at every seat, and the two checker-passed kill routes are a doubled attack drain and a two-card lifegain loop, neither of which needs combat damage to land.

5.5Time to kill

What this model does not do (26 stated assumptions)
  • Simulates resource development, not full games (no interaction, no removal).
  • Draw every turn including turn 1 (multiplayer Commander).
  • Mulligan: keep 7-card hands with 2-5 lands; up to 2 fresh redraws, keep the last.
  • One land drop per turn when available.
  • LANDS ENTER UNTAPPED, ALWAYS, and the one in hand longest is the one played. A tapland costs nothing here and no land is ever chosen over another, so this model CANNOT rank two lands that make the same colours — `mana-analysis` and `mana-fit` are what answer a land question, and they are deterministic for exactly this reason.
  • Mana rocks ('{T}: Add') contribute from the turn after they are cast.
  • Commander cast on first affordable turn (highest spending priority).
  • Bodies count = creatures cast + tokens parsed from 'create ... token' text.
  • Target assembly counts cards DRAWN by a turn (cast cards still count).
  • CARD DRAW IS ONE A TURN UNLESS `model_draw` IS SET. With the flag on, four channels are modelled — a card's own ETB draw, an instant or sorcery that draws, an upkeep trigger, and an ARRIVAL trigger that draws when other creatures or tokens enter (Welcoming Vampire, Caretaker's Talent), which rides the same single door the ETB damage payoffs use. Activated draw (needs a spending policy this model has no opinion on), X-based draw (board-dependent), and death- or attack-triggered draw (no deaths, and attacks only under `model_combat`) are NOT modelled and the cards are named in `meta.card_advantage.draw_not_modelled`. A conditional trigger — 'if you control an artifact', 'you may draw' — is treated as unmodelled rather than assumed to be on: 39 of the 348 ETB-draw cards in the corpus carry one.
  • AN ARRIVAL-DRAW ENGINE NEVER SEES ITS OWN ARRIVAL. Welcoming Vampire is a 2/3 that draws 'whenever one or more OTHER creatures you control with power 2 or less enter'; registered before its own entry it passed its own gate and drew a card it does not draw, overstating edgar-vampires by 22% at turn eight. The deferral applies to every card and not only the ones worded 'other', which understates Tocasia's Welcome and two relatives by one draw on the turn they land.
  • ARRIVAL DRAW IS SMALLER WITHOUT `model_combat`, and legitimately so. The channel rides the one door onto the battlefield, which `model_combat` also uses to spawn token copies and ETB-payoff tokens — those are real additional arrivals that do not exist in the resource-only model. Measured on edgar-vampires: 1.264 extra cards by turn ten with both flags against 1.106 with draw alone. Before 2026-08-28 the gap was 1.264 against 0.323, because every call to that door sat inside `if model_combat:` and a draw-only deck silently lost three quarters of its arrival draws.
  • HELD-UP INTERACTION is reported as two series and both are FLOORS. This model casts everything it can afford every turn, so `interaction_castable` is what a pilot developing at full speed has left over, not what a pilot choosing to hold up two mana would have. A low figure is therefore a real finding and a high one is unambiguous good news.
  • Tutors are modeled as wildcards: a CAST tutor that fetches to hand or the top — including a TYPED one (Worldly Tutor, Sarkhan's Triumph), which the old literal 'a card' match missed entirely — fills ONE missing any_of group. Consumed once, mana paid, and a tutor that puts the card on top of the library costs a turn. An ACTIVATED, ETB or death-triggered tutor does not count, because this model only knows 'drawn and affordable'; nor does a land fetch, which is ramp and has no channel here yet. Reported as the *_assisted figures; the unassisted figures beside them exclude tutors entirely.
  • ENTERS-THE-BATTLEFIELD PAYOFFS are modelled: damage equal to the entering creature's power (Terror of the Peaks), X damage where X counts your board (Scourge of Valkas, Dragon Tempest), a token (Lathliss) and a token COPY of what entered (Miirym). Every arrival fires them — cast, token or copy — and a copy is itself an arrival, so they compound, which is the deck working rather than a bug. A payoff worded 'another NONTOKEN' does not re-trigger on its own tokens, which is the brake the rules already had. A chain is capped at 12 deep. X counts the whole board rather than one subtype: exact where the creatures are Dragons, generous otherwise.
  • The COMMANDER joins the battlefield when cast, attacks, and fires its own triggers. It used to be a mana sink and a flag — cast, then dropped — so a 10/10 flier contributed no power and never swung. Commander tax, death and recasting are still not modelled: it is cast once and it stays.
  • STATIC cost reduction IS modeled: a commander's eminence from the command zone (live from turn one, unremovable) and typed reducers once they are on the battlefield. It pays GENERIC only and is floored at the coloured pip count, because a discount can never pay a pip. A reduction that SCALES with a board state (Animar, Rakdos, Hamza) is refused rather than counted flat, and cost reduction on artifacts, noncreature spells or a colour is not modeled at all.
  • Rituals are not modeled (conservative).
  • Extra card draw is NOT modeled: one card per turn, always. That understates every deck, but by an amount proportional to how much draw it runs — so it is conservative WITHIN a deck and not neutral BETWEEN them. `meta.card_advantage` reports how much of each list this hides. Card advantage is measured nowhere in this suite.
  • COMBAT: one opponent at 40 life who does nothing — no blockers, no removal, no interaction. This is a goldfish in the literal sense, so `kill_turn` is the turn an UNOPPOSED board would finish ONE seat, not a win rate.
  • Attacks with every creature that is not summoning-sick; haste is read from the ORACLE TEXT (this line said 'type line' and was simply wrong). There is nothing to block, so nothing is ever held back — in a real four-player game you would keep blockers, which makes this an optimistic clock and a pessimistic board.
  • Attack triggers, combat-damage triggers and additional combat phases are modelled, which is what makes Treasure sources gated on combat produce here when they produce nothing without this flag. Effects the parser cannot read are named in meta.combat_effects_not_modelled.
  • DAMAGE MULTIPLICATION is modelled and stacks MULTIPLICATIVELY as the rules do: doubling the power, swinging twice and doubling the damage dealt is eight times, not four. Three wordings are read — a replacement effect on damage you deal (Twinflame Tyrant), a granted double strike (Atarka) and a power doubling (Thrakkus). A grant worded 'each Dragon you control' is treated as applying to the WHOLE team: exact in a deck whose attackers are Dragons, generous in one where they are not. A creature's own double strike multiplies only itself. Board power is unaffected — a double-striker is not a bigger creature.
  • Bodies count CREATURES only under this flag. Without it a Treasure token scores as a creature, which inflates `mean_bodies_by_turn` for any deck that makes non-creature tokens.
  • LIFE LOSS ON ARRIVAL COUNTS AS DAMAGE. 'Each opponent loses 1 life' (Corpse Knight) and 'deals 1 damage to each opponent' (Impact Tremors) are the same event, the same cadence and the same number against one opponent at 40 life, and only the second was priced until 2026-08-28. They are NOT the same in the rules — lifelink, damage prevention and 'whenever an opponent loses life' all separate them — and this model has none of those. Nine corpus cards read through this channel, plus Mirkwood Bats through a token-creation channel of its own.
  • DEATH-TRIGGERED DRAIN IS NOT MODELLED AND IS THE LARGEST KNOWN GAP HERE. Blood Artist, Cruel Celebrant, Zulaport Cutthroat, Bastion of Remembrance and Elas il-Kor all key on a creature DYING, and nothing dies in this model: no blockers, no removal, no sacrifice outlets. Their contribution is ABSENT, not zero, and a deck whose kill runs through them is understated here by however much that line is worth. `simulate` against a real pod is where that number lives.

6.1Mana

Thirty-two lands plus five rocks, and one body that turns into mana. The declared ramp component (Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Talisman of Indulgence, Herald's Horn, Patchwork Banner) is drawn in 67.0% of games, mean turn 3.54, and deck-audit still reads the axis UNDER at 8 copies against a 10-12 target while colour-sources sits 4 short in the worst colour. That shortfall has one address: Bloodletter of Aclazotz is the ONLY cost in the 99 with three or more identical coloured pips ({1}{B}{B}{B}) - enumerated over every mana cost in cards.json - and it is half the deck's fastest verified kill. Master of Dark Rites files here rather than with the sacrifice outlets because its output is mana, but deck-facts flags the catch: its {B}{B}{B} is spend-only-on-Vampire-Cleric-Demon SPELLS, and an activated ability is not a spell, so it accelerates toward a commander without paying for what the commander then does. Bloodletter is a Vampire Demon, so it qualifies twice over. One land is also an engine piece: Midgar, City of Mako's Adventure half, Reactor Raid, sacrifices a creature to draw two.

6.2Ignition

Nothing in this deck happens until a Vampire spell is CAST. Edgar's eminence fires from the command zone - checker-passed twice, in stacks 002 and 010, which read the intervening-'if' again on resolution and confirm the ability functions from the zone it names - so the ignition half the pilot owns is free and undrawable in 100% of games. The half that must be found is the spell: a castable Vampire is drawn in 95.1% of games at mean turn 2.19, the healthiest figure in the brief, and it is the leg every checker-passed kill proof opens on. The six cards beside Edgar are the members whose contribution is dominated by being that spell - four are printed keywords and nothing else, Knight of the Ebon Legion adds a pump activation and Voldaren Epicure an entry ping plus a Blood token - so they can be cast alongside the plan rather than instead of it. Nighthawk Scavenger is the same card at {1}{B}{B} and sits above the declaration's mana-value-2 line. Voldaren Epicure adds one damage to each opponent and a Blood token on the way past. The point of the stage is not that these bodies are good; it is that casting one of them is what turns Edgar on, and everything downstream is priced off that 95.1%.

6.3Fodder

The thing this engine converts is 1/1 Vampire tokens, and the deck holds no card that IS one - so the stage's members are the cards that decide the count. Three replacement doublers stack multiplicatively on the single eminence event: stack 002 records Anointed Procession + Mondrak taking one token to four (1->2->4), and stack 010 records all three taking it to eight (1->2->4->8), order-independent because each doubler gets exactly one opportunity at the event and any modified event replacing it. The declared multiplier component is drawn in 67.3% of games, mean turn 3.61. Five of these seven also make bodies without Edgar - Bloodline Keeper taps for a 2/2 flier, Mavren Fein mints on every nontoken Vampire attack, Charismatic Conqueror taxes opponents' untapped entries, Elspeth's +1 makes a Soldier, and Legion's Landing arrives as a token and flips into a land that makes more. Read the 400-game Forge result before pricing this stage as a win: on a go-wide refactor, combat damage to players fell 29.07 to 18.20 because 1/1 tokens do not connect. Eight tokens is not eight damage. It is eight things to convert.

6.4Fuel

Four logged games say the same thing: the deck ran out of steam on card draw. This stage is the answer bloodline bought. Ten of the eleven are draw; the declared component is nine cards and reads 84.1% assembled, mean turn 2.68, second only to the castable-Vampire leg among the eleven declared components - and yet deck-audit still reads card-advantage UNDER at 7 copies against a 10 target, because the taxonomy cannot see Bloodtracker, Champion of Dusk, Preacher of the Schism, Castle Locthwain, Minas Tirith or Midgar's Adventure half, among others it names. Five of these turn a body LEAVING THE BATTLEFIELD into cards rather than reading off casts - Crossway Troublemakers (2 life per Vampire death), Liliana (every creature death), Skullclamp (two cards per equipped body), Bloodtracker (a card per counter when it leaves), and High-Society Hunter, whose clause is 'another NONTOKEN creature' and therefore never sees an eminence token. That is the shape the pilot's 2026-09-01 game demonstrated: an opponent's Goblin Sharpshooter pinging the token board fed the draw rather than emptying it. Vampiric Tutor is the eleventh, and it is not card advantage - it is the reason a 4.2% component reads 9.6% assisted and a 7.6% one reads 14.6%.

6.5Conversion

This is the component bloodline actually added, and until 2026-09-01 nothing in the declaration described it - the goldfish's sacrifice cap only started firing (0.0 -> 0.003) once the target existed. Seven of these are free outlets: no mana, no tap, sacrifice a creature and get something. Drawn in 77.6% of games, mean turn 3.04. They are the machine that turns a stage of disposable bodies into cards (Skullclamp, Crossway Troublemakers), drain (Blood Artist, Cruel Celebrant), mana (Ashnod's Altar), and counters. Cordial Vampire files here rather than with the lords because it is the piece that answers the 400-game finding: every death puts a +1/+1 counter on EACH Vampire, which is how a board of 1/1s that cannot connect becomes a board of 2/2s and 3/3s that can. Stack 002 is explicit that it never triggers on a board where nothing dies - the mint alone is not the engine, the mint plus an outlet is. One warning that is about the bench and not the deck: Forge's evaluator will not pay a cost it cannot price, and Ashnod's Altar activated 0 times in 59 castings under both AI profiles - a bench measurement on a sacrifice deck, not one taken on this list. A Forge figure on this stage is a floor, not a measurement.

6.6Output

What the converted bodies actually buy. Two death drains bill on every creature that dies - Blood Artist one target player, Cruel Celebrant EACH opponent, a difference of three-to-one in a pod and the reason they are not interchangeable; the pair is drawn in 37.2% of games and is a two-card component, the thinnest declared here apart from the two one-card kill pieces. Two lifegain converters turn the deck's heavy lifelink into life loss: Vito and Sanguine Bond share the same trigger word for word. Five lords and counter-payoffs raise what a body is worth in combat - a lord or anthem is drawn in 80.2% of games, mean turn 3.14 - and five removal spells answer what the drain cannot. deck-audit reads interaction OVER at 12 copies against a 5-6 target and interaction-breadth UNDER at 4 classes, with land the class it detects no answer for; that is an oracle-text heuristic and a floor, not a verdict. The stage's job is that none of it needs combat damage to land, which is exactly what the 400-game run said this deck could not rely on.

6.7Protection

Held, not cast. Deflecting Swat and Flawless Maneuver cost nothing while a commander is on the board or in the zone, and Edgar is in one of those places in every game; Boros Charm is two mana for indestructible on EVERY permanent you control and Teferi's Protection phases the whole board out - reading all five, those are the two that reach the enchantment doublers, since Flawless Maneuver and Akroma's Will name creatures only. Teferi's is also the answer to a sweeper this deck has no other reply to: deck-audit reads sweepers at 0 copies. Akroma's Will is the exception: with a commander it is both modes, which is a kill spell as often as it is insurance. The component is drawn in 68.3% of games, mean turn 3.55, and deck-audit reads protection OVER at 8 copies against a 5-7 target, counting self-protecting bodies like Mondrak and Yahenni that are filed in other stages here.

6.8Win condition

Route one is combat-adjacent and does not need damage to land. Stack 003: seven attacking Vampires give seven Sanctum Seeker triggers; Bloodletter doubles each opponent's loss independently because it is your turn, so each seat loses 14, and the opponent at 14 is eliminated by state-based actions BEFORE blockers are declared. Your own lifegain is not doubled - Bloodletter replaces only opponents' loss. Route two is the infinite: Bloodthirsty Conqueror with Vito (stack 004) or Sanguine Bond (stack 006), both checker-passed as deterministic, shortcut-legal, zero-additional-mana table kills. The honest part is the price. Both routes are single points of failure that deck-audit has already priced: Bloodletter is 7.1% of opening sevens and 4.2% assembled (9.6% with the tutor), Conqueror 7.1% and 7.6% assembled (14.6% assisted). The Haunt of Hightower sits here as the combat close - a 4/4 flying lifelink that grows on every card entering an opponent's graveyard - and it is a six-mana body, not a plan. Two one-card lines and a fatty is a ceiling, and stating it is not the same as calling it a defect.
