1General

What the machine is and what it is for.

Hapatra does not make -1/-1 counters outside combat — she PRICES them: every placement another of your cards makes onto a creature mints one 1/1 deathtouch Snake (one per creature per placement, not one per counter), twelve cards eat those Snakes for mana, cards and damage, and five drain pieces bill for each death — though only three charge the whole table (Blood Artist and Sephiroth name one seat) and Obelisk Spider charges on counter PLACEMENT rather than on a death at all; the counter sources are eight cards deep, every unbounded line a CHECKER HAS UPHELD routes through Mikaeus — 11 of bracket_report's 19 two-card infinites do not name him and stack 001 explicitly declines to close nine of those — and exactly one board in this deck has ever been checked, a board on which nothing came back.

What this model does not do (19 stated assumptions)
  • Simulates resource development, not full games (no interaction, no removal).
  • Draw every turn including turn 1 (multiplayer Commander).
  • Mulligan: keep 7-card hands with 2-5 lands; up to 2 fresh redraws, keep the last.
  • One land drop per turn when available.
  • LANDS ENTER UNTAPPED, ALWAYS, and the one in hand longest is the one played. A tapland costs nothing here and no land is ever chosen over another, so this model CANNOT rank two lands that make the same colours — `mana-analysis` and `mana-fit` are what answer a land question, and they are deterministic for exactly this reason.
  • Mana rocks ('{T}: Add') contribute from the turn after they are cast.
  • Commander cast on first affordable turn (highest spending priority).
  • Bodies count = creatures cast + tokens parsed from 'create ... token' text.
  • Target assembly counts cards DRAWN by a turn (cast cards still count).
  • CARD DRAW IS ONE A TURN UNLESS `model_draw` IS SET. With the flag on, four channels are modelled — a card's own ETB draw, an instant or sorcery that draws, an upkeep trigger, and an ARRIVAL trigger that draws when other creatures or tokens enter (Welcoming Vampire, Caretaker's Talent), which rides the same single door the ETB damage payoffs use. Activated draw (needs a spending policy this model has no opinion on), X-based draw (board-dependent), and death- or attack-triggered draw (no deaths, and attacks only under `model_combat`) are NOT modelled and the cards are named in `meta.card_advantage.draw_not_modelled`. A conditional trigger — 'if you control an artifact', 'you may draw' — is treated as unmodelled rather than assumed to be on: 39 of the 348 ETB-draw cards in the corpus carry one.
  • AN ARRIVAL-DRAW ENGINE NEVER SEES ITS OWN ARRIVAL. Welcoming Vampire is a 2/3 that draws 'whenever one or more OTHER creatures you control with power 2 or less enter'; registered before its own entry it passed its own gate and drew a card it does not draw, overstating edgar-vampires by 22% at turn eight. The deferral applies to every card and not only the ones worded 'other', which understates Tocasia's Welcome and two relatives by one draw on the turn they land.
  • ARRIVAL DRAW IS SMALLER WITHOUT `model_combat`, and legitimately so. The channel rides the one door onto the battlefield, which `model_combat` also uses to spawn token copies and ETB-payoff tokens — those are real additional arrivals that do not exist in the resource-only model. Measured on edgar-vampires: 1.264 extra cards by turn ten with both flags against 1.106 with draw alone. Before 2026-08-28 the gap was 1.264 against 0.323, because every call to that door sat inside `if model_combat:` and a draw-only deck silently lost three quarters of its arrival draws.
  • HELD-UP INTERACTION is reported as two series and both are FLOORS. This model casts everything it can afford every turn, so `interaction_castable` is what a pilot developing at full speed has left over, not what a pilot choosing to hold up two mana would have. A low figure is therefore a real finding and a high one is unambiguous good news.
  • Tutors are modeled as wildcards: a CAST tutor that fetches to hand or the top — including a TYPED one (Worldly Tutor, Sarkhan's Triumph), which the old literal 'a card' match missed entirely — fills ONE missing any_of group. Consumed once, mana paid, and a tutor that puts the card on top of the library costs a turn. An ACTIVATED, ETB or death-triggered tutor does not count, because this model only knows 'drawn and affordable'; nor does a land fetch, which is ramp and has no channel here yet. Reported as the *_assisted figures; the unassisted figures beside them exclude tutors entirely.
  • ENTERS-THE-BATTLEFIELD PAYOFFS are modelled: damage equal to the entering creature's power (Terror of the Peaks), X damage where X counts your board (Scourge of Valkas, Dragon Tempest), a token (Lathliss) and a token COPY of what entered (Miirym). Every arrival fires them — cast, token or copy — and a copy is itself an arrival, so they compound, which is the deck working rather than a bug. A payoff worded 'another NONTOKEN' does not re-trigger on its own tokens, which is the brake the rules already had. A chain is capped at 12 deep. X counts the whole board rather than one subtype: exact where the creatures are Dragons, generous otherwise.
  • The COMMANDER joins the battlefield when cast, attacks, and fires its own triggers. It used to be a mana sink and a flag — cast, then dropped — so a 10/10 flier contributed no power and never swung. Commander tax, death and recasting are still not modelled: it is cast once and it stays.
  • STATIC cost reduction IS modeled: a commander's eminence from the command zone (live from turn one, unremovable) and typed reducers once they are on the battlefield. It pays GENERIC only and is floored at the coloured pip count, because a discount can never pay a pip. A reduction that SCALES with a board state (Animar, Rakdos, Hamza) is refused rather than counted flat, and cost reduction on artifacts, noncreature spells or a colour is not modeled at all.
  • Rituals are not modeled (conservative).
  • Extra card draw is NOT modeled: one card per turn, always. That understates every deck, but by an amount proportional to how much draw it runs — so it is conservative WITHIN a deck and not neutral BETWEEN them. `meta.card_advantage` reports how much of each list this hides. Card advantage is measured nowhere in this suite.

6.1Mana

Thirty-three entries pay for a deck whose curve peaks at two (19 cards) and three (17). Read them in COPIES, not entries: the 22 land entries here are 34 land copies, because Forest and Swamp are seven each. Deck-wide, deck-audit measures 36 land copies across 24 entries (target 36-38, verdict 'at'), 50 mana sources against a Burgess floor of 35, and zero taplands. Hapatra costs {B}{G}. Goldfish casts her on turn 2 in 9,970 of 10,000 runs, mean 2.007, 99.9% by turn 6 — but she is a TWO-drop off two untapped lands, so the land base buys that figure and the accelerants buy something else: a turn-one Hapatra, or a turn-two Hapatra with a counter source in the same turn. Three dorks double as fodder the moment an outlet is down. Grim Backwoods and Lazotep Quarry are filed under conversion because they eat creatures; Svogthos stays here as a land that becomes a threat late. What this stage does NOT do is feed the headline loop. Stack 001 is explicit: Mikaeus + Devoted Druid yields three green on the first pass and ZERO on every pass after, because the returned Druid is a new object that has not been under your control since your turn began and 302.6 blocks its tap ability. The loop is infinite in deaths, counters, Snakes and drain — not in mana. Any plan that spends the loop's mana is spending mana that does not exist.

6.2Ignition

Hapatra has to resolve, and then something else has to place a -1/-1 counter. Her second ability is the whole engine — whenever YOU put one or more -1/-1 counters on a creature you get ONE 1/1 deathtouch Snake, per creature per placement rather than per counter, and never off a counter an opponent placed; Flourishing Defenses is the card here that really reads 'by anyone', and it makes Elf Warriors — but her own placement is a combat-damage trigger and nothing more, which stack 001 step 11 establishes while refuting the scenario's claim. She is an exchange rate, not a source, so the source class is the ignition and it is eight cards: Fume Spitter and Grim Affliction are one-shots, Serrated Arrows is three uses, Contagion Clasp proliferates for {4}, Black Sun's Zenith is the mass version that also turns on Necroskitter, and Devoted Druid puts one on itself for free, repeatably. This stage has NO single point of failure — it is eight sources deep, two of them free and repeatable. deck-audit does price Blowfly Infestation as one of the deck's two, at 7.1% of opening sevens and 9.1% by turn three, but that is a property of a declared ONE-CARD component, not of this stage. Read Blowfly's intervening 'if': it triggers on a creature dying that HAD a -1/-1 counter on it, so the pair cannot start itself without a connected attack or a third card. That is open question 1, and a card whose function the model formally doubts cannot also be the card the stage rests on. Host of the Hereafter moves the counters off a dying creature onto one you control and looks like a second copy of that shape; nothing has checked it (open question 2). Hapatra herself is the one piece removal does not answer — she comes back from the command zone.

6.3Fuel

Fourteen cards that find a piece or refill the hand: eight tutors, four draw pieces and two recursion spells. Search is what makes a two-deep enabler class playable at all. Goldfish has a tutor drawn in 78.0% of games, 67.6% by turn 6, mean turn 2.70, and tutor assistance is precisely what moves Mikaeus + Devoted Druid from 2.5% assembled to 31.7% (1.3% to 19.8% by turn 6). strategy:deckbuilding.redundancy-vs-tutors prices the alternative: '5 copies is 31% of openers, 7 is 41% (WitchPHD's 7-of, the singleton 4-of, 41.1%), 10 is 54%'. Stack 001's closing finding names the default fetch, and it is not an altar: it is a free repeatable -1/-1 source, and the population it counts over is the OUTLETS — 'exactly one of the deck's eight mana-free sacrifice outlets qualifies: Yawgmoth' (filed under conversion). Not one in the deck: Devoted Druid is a second free repeatable source and is not an outlet, which is why stages[1] can be eight sources deep with two of them free. Diabolic Intent eats a body and still sits here rather than in conversion, because its job is search and the declaration prices it inside the eight-card tutor class. deck-audit counts eight card-advantage copies against a floor of ten while naming three the taxonomy files elsewhere — Grim Backwoods, The One Ring and Yawgmoth all draw — so that axis is a floor, not a shortfall.

6.4Fodder

Five cards whose job is to put a disposable body on the table. Nest of Scarabs and Flourishing Defenses are counter payoffs — a body per placement, which is what makes one -1/-1 counter worth two or three creatures once Hapatra is down. Bitterblossom is a body per upkeep for one life and asks the engine for nothing. Scurry Oak and Ivy Lane Denizen are the odd pair: they trade in +1/+1 counters, not -1/-1, and their listed loop needs neither Hapatra nor a -1/-1 counter anywhere — which makes it the only line here that needs no -1/-1 COUNTER, not the only one that survives an answer to the commander: lines[11] (Mikaeus + Devoted Druid, the Druid its own fodder) and lines[12] (Mikaeus + Walking Ballista + a free outlet) run with Hapatra answered too. Nothing dies in it, though, so it needs a free outlet to become a kill (open question 3). Ivy Lane Denizen also carries six of bracket_report's nineteen two-card infinites on its own, paired with each free outlet in turn; on oracle text those look inert, since the Denizen triggers only when another GREEN creature ENTERS and an outlet makes nothing enter (open question 9). The declaration files Nest of Scarabs and Flourishing Defenses alongside Blood Artist, Zulaport Cutthroat, Bastion of Remembrance and Obelisk Spider under one label, 'Commander plus any -1/-1 payoff'. Two of those six make bodies and four drain life; they are at opposite ends of this engine and the group's size is doing double duty as a redundancy claim for both. Price it with the right instrument: deck-audit's hypergeometric puts the six-card component in 36.4% of opening sevens and 44.4% by turn three, while goldfish measures the same target assembled in 67.2% of games and 55.7% by turn six.

6.5Conversion

The engine proper: thirteen cards, twelve of which consume a body and hand back mana, a card, damage or a counter. The thirteenth is Pitiless Plunderer, and it consumes nothing — it makes a Treasure whenever another creature you control dies, so it is a death PAYOFF profiting from a body something else ate. Stack 001's checker re-derived the outlet census from every 'sacrific' card in cards.json and the number is eight that need no mana — Ashnod's Altar, Phyrexian Altar, Carrion Feeder, Viscera Seer, Yahenni, Yawgmoth, Blasting Station (it untaps itself whenever a creature enters) and Lazotep Quarry (once per turn, no untapper). Exactly ONE of the eight also places a -1/-1 counter. That asymmetry is the deck: outlets are abundant, sources are thin, and the tutor priority follows from it. Yawgmoth is therefore the real target — pay 1 life, sacrifice another creature, place a counter on up to one target creature, draw. The ability has a single instance of the word 'target', so an illegal target means it does not resolve at all and you do not even draw: a second body is mandatory. His A/B loop is real and library-bounded by his own mandatory draw, which is a strong engine rather than a true infinite. Carrion Feeder is actively anti-synergistic with Mikaeus, since it adds +1/+1 counters. Skullclamp is filed here rather than under draw because on a 1/1 Snake it is not equipment, it is an outlet that pays two cards.

6.6Output

Four cards, and the small number is the finding. Almost everything this engine emits is emitted by a card filed in another stage — a Snake from ignition, a card from conversion, a life from wincon — so only four cards exist purely to point outward. Orcish Bowmasters flashes in as a ping plus two bodies and taxes an opponent's draw. Crumbling Ashes destroys a creature with a -1/-1 counter on it every upkeep, which is the deck's counters aimed at the table rather than at its own Snakes. Necroskitter returns an opponent's creature that dies with a -1/-1 counter under your control, so the removal output comes back as fodder. Toxic Deluge is the one to read carefully: it gives all creatures -X/-X until end of turn and places NO counters, so it triggers Hapatra, Nest of Scarabs, Flourishing Defenses and Necroskitter exactly zero times. It is a reset button in a deck where every other sweeper is an engine turn, and the map files it under a city named THE COUNTER SUPPLY — the one thing it does not supply.

6.7Protection

Three cards, for a deck deck-audit prices with two single points of failure. Heroic Intervention answers a wrath or a targeted removal spell; Lightning Greaves and Swiftfoot Boots answer a targeted one and give haste. Greaves is not free, and stack 001 says where the cost lands. Shroud stops your own Hapatra and Yawgmoth from targeting whatever wears it, which is a real problem because the card that most wants haste is Devoted Druid. On the Druid the equipment restores the mana the loop otherwise does not make — four green a cycle with Greaves (equip {0}), three with Boots (equip {1}) — but it is main-phase-only, because equip is sorcery-speed and the returned Druid is a new object that must be re-equipped every single cycle after the equipment falls off. So this stage is also a mana line, and a fragile one. The One Ring's protection-from-everything turn is filed under fuel, where its draw does the heavier work; counting it here would make three look like four.

6.8Win condition

Three ways to end it, and the ceiling rests on one card. The drain class — Blood Artist, Zulaport Cutthroat, Bastion of Remembrance, Obelisk Spider and Sephiroth — turns deaths into life loss. Zulaport, Bastion and Obelisk Spider drain EACH opponent; Blood Artist names one player per death, which makes it a value card and a wrath tax rather than a table clock. Obelisk Spider is the outlier and the best of them here: it drains on counter PLACEMENT, so against the Druid steady state of four placements and one death per cycle it converts four times faster than any death trigger. Second, Walking Ballista. Mikaeus's +1/+1 is a layer-7c modification applied alongside counters, so a counterless Ballista is a 1/1 under him and 704.5f never fires — the 'remove the last counter and it dies' pattern is switched off and an external free outlet is mandatory. That is a three-card assembly, genuinely unbounded, and it needs no drain payoff at all. Third, Mikaeus + Devoted Druid, a true two-card infinite of deaths, -1/-1 placements, Snakes and drain. Price it honestly: Mikaeus is a one-card component at 7.1% of opening sevens, the pairing measures 2.5% assembled and 1.3% by turn 6 unassisted, 31.7% and 19.8% with a tutor, mean turn 5.69. Six of the nineteen listed two-card infinites route through Mikaeus plus a counter-neutral outlet and stack 001 refutes all six — step 10 gives a NON-TOKEN, non-Human creature exactly one undying return, and on the board that was actually checked, where the only body was a Snake TOKEN, it gives zero: the token ceases to exist before undying reaches the stack.
