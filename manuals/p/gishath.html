1General

What the machine is and what it is for.

This is a big-mana Dinosaur deck with two engines that share a board: an eight-mana commander who converts combat damage into free Dinosaurs off the top of the library, and a four-mana Human who converts a Dinosaur entering into one damage to everything, which four enrage bodies plus Wrathful Raptors then turn into cards, tokens, lands and exile — and the second engine is the one that is actually online, because goldfish never casts the first one by turn 10 in 55% of games.

What this model does not do (19 stated assumptions)
  • Simulates resource development, not full games (no interaction, no removal).
  • Draw every turn including turn 1 (multiplayer Commander).
  • Mulligan: keep 7-card hands with 2-5 lands; up to 2 fresh redraws, keep the last.
  • One land drop per turn when available.
  • LANDS ENTER UNTAPPED, ALWAYS, and the one in hand longest is the one played. A tapland costs nothing here and no land is ever chosen over another, so this model CANNOT rank two lands that make the same colours — `mana-analysis` and `mana-fit` are what answer a land question, and they are deterministic for exactly this reason.
  • Mana rocks ('{T}: Add') contribute from the turn after they are cast.
  • Commander cast on first affordable turn (highest spending priority).
  • Bodies count = creatures cast + tokens parsed from 'create ... token' text.
  • Target assembly counts cards DRAWN by a turn (cast cards still count).
  • CARD DRAW IS ONE A TURN UNLESS `model_draw` IS SET. With the flag on, four channels are modelled — a card's own ETB draw, an instant or sorcery that draws, an upkeep trigger, and an ARRIVAL trigger that draws when other creatures or tokens enter (Welcoming Vampire, Caretaker's Talent), which rides the same single door the ETB damage payoffs use. Activated draw (needs a spending policy this model has no opinion on), X-based draw (board-dependent), and death- or attack-triggered draw (no deaths, and attacks only under `model_combat`) are NOT modelled and the cards are named in `meta.card_advantage.draw_not_modelled`. A conditional trigger — 'if you control an artifact', 'you may draw' — is treated as unmodelled rather than assumed to be on: 39 of the 348 ETB-draw cards in the corpus carry one.
  • AN ARRIVAL-DRAW ENGINE NEVER SEES ITS OWN ARRIVAL. Welcoming Vampire is a 2/3 that draws 'whenever one or more OTHER creatures you control with power 2 or less enter'; registered before its own entry it passed its own gate and drew a card it does not draw, overstating edgar-vampires by 22% at turn eight. The deferral applies to every card and not only the ones worded 'other', which understates Tocasia's Welcome and two relatives by one draw on the turn they land.
  • ARRIVAL DRAW IS SMALLER WITHOUT `model_combat`, and legitimately so. The channel rides the one door onto the battlefield, which `model_combat` also uses to spawn token copies and ETB-payoff tokens — those are real additional arrivals that do not exist in the resource-only model. Measured on edgar-vampires: 1.264 extra cards by turn ten with both flags against 1.106 with draw alone. Before 2026-08-28 the gap was 1.264 against 0.323, because every call to that door sat inside `if model_combat:` and a draw-only deck silently lost three quarters of its arrival draws.
  • HELD-UP INTERACTION is reported as two series and both are FLOORS. This model casts everything it can afford every turn, so `interaction_castable` is what a pilot developing at full speed has left over, not what a pilot choosing to hold up two mana would have. A low figure is therefore a real finding and a high one is unambiguous good news.
  • Tutors are modeled as wildcards: a CAST tutor that fetches to hand or the top — including a TYPED one (Worldly Tutor, Sarkhan's Triumph), which the old literal 'a card' match missed entirely — fills ONE missing any_of group. Consumed once, mana paid, and a tutor that puts the card on top of the library costs a turn. An ACTIVATED, ETB or death-triggered tutor does not count, because this model only knows 'drawn and affordable'; nor does a land fetch, which is ramp and has no channel here yet. Reported as the *_assisted figures; the unassisted figures beside them exclude tutors entirely.
  • ENTERS-THE-BATTLEFIELD PAYOFFS are modelled: damage equal to the entering creature's power (Terror of the Peaks), X damage where X counts your board (Scourge of Valkas, Dragon Tempest), a token (Lathliss) and a token COPY of what entered (Miirym). Every arrival fires them — cast, token or copy — and a copy is itself an arrival, so they compound, which is the deck working rather than a bug. A payoff worded 'another NONTOKEN' does not re-trigger on its own tokens, which is the brake the rules already had. A chain is capped at 12 deep. X counts the whole board rather than one subtype: exact where the creatures are Dragons, generous otherwise.
  • The COMMANDER joins the battlefield when cast, attacks, and fires its own triggers. It used to be a mana sink and a flag — cast, then dropped — so a 10/10 flier contributed no power and never swung. Commander tax, death and recasting are still not modelled: it is cast once and it stays.
  • STATIC cost reduction IS modeled: a commander's eminence from the command zone (live from turn one, unremovable) and typed reducers once they are on the battlefield. It pays GENERIC only and is floored at the coloured pip count, because a discount can never pay a pip. A reduction that SCALES with a board state (Animar, Rakdos, Hamza) is refused rather than counted flat, and cost reduction on artifacts, noncreature spells or a colour is not modeled at all.
  • Rituals are not modeled (conservative).
  • Extra card draw is NOT modeled: one card per turn, always. That understates every deck, but by an amount proportional to how much draw it runs — so it is conservative WITHIN a deck and not neutral BETWEEN them. `meta.card_advantage` reports how much of each list this hides. Card advantage is measured nowhere in this suite.

6.1Mana

Thirty-seven entries whose whole job is an eight-drop, and the most reliable class in the deck: 'Ramp or fixing drawn' is 14 cards, 66.8% of opening sevens, 93.9% assembled, 86.9% by turn 6, mean turn 2.044. It still does not get there. Goldfish puts the commander's mean cast turn at 7.969, median 8, 8.3% by turn 6, and 5,533 of 10,000 iterations never cast him by turn 10 — a conservative bound, since the simulation models no cost reduction at all. Two pressures pull apart here. Land drops hold at 89.4% on turn 3 and fall to 52.6% by turn 6, so the back half of the climb rides five rocks — one of them Mirari's Wake, a land-doubler rather than a rock in play terms — and three dorks rather than lands. And the strain is COLOUR, not quantity: 48 mana sources against Burgess' 42, but colour totals of G26/R20/W20 against a 36-source yardstick, with Wakening Sun's Avatar asking WWW off twenty white. Two traps, and the second needs its WHOLE clause read. Unclaimed Territory's restricted mana is spells-only. Secluded Courtyard's adds 'or activate an ability of a creature source of the chosen type' — and Belbe's Portal is an Artifact, Monster Manual an Artifact — Book, so neither land's coloured mana pays those activations either. What pays there is each land's plain '{T}: Add {C}' against the Portal's generic {3} and the Manual's {1}, with the Manual's {G} coming from elsewhere. Courtyard is still the better land named on Dinosaur: it pays Zacama's {2}{R}/{2}{G}/{2}{W} and Bronzebeak Foragers' {X}{W}, which ARE creature sources of the chosen type. Rogue's Passage and Kessig Wolf Run are lands and are deliberately NOT here: in this deck their job is getting seven damage through, and they are filed under ignition.

6.2Ignition

The deck has two first windows and they cost 8 mana and 4 mana. THE HEADLINE: Gishath resolves and deals combat damage to a player, revealing that many cards and free-rolling every Dinosaur among them (stack 001). 'Vigilance, trample, haste' is PRINTED on him, so haste is not what he needs; he needs to CONNECT. Trample means a chump block only shrinks the reveal, Rogue's Passage guarantees all seven, Kessig Wolf Run makes it more. Otepec Huntmaster and Urza's Incubator are the only cards in the 99 that make a COMMAND-ZONE cast cheaper ({1} off Dinosaur spells, {2} off the chosen type); Regisaur Alpha's haste is for the Dinosaurs that arrive, not for the 7/6. THE CHEAP ONE: Forerunner of the Empire, four mana, 'whenever a Dinosaur you control enters, you may have this creature deal 1 damage to each creature' — optional, free, repeatable, and the only such source in the deck (stacks 003 and 005 are both ignited by it). Its ETB also puts a Dinosaur on top of the library — the commander's reveal, pre-loaded. Itzquinth is NOT a cheap version of it: two mana, no tutor, an ETB reading 'you may pay {2}. When you do, target Dinosaur you control deals damage equal to its power to another target creature' — one target, one enrage payoff at most, at that Dinosaur's power, so aiming a Polyraptor at your own board is 5 into a 5/5. It is here because a Dinosaur ENTERING puts Forerunner's trigger on the stack — its whole contribution in stack 005. Forerunner, not the commander, is the single point of failure: Gishath returns from the command zone at tax, Forerunner does not, and it is a Human Soldier outside every tribal protection layer here.

6.3Fuel

The cascade eats the top of the library, so the fuel is density plus stacking. Density is structural: 29 of the deck's 34 creature copies are Dinosaurs, and the five that are not include Forerunner itself, so the trigger can never free-roll the enrage web's own damage source. Stacking is deliberate — Congregation at Dawn puts three creature cards on top at INSTANT speed, which is the reveal pre-loaded rather than a tutor; Worldly Tutor does one; Enlightened Tutor finds Urza's Incubator, Garruk's Uprising or Descendants' Path. The other half of this stage skips the commander entirely: Descendants' Path REVEALS the top card each upkeep and lets you cast it free only if it is a creature sharing a type with a creature you already control — otherwise it goes to the bottom, so it is a coin flipped once a turn, gated on the board the deck is trying to establish; Belbe's Portal and Monster Manual put one from HAND onto the battlefield, and Huatli's Roar chapter III searches a Dinosaur to hand. That matters for how the declaration reads: all four sit in the 'Cost reducer or cheat-into-play drawn' target (6 cards, 67.3% assembled, 56.2% by turn 6), and none of the four can reach the command zone — but the target's other two members, Otepec Huntmaster and Urza's Incubator, are the command-zone discounts ignition names. The rate therefore fires on a body arriving OR on a cheaper commander and cannot say which; proposed_goldfish_edits[0] asks for that split. Bonehoard Dracosaur, Etali, Hunter's Insight and Rishkar's Expertise are the refills; three of the five cards with no entry in card_roles.json at all (Descendants' Path, Hunter's Insight, Rishkar's Expertise) are in this stage, which is why the card-advantage axis under-reads.

6.4Conversion

The engine proper, and it runs on two conditions. (A) SOMETHING ENTERS: Pantlaza discovers X equal to the entering Dinosaur's toughness; Garruk's Uprising and Vaultborn Tyrant share a power-4 gate — Uprising draws, Vaultborn gains 3 and draws, and stack 001's 3/3 Regisaur token misses BOTH. Declared 'Dinosaur payoff', 7 cards, 73.7% assembled, 62.3% by turn 6 — members that are not interchangeable: only Pantlaza and Forerunner of the Empire trigger on EACH Dinosaur entry, and PANTLAZA is the rationed one — stack 001 puts four of its triggers on the stack for one discover, while Forerunner's ping has no rider. Curious Altisaur and Quartzwood Crasher want combat damage to a player. (B) A DINOSAUR IS DEALT DAMAGE. Four carry Enrage: Ripjaw draws, Polyraptor copies itself, Ranging Raptors fetches a basic, Trapjaw Tyrant exiles an opponent's creature. Wrathful Raptors is NOT one of them — it triggers on ANY Dinosaur you control taking damage and throws that much at a non-Dinosaur target, which is how four damaged Dinosaurs, three at 1 and a dying Temple Altisaur at 13, became 16 points in stack 004. Declared at 5 cards, 60.8% assembled, 50.6% by turn 6, mean turn 3.053; stack 005 turns one optional ping into four enrage triggers, five payoffs banked and every creature of yours surviving. THERE IS NO FODDER STAGE, and that is the point of enrage: the resource converted is damage marked on your own board — no card, cleared at cleanup. The exceptions are priced elsewhere: Temple Altisaur dies to the sweeper in stack 004, Forerunner to its own third sweep in stack 003, which bounds the Polyraptor loop at seven — the real price of no fodder stage, since the maximum line pays for the board with ignition's single point of failure.

6.5Output

Eleven cards, and the least typal thing about the deck: 11 removal copies in an 18-card interactive suite, two sweepers, five permanent classes with nothing uncovered — though graveyard rests on Intrepid Paleontologist alone and land on Beast Within alone, so the coverage is thin, not surplus. Exactly ONE sweeper spares your side, and only conditionally: Wakening Sun's Avatar destroys all non-Dinosaur creatures only 'if you cast it from your hand', so a copy delivered by the commander's trigger, Belbe's Portal, Monster Manual or Descendants' Path is a vanilla 7/7, which makes the WWW off twenty white sources a requirement, not a preference. Austere Command does NOT: its creature modes read mana value 3 or less and 4 or greater, neither reads Dinosaur, and the deck's 34 creature copies split 11 against 23 — either mode takes a third or two thirds of your own board. Blasphemous Act, made one-sided by Temple Altisaur in protection, is the one single point of failure deck-audit priced here, doing two jobs: a 13-point wipe costing {R} off nine creatures, and the largest self-damage source the enrage web has. Stack 004 resolves it end to end and bounds it: all five opposing creatures die to state-based actions BEFORE the redirect targets are chosen (the stack's 'six lethally-damaged creatures' counts your own Temple Altisaur), and the four Wrathful triggers total 16 at one seat, taking that opponent from 26 to 10 and leaving the other at 31. That dual role is why an arrow runs BACKWARD from here into conversion: three more cards point damage at your own board (Triumphant Chomp, Trumpeting Carnosaur's {2}{R} discard mode, Zacama's {2}{R}), making the removal suite a second ignition for the enrage web when Forerunner is not on the table.

6.6Protection

Six cards, and the declared group measures only five of them: 'Protection held for the board' is the five instants at 59.0% assembled, 48.5% by turn 6, and Temple Altisaur is not in it. Read the five instants rather than counting them. Heroic Intervention's hexproof and Akroma's Will's protection from each colour answer targeted exile and bounce, so the class is broader than indestructible; Eerie Interlude is the only member that dodges a non-targeted mass exile; and Flawless Maneuver is the only one castable for nothing, which is the whole argument for it, because on the turn you tap out for an eight-drop it is the only shield you can hold up. A second duty nobody declares: the board-wide instants are the only cards that cover Forerunner of the Empire, a Human Soldier standing outside every tribal layer here. Temple Altisaur is the sixth and is not an enrage payoff — it is a static prevention effect ('prevent all but 1' of any damage to ANOTHER Dinosaur you control) and it is what turns a symmetrical 13-point wipe into a one-sided one. Stack 004 prices what that costs: Altisaur is not 'another Dinosaur' to itself, eats the unprevented 13 and dies. Named as this stage's single point of failure because the pair with Blasphemous Act is two named singletons and the LOWEST-measured target on the deck — 2.5% assembled, 1.4% by turn 6, mean turn 5.462, each half 7.1% of an opening seven.

6.7Win condition

The game ends in combat, with a board the other six stages built. Overwhelming Stampede is the alpha strike — trample plus +X/+X where X is your greatest power, which on a cascade board is a 7/6 commander or a 12/12 — and Garruk's Uprising has already given the whole team trample from the conversion stage. Ghalta, Primal Hunger costs {X} less for the total power of your creatures, so a cascade board casts a 12/12 for {G}{G}; Ghalta and Mavren converts an attack into a tapped-and-attacking X/X or a row of lifelinking Vampires; Regal Imperiosaur is a flat +1/+1 anthem for the other Dinosaurs; Carnage Tyrant is the one threat that beats both a counterspell and targeted removal. Pugnacious Hammerskull is the weakest card in the stage and is honest about it — a 6/6 for {2}{G} that stun-counters itself whenever it attacks while you control no other Dinosaur, which is a card asking you to already have the board it is meant to build. THE GAP WORTH STATING: none of the five checker-passed stacks resolves a kill. They resolve a cascade, two enrage webs, a bounded token loop and a sweeper explicitly measured as NOT lethal (16 redirected damage taking one opponent from 26 to 10). There is exactly ONE arrow into this stage — conversion to wincon, Polyraptor plus Overwhelming Stampede — and it is dashed, as is the single arrow back out, and the lethal threshold this deck actually needs is an open question rather than a figure.
