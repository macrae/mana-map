The Plan

What this deck is trying to do, and what it is assuming away. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

Every table you sit down at reads the mana cost, decides it has until turn eight, and relaxes. Here is what is actually true: Gishath, Sun's Avatar has a mean cast turn of 7.969, and in 5,533 of 10,000 goldfish games he never lands by turn ten at all. So stop treating turns one through seven as a waiting room. You are building two engines, and only one of them needs him.

Engine one is the swing, and it pays twice if you order it right. Stack 001 turns a single seven-damage connection into four Dinosaurs off the top of your library, five new bodies on the battlefield, three cards off Garruk's Uprising and a discover at X=6. Resolve Pantlaza's biggest-toughness trigger before Regisaur Alpha's token trigger. Get that backwards and the once-per-turn discover spends itself on a 3/3.

Engine two never attacks and never needs eight mana. Five cards convert damage on your own board into something real — Ripjaw Raptor, Polyraptor, Ranging Raptors, Trapjaw Tyrant and Wrathful Raptors — and stack 005 cashes one free point of damage five ways: a dead token, an opposing 3/3 exiled, a card, a tapped Forest and a fresh 5/5. They show up in 60.8% of games, half of those by turn six. They convert nothing on their own. Forerunner of the Empire is the switch, so play him before the payoffs, not after him.

Now the thing that beats you, said out loud: you deploy in one wave, so one sweeper takes the whole deck. Hold Flawless Maneuver over your tap-out turn — with your commander out it is the only shield that costs you nothing while you are spending eight. And check the board for Temple Altisaur before you cast Blasphemous Act. With the Altisaur down, your Dinosaurs take 1 apiece and walk out of a 13-damage sweep (stack 004). That pairing arrives in 2.5% of games, so never plan on it — just never miss it.

Keep or Ship

What a keepable hand looks like, and how often you get one. ◆ DATA-DERIVED★ COACHING

The table mulligans this deck like it is a combo deck. Don't. Opening sevens keep 77.1% of the time here and an average game costs you 0.28 of a mulligan. Your job is to spot the two hands that are genuinely broken, not to hunt for a perfect one.

The standard: two to four lands and one card that moves mana before turn four. Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Boros Signet, Talisman of Unity, Nature's Lore, Cultivate, Kodama's Reach, Skyshroud Claim, Topiary Stomper, Wayward Swordtooth — any one of them and you have a keep. Otepec Huntmaster and Urza's Incubator count for more than the rest, because they are the only two cards in the 99 that make the commander cheaper out of the command zone.

Snap-keep anything holding Forerunner of the Empire plus a Dinosaur. That is a two-card engine with no combat step and no eight-mana bill, and it is doing work on turn four while everyone else is still fixing.

Ship the shrine to the top end. Ghalta, Primal Hunger, Zacama, Primal Calamity, Wakening Sun's Avatar and Vaultborn Tyrant with no ramp under them is a second act with no first act. Six of your nonland entries cost eight or more; you cannot keep two of them on faith.

Ship the payoff pile too. Ripjaw Raptor, Polyraptor and Trapjaw Tyrant with nothing on board to hurt them are three blanks in a row. Damage is the enabler and the converters are not — a hand of converters is a hand of cards waiting for a different card.

And ship the land-light hands without arguing. 422 of 10,000 opening sevens come with zero lands and 1,793 with one. Those are what the button is for, no matter how good the Dinosaurs look.

Gishath connects

what seven damage actually unleashes

The clause that decides this is CR 603.2h: an ability reading 'Do this only once each turn' triggers only if its controller has not already taken that action this turn. Pantlaza, Sun-Favored triggers four times off one simultaneous entry and discovers exactly once.

Which once is a choice, and it is yours. CR 603.3b puts your triggers on the stack in any order you choose, and CR 405.5 resolves the top one first. Carnage Tyrant's trigger on top gives a discover of X=6. Regisaur Alpha's token trigger resolving earlier spends the once-per-turn action on a 3-toughness token, and the later trigger then does not trigger at all.

Garruk's Uprising is arithmetic rather than judgment. CR 603.2c lets one event trigger an ability once per qualifying occurrence: three of the four entering creatures have power 4 or greater. Ravenous Sailback, at 3 power, does not, and neither does the 3/3 token.

The holding: four Dinosaur creature cards enter, three cards are drawn, one discover resolves, and it resolves at X=6 only because it resolved before the token existed.

Marauding Raptor + Polyraptor

infinite tokens, or a mandatory draw?

The clause is CR 732.4, and it is nine words long: 'If a loop contains only mandatory actions, the game is a draw.' Everything here turns on a word that is absent from both cards — 'may'.

Marauding Raptor's ping is not optional. Polyraptor's enrage is not optional. And 2 damage never resolves the loop by killing anything, because damage is marked on a creature rather than on a card (CR 302.7), so every copy enters fresh and is pinged exactly once before the next one exists.

The record is explicit that the pilot has no exit from inside this position: no instant, no sacrifice outlet, no choice anywhere in the chain. Opponents do receive priority between resolutions under CR 117.3b and may break it by killing Marauding Raptor — at a token count they choose, not one you choose.

The holding: this pairing is a drawn game for every player under CR 732.4 and CR 104.4b, not a win, and Marauding Raptor is not in this 99. The finding generalises further than the pair does. An optional trigger is a brake; a copy loop with a mandatory pinger has none.
